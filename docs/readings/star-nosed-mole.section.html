What the Star-nosed Mole means

In the first chapter of the Zhuangzi, a hermit turns down a throne with one line: 偃鼠飲河，不過滿腹 (yǎn shǔ yǐn hé, bùguò mǎn fù). The mole drinks from the river and takes only a bellyful1. Most translators give the animal as a mole1. You were born knowing that measure. Enough is a real amount to you.

Three parts. Feel your way in.

Touch first. Then decide.

Your temperament

Virgo is mutable Earth, ruled by Mercury2. It checks everything and finds it hard to switch off2,3. The Pig is yin Water, kind and calm under pressure4,5. In you the sorter meets the feeler. You take in a lot and say little. Then you move fast.

The mole does it too

The mole shifts its star so the two small rays by its mouth touch each thing first6. Under water it blows bubbles and breathes them back in, eight to twelve a second, to smell7,8. The biologist Kenneth Catania says the moles use the star the way we use our eyes9. You read people like that, by the grain of a voice and by what shifted since last week.

One branch. One bellyful.

Your appetite

The Pig enjoys the good life4. Virgo's old correspondences are the harvest and the granary10. So you enjoy the meal and count the grain. Warm bread. A soft bed. Then you stop. Others keep reaching. You leave the river.

Your reserve

In winter the mole's tail swells with stored fat7,11. It carries its reserve close and small. Yours sits the same way, where nobody has to see it.

Your blind spot

The Pig's known weak spot is trusting too easily4. You can miss a motive while you study the details. Check the reason as closely as you check the facts.

A small, warm burrow with space for two.

You love through attention, and it shows in the small things. When you are hurt, you withdraw and say nothing for a while. The old wheel gives you three easy burrows to share.
