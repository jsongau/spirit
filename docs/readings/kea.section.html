What the Kea gets into

Say the bird's name out loud and you are doing its call. Kea copies the sound it makes, keee aaa1,2. The Waitaha people held it among their kaitiaki, the guardians of the people, beside the harrier and the morepork1. Loud, nosy, and on the guard roster. That is you.

Three parts. Poke at them in any order.

Air that asks. Metal that handles.

Your temperament

Gemini is mutable Air, ruled by Mercury, the planet of quick talk and new information3,4. The Monkey is yang Metal, read as witty, mischievous and a fast learner5,6. Put the two together and every question heads straight for your hands. You understand a thing best once it is in pieces on the table, and you narrate the teardown while you do it.

The kea does it too

The kea is the world's only mountain parrot, at home in New Zealand's Southern Alps2. It investigates with its beak, its tongue and its claws7. Car accessories and ski lodge gear come apart under that attention; researchers call it opportunistic exploration8. Hut stories add stolen nails, hidden away sorted by size7. Someone always has to check how the thing works. You volunteer.

Three birds for your flock, and one to give room.

In love your laugh does the courting. Researchers played wild kea a recording of the play call, and the birds that heard it played more and for longer9. The old Chinese pairings put three of them in your flock.

Get paid for the poking.

At work

Pick work where taking things apart is the job. Testing, repair and security research all pay someone to find the weak latch before anyone else does.

In a gang

Young kea travel together in flocks for two or three years before they settle8. Their pecking order is not necessarily a straight line8. You do best in a loose crew where the youngest can still win a round.

Blamed, then crownedWhen sheep farming reached the high country in the 1870s, farmers blamed the kea, a bounty followed, and an estimated 150,000 birds were shot before full protection came in 19867. In 2017 New Zealand voted it Bird of the Year, with 7,311 votes11. Same bird. Same cheek.

Keep poking. Sort the nails by size afterward, so they know it was you.
