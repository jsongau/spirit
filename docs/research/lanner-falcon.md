## The Lanner Falcon as a symbol
- **Chinese, Book of Changes (hexagram 40 解 xiè, top line).** 公用射隼于高墉之上 gōng yòng shè sǔn yú gāo yōng zhī shàng: a prince shoots at a falcon (隼 sǔn) on top of a high wall and hits it; 'in every way advantageous' (Legge). The hexagram is about release from difficulty. For a Sagittarius, the archer sign, this is the rare classical text where an archer and a falcon meet in one line. Our reading: the crossing is both the bow and the bird on the wall. [s9]
- **Chinese, Book of Songs (采芑 Cǎi Qǐ, Xiaoya).** 鴥彼飛隼，其飛戾天，亦集爰止 yù bǐ fēi sǔn, qí fēi lì tiān, yì jí yuán zhǐ: 'rapid is the flight of the hawk, soaring to the heavens, and again descending and settling in its place.' The falcon in a war ode: it goes high and far, then comes back to its perch. A clean image for Sagittarian range with a home ledge. [s10]
- **Chinese, Book of Changes, Xici commentary (the page's proverb).** 二人同心，其利斷金 èr rén tóng xīn, qí lì duàn jīn, 'when two people share one heart, their edge cuts metal', from 周易 繫辭上 Zhōuyì Xìcí shàng. The page's attribution to the Book of Changes is correct. Note: in the five phases Metal is what checks Wood, so 'cutting metal' is a neat Wood-animal image (our reading). [s12]
- **Medieval English falconry (Book of St Albans, 1486).** The hawk-by-rank list gives the lanner to the squire: below the knight's saker and the earl's peregrine. The list is described as what each rank was 'supposedly appropriate' for, not practice. Our reading: the squire is the young rider attached to one knight, a partner role, and a travelling one, which suits Sagittarius. [s11]
- **Ancient Egypt.** Wikipedia says the lanner, or perhaps the peregrine, was likely the sacred falcon of ancient Egypt, and that Ra and Horus were often shown with lanner-falcon heads. Hedged in the source itself; use only with 'likely' and never as settled. [s5]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'falcon spirit animal = focus, vision, freedom' and any lanner-specific totem meaning: no traditional source found. Also unsourced: Arabic falconry names or lore specific to the lanner (not opened). 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Fire (Sagittarius) on yang Wood (Tiger). Wood feeds Fire, so the Tiger's drive is fuel for the Sagittarian need to range. The real bird adds the twist: it ranges wide over open country but nests on the same cliff year after year, often switching ledges (s7), and on hard prey it hunts with its mate, one flushing and one striking (s6). The Lanner is the restless one with a fixed partner and a fixed cliff.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: reading open ground fast, an honest eye for who does which job, the Tiger's nerve to commit once the line is chosen, Jupiter generosity toward the one partner who earned it, real stamina for long low coursing flights rather than one showy dive.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Sagittarian restlessness plus Tiger impatience. Ranging so long that the partner stops waiting at the ledge; deciding alone and presenting it as a plan for two; the Tiger's trouble showing tenderness (s4) read as coldness; overconfidence that a fast bird can take anything.
- **Love.** Our reading: loyal to one, slow to say so. Courts in motion (pairs perform synchronized soaring before breeding, s5) rather than in speeches. Needs a partner who does not take the wide circuits personally and who has their own skill, because the bond is built on two different jobs.
- **Money.** Our reading: Jupiter expansion with Tiger appetite: earns well in bursts, spends on travel and gear. Does best with a partner or advisor who handles the steady side, the way the female often makes the catch the male set up (s5).
- **Conflict.** Our reading: Sagittarius is blunt, the Tiger is quick to flare, so fights are short and loud. The risk is flying off to cool down without saying you will return. Practice: say where you are going and when you will be back on the ledge.

- **Journalism or investigation in the field.** Our reading: range plus a clean strike on the one story worth taking.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 隼 shown for the Lanner Falcon.
  隼 sǔn is the classical and modern word for falcon, used in the Book of Songs and Book of Changes passages above. Correct for a falcon. Pinyin is not shown next to the header glyph; add sǔn. [s9] [s10]
- **WRONG**: Intro: the lanner is 'one of the very few raptors on earth documented to hunt cooperatively with a partner'.
  Cooperative hunting is documented in the genera Aquila, Haliaeetus, Parabuteo, Hieraaetus and Falco; the Aplomado Falcon hunts cooperatively on 66 per cent of occasions and the Brown Falcon on 16 per cent (Mooney 1989). The lanner is a good example, not a rare exception. Say 'one of the falcons known to hunt in pairs'. [s8] [s5]
- **OK (cut the rarity clause)**: 01 Cooperative Hunting: pairs documented hunting cooperatively, one driving prey toward the other, coordinating stoops in real time; 'extremely rare among falcons'.
  Pair hunting is well supported: one bird flushes, the other grabs (Peregrine Fund); high success on jackdaws, male usually attacks first and female usually makes the catch (Wikipedia citing Leonardi 1999); Eastern Cape pairs often hunted together and these hunts appeared more successful (Stephenson 2001). 'Extremely rare among falcons' is WRONG (see Mooney 1989). Leonardi full text could not be opened, so do not quote success figures. [s5] [s6] [s7] [s8] [s13]
- **OK (with softening)**: 02 The Long Pair Bond: long-term pair bonds, return to the same nesting cliff for multiple years, elaborate aerial mutual displays.
  Stephenson: 'The same nest sites may be used year after year, especially cliff sites', and pairs often switched ledges on the same cliff. Colour-ringed pairs that had bred before did better than new pairs. Wikipedia: synchronized mutual soaring and flapping before breeding, pairs power-diving together near nests. 'Long-term pair bond' is implied, not measured; say 'pairs often keep the same cliff for years'. Also note lanners often use old crow nests and other species' nests (s5, s6). [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **OK (fix the word 'stoop')**: 03 The Coordinated Stoop: one bird drives prey while the other positions for the intercept, real-time adjustment to the partner's line.
  The flush and strike split is sourced. But Wikipedia says the lanner 'usually hunts by horizontal pursuit' rather than the peregrine's stoop from height; Peregrine Fund lists both diving from above and low flights along slopes. Calling the pair hunt a 'coordinated stoop' overstates the dive; say 'coordinated attack' or 'coordinated chase'. [s5] [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: 04 Strategic Over Fast: falconers historically note the lanner as more strategic than the peregrine, prized for tactical judgment over raw speed.
  No source found for falconers ranking the lanner as 'more strategic'. Sources confirm only that it is commonly used and bred for falconry and differs from the peregrine by hunting mainly in horizontal pursuit. The Book of St Albans ranks it below the peregrine (squire vs earl). Cut, or replace with the horizontal-pursuit fact. [s5] [s6] [s11]
- **WRONG (overstated)**: 05 Arid Terrain Specialist: specializes in open, arid environments, desert edges, semi-arid scrub, savanna.
  It is an open-country generalist, not an arid specialist: 'open habitats, from forest edges to deserts', most common in open savannah and sour grasslands (Wikipedia); 'deserts, forests, woods, plains, and savannas', needing cliffs for nesting (Peregrine Fund). Say 'open country, from desert edge to grassland, with a cliff nearby'. [s5] [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: 06 In-flight Food Passes: bonded pairs transfer prey mid-flight.
  None of the sources opened describes mid-air food passes in lanners. Stephenson records food transfers at the nest site and males provisioning the female and chicks. Mid-air passes are known in some other raptors, so this may be borrowed. Say 'the male brings food to the female at the nest' or cut. [s7] [s6] [s5]
- **OK**: Intro: medium-sized falcon of Africa, southeast Europe and the Middle East.
  Breeds in Africa, southeast Europe and just into Asia; mostly resident with local movements tied to rains and altitude. [s5]
- **OK**: Proverb: 二人同心，其利斷金 from the Book of Changes, paired with the Tiger as our own reading.
  Source is 周易 繫辭上 Zhōuyì Xìcí shàng. The page labels the Tiger pairing as its own reading, which is honest. [s12]
- **OK (none found)**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) star claims.
  The page only links to casting a chart from lunar birth date and hour; no star is assigned from the sign pair.
