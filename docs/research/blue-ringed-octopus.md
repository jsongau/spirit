## The Blue-ringed Octopus as a symbol
- **Australian medical history.** The first recorded Australian death was in 1954: Kirk Dyson-Holland, a 21-year-old sailor, died within about two hours of a bite (Flecker and Cotton 1955). In 1962 Arthur Ricketts felt 'no sensation of sting or bite' and survived after about an hour of artificial breathing. The painless bite and the need only to keep someone breathing are the real story under the page's 'bite you don't feel'. [s8]
- **Film (Octopussy, 1983).** In the James Bond film Octopussy the blue-ringed octopus is the emblem of a secret order of women bandits and smugglers. Pop culture, low weight, but it is species-specific and fits Leo glamour over Snake secrecy. [s5]
- **Hawaiian (Kumulipo).** The god Kanaloa is listed as the octopus among the four divinities of the eighth era. Dixon and Bastian read one verse as the octopus being the lone survivor or observer of an earlier world; Beckwith said she reached 'no satisfactory translation'. Octopus in general, not this species. Use with the translation dispute stated, and do not quote the chant itself. [s9]
- **Japanese.** 蛸 is the everyday Japanese kanji for octopus, read tako. In Chinese it is mainly a scientific-name character (read shāo), which explains why the page's header looks right to some readers and odd to others. [s10] [s11]
- **Chinese (Snake half; already on page).** Page proverb 打草驚蛇 dǎ cǎo jīng shé, 'beat the grass and startle the snake', labeled as Zodi Animal's own pairing. Honest label; keep. (no source)
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Kiribati octopus god Na Kika, octopus 'spirit animal' meanings, and blue-ringed octopus totem claims: only on godchecker, mythlok and totem blogs. No primary or academic source opened. (no source)

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (reading) / imprecise (glyph)**: Header hanzi 蛸 on rail and serial, voiced and labeled 'xiāo'
  蛸 does mean octopus, but in that sense Mandarin reads it shāo (as in the genus 豹紋蛸屬 bàowén shāo shǔ); xiāo is the reading for 螵蛸 piāoxiāo, the mantis egg case. As a standalone word 蛸 is Japanese (tako) more than Chinese. Best fix: 藍環章魚 lánhuán zhāngyú ('blue-ring octopus', used by Taiwan's National Museum of Natural Science), or keep 蛸 and correct the pinyin and audio to shāo. Dictionary source is unreviewed; a Chinese reader should confirm. [s11] [s12] [s13] [s10]
- **OK**: Skin changes color and texture to match rock or coral, via chromatophores controlled by the nervous system
  Standard cephalopod camouflage. Mäthger et al. add that during the warning display the chromatophores around the rings darken while the body pales. [s7] [s5]
- **OK**: Rings stay faint until handled or threatened, then flash brilliant iridescent blue as an aposematic warning
  About 50 to 60 rings flash within a third of a second (Mäthger et al. 2012). Mechanism worth using: the blue comes from iridophores in muscular pockets that open and close, not from pigment. ADW describes the rings as warning coloration. [s7] [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: Saliva carries tetrodotoxin; a bite can go unnoticed; no antivenom
  Wikipedia: venom includes TTX made by bacteria in the posterior salivary gland; bites 'tiny and often painless'; 'No blue-ringed octopus antivenom is available.' ADW agrees. [s5] [s6] [s8]
- **WRONG (size)**: Adult greater blue-ringed octopus weighs 10 to 100 grams and measures roughly 10 centimetres
  Weight 10 to 100 g matches ADW. Length is understated: ADW gives about 20 cm at maximum arm spread; Wikipedia gives 12 to 20 cm for the genus. Rewrite: 'about 12 to 20 cm across the arms'. The phrase 'can weigh as little as 10 to 100 grams' also reads oddly. 'One of the most venomous marine animals' is supported (Wikipedia). [s6] [s5] [s7]
- **OK**: Not aggressive, hides or flees when disturbed; bites almost always defensive after handling
  ADW: not aggressive, hides or flees, reputation of biting only if provoked (though it is territorial toward rivals). Wikipedia: flees first, bites if cornered and touched. [s6] [s5]
- **OK (single soft source)**: Female lays one clutch of 60 to 100 eggs, broods about 50 days without eating, dies shortly after hatching
  Matches ADW for H. lunulata, a student-written account. Wikipedia's genus-level figure differs (about 50 eggs, about six months, likely a different species). Keep it species-labelled and say 'about'. [s6] [s5]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Habitat section: 'keeps a single crevice as a base to return to between hunts'
  ADW says it hides in crevices, shells and discarded cans; no source found for one fixed home crevice. Soften to 'hides in crevices and empty shells'. [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Source list: IUCN Red List, Hapalochlaena lunulata
  Not opened in this pass; IUCN pages block automated fetch. A person should confirm the listing exists and its category. 
- **OK**: Proverb 打草驚蛇 (dǎ cǎo jīng shé) labeled as Zodi Animal's own pairing with the Snake year
  Real idiom, honestly labeled, pinyin present.
