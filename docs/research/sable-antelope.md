## The Sable Antelope as a symbol
- **Angola (giant sable, Hippotragus niger variani).** Treated as totemic by communities in its range (local names kolo and sumbakaloko, language not stated by the source) and revered before Frank Varian described it in 1916. First animal given full legal protection in colonial Angola; since 1975 a national symbol on stamps, banknotes and passports, emblem of TAAG airline, and the nickname of the national football team, the Palancas Negras. Many Angolans regarded hunting it as close to sacrilege, one reason any survived the 27-year civil war; photographed again on trail cameras in Cangandala National Park in January 2004, all females, until a bull was found by dung DNA in 2009. Strongest item: a whole nation guarding one stubborn animal. [s8]
- **Heraldry and naming (English, Latin, Greek).** 'Sable' is the heraldic word for black, after the mature bull's coat; Hippotragus means 'horse-goat' and niger is Latin for black. Note: the Goat in the genus name is a naming coincidence, not a zodiac link; do not use it as a hook. [s7]
- **Southern Africa, state emblem.** The sable appeared on the Rhodesian flag and coat of arms and on the arms of the Federation of Rhodesia and Nyasaland; Zimbabwe's rugby union team carries its name. Use with care: Rhodesia is a loaded political symbol. The rugby nickname is the safe line. [s5]
- **Chinese (Tiger half).** 虎落平陽被犬欺 hǔ luò píng yáng bèi quǎn qī: a tiger come down to the flat plain gets bullied by dogs, meaning strength fails off its own ground. The sable avoids extensive open land and keeps to the woodland-grassland edge (s6), so this proverb fits the crossing with no stretching. Source is a learner site; it gives no classical origin, so present it as a common saying. [s10] [s6]
- **Ecology vs legend (a useful shadow).** Estes (1991) as quoted by Milewski: the horns and stabbing technique 'do not deter predators from hunting them'; Capon (2011) blames lions for driving the Kruger decline. The fearsome-stand image is partly legend. Our reading: the Tiger's shadow, trusting the weapon more than the ground. [s9]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'sable antelope spirit animal' meanings (courage, nobility) on totem blogs; no San, Shona or other traditional source found linking a specific meaning to the sable. San rock art searches returned no sable-specific interpretation I could open. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: yang Wood (Tiger) inside fixed Earth (Taurus). Wood controls Earth, so the Tiger's push keeps Taurus from going soft, while Taurus slows the Tiger into something that holds ground instead of charging. The Sable is courage that stays near water and cover: brave on its own patch, careful about which patch.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: steady nerve under real pressure, a protective streak that does not need an audience, patience with slow rank and seniority, taste for quality (the sable takes the grass leaf and leaves the stem, s6).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: 虎落平陽. Strong on home ground, brittle off it. Trusting the horns, meaning reputation or force, to do work that only the right ground can do (s9). Stubbornness doubled: Taurus digs in, the Tiger will not back down.
- **Love.** Our reading: Venus-ruled loyalty with a Tiger's guard. Slow to let anyone into the herd, then fiercely protective. Needs a partner who respects their territory and their routine, and who does not test them for sport.
- **Money.** Our reading: Taurus saver with a Tiger's appetite for one bold position. Keeps the base close, like water within two miles, and takes risk only from secure footing.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: with peers, goes down to the knees and wrestles it out face to face (male horn-wrestling, s6, s7); rarely fatal, rarely forgotten. With outside threats, stands rather than flees once the critical distance is crossed (s7). Practice: pick the ground before the fight.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Security, emergency management, park ranging or anti-poaching work.** Our reading: a guard who works the edge of the group, tied to the bull defending 300 to 500 metres around the herd (s6).
- **Estate, land or property management.** Our reading: Taurus Earth and the sable's fussiness about habitat; choosing ground is the job.
- **Craft trades with a long apprenticeship (joinery, stonework, instrument making).** Our reading: seniority-based rank (s6) and Venus taste; respect earned by years.
- **Litigation or negotiation.** Our reading: the Tiger's nerve, Taurus's refusal to move; best when they have chosen the battlefield.
- **Conservation and wildlife management.** Our reading: the giant sable's rescue story (s8) as a calling; protecting what is rare and stubborn.

## Candidate lines
1. Against your own kind you drop to your knees and wrestle horn to horn. From the outside it looks like bowing, and it is the furthest thing from it.
2. You keep to the strip where the woodland meets the grass. Out on the wide plain you know the old warning about tigers and dogs, and you would rather not learn it twice.
3. Somewhere within two miles of you there is always water: one person, or one quiet habit. When you wander past it, your horns get sharper than the moment needs.
4. Your black came in slowly, the way a bull's coat darkens near its third year, and the people who knew you as chestnut still talk to the younger version.
5. In your herd the order was settled by who arrived first and stayed longest, and you trust that ranking more than anyone's horns, including your own.
6. You take the leaf and leave the stem. Your one luxury is being exact about what you let in.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (range) / UNVERIFIABLE (most antelope)**: Both sexes grow long, ridged horns; most antelope species reserve horns for males; a mature bull's horns run 130 to 165 cm, a cow's 60 to 100.
  Both sexes horned: OK. Male range in all three sources is about 80 or 81 to 165 cm, not 130 to 165 (s5, s6, s7); female 60 to 102 OK. 'Most antelope species reserve horns for males' is unsourced and shaky (females are horned in many bovid tribes); cut it or say 'unlike many antelope'. [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **WRONG (context)**: Under serious predator threat the sable drops onto its carpal 'knees' and sweeps its horns; the same posture appears in male dominance fights and genuine predator defense.
  Every source describes kneeling in male horn-wrestling fights only (s5, s6, s7). Wikipedia says this explicitly and does not describe kneeling against predators. Predator defense in sources is lowering the horns, feinting and stabbing, flailing forehooves (s9 quoting Estes). Keep kneeling for rivals; drop it from predator defense. This is the page's archetype ('the Kneeling Sovereign'), so the rewrite must reframe it. [s5] [s6] [s7] [s9]
- **OK (first half) / UNVERIFIABLE (second half)**: Lions rarely press an attack on a healthy adult; documented cases of a cornered sable wounding or killing a lion outright.
  ADW: 'Lions seldom attack adults, because of their size and the formidable fighting abilities' (s6). No documented sable-kills-lion case found; Estes (1991) says lions are 'sometimes fatally stabbed' by hippotragine antelopes generally, from hunters' accounts, and also that the horns 'do not deter predators from hunting them'; Capon (2011) blames lions for the Kruger decline (all via s9). Wikipedia: some predators have died in fights (s5). Soften to 'predators have been killed in fights' and drop 'a lion learns to skip'. [s5] [s6] [s9]
- **OK**: Calves and young of both sexes share a chestnut coat; males darken around three years into glossy black with white face and belly; females outside the southernmost populations stay chestnut.
  Males turn black after about three years (s5); females chestnut except southern populations where they go brown-black (s6); white belly, cheeks and 'eyebrows' (s5, s6). Small fix: calves under two months are light tan, not chestnut. [s5] [s6]
- **OK (size, rank) / UNVERIFIABLE (displays, bull role)**: Herds of roughly 15 to 25 females and young keep a rank order maintained mostly through small, low-intensity displays; the single territorial bull defends ground but does not run the herd's order.
  ADW: 15 to 25 females and young, one dominant male, female 'rank hierarchy based on seniority' (s6). Wikipedia and Ultimate Ungulate give 10 to 30 and describe herds as 'led by' an adult male (s5, s7), which cuts against 'does not run the order'. 'Low-intensity displays' unsourced. Say 'rank among females follows seniority' and stop. [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **OK (with softening)**: Favors mixed grassland and wooded cover, avoids extensive open land, grazes a narrow band of young grass, stays within roughly two miles of water.
  ADW: mix of savanna woodland and grassland, avoids 'extensive open lands', grazes 40 to 140 mm above ground taking only the leaf, 'no sable antelope will travel more then 2 miles' from water (s6). The two-mile line is ADW's absolute; soften to 'usually stays within about two miles'. Note Wikipedia's '90% tree leaves' contradicts ADW's '90% grass'; ADW matches the general literature, do not use Wikipedia's diet line. [s6] [s5]
- **WRONG (too generic)**: Header hanzi 羚 (rail__han and serial-han).
  羚 líng means antelope or gazelle in general, not this species. Chinese Wikipedia names Hippotragus niger 黑馬羚 (traditional; simplified 黑马羚) hēi mǎ líng, 'black horse antelope' (s11). Use 黑馬羚 with pinyin, or keep 羚 only if labelled 'antelope'. [s11]
- **WRONG (no traditional basis)**: Purple Star section: Wu Qu (武曲 Wǔqū) and Qi Sha (七殺 Qīshā) 'tend to sit close to the Life palace' for a Taurus born in a Tiger year.
  Zi Wei Dou Shu charts are cast from lunar birth date and hour, not the Western sun sign; no traditional source assigns stars by sun sign plus year animal (same finding as REVIEW-mojo.md). Relabel as our own symbolic pairing or cut. [s12]
- **OK (with nuance)**: Hero: 'known for facing predators down rather than outrunning them'.
  Sources: turns to fight rather than flee (s5); smaller critical distance than comparable species, but also runs up to 57 km/h when pursued (s7). Fine as a tendency; avoid absolutes. [s5] [s7]
