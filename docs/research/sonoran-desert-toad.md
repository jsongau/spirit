## The Sonoran Desert Toad as a symbol
- **Chinese (the header glyph 蟾 chán, toad).** In the older version of the moon myth, Chang'e (嫦娥 Cháng'é) swallows the elixir, flies to the Moon and becomes a toad (蟾蜍 chánchú); the account is attributed to Zhang Heng's 靈憲 Língxiàn (2nd century CE). The toad in the Moon is why 蟾 is also a literary word for the Moon. [s11]
- **Chinese, Western Han (Mawangdui tomb 1, Hunan).** The T-shaped silk painting from Lady Dai's tomb shows the toad and the jade hare in the heavenly world beside Chang'e. Toad and Moon were paired more than 2,000 years ago. Good, citable visual anchor for a nocturnal animal. [s10]
- **Chinese folk and feng shui.** 金蟾 jīn chán, the three-legged money toad with a coin in its mouth, linked to the Daoist immortal 劉海蟾 Liú Hǎichán, kept as a wealth charm and said to appear at the full moon. Source is weak (Wikipedia page tagged for citations); use one plain sentence at most, or skip. [s12]
- **Sonoran Desert households (recorded anecdote, not tradition).** The Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum records a Tucson-area family whose toad came in through the dog door every summer for five years; they took its arrival as the sign the summer rains were close. A real, sourced image of the toad as a rain herald, but it is one family, not a culture. [s2]
- **Tohono O'odham (context only, not toad lore).** For the Tohono O'odham the arrival of the summer rains traditionally marks the new year. I found NO sourced O'odham story about this toad. Do not write one; at most use the rains-as-new-year fact to frame the monsoon. [s13]
- **Mesoamerican and 'ancient ritual' claims.** Unsourced, do not use. Claims that ancient Mesoamericans or the Seri (Comcaac) used this toad ritually are disputed: Davis and Weil (1992) found the art evidence insufficient, and the modern 'revived ritual' story is tied to one promoter whose ceremonies are linked to deaths (Horák et al. 2019; de Greef 2022, as summarized in Wikipedia). Leave all of it off the page. [s1]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'toad spirit animal means change or cleansing' meanings from totem blogs. No traditional source located. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Air (Aquarius) meets yang Water (Rat). Aquarius holds an idea steady and slightly apart from the crowd; the Rat stores and waits for the opening. The toad's real year bears it out: active from late May, breeding only after the summer rains fill the pools, quiet underground in borrowed rodent burrows the rest of the time (s2, s3). The crossing is the patient keeper of an unusual plan, who moves the moment the water comes.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: long patience without losing the thread; independent thinking that does not need a crowd; timing; the ability to live lean (it lives mostly on beetles) and still outlast showier creatures; a defense that works without a fight.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Aquarian detachment plus Rat caution can turn into waiting past the season. Also a chemistry others want to take from you: the real toad is trapped and milked by people who want its secretions, and populations in Mexico have been hit hard (s1). The shadow is being valued for what you produce instead of who you are.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow to surface, then present all at once. Air wants a friend first; the Rat is loyal once it commits. The real male's call is a weak whistle, likened to a ferryboat (s2); love here is quiet signal, not display. Needs a partner who does not take silence in the dry months as absence.
- **Money.** Our reading: a Rat saver with an Aquarian streak for unusual bets. Keeps reserves underground and spends in bursts when the window opens. Risk: hoarding so long the opportunity dries up.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: does not strike first. The toad's poison sits in the glands behind its eyes and only works on whatever grabs it (s1, s2). Crosses this one and you learn once. Practice: say the boundary before someone has to bite it.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (generic)**: Header hanzi 蟾 (rail__han and serial-han), voiced as chán
  蟾 chán means toad (as in 蟾蜍 chánchú) and carries the Moon-toad association, so it fits. It is not the species name: the Chinese name of Incilius alvarius is 科羅拉多河蟾蜍 kēluólāduō hé chánchú (Colorado River toad). Keep 蟾 with pinyin; if a species name is wanted, use that. [s5] [s11]
- **WRONG**: Spends nine to ten months each year in estivation, sealed in a cocoon of shed skin and mucus
  The Desert Museum gives the active season as late May to September, so it is inactive roughly seven to eight months, not nine to ten. No source I could open documents a cocoon for this species (the Desert Museum describes a skin cocoon for the casque-headed frog, a different animal). ADW says it often uses rodent burrows. Rewrite: 'active from late May to September, spending the rest of the year underground, often in old rodent burrows'. [s2] [s3]
- **WRONG**: Intro: appears at the surface only during the brief monsoon window of July and August
  Active from late May to September; breeding begins in July with the rains and can run into August. The surface season is longer than the breeding window. [s2] [s1]
- **WRONG / UNVERIFIABLE**: Emerges within hours of the first heavy rainfall and compresses mating, egg-laying and tadpole development into about six weeks
  'Within hours' is unsourced; adults are already active before the monsoon. Females lay eggs one to three days after rain (Wikipedia, citing Audubon guide), up to about 8,000 eggs. Tadpole development alone takes six to ten weeks (Desert Museum), so the whole cycle cannot fit six weeks. [s1] [s2]
- **WRONG (superlative) / UNVERIFIABLE (mass)**: Up to 190 mm long and 750 g; the largest native toad on the North American continent
  Length about 190 mm is fine (Wikipedia; ADW gives 110 to 187 mm). 750 g: no source found. 'Largest native toad in North America' is false: the cane toad is native from the Rio Grande Valley of Texas south, and reaches 24 cm. Wikipedia: largest toad in the US apart from the cane toad; Desert Museum: 'one of the largest toads native to North America'. Use the Desert Museum wording. [s1] [s3] [s4] [s2]
- **PARTLY WRONG**: Parotoid secretions contain 5-MeO-DMT and bufotenin, compounds that 'in other mammals' produce altered states; 'a chemistry no other animal carries'
  The two compounds are correct (Erspamer et al. 1967, cited in Wikipedia). 'Other mammals' is an error: a toad is an amphibian. 'No other animal carries' is unsupported: bufotenin occurs in several Bufo species; Wikipedia says only this toad has it in psychoactive amounts. The secretion also contains cardiac glycosides and can kill dogs (Desert Museum). Per brief, keep it brief and factual, e.g. 'The glands behind its eyes make a toxin strong enough to kill a dog that mouths it.' No mention of human use. [s1] [s2]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Returns to the same breeding pool in the same wash year after year; homing fidelity documented across individual lives
  No study on I. alvarius homing found. The only related item is one household's toad returning through a dog door for five summers (Desert Museum anecdote). Cut the claim, or use the anecdote as an anecdote. [s2]
- **WRONG**: Tadpoles metamorphose in as few as 18 to 24 days, among the fastest of any North American toad
  Desert Museum: 6 to 10 weeks. ADW: larval period believed to be about 1 month. The fast-metamorphosis record belongs to Couch's spadefoot (about 2 weeks, sometimes 9 days, same Desert Museum page). Looks like a borrowed spadefoot fact. [s2] [s3]
- **OK (needs update)**: Range: southeastern California and southern Arizona into northwestern Mexico
  Add extreme southwestern New Mexico and Sonora, Sinaloa, Chihuahua. California population is possibly extirpated; listed threatened in New Mexico and California. [s1]
- **MISLEADING**: Element section: 'Aquarius, in Western tradition, is the Water Bearer' used to argue a 'double pressure toward depth'
  Aquarius is an Air sign (fixed), despite the water-bearer image. The page never states the Air element and builds a Water-on-Water reading on the name. [s6]
- **STYLE / UNVERIFIABLE source**: Proverb tiles 水善利萬物 (shuǐ shàn lì wàn wù) translated 'Water is good at benefiting ten thousand things without contending'
  The tiles stop before 而不爭 (ér bù zhēng, 'without contending'), so the translation includes words not shown. This is the opening of Daodejing ch. 8; I could not open a text source this session (ctext blocked). Show all seven characters or trim the translation. (no source)
- **OK**: Lunar New Year note: a January or early February birthday can belong to the Pig
  Applies to Aquarius (about 20 January to 18 February); a birthday before lunar New Year in a Rat year belongs to the preceding Pig year. [s6] [s8]
