# Weaverbird dossier: Virgo x Year of the Rooster

Slug `weaverbird`. Element: Metal (from the Rooster); Virgo adds Earth, mutable. Research date 2026-11-08. Everything marked 'Our reading' is interpretation, not tradition.

## Western half: Virgo
- Temperament: Skilled, with talent 'attributed to their technical skill'; thoughtful, detail-focused, helpful, the most adaptable of the Earth signs, kind and trustworthy. Shadow: perfectionism, nitpicking self and others, can seem curt, moody about connecting, hard time relaxing, hides feelings to keep the peace.
- Planet: Mercury (also exalted in Virgo); Ceres as a modern co-ruler
- Element: Earth (with Taurus and Capricorn)
- Modality: Mutable; opposite sign Pisces
- Sources: [v1] [v2]

## Chinese half: Rooster
- Temperament: Hardworking, resourceful, courageous and talented; honest, loyal, keeps promises; lively and sociable. Shadow: can 'always brag about themselves and their accomplishments', expects to be listened to closely. Classical image: the Han Shi Waizhuan credits the rooster with five virtues, civility (the comb), martial prowess (the spurs), courage, benevolence and trustworthiness (keeping time at dawn).
- Yinyang: Yin
- Element: Metal (fixed); earthly branch 酉 yǒu
- Trine: Rooster, Ox, Snake
- Secret friend: Dragon
- Clash: Rabbit (Wikipedia also lists Dog as the harm pair)
- Lucky: null (dropped per brief)
- Sources: [v3] [v4] [v24]

## The Weaverbird as a symbol
- **India (folk belief about the baya weaver).** 'A widespread folk belief in India is that the baya sticks fireflies with mud to the nest walls' to light the chamber at night. The real behavior under it: bayas do press mud or dung inside the nest chamber, possibly to steady it in wind. A lovely bridge of myth and fact, as long as it is told as a belief, not a fact. [v9]
- **Mughal India (Ain-i-Akbari, about 1590).** The Ain-i-Akbari describes the baya as 'extremely intelligent, obedient and docile', and trained bayas performed tricks for street audiences; Edward Blyth called the feats 'really very wonderful'. [v9]
- **Ghana, Ewe poetry (Kofi Awoonor).** Awoonor's much-anthologized poem 'The Weaver Bird', written in the Ewe dirge tradition, opens with the bird building in 'our house' and imagines sacred sites soiled by imported faiths; the weaver stands for the colonial intruder who builds where it was not invited. A shadow image, worth using only as the flip side of the builder: a structure that takes over the tree. [v10]
- **Chinese (Rooster half).** 闻鸡起舞 wén jī qǐ wǔ, 'rise and dance at the cock's crow': Zu Ti, later an Eastern Jin general, was woken at midnight by a rooster and went out with Liu Kun to practice the sword; Hongkong Post glosses the moral as 'seize the day and get prepared'. Pairs with the page's proverb 心靈手巧 xīn líng shǒu qiǎo, 'clever and deft' (MDBG), which the page honestly labels as its own pairing. [v23] [v25]
- **Unsourced or misattributed, do not use.** (1) Sanskrit सुगृह sugṛha, 'having a good house', is listed on Wikipedia as a baya name, but every dictionary on Wisdom Library (Monier-Williams, Apte, Shabda-Sagara) defines the bird as the tailor bird, not a weaver. (2) The Panchatantra tale of the bird who tells the shivering monkey to build a house and loses her nest is often retold as a weaverbird story, but Ryder's translation calls her a hen-sparrow. (3) Generic 'weaverbird spirit animal = creativity, home-building' blog claims; no tradition behind them. (4) No Zulu or Setswana weaver proverb could be found in the sources reached. [v19] [v20]

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Earth (Virgo, Mercury) meets yin Metal (Rooster). In the five phases Earth generates Metal, so Virgo feeds the Rooster. Both halves are work-proud: Virgo's talent is 'technical skill' (v2), the Rooster is 'hardworking, resourceful, talented' (v4). They split on display: Virgo hides feelings to keep the peace, the Rooster wants to be heard. The weaverbird resolves that split, because its display is the work: the woven nest is the courtship display (v5).
- **Gifts.** Our reading: craft that speaks for you, patience over hundreds of trips for one structure (a baya male may make up to 500, v9), skill that improves with practice (v12), reliability (the Rooster keeps time and keeps promises, v4, v24), the ability to build inside a community without losing your own chamber.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: proof that waits to be inspected. Virgo perfectionism and nitpicking (v2) plus Rooster bragging (v4) make a builder who either says nothing or says too much about the work. The tear-it-down reflex: a nest goes brown in days, and the male would rather demolish than wait (v11). In Awoonor's poem the builder becomes the intruder (v10): the structure takes over other people's tree.
- **Love.** Our reading: shows love in made things, plans and fixes. Needs a partner who actually looks closely, the way a female baya inspects the helmet-stage nest (v9). Risk: the made thing arrives where a sentence was needed. Rooster honesty (v4) helps if it is aimed at feelings, not only at the work.
- **Money.** Our reading: Virgo caution with an occasional surprise splurge (VICE notes Virgos 'may hit their credit card limit quickly', v2); the Rooster's discipline turns that into steady saving when there is a project to fund. Spends on tools and materials before comfort.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: goes quiet and starts fixing; when criticized, rebuilds rather than argues. Rooster bluntness can arrive late and sharp. Practice: say what the structure is for before you build it.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Structural or civil engineering, architecture, joinery.** Our reading: the nest as engineering under inspection; Virgo detail with Rooster discipline.
- **Textiles, tailoring, bookbinding, instrument making.** Our reading: literal weaving and knotting; the baya cuts and knots strips 20 to 60 cm long (v9).
- **Quality assurance, editing, surgery, lab technician.** Our reading: Virgo zeroing in on details (v2); work that is judged by whether it holds.
- **Community housing, co-operatives, facilities management.** Our reading: the sociable weaver's shared roof with private chambers, maintained over generations (v16).
- **Early-shift professions: baking, broadcast morning desks, hospital rounds.** Our reading: the Rooster's dawn timekeeping and the Zu Ti story (v23, v24).

## Candidate lines
1. You say it with grass. A whole season of feeling goes into the strips you knot on that branch, and the words arrive late, if they arrive at all.
2. Green matters to you. A nest woven last month already looks brown in your eyes, so you pull it down and weave it fresh before anyone gets the chance to call it old.
3. When someone you love tugs at what you built, you hold still on the twig and watch. That tug is the only review you have ever fully trusted.
4. Your Rooster half wants to crow at first light. Your Virgo half wants one more pass over the knots first. Most dawns, the knots win.
5. Twenty minutes to tear it down, a week to weave it again. You have made that trade more often than you admit, and you would make it again tomorrow.
6. In a colony of a hundred chambers, yours has the neatest entrance and the fewest callers, and you are still the one patching the roof everybody shares.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Intro: weaverbirds are found across Africa, Asia, and parts of the Pacific.
  Most species live in Africa south of the Sahara, with a few in tropical Asia; some have been introduced elsewhere (v5). Chinese Wikipedia: all native to the Old World, mostly sub-Saharan Africa, a few in tropical Asia (v6). No native Pacific range. Drop 'parts of the Pacific'. [v5] [v6]
- **OK (soften)**: Males weave with a specific over-under technique using grass strips, interlocking rather than random bundling, strong enough for a breeding pair and clutch.
  Baya males 'cut, collect, weave, and knot strips of between 20 and 60 cm' and may make up to 500 trips per nest (v9); Hall et al. describe the skill to 'shape, stitch, and weave' (v21). The weave is real, but they also tie knots, so 'a specific over-under technique' oversimplifies. Say 'weave and knot'. [v9] [v21]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (overstated)**: In most Ploceidae species the female pulls at the nest to test it; if inadequate she rejects it and the male starts again; in some species up to half of completed nests are rejected on first inspection.
  Female inspection is real: baya females inspect at the helmet stage and signal acceptance (v9); a Singapore field note saw a female rip at loose strands and hammer the nest for 20 minutes (v15, blog-level). But 'most species' and 'up to half rejected' have no source I could find, and in the baya Quader (2003) found 'little evidence linking female choice to nest structure'; height and site mattered more (v14, v9). Cut the percentage and the 'most species' claim. [v9] [v15] [v14]
- **OK**: Females assess the freshness of green material; dry or old nests are more likely to be rejected.
  Weaver Watch (UCT): 'Females usually only accept green nests, ie built within the last few days as the material soon fades to brown' (v11). Well supported; this is the page's best fact. [v11]
- **OK (with notes)**: The Social Weaver (Philetairus socius) builds permanent communal nests the size of haystacks, up to five metres wide and three metres tall, with over a hundred chambers kept up for decades; one of the largest structures built by any bird.
  Audubon: up to 20 feet (about 6 m) wide and 10 feet (3 m) tall, up to a ton or more, a hundred or more chambers, reused for more than a century (v16). Wikipedia: 'amongst the largest built by any bird' (v18). So 'decades' undersells it. The usual English name is sociable weaver, and its family is variably placed in Ploceidae or Passeridae (v17), so call it 'a weaver relative' or note the placement. [v16] [v18] [v17]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Different weaverbird populations produce recognisably different nest designs within a species, transmitted by social learning between geographically separated groups.
  Published work shows individual differences between males and improvement with experience (Walsh et al. 2011: experienced males dropped grass less often; behaviours repeatable within individuals) (v12, v13), and Collias and Collias documented weaving developing with practice (v21, v22). I found no study of population-level nest 'traditions' passed on socially. Cut. [v12] [v13] [v21] [v22]
- **WRONG (reason)**: When a nest is rejected, males dismantle it and rebuild from the foundation, reconstructing rather than patching; linked to female preference for structurally sound construction.
  Males do tear down old nests (20 to 30 minutes each) and build fresh ones, and some build a new nest under the old one instead (v11). The trigger is age and color (a nest fades brown in days and females want green), not structural soundness (v11). 'Reconstructing the weave rather than patching' has no source. Rewrite around freshness. [v11]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (likely fabricated)**: Source list: Theunissen, B.I. and Shawe-Taylor, L. (2009). Nest construction and female mate choice in southern masked weavers. Animal Behaviour 78.
  A search for these authors with weavers and Animal Behaviour returned nothing. The real southern masked weaver work is Walsh, Hansell, Borello and Healy (v12, v13). Remove or replace. Collias and Collias 1964 is real (library record, v22). [v12] [v13] [v22]
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi (rail__han and serial-han): 雀.
  雀 què means sparrow (especially the tree sparrow) or any small bird (v7, v8). The weaver family is 織布鳥科 (simplified 织布鸟科) and the genus Ploceus is 织布鸟属 (v6). Use 織布鳥 zhībùniǎo (simplified 织布鸟). The page also shows no pinyin next to the rail glyph. [v7] [v8] [v6]
- **OK**: Proverb 心靈手巧 xīn líng shǒu qiǎo, 'a nimble mind and dexterous hands', labelled as our own pairing.
  MDBG: capable, clever, dexterous (v25). Pinyin correct; label honest. [v25]
- **OK**: Live page matches: Galápagos Tortoise (Dragon, secret friend), Asian Elephant (Cancer x Ox, trine), Devil's Flower Mantis (Scorpio x Rooster, same year).
  Relations correct per the traditional table (v3, v26). Asian Elephant and Devil's Flower Mantis are Water signs, the classic complement to Virgo's Earth, so element harmony also holds. [v3] [v26]
