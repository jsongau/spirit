## The Asian Elephant as a symbol
- **Buddhist (Gandhara relief, 2nd century, Met Museum).** Queen Maya dreams that a six-tusked elephant descends from heaven and enters her right side: the conception of the Buddha. Later tradition makes the elephant white. The elephant here is the arrival of the one who will carry everyone. [s5] [s6]
- **Thailand and Myanmar.** White elephants are sacred, a symbol of royal power and good fortune, and are presented to the king. Lesser white elephants were sometimes given to rivals because they could not be worked and their upkeep could ruin the owner, the root of the English 'white elephant'. The Asian elephant is the national animal of Thailand and Laos. [s6] [s11]
- **Hindu.** Ganesha, the elephant-headed god of beginnings and remover of obstacles, is worshipped before major undertakings and is patron of scribes, authors and bankers. Indra rides Airavata, a white elephant born from the churning of the Ocean of Milk. [s7] [s6]
- **Chinese.** 太平有象 tàipíng yǒu xiàng, 'peace has its sign': an elephant carrying a vase (瓶 píng puns on 平 píng, peace). The phrase first appears in a line by the Song poet Lu You; the motif returned in Ming and Qing palaces. 象 xiàng also stands for 祥 xiáng, good fortune, and an elephant loaded with tribute meant abundance. Legend says elephants tilled the ground at the tomb of Shun. [s8]
- **Chinese, Records of the Three Kingdoms (三國志 Sānguózhì, vol. 20).** 曹沖稱象 Cáo Chōng chēng xiàng: when Sun Quan sent Cao Cao an elephant nobody could weigh, the boy Cao Chong put it on a boat, marked the waterline, then loaded the boat with goods to the same mark. Water is what finally measures the elephant. A rare honest bridge to a Cancer (Water) and Ox (Earth) crossing; the bridge is our reading. [s10]
- **Buddhist, Jain and Hindu; Chinese idiom.** The blind men and the elephant (earliest Buddhist version Udana 6.4): each touches one part and insists it is the whole. In Chinese, 盲人摸象 máng rén mō xiàng means judging the whole from a part. [s9]
- **Dai people of southern China.** The elephant is the twelfth animal of the Dai zodiac. Worth one line only, as a curiosity. [s11]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'elephant spirit animal means wisdom, memory, good luck when the trunk points up' claims from totem blogs. 'Elephants never forget' as a proverb has no single traceable origin I could open. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water (Cancer, ruled by the Moon) meets yin Earth (Ox). Cancer starts things for the family and feels everything; the Ox carries what was started for decades without comment. Earth controls Water, so the Ox gives Cancer's tides banks to run in. The Asian Elephant is the carrier who feels the whole load and keeps walking anyway.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: long loyalty, care that shows up as provision and labor, a calm that has been tested, a memory for who belongs, the strength to hold a household through a hard season.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the white-elephant problem. A sacred burden nobody is allowed to put down, kept out of duty long after it costs more than it gives. Ox stubbornness hardens Cancer's slowness to forgive; grief goes underground and the weight is never shared.
- **Love.** Our reading: devoted, protective and slow to open, the Moon's need for a home plus the Ox's slow affection. Loves by remembering and provisioning. Needs a partner who notices the load without being asked and does not mistake quiet for distance.
- **Money.** Our reading: a saver and provider. Builds steadily, keeps reserves for the family, dislikes speculation. Risk: spends on others before self, and holds a costly asset (a house, a business, an obligation) too long out of loyalty.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: absorbs it, carries it, and goes quiet. Slow to anger, slower to forgive. When it finally moves, it moves with its whole weight, and the herd feels it.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 象 (rail__han and serial-han).
  象 xiàng is the Chinese word for elephant; the page pairs it with pinyin. The full name of the Asian elephant is 亞洲象 Yàzhōu xiàng (traditional), but 象 alone is correct for the header. Source for the meaning: s8, s9 (盲人摸象), s10 (曹沖稱象). [s8] [s9] [s10]
- **WRONG (borrowed species; number unverifiable)**: Elephants return to the remains of dead herd members years after death, touching skull and tusks; one study recorded a family returning to a matriarch's skeleton after twenty-two months.
  Bone-visiting is documented in African elephants (Samburu, Kenya: s20, s22, where returns were over days and weeks). I could not find any study recording a return after 22 months, in either species. The one Asian study I found shows the opposite pattern: in five calf burials in North Bengal (Kaswan and Roy 2024) herds carried the dead calf, buried it, left within 40 minutes and then avoided the site, using parallel routes (s21). Asian elephants do attend dying and dead companions (s19). Rewrite around the Asian burial study or cut. [s20] [s22] [s21] [s19]
- **OK (soften the lip clause)**: The Asian elephant's trunk tip has one dorsal finger (African has two) and uses its lower lip to wrap rather than pinch.
  Deiringer et al. 2023: Asian elephants have only a dorsal finger and mainly grasp by wrapping; African elephants mainly pinch with two fingers (s13, s11). The paper does not describe the lower lip as the wrapping tool; drop 'and a lower lip'. [s13] [s11]
- **OK (first half); UNVERIFIABLE for Asian elephants (second half)**: Asian elephants produce infrasonic calls as low as 14 to 24 hertz, which travel through ground and air; seismic detection through feet and trunk.
  Payne, Langbauer and Thomas 1986: captive Asian elephants made calls mostly 14 to 24 Hz, 10 to 15 s long (s14). Write 'mostly 14 to 24 Hz', not 'as low as'. The seismic-detection work I know of is on African elephants; I did not open a source tying it to Asian elephants. Keep 'thought to' or cut. [s14] [s12]
- **PARTLY OK; illness and crisis UNVERIFIABLE**: Female Asian elephants' temporal gland is largely nonsecretory, activating mainly during labor, illness or genuine crisis, unlike African females who secrete during excited reunions.
  Wikipedia (Temporin): Asian females secrete in advanced pregnancy or soon after calving, while African females secrete freely (s15). ElephantVoices records African females streaming during socially stimulating family interactions (s16). Nothing I could open supports 'illness or genuine crisis'. Narrow to late pregnancy and calving. [s15] [s16]
- **WRONG (60 km); 19 hours UNVERIFIABLE; 150 kg OK**: A wild adult may spend up to 19 hours a day feeding, eating about 150 kg of plants; dung disperses seeds up to 60 km.
  150 kg is the upper figure: Smithsonian gives 74 to 150 kg a day (s12, s11). Smithsonian says about three quarters of the day eating or moving to food or water; Wikipedia says 50 to 75% of the day eating, which is 12 to 18 hours, not 19 (s12, s11). Seed dispersal by Asian elephants: half of seeds moved over 1.2 km, maximum 5.8 km (Campos-Arceiz et al. 2008, s17). Sixty km is the range of African forest elephant studies. Correct to 'more than a kilometre, up to almost 6 km'. [s12] [s11] [s17]
- **WRONG (borrowed species)**: Older matriarchs carry decades of spatial memory of water and safe routes; in droughts, herds led by older matriarchs found water that younger-led groups missed.
  The drought study is Foley, Pettorelli and Foley 2008 on African elephants in Tarangire, Tanzania; it found calves of older mothers survived the 1993 drought better, and it measured the mother's age, not a matriarch leading to water (s18). The Smithsonian states that, unlike African elephants, Asian elephants do not have a matriarch, though some females may be more dominant (s12). Cut or rewrite the whole 'matriarch's map' block for this species. [s18] [s12]
- **WRONG (no traditional basis)**: Purple Star section: 'read for the Cancer Ox crossing it keeps landing on the same two' stars, 天梁 Tiān Liáng and 天府 Tiān Fǔ.
  Zi Wei Dou Shu charts are cast from the lunar birth date and hour, not a Western sun sign plus year animal. The block is labeled 'Our Purple Star reading' but also says Purple Star 'tends to seat the Elder star' for this crossing, which presents our pairing as the tradition's. Relabel plainly as our own reading or cut (same issue MOJO flagged on 29 pages). [s23]
- **OK as labeled**: Proverb 一步一個腳印 yī bù yī gè jiǎo yìn, tied to the Year of the Ox.
  The page says the pairing is our own reading, not tradition. Honest. No source needed for the label; the idiom itself is common.
