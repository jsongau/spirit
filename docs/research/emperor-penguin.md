## The Emperor Penguin as a symbol
- **Chinese (Rooster half): Han Ying, Hanshi Waizhuan.** The rooster's five virtues: 文 wén civility (the comb like an official's cap), 武 wǔ martial skill (the spurs), 勇 yǒng courage (it fights when faced with an enemy), 仁 rén benevolence (it calls the others when it finds food), 信 xìn trustworthiness (it crows on time at dawn whatever the weather). 信, keeping time in any weather, is the emperor penguin's whole winter. [s17] [s18]
- **Chinese idiom (Rooster half).** 闻鸡起舞 wén jī qǐ wǔ (traditional 聞雞起舞): Zu Ti and his friend Liu Kun of the Jin dynasty got up whenever they heard a rooster crow, even at midnight, to practice the sword. Already on the live page as the year proverb, and the source confirms the story. Year-level material: avoid repeating it on all 12 Rooster pages. [s19]
- **British polar exploration (1911).** The Worst Journey in the World: in July 1911, in the Antarctic winter dark at about minus 40, Edward Wilson, Henry Bowers and Apsley Cherry-Garrard hauled sledges from Cape Evans to the Cape Crozier emperor colony to collect eggs, hoping the embryos would show a reptile-bird link (recapitulation theory). They came back with eggs; the Natural History Museum showed little interest, and the eggs were later judged to have produced 'negative information'. A true story of a vigil kept for a purpose that quietly stopped mattering, which is exactly this page's shadow. [s14]
- **Names (Greek, Latin, Chinese).** Aptenodytes is Greek for roughly 'without-wings diver'; forsteri honors the naturalist Johann Reinhold Forster. In Chinese the bird is 帝企鵝 dì qǐ é, the emperor tiptoe-goose: 企 qǐ means to stand on tiptoe and to wait in hope. The name itself is a standing vigil. [s6] [s15] [s16]
- **Modern film.** March of the Penguins (2005) told the emperor's breeding cycle; Happy Feet used emperors as its characters. Modern popular image only, not tradition; low value for citation. [s6]
- **No indigenous tradition.** Antarctica has no indigenous human culture, so there is no folk or spirit-animal tradition for this species. Any 'emperor penguin totem' meaning online is invented. Unsourced, do not use. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water (Cancer) meets yin Metal (Rooster). Cancer guards the small and the home; the Rooster keeps the hour and the rule. The Emperor Penguin is the keeper who protects by schedule: the egg stays on the feet because that is the job, and the job is done on time. In the five phases Metal generates Water, so the Rooster's discipline feeds Cancer's feeling rather than freezing it.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: endurance that does not need an audience, fairness about the hard shifts, a loyal ear that finds its own people in noise, and depth when the surface runs out.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Cherry-Garrard's eggs. Keeping the vigil after the reason for it has gone, because stopping feels like failure. Cancer's self-sacrifice plus the Rooster's pride in doing it exactly right can turn into fasting for show, or keeping a rule that no longer fits the weather.
- **Love.** Our reading: Moon-ruled and yin. One mate, found again each season by a call only the two of them know; love shown by taking a turn at the cold edge. Needs a partner who notices the fast and brings food back.
- **Money.** Our reading: stores up before the long winter and lives on reserves without complaint; a planner and saver. Risk: spending the reserve down to nothing for others. Rooster pride may hide a shortfall.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: holds the line quietly and keeps score of fairness, especially who took the cold edge. The Rooster can turn sharp and precise when a rule is broken. Best move: say the need before the fat runs out.

## Candidate lines (draft, ours)

2. In a crowd of thousands, in wind that strips every other sound away, your person's two-note call still finds you.
3. On your turn you take the outer edge of the huddle, and you keep a private tally of who skipped theirs.
4. The Rooster in you crows on time even in the polar night, when there is no dawn to announce.
5. You walk the long way inland, unfed, because the colony is where it was last year and you keep your appointments.
6. Past four hundred metres the light gives out. You keep going down, and come back up carrying dinner for someone else.

## Notes for the editor
- Two biology facts are wrong or inflated: incubation 64 to 66 days (sources say 65 to 75) and the unsourced 'nine attempts out of ten' recognition rate. The two-month framing understates a roughly four-month fast, which is actually the stronger story.
- Best new symbolic material: Han Ying's five virtues of the rooster, especially 信 xìn (crowing on time in any weather), and the 1911 winter journey for emperor eggs. Both are specific and citable.
- The rooster virtues and 聞雞起舞 are year-level and could repeat on all 12 Rooster pages; use the 信 angle only here, tied to the polar night.
