## The Cleaner Wrasse as a symbol
- **Chinese (common name).** In Chinese the species is 裂唇魚 lièchún yú, split-lip fish, nicknamed 魚醫生 yú yīshēng, the fish doctor, because it clears parasites from other fish. The doctor image is the one popular symbol this fish has. [s6]
- **Indo-Pacific reefs (biology as parable).** The false cleanerfish, Aspidontus taeniatus, wears the wrasse's blue and black stripe and copies its bobbing dance, then sometimes bites the posing client. Adult fish that have been bitten learn to tell the fake from the real cleaner. A sourced counterfeit story: the honest worker's look is valuable enough to be forged. [s18]
- **Classical Greek (parallel, not this fish).** Herodotus told of the trochilus bird that walks into a basking crocodile's open mouth to pick its teeth clean. It is the oldest written cleaner-and-predator image, but it has never been documented and is about a bird. Use only as 'the oldest version of this bargain', named as legend. [s19]
- **Science as story (Japan, Switzerland).** Kohda et al. 2019 (PLOS Biology) reported cleaner wrasses scraping a mark off their own throats after seeing it in a mirror, the first fish claimed to pass the mark test; Frans de Waal urged caution about what it means. Kohda et al. 2023 (PNAS) found the fish did not attack photos of their own face but did attack strangers' faces. [s12] [s13]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Any Indigenous Pacific or Hawaiian spirit meaning for this species; spirit-animal blog claims that the cleaner wrasse means healing or service. No traditional source found.

## The fusion
- **Temperament.** Our reading: double Earth. Mutable Virgo under Mercury is the fixer who notices the flaw; the yang Earth Dog is the loyal guard who keeps a post. Together: the one who runs a station, reads every visitor, and keeps the deal honest because the whole reef is watching. Few crossings are this practical.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: making an exchange clear before it starts, reading body language (the client's pose, the jolt), trustworthiness that brings in even dangerous people, fast self-correction (the mark on the throat).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: running the station after the terms have changed; the small cheat that seems harmless (mucus over parasites) and costs the queue; service that becomes identity; resentment at being copied by someone who wears your stripe and not your ethics.
- **Love.** Our reading: shows love by being useful, removing the small irritants from a partner's life. Loyal (Dog) and attentive (Virgo). Needs a partner who holds still and lets themselves be cared for, and who notices when the care is one-way.
- **Money.** Our reading: steady earner from repeat clients, prices fairly, knows reputation is the real capital. Risk: undercharging because the work feels like duty.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: reads the jolt and adjusts at once. Avoids drama, but when someone breaks the deal the station quietly closes to them.

| 02 The dance: undulating signal before approach; clients pose with fins out, mouth open, gills spread | **OK** | Wikipedia/FishBase describe a dance-like raising and lowering of the rear; ADW notes the oscillating swim; the false cleanerfish page describes clients posing in response to the same dance. | [s5] [s7] [s18] |
| 03 Passed the mirror test, Kohda et al. (2019) PLOS Biology; 'one of very few fish species, alongside primates, elephants, and corvids' | **OK** | Paper and journal right. Add Kohda et al. 2023 PNAS photo study if a stronger line is wanted. | [s12] [s13] [s5] |
| 04 Cheating: wrasse bites client mucus because it is preferred; clients jolt, end the visit or chase | **OK** | Grutter and Bshary 2003 showed cleaners prefer client mucus over most parasites. Client reactions are part of the standard account. | [s10] [s11] |
| 04 'Bshary & Grutter (2006) showed that bystander fish observed these events and subsequently gave cheating wrasses lower service' | **WRONG** | Clients do not give service. The 2006 Nature paper showed clients use image scoring (they watch cleaners and prefer ones seen cooperating) and cleaners cooperate more when watched. Correct: 'bystanders watched, and avoided cleaners they had seen cheat; cleaners behaved better with an audience'. | [s11] |
| 05 All born female; dominant female changes sex 'within hours'; 'documented across the Labridae family' | **OK** | Born female: yes (Live Science on Raihani et al.). Robertson 1972 says the dominant female changes sex immediately after the male dies; ADW says within 24 hours. Fine for behavior; full functional change takes longer, so 'begins acting as the male within hours' is safer. 'Across the Labridae family' overreaches: many wrasses change sex, not all are documented. Reversed sex change is also known (Kuwamura et al. 2002). | [s8] [s7] [s9] [s5] |
| 06 Reputation effects: witnessed cheating affects later client behavior at that station | **OK** | This is the Bshary and Grutter 2006 image-scoring result. | [s11] |
