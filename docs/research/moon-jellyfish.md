## The Moon Jellyfish as a symbol
- **Chinese classical (Guo Pu, Jiang Fu, 4th century; Nan Yue Zhi).** 璅蛣腹蟹，水母目蝦 (suǒ jié fù xiè, shuǐmǔ mù xiā): 'the pea crab lives in the clam's belly, the jellyfish uses a shrimp for eyes'. The Nan Yue Zhi, quoted in Li Shan's commentary, says the jellyfish has no ears or eyes, so shrimp ride with it; when the shrimp is startled and dives, the jellyfish sinks too. The idiom 水母目蝦 came to mean a person with no view of their own who follows others. Strong, primary-text hook for the Pisces and Rabbit shadow (drift, following). [s6]
- **Chinese naming.** The species' Chinese name is 海月水母 hǎiyuè shuǐmǔ, literally 'sea-moon water-mother'; The Paper titles its piece 荧荧如月 yíngyíng rú yuè, 'glowing like the moon'. With the Rabbit half, the Jade Rabbit who lives on the Moon (s5), this crossing is a moon in the sea under a rabbit in the sky. The pairing is our image; the name and the Jade Rabbit are sourced. [s9] [s5]
- **Japanese creation myth (Kojiki, 712).** At the beginning, 'when the land was still young, like floating fat, and drifting about like a jellyfish', the first deity sprouted 'like a reed shoot'. The jellyfish is the image of the world before it had a shape. Kokugakuin University translation. [s7]
- **Japanese folktale (Ozaki, Japanese Fairy Tales, 1908).** The Jelly Fish and the Monkey: the Dragon King of the Sea sends the jellyfish to fetch a live monkey's liver for his sick queen. Halfway across the water the jellyfish lets slip the plan; the monkey says he left his liver in a tree, gets carried back and escapes. As punishment the King has the jellyfish's bones pulled out, 'and his descendants have all been soft and boneless'. A story about a soft heart that cannot keep a secret. [s8]
- **Western (Pisces myth).** Aphrodite and Eros became fish to flee the monster Typhon and were placed in the sky. A sign born from escaping by dissolving into water. [s1]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Jellyfish spirit animal' meanings (go with the flow, transparency, resilience) appear only on totem and dream blogs. No Indigenous or traditional source located. Note that the ancient Chinese and Japanese texts speak of jellyfish in general, not Aurelia specifically. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Water (Pisces) feeds yin Wood (Rabbit), a generating pair in the five phases. Pisces dissolves into the current; the Rabbit avoids collisions. The Moon Jellyfish has no brain, only nerve nets with eight small sensory and pacemaker clusters around the rim (s11). It reads the water through its whole edge. Gift and risk come from one fact: it mostly drifts with the current, even while swimming (s10).
- **Gifts.** Our reading: sensing the mood of water before it changes, gentleness that lowers friction, beauty without effort (aquariums worldwide keep it as a star exhibit, s9), and a talent for going back to an earlier form when a life stops working. Aurelia medusae under stress can settle and turn back into polyps (s15).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: 水母目蝦. Borrowing someone else's eyes, then sinking when they sink. The Rabbit's dislike of confrontation plus Pisces' permeability can make a life that goes wherever the shrimp goes. Also the folktale's leak: a soft heart that tells the plan halfway across.
- **Love.** Our reading: Neptune and yin Wood. Loves by attunement, feels a partner's weather in its own body, gives generously. Needs one person who will name a direction aloud and not take advantage of the drift. Fears hard edges, sudden noise and being asked to choose fast.
- **Money.** Our reading: money follows the current here, and that can work well in good seasons (a bloom) and badly in lean ones. Best with automatic structures that keep a pulse going without daily decisions: standing transfers, a steady salary, one trusted adviser.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids it, moves around it, and stings only when touched, and mildly (the sting is weak to humans, s10). Stays too long in bad water. Practice: say which way you want to go before the tide decides.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (truncated name)**: Header hanzi 月水母 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin yuè shuǐmǔ.
  The Chinese name is 海月水母 hǎiyuè shuǐmǔ ('sea-moon jellyfish'); 月水母 drops the 海 and is not the standard name. Traditional and simplified forms are identical for these four characters. Use 海月水母 hǎiyuè shuǐmǔ, or 水母 shuǐmǔ alone for 'jellyfish'. [s9]
- **WRONG (outdated)**: Intro: Aurelia aurita is 'found in oceans worldwide'.
  The old cosmopolitan A. aurita has been split into at least 28 species; A. aurita in the strict sense is now placed in the North Atlantic and Argentina. Say 'moon jellyfish (genus Aurelia) live in coastal seas worldwide'. [s10]
- **OK**: Intro: four visible gonads in a four-leaf-clover shape.
  Wikipedia: four horseshoe-shaped gonads visible through the bell. 'Four-leaf clover' is a common description; fine. [s10]
- **PARTLY WRONG**: Hero: 'No brain. No heart. No eyes.'
  No brain and no heart: OK (s14, s11). 'No eyes' is wrong as stated: each of its eight rhopalia carries ocelli, including a pigment-cup ocellus that is primarily photosensory; removing them abolished positive phototaxis. Houston Zoo says 'no eyes', so say 'no eyes like ours' or 'only simple light-sensing eyespots'. [s12] [s11] [s14]
- **UNVERIFIABLE as worded**: Hero and dossier: 'doing this for 500 million years' / 'half a billion years' / 'since before fish existed'.
  Swimming medusozoan jellyfish are known from 505 million years ago (Burgessomedusa, Burgess Shale). That is jellyfish in general, not this species. 'Before fish existed' is not supported: early fish-like vertebrates are of similar Cambrian age; cut it. Safer: 'jellyfish have swum the seas for more than 500 million years'. [s18]
- **OK (with nuance)**: No brain; a nerve net, decisions processed locally, no single site where decisions are made.
  Aurelia has two nerve nets (motor and diffuse) and no central brain; but swimming pulses are started by pacemakers in each of the eight rhopalia, which are the concentrations of neurons. 'No single control site' is fine; 'processed locally and distributed' slightly oversimplifies. [s11]
- **WRONG**: Pulse rate approximately one contraction per second.
  Xu and Dabiri 2020 measured natural free swimming in Aurelia aurita at 0.24 plus or minus 0.11 Hz, about one pulse every four seconds (constrained animals 0.16 Hz). The muscle's physiological limit is about 1.4 Hz. Say 'a slow pulse every few seconds'. [s13]
- **OK**: Body approximately 95% water; mesoglea is mostly water in a collagen matrix.
  Houston Zoo: 95% water. eLife describes the mesoglea as fluid with elastic fibers. [s14] [s11]
- **WRONG (relationship and mechanism)**: Turritopsis dohrnii, 'a close relative', reverts to its polyp stage, theoretically without limit; the moon jellyfish 'can reabsorb into a cyst-like form under stress and restart development'.
  Turritopsis is a hydrozoan; moon jellyfish are scyphozoans, a different class, so 'close relative' is wrong. The Aurelia claim is garbled: He et al. 2015 showed that Aurelia sp.1 medusae, including sexually mature ones, can turn back into polyps after starvation, hypoxia or injury, the first such record for a scyphozoan medusa. Cysts (podocysts) are made by polyps, not by reverting medusae. Rewrite around He et al. and drop the Turritopsis detour. [s15]
- **OK for 'huge blooms' / UNVERIFIABLE for 'billions'**: Blooms of millions to billions of individuals under favorable conditions.
  Moon jellyfish blooms are real and large enough to shut reactor 3 at Oskarshamn, Sweden, in 2013; no source located with a billions count. Say 'blooms big enough to shut a nuclear plant'. [s16]
- **UNVERIFIABLE / misleading**: Maintains a permeable but selective membrane; oral arms filter particles rather than absorbing everything.
  'Selective membrane' is not a described feature. Moon jellies capture prey with stinging cells and mucus on tentacles and oral arms, then move it along grooves to the mouth; jetting vortices move water past the feeding parts. That is predation by mucus and nematocysts, not 'filtering'. Rewrite or cut. [s17]
- **OK (lightly)**: Hero: 'pulses toward light'.
  Wikipedia's rhopalium article reports positive phototaxis that depends on the pigment-cup ocelli. Fine as an image; it contradicts the same sentence's 'No eyes'. [s12]
- **OK (no star claim)**: Purple Star.
  The page only links to the Purple Star chart and says it is cast from exact birth hour. No sign-based star assignment found. [s19]
