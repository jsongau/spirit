## The Hoatzin as a symbol
- **Guyana (national symbol).** The hoatzin is Guyana's national bird, known locally as the Canje pheasant. It sits at the foot of the shield on Guyana's coat of arms (arms granted 8 December 1954), where it stands for the country's rich fauna. Motto: One People, One Nation, One Destiny. [h6] [h11]
- **Amazon and Orinoco basin common name.** Local people call it the stinkbird, for a smell of fermenting leaves likened to fresh cow manure. The smell is the outward sign of the gut that lets it live on leaves. [h6] [h9] [h15]
- **Science as myth-maker: the 'prehistoric bird'.** People once took the hoatzin for a prehistoric survivor because chicks have wing claws. Abourachid et al. (2019) found chick claw and finger proportions 'strikingly similar to those of Archaeopteryx' and proposed the chick as a living analog for how early birds may have climbed, without claiming the claws are inherited unchanged. A clean story of a young form that looks ancient and is in fact its own thing. [h10] [h15]
- **Nahuatl and colonial Spanish naming.** The name hoatzin comes from Nahuatl huāctzin, which first named the laughing falcon, a loud Mexican bird; 18th-century European naturalists moved the name to this South American bird. The suffix -tzin is honorific. Use only as a footnote (the brief bans name trivia as a hook). [h12]
- **Chinese name.** 麝雉 shè zhì, 'musk pheasant', names the bird by its smell. The header glyph on the live page is this name and is correct. [h14]
- **Indigenous Amazonian myth or spirit-animal meaning.** Unsourced, do not use. No named-nation story about the hoatzin was found in any source I could open; blog 'hoatzin spirit animal' pages are not traditional. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Air meets yin Metal. Aquarius designs from first principles and does not mind being the odd bird; the Rooster keeps the hour, speaks plainly and finishes what it starts. The Hoatzin is the oddity that runs on schedule: a bird that eats what no flying bird should and does it with a cow's patience. Its family tree has no close branches, which suits an Aquarian.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: living on material others cannot process (the leaf diet); a frank, unbothered presence that filters out casual interest (the smell); loyalty to a home stretch of river and to the colony that helps raise the young; a young self that was fully equipped for its stage (the wing claws).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Rooster pride plus Aquarian detachment can harden into 'take me as I smell or leave'. The other risk is timing: the Rooster wants to keep the form that works, Aquarius wants to move on early, so the claw gets dropped either too soon or held too long.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow to warm, stays put. Hoatzin young often stay on as helpers at the parents' nest for a couple of years (ADW), so this crossing reads love as staying and pitching in. Needs a partner who is not put off by the long digestion period before a feeling becomes words.
- **Money.** Our reading: a frugal, low-energy budget; the hoatzin lives on a food with little energy by spending little. Good with slow, compounding returns; weak spot is pride in doing it the hard way when an easier source exists.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids open fights, makes itself not worth the trouble. Under real threat the colony gets loud together (adults fly noisily to divert a hawk, per Wikipedia) and the young drop into the water. Says the blunt Rooster thing, then leaves the branch.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 麝雉 (rail__han and serial-han)
  麝雉 shè zhì is the Chinese name of the hoatzin (zh.wikipedia title). Add pinyin shè zhì; the page shows none. [h14]
- **WRONG (range)**: Meet intro: 'lives only on the banks of slow Amazonian rivers ... a colony of the same species within calling distance'
  Range is swamps, riparian forest and mangroves of the Amazon AND Orinoco basins (Wikipedia; zh.wikipedia). 'Small colonies' is supported; 'within calling distance' is unsourced. Rewrite: 'lives in the swamps and river forests of the Amazon and Orinoco basins, nesting in small colonies'. [h6] [h14]
- **OK (soften 'reabsorbed')**: Chicks hatch with functional claws on the first and second wing digits, used to grip branches and climb back after falling into water; claws reabsorbed during development and absent in adults
  First and second fingers, lost in adulthood (ADW; Alaska Science Forum). Chicks jump into water and swim back (Abourachid 2019). 'Reabsorbed' is not in any source I opened; say 'lost by adulthood'. Note Wikipedia: wing claws are not unique to hoatzins (chickens have them); what is unusual is using them to climb. [h7] [h9] [h10] [h6]
- **OK**: Only bird known to use foregut fermentation; bacterial colonies in an enlarged crop break down cellulose
  'The only known example of an animal with crop fermentation in the class Aves' (Godoy-Vitorino 2008, citing Grajal 1989). Wikipedia adds it is the smallest known animal with foregut fermentation. [h8] [h13] [h6]
- **WRONG (predators); UNVERIFIABLE (cause, hunting)**: Fermentation produces volatile fatty acids that give a manure-like odor; local name 'stinkbird'; most predators avoid it; rarely hunted
  Smell like fresh cow manure and the name stinkbird are OK (Alaska Science Forum; Wikipedia). Volatile fatty acids are present at rumen-like levels (Godoy-Vitorino 2008), but no source I opened ties the smell to VFAs; Wikipedia credits aromatic compounds in the leaves plus fermentation. 'Most predators avoid it' is WRONG: ADW lists monkeys, hawks and snakes as predators and Wikipedia describes great black hawks attacking colonies. Hunting by people: sources conflict (ADW 'frequently hunted'; Wikipedia 'only in times of dire need', eggs collected in Brazil). Cut the predator and hunting claims. [h9] [h6] [h8] [h7]
- **OK**: Cooperative breeders: non-breeding helpers assist the breeding pair in feeding and guarding chicks
  ADW: young stay in the parents' territory for a couple of years and act as helpers with the new brood. [h7]
- **OK (contested date)**: Lineage diverged from all other modern birds about 64 million years ago; no close living relatives
  Wikipedia cites a 2015 genetic study for 64 million years and a 2024 study suggesting about 70 million years (Late Cretaceous). Placement is unresolved (cranes and plovers in 2014; sister to Phaethoquornithes in 2024). Say 'an ancient lineage with no close living relatives; one 2015 study puts the split at about 64 million years ago'. [h6]
- **WRONG**: Leaves make up nearly 100% of the diet
  A Venezuelan study found 82% leaves, 10% flowers, 8% fruit (Wikipedia). Rewrite to 'about four fifths leaves'. The low-energy-food point is OK (Alaska Science Forum). [h6] [h9]
- **OK as labeled, but say what it is**: Proverb 物競天擇，適者生存 (wù jìng tiān zé, shì zhě shēng cún) tied to the Rooster
  The page honestly labels the pairing as its own. The phrase is a modern one from the Chinese reception of Darwin through Yan Fu's Tianyan lun 天演論 (1896-1898), not an old proverb; Yan Fu's rendering of natural selection as 天擇 tiān zé is confirmed. The exact first use of the eight-character phrase was not verified. Its link to the Rooster is ours only. [h16]
- **OK**: Lunar New Year note: an Aquarius born in late January or early February can belong to the Monkey
  Applies to Aquarius (dates about 20 January to 18 February); the year before the Rooster is the Monkey. [h1] [h3]
- **OK**: Purple Star section
  No star is assigned from the sign pair; the page says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. No overreach found. 
- **UNVERIFIABLE (our framework)**: Element section: 'Air ... sits nearest to Metal in quality'
  Wu Xing has no Air phase; the Air-to-Metal mapping is Zodi Animal's own and should be labeled 'our reading'. [h17]
