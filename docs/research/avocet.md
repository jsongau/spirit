## The Avocet as a symbol
- **British conservation (RSPB).** The avocet was lost as a British breeding bird by 1840 as wetlands were drained. In 1947 four pairs bred at Minsmere and Havergate Island in Suffolk on marsh flooded in wartime (soldiers opened sluices at Minsmere against invasion; Havergate's neglected defences breached). It became the RSPB's logo in the 1970s and is now a national symbol of a species brought back. About 1,950 UK breeding pairs today. Strongest, best-sourced symbol item for this page: the return after absence, made possible by an accident of war. [s7] [s6]
- **English dialect names.** Old names name the two halves of this crossing. 'Cobbler's awl' (Merriam-Webster) names the bill as a precise tool; 'yelper' (UK dialect, Wiktionary) names its shrill alarm cry. Precision and alarm, Virgo and the defending parent. [s8] [s9]
- **Italian (Venetian) and the lawyer suggestion.** 'Avocet' comes from Venetian avosetta, first recorded by Aldrovandi in 1603; origin uncertain. One suggestion links it to the black and white dress of European lawyers (avvocato). Wikipedia calls this only a suggestion: use as 'one theory', never as fact. [s6]
- **Latin name.** Recurvirostra = recurvus 'curved back' + rostrum 'beak'. The French 'avocette' spelling comes from a 1760 slip by Brisson. [s6]
- **Chinese name.** 反嘴鷸 fǎnzuǐyù, literally 'reversed-beak sandpiper'. A common winter visitor to Hong Kong's wetlands. No classical Chinese folklore for the avocet was found. [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'avocet spirit animal' meanings (balance, grace, intuition) found on totem blogs. No Indigenous, Celtic, African or Chinese folklore for the avocet could be found in a reliable source. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Earth (Virgo) meets yang Fire (Horse). The Horse wants open ground and motion; Virgo wants the work done right. The avocet joins them as a bird that walks and sweeps at the same time: method carried at a steady pace across a wide flat. Fire generates Earth in the Wu Xing cycle, but Virgo's 'Earth' is the Western element, so treat that link as our image, not a Wu Xing calculation.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: finds what is buried in murky conditions; works by feel when the evidence is not yet visible; comes back to the same shore and the same people; turns fierce, fast, when its own ground is threatened.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: one more sweep instead of a decision. Virgo's worry plus the Horse's dislike of orders can make the avocet thorough on its own terms and deaf to a deadline. The real colony record is humbling: at Newport Wetlands avocets mobbed crows only about half the time and lost chicks anyway (s11). Fierce is not the same as enough.
- **Love.** Our reading: Virgo care is practical; the Horse is honest and warm in company. Slow to name a feeling, quick to defend the bond. Needs a partner who does not read the long search as doubt, and who will ask directly.
- **Money.** Our reading: careful with detail, restless with routine. Likely to audit every line, then make one bold Horse move. Best with a system that runs while it roams.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: calm until the nest is touched, then loud and direct. Rarely holds a grudge in silence; the yelper says it out loud. Practice: name the problem on the first pass, not the fifth.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi (rail__han / serial-han) is 弧.
  弧 hú means 'arc' (a metaphor for the bill), not the bird. The avocet's Chinese name is 反嘴鷸 fǎnzuǐyù, 'reversed-beak sandpiper' (Hong Kong Wetland Park). Use 鷸 yù alone if one glyph is needed, always with pinyin. [s10]
- **OK (core) / UNVERIFIABLE (uniqueness)**: Forages by sweeping its upturned bill side to side through shallow water, detecting invertebrates by touch; a method unique among European shorebirds, independent of visibility.
  Side-to-side 'scything' is confirmed. But Wikipedia also records lateral pecks into sediment and catching prey by sight, so 'by touch' is one method, not the only one. 'Unique among European shorebirds' and 'independent of visibility' found in no opened source. Cut both. [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: The bill contains sensory receptors that detect prey through tactile pressure in the water column, working in turbid water.
  No source found for avocet bill receptors. Cunningham et al. 2013 found no sensory pits in the black-winged stilt (same family, Recurvirostridae) and said stilts detect prey by direct touch, not remote pressure cues. Avocets were not examined. The 'pressure in the water column' wording describes remote touch, which is documented for sandpipers, kiwi and ibises, not avocets. Possible borrowed-species claim. Cut. [s12]
- **OK (soften)**: High site fidelity: returns to the same nesting locations in consecutive seasons; ranges widely in winter and comes back precisely each spring.
  Philopatric; ringing shows birds nesting at their sites of origin; adult return rate averages about 90% (Wikipedia). But many Mediterranean and African populations are resident, so 'ranges widely in winter' fits northern and eastern birds only. [s6]
- **OK in part / UNVERIFIABLE in part**: Aggressive nest defenders, recorded mobbing humans, dogs, foxes and large raptors; alarm-calling, feigning injury and diving at intruders until they leave.
  Distraction displays such as a feigned broken wing are confirmed (Wikipedia). Mobbing of Carrion Crows (52% of sightings) and Common Buzzards (all six, by several birds) is recorded at Newport Wetlands (Bishop et al. 2021). Mobbing of humans, dogs and foxes, and 'until they leave', was not found in an opened source. Narrow the claim to crows and buzzards plus the broken-wing display. [s6] [s11]
- **OK**: Nests in loose colonies, close for communal defence but spaced for territory.
  Semi-colonial, usually 10 to 70 pairs; each family defends a rearing territory sized to its brood (Wikipedia). Communal mobbing is recorded (s11). [s6] [s11]
- **OK**: Clean black-and-white plumage, long blue-grey legs, instantly recognisable silhouette.
  Black cap and hindneck, black-and-white plumage, long upturned black bill, bluish legs (Wikipedia; Hong Kong Wetland Park). [s6] [s10]
- **OK (incomplete)**: Intro: 'a wader of European and Asian estuaries'.
  Range also includes Africa, where many birds are resident (Wikipedia). Add Africa. [s6]
- **STYLE FLAG**: Element section: 'Fire generates Earth: the Horse's energy feeds Virgo's systematic method.'
  Virgo's Earth is the Western element, which is not a Wu Xing phase. The African Grey page handles this correctly (Air sits outside the cycle). Label as Zodi Animal's image, or drop. [s3] [s1]
- **OK**: Proverb 一絲不苟 yī sī bù gǒu tied to the Horse year.
  Real idiom; the page honestly labels the pairing as its own reading. Not checked further. 
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Cited books: Cramp and Simmons (1983) BWP Vol. 3; Piersma and van Gils (2011) The Flexible Phenotype.
  Both are real books, but I could not open them, so I cannot confirm they support any specific claim on the page. Do not list them as sources unless a person checks the page numbers.
