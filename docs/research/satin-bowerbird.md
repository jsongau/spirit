## The animal as a symbol
- **Bundjalung (northeast NSW and Gold Coast/Tweed).** The Bundjalung dictionary records one word, yahdj (Gold Coast Tweed form yaheh), glossed as catbird, satin bowerbird and rifle bird. One name covers this bird and the Paradise Riflebird, the other animal in this batch; both live in Bundjalung country. A separate word, dulum, is given for bowerbird. Dictionary entries only; no story is attached, so do not invent one. [s15, s16]
- **Victorian natural history (Darwin, 1871).** Darwin called the Australian bower-birds 'the best evidence' of a taste for the beautiful in birds. He wrote that the Satin bower-bird collects gaily coloured articles such as the blue tail-feathers of parrakeets, that the objects are continually re-arranged, and that Gould found in one bower a neatly worked stone tomahawk and a slip of blue cotton. The bird became the founding example for the idea that animals choose mates on beauty. [s13]
- **Australian English slang.** In Australian slang a 'bower-bird' is a petty thief (first cited 1943) or a scavenger of waste and trifles (1941). It is the human echo of two real habits: hoarding found objects and stealing decorations from rival bowers. [s14, s12]
- **Chinese naming.** The Chinese name is 緞藍園丁鳥 duànlán yuándīngniǎo (simplified 缎蓝园丁鸟), literally 'satin-blue gardener bird'. Bowerbirds as a group are 'gardener birds' in Chinese. Useful as a gloss; do not lead with it as trivia. [s17, s18]
- **Rooster-year classical text (page proverb).** 呆若木雞 dāi ruò mù jī, 'still as a wooden rooster', comes from Zhuangzi ch. 19: Ji Xingzi trained a fighting cock until it no longer reacted to other birds' crows; it looked carved from wood and rivals turned and ran. Modern usage means 'dumbstruck', so any page use must say the original sense. Year-animal lore: belongs to the Rooster hub as much as here. [s19, s20]
- **Aboriginal Dreaming story about the satin bowerbird.** Unsourced, do not use. I found no named-nation story in a source I could open; only blogs.

## The fusion (our reading unless cited)
- **Temperament:** Our reading: Libra's eye for proportion and Venus taste meets the Rooster's daily discipline and need to be properly heard. The result curates rather than collects. Like the male who keeps a bower all year and spends roughly seven years in plain green before the blue-black comes in, this crossing builds long before it is seen and expects the eventual judgment to be fair.
- **Gifts:** Finished work that holds up under inspection; patience through a long apprenticeship; a sharp eye for the one wrong piece; loyalty once chosen (Rooster keeps promises).
- **Shadow:** The standard becomes the gate. Libra's indecision plus the Rooster's wish to be listened to can turn into waiting at the bower for a judge who never qualifies. Rooster bragging and Libra people-pleasing can alternate.
- **Love:** Courts with a made thing: the arranged evening, the right object carried a long way. Females in this species return to inspect more than once, and removing paint cut return visits (Hicks 2011); this crossing reads repeat attention as the real yes. Needs a partner who looks closely and comes back.
- **Money:** Our reading: spends on a few exact things, not many cheap ones; can over-invest in presentation. Rooster resourcefulness keeps a careful ledger.
- **Conflict:** Withdraws to rebuild the case alone, then returns with it finished. Rivalry is real in the species (bower destruction, stolen decorations), so this crossing guards its work and keeps score, but Libra wants the outcome to look fair.

## Careers
- **Exhibition, set or window design:** Arranging found objects to be judged by a viewer is the bower itself; Libra taste plus Rooster finish.
- **Conservator or restorer:** Repainting the same wall to standard is a sourced behavior; patience and exactness.
- **Editor or curator of a collection:** Choosing what belongs, discarding the rest; Rooster observation, Libra proportion.
- **Brand or packaging designer:** Blue was the deliberate signal; this crossing knows a palette is a promise.
- **Quality auditor or inspector:** China Highlights lists observant, hardworking Roosters for structured roles; fits the judging side of the bower.

2. For years you wore green and were taken for one of the females, and the whole time you were building practice bowers and taking notes.
