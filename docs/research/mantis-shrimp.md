## The Mantis Shrimp as a symbol
- **Chinese mythology (the Dragon King's court).** 蝦兵蟹將 xiā bīng xiè jiàng, 'shrimp soldiers and crab generals', are the troops of the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea. In 西遊記 Xīyóu Jì (Journey to the West) ch. 3, Dragon King Ao Guang comes out with his sons, grandsons and shrimp soldiers and crab generals to greet Sun Wukong. The idiom later came to mean useless underlings. For a Dragon-year shrimp that hits harder than almost anything its size, the irony is the hook: the Dragon's lowliest foot soldier turned out to be the heavyweight. The idiom names shrimp in general, not mantis shrimp. [s14]
- **Chinese and Japanese names.** Header name 螳螂蝦 tángláng xiā, 'praying-mantis shrimp', for its mantis-like posture and strike. In Taiwan, Hong Kong and Guangdong it is popularly 瀨尿蝦 lài niào xiā or 撒尿蝦 sā niào xiā, 'peeing shrimp'; FoodNEXT ties the Taiwan name's spread to a film. In Japanese cuisine it is shako 蝦蛄 (xiāgū in Mandarin), a sushi item. [s15] [s4]
- **Folk names (Assyrian, fishers).** Wikipedia records that Assyrians called them 'sea locusts', fishers call them 'thumb splitters' for the injuries they cause when handled, and Australians 'prawn killers'. The Assyrian claim rests on Wikipedia alone; check its footnote before publishing. [s4]
- **Science as symbol (a private channel).** Chiou et al. (2008, Current Biology) found mantis shrimp can see circularly polarized light, and in three Odontodactylus species males reflect it from body parts used in displays. The researchers suggested a 'secret communication channel'; its exact function is not yet known. A strong Aquarius image, but state the uncertainty. [s12]
- **Science as symbol (long partnership).** Wikipedia: in the monogamous species, mantis shrimp stay with the same partner up to 20 years. Species-specific (not all stomatopods); fine for the love section if worded 'some mantis shrimp'. [s4]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Mantis shrimp 'spirit animal' or totem meanings (blogs only). Any 'punch as hot as the sun' claim: that temperature (at least 5,000 K) was estimated for snapping shrimp bubbles, a different animal. The 皮皮蝦 pípíxiā internet meme was not opened from a reliable source. [s8]

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Air meets yang Earth. Aquarius brings the different eye and the comfort of standing apart; the Dragon brings power and the urge to strike first. The mantis shrimp fits both: a perceiver whose visual system is unlike any other (s9, s10) and a striker that lives at one fixed burrow address (s4).
- **Gifts.** Our reading: fast, categorical reads (the wavelength-by-wavelength colour system, s9); force that lands twice, once on impact and once in the aftermath (s7); a nerve to hold ground even when soft, as newly moulted animals bluff off intruders (s13).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: fast categories miss fine shades (they discriminate colours only 15 to 25 nm apart, worse than humans, s9). The Dragon's easy conflict (s3) plus Aquarian distance can turn into striking first and explaining never.
- **Love.** Our reading: guarded at the burrow mouth, then strikingly loyal; some mantis shrimp keep one partner up to 20 years (s4). Signals love on channels only a close partner learns to read (s12).
- **Money.** Our reading: decisive and early; sees value before the market names it, then defends the position hard. Risk: a fixed burrow becomes a fixed opinion.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: the meral spread first, the display of the weapon; the strike only if the display fails. When weakened, bluffs rather than retreats, and the bluff often holds (s13).

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 螳螂蝦, aria-label 'tángláng xiā'
  螳螂蝦 tángláng xiā is a real Chinese name for mantis shrimp (FoodNEXT renders 'mantis shrimp' as 螳螂蝦). Other names: 蝦蛄 xiāgū, 瀨尿蝦 lài niào xiā. Pinyin is present in the aria-label; make it visible beside the glyph per the house rule. [s15]
- **WRONG (borrowed species)**: Subtitle: 'a punch that creates miniature suns'.
  The sun-like temperature (at least 5,000 K inside the collapsing bubble) was estimated for snapping shrimp (Lohse et al. 2001), not mantis shrimp. For mantis shrimp, Wikipedia says the collapsing bubbles produce light (sonoluminescence); no temperature measurement was found. Rewrite to 'a punch that boils the water into bubbles that flash' only if sourced, or simply 'a punch that hits twice'. [s8] [s4]
- **OK (soften)**: Up to sixteen types of photoreceptor cells; humans three, birds four; identifies wavelengths independently, faster at colour decisions, less able to distinguish fine gradations.
  Guinness and Wikipedia give 12 to 16 photoreceptor types; 12 are colour types (8 visible, 4 UV), the rest polarization. Thoen et al. showed poor discrimination (15 to 25 nm apart vs about 5 nm for humans). 'Faster' is Marshall's hypothesis, not a measurement; say 'probably faster'. Live page cites Cronin and Marshall 1989 (Nature 339:137), which reported at least ten spectral types; that citation does not support sixteen. [s9] [s10] [s4]
- **OK / UNVERIFIABLE (temperature)**: Smashing claw accelerates up to 10,400 g, 23 m/s; cavitation bubbles collapse at near-boiling temperatures and give a second impact; target is hit twice.
  10,400 g and 23 m/s for the peacock mantis shrimp per Wikipedia citing Patek et al. 2004. Caution: the Berkeley release on the same study gives peak accelerations of 6,300 to 8,000 g and speeds of 14 to 23 m/s; a human should check the Nature PDF. Two force peaks 390 to 480 microseconds apart, the second from bubble collapse, confirmed by Patek and Caldwell 2005. 'Near-boiling temperatures' has no source and contradicts the snapping shrimp estimate; cut it. Applies to smashers, not spearers; name the species. [s4] [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **UNVERIFIABLE as written**: Complex iridescent body patterns used for species recognition, territorial communication and mating; they broadcast on the channels their eyes receive best.
  What is documented: males of three Odontodactylus species reflect circularly polarized light from display body parts, and the eyes detect it (Chiou et al. 2008); the researchers say the precise function is not yet known. Rewrite around that finding and drop the three listed functions. [s12]
- **OK**: Excavate or occupy burrows in reef substrate, defend them with threat displays, meral spread and physical engagement.
  Wikipedia: live in burrows where they spend most of their time. Meral spread in cavity defence: Steger and Caldwell summary. [s4] [s13]
- **OK / UNVERIFIABLE (duration)**: Must molt to grow; afterwards soft and cannot use the strike at full force, vulnerability lasting several days.
  Newly moulted animals could not use their raptorial appendages without serious self-damage and instead bluffed with the meral spread, often deterring intruders (Steger and Caldwell 1983, via keyapa summary). No source found for 'several days'; cut the duration or find one. [s13]
- **OK (narrow it)**: Eyes trinocular with independently moving components; each eye assesses distance, direction and colour across a full hemisphere; eyes point in different directions.
  Eyes on mobile stalks move independently; each eye has trinocular vision and depth perception for objects near its mid-plane (Wikipedia; The Scientist). 'Across a full hemisphere' is not supported; cut it. [s4] [s11]
- **OK**: Intro: most complex visual system of any animal.
  Guinness record 'most complex eyes of any animal', for certain stomatopod species. [s10]
- **OK**: Lunar New Year note: January or early February birthdays can belong to the Rabbit.
  Applies to Aquarius; the year before the Dragon is the Rabbit. [s2]
