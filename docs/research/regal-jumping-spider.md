## The Regal Jumping Spider as a symbol
- **Chinese (name of the animal itself).** The ordinary Chinese word for a jumping spider is 蝇虎 yínghǔ (traditional 蠅虎), literally 'fly tiger'. In Hong Kong, the fighting jumping spiders children kept in matchboxes in the 1960s to 1980s were called 'golden cat' and 'leopard tiger' (Green Power gives the English only). A tiny animal named after big cats is a natural bridge to Leo the lion. [s12] [s13]
- **Chinese folk omen (spiders in general).** Spiders were 喜子 xǐzi (also written 蟢子), a pun on 喜 xǐ, joy. A spider dropping on a thread meant 喜从天降 xǐ cóng tiān jiàng, joy falling from the sky. Lù Jī's commentary on the Shijing says that when the insect lands on your clothes, a dear guest is coming. At 七夕 Qīxī, girls read skill from whether a spider spun on the fruit plate overnight (Jingchu Suishi Ji). Sotheby's dates the good-omen idea to the Han. Applies to spiders broadly, not jumping spiders. [s14] [s15] [s16]
- **Asante (Ghana), Akan diaspora.** Anansi the spider wins the Sky-God Nyame's stories by wit, and later became a figure of resistance for enslaved peoples. A clever small hero who beats the powerful: the Monkey's trickster streak in spider form. [s14]
- **Singapore and Malaysia; Hong Kong.** Spider fighting with male jumping spiders (Thiania bhamoensis and relatives), at its peak in the 1960s and 1970s; the loser flees. The jumping spider as a pocket-sized champion with a fighter's pride. Different genus from Phidippus, so use as 'jumping spiders' only. [s17] [s13]
- **Modern North American pop culture.** Phidippus regius is the species behind the pet-trade boom after 2017, helped by the animated short series Lucas the Spider. A rare case of a spider people find charming on sight: Leo's stage presence. [s2]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'jumping spider spirit animal means taking the leap of faith' lines found on totem blogs. No traditional source located. Japanese 'Honchi Asobi' jumping-spider contests rest on one unsourced amateur page; do not use. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Fire (Leo) meets yang Metal (Monkey). Leo wants to be seen making the big move; the Monkey wants to be clever about it. In this animal the boldness is real but calculated: a showy hunter that measures every gap. Fire controls Metal in the five-phase cycle, so Leo's heat keeps testing the Monkey's sharp edge, and the result is nerve with a plan.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: accurate judgment of distance and risk, theatrical confidence that is earned, inventive routes around obstacles, the ability to keep a goal in mind after it drops out of sight, warmth toward the people it chooses.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: measuring a solved gap one more time; pride that hates to be seen missing; the Monkey's restlessness turning into showing off or stinging jokes; keeping a private tally of who noticed the leap. 朝三暮四 zhāo sān mù sì: getting stuck on how the total was offered instead of what it is.
- **Love.** Our reading: courts openly, with display and charm (Leo), then lets the other person decide at close range. Slow to settle (Monkey) but loyal once in (Leo). Needs a partner who enjoys the show and is not frightened by how closely they are being read.
- **Money.** Our reading: Monkey's head for numbers plus Leo's taste for the grand gesture. Good at calculated bets with a safety line attached; the risk is spending for applause. Keeps a fallback, like the dragline.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: goes still, works out the angle, then strikes once with a precise point. Pride makes backing down hard; the Monkey's wit can wound. Better tactic: say the measured view early, before it hardens into a verdict.
