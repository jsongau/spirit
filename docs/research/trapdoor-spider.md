## The Trapdoor Spider as a symbol
- **Chinese (Tang dynasty materia medica).** Chen Cangqi's 本草拾遺 Běncǎo Shíyí records the 螲蟷 diédāng: 螲蟷是處有之，形似蜘蛛，穴土為窩，穴上有蓋覆穴口 (it is found everywhere, shaped like a spider, digs its nest in the earth and covers the hole with a lid). It was used in medicine for boils. The lidded burrow has a written Chinese name and a 1,200-year paper trail. Present as a recorded text, not a symbol meaning. [s12] [s13]
- **Chinese (folk name, modern).** The Chinese hourglass trapdoor spider is nicknamed 金錢活板門蛛 jīnqián huóbǎnmén zhū, the money trapdoor spider, because the hard disc of its abdomen, which it uses to plug the burrow, looks like an old coin. A sourced bridge between hidden doors and stored wealth. Species: Cyclocosmia ricketti, not every trapdoor spider. [s12]
- **Western Australia (science as story).** Number 16, a female Gaius villosus at North Bungulla Reserve, was tracked by arachnologist Barbara York Main from 1974 and lived about 43 years in one burrow, the oldest spider on record. She died in 2016 when a parasitic wasp pierced the silk plug of her door. Mason, Wardell-Johnson and Main 2018, Pacific Conservation Biology. David Attenborough filmed Main's work in 1981 (The Lady of the Spiders). [s7] [s8] [s9]
- **Japanese.** Tsuchigumo 土蜘蛛, earth spider, began as a Yamato court slur for clans who refused imperial rule, thought to come from tsuchigomori 土隠, those who hide in the ground (people in caves and earthen dugouts). Later a giant spider monster slain by Minamoto no Raikō; Noh play Tsuchigumo. Shadow material only: the hidden one feared by the centre. Not a trapdoor-spider legend; name the earth-spider link honestly. [s15]
- **Hopi (spider in general).** Spider Grandmother, Kokyangwuti, works with Tawa to make the world and leads people through the successive worlds; in spider form she lives underground in a hole that is like a kiva. A general spider figure, not a trapdoor spider. Tell it as Hopi tradition, attributed, with no 'you' reading laid over it. [s16]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Totem-blog claims that the trapdoor spider spirit animal means patience, ambush or 'waiting for the right moment'. No traditional source found.

## The fusion
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Earth under Mercury meets yin Fire. Virgo builds, checks and rechecks; the Snake keeps its heat coiled and its plans private. Fire produces Earth in the generating cycle, so the Snake's heat feeds Virgo's patient building. The Trapdoor Spider is the craftsman with a secret: a perfect, unseen structure and a fast, warm strike from inside it.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: finishing work nobody will praise, reading a situation through small signals (the twig lines), staying put long enough to get very good, discretion people can trust.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the door that never opens; perfectionism (Virgo) sealed with secrecy (Snake). And the Number 16 lesson: the seal you trusted most is the one to check, because the threat came through the plug.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow to open, watches a person for a long time through a crack in the lid, then lets them all the way in. Devoted and private. Needs a partner who does not take the closed door personally and does not pry it open.
- **Money.** Our reading: a saver. Spends close to home, distrusts schemes that need it to leave the burrow, quietly builds reserves (the money trapdoor spider's coin plug is a fair image).
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids open fights, says little, then acts once and decisively. Can hold a grievance in silence far too long.

## Fact audit of the live page

| Fact | Verdict | Note | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Header hanzi 門 (rail__han and serial-han), shown with pinyin mén | **WRONG** | 門 mén means door, not the animal. The trapdoor spider's Chinese name is 螲蟷 diédāng (classical, Bencao Shiyi; pronounced like 迭當); the modern descriptive name is 活板門蛛 huóbǎnmén zhū. Recommend 螲蟷 diédāng. | [s12] [s13] [s14] |
| Intro: 'a burrowing spider of the family Ctenizidae found across warm regions worldwide' | **WRONG** | Ctenizidae now holds 2 genera, 5 species, in Europe and Turkey, after Godwin et al. 2018 split off Halonoproctidae and Stasimopus moved out in 2020. 'Trapdoor spider' covers about a dozen families (Idiopidae, Halonoproctidae, Barychelidae, Nemesiidae and others). Say 'spiders from several families'. | [s5] [s6] |
| Page source list cites Main BY (1957), trapdoor spiders of the genus Aganippe | **WRONG** | Aganippe is not Ctenizidae; it is now a synonym of Idiosoma in Idiopidae. The citation does not support the Ctenizidae framing. | [s17] |
| 01 The sealed door: silk-hinged lid covered with soil and debris, camouflaged so the closed door is invisible to most observers | **OK** | Supported in general: the Gaius villosus lid is 'very well camouflaged' (WA Naturalists). 'Invisible to most observers even at close range' is a little strong; trained observers learn to spot the radiating sticks. | [s9] |
| 02 Trip-lines: silk trip-lines radiate from the door and detect prey by vibration | **OK** | True for some species, e.g. Gaius villosus attaches silk or twigs to the burrow rim that spread outward and carry vibrations. Not universal; EBSCO describes others sensing movement with the front legs at the door. Narrow to 'many species'. | [s9] [s10] |
| 03 The ambush: opens the door 'in under a millisecond' | **UNVERIFIABLE** | No measured strike time for trapdoor spiders found. Sub-millisecond strikes are documented in trap-jaw spiders (Mecysmaucheniidae, Wood et al. 2016), a different group: likely a borrowed fact. Cut the number. | [s11] [s10] |
| 04 Females 'documented living 20 to 30 years, among the longest lifespans of any spider', same burrow for life | **UNVERIFIABLE** | The 20 to 30 range has no source found (EBSCO gives 5 to 20 years on average). Burrow fidelity is right. The documented record is Number 16, Gaius villosus, about 43 years in one burrow (Mason et al. 2018). Replace the range with the record, named. | [s7] [s8] [s10] |
| 05 Entire territory 'less than one metre' from the burrow | **UNVERIFIABLE** | No source gives a radius. Sources only say females stay in one burrow and spiderlings burrow near the mother. Cut the number. | [s9] |
| 06 Door quality 'directly determines hunting success and predator avoidance' | **UNVERIFIABLE** | Interpretation dressed as an Animal fact. No study found. Note: Number 16 died when a wasp pierced her silk plug, which is a real, sourced version of the point. | [s7] |
| Element Fire; recent Snake years 2001, 2013, 2025 | **OK** | Snake's fixed element is Fire. | [s3] |
| Proverb 厚積薄發 hòu jī bó fā, labeled as our own pairing | **OK** | Real phrase, from Su Shi, 稼說送張琥 Jià Shuō Sòng Zhāng Hǔ: 博觀而約取，厚積而薄發 bó guān ér yuē qǔ, hòu jī ér bó fā. Could credit Su Shi on the page. | [s18] |
