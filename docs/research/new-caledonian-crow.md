# New Caledonian Crow dossier: Virgo x Year of the Monkey

Slug `new-caledonian-crow`. Element: Metal (from the Monkey); Virgo adds Earth, mutable. Research date 2026-11-08. Everything marked 'Our reading' is interpretation, not tradition.

## Western half: Virgo
- Temperament: Analytical, practical, helpful, dependable, detail-minded, cautious in love, improves things through mastery of a craft. Shadow: perfectionism that tips into nitpicking, can seem curt, moody withdrawal.
- Planet: Mercury (Wikipedia also lists Ceres as a modern ruler)
- Element: Earth (with Taurus and Capricorn)
- Modality: Mutable, the most adaptable earth sign; opposite sign Pisces
- Sources: [s1] [s2]

## Chinese half: Monkey
- Temperament: Witty, intelligent, quick learners and crafty opportunists, curious, flexible at seizing chances. Shadow: passions fade fast, restless, pranks that sting, reluctant to admit failure.
- Yin/yang: Yang
- Element: Metal (fixed); earthly branch 申 shēn; Monkey hour 15:00 to 16:59
- Trine: Rat, Dragon, Monkey
- Secret friend: Snake
- Clash: Tiger (Wikipedia also lists Pig as the harm pair)
- Lucky colours and numbers: not included (sources disagree, per brief)
- Sources: [s3] [s4] [s5]

## The New Caledonian Crow as a symbol
- **Aesop (Greek), Perry 390.** The Crow and the Pitcher: a thirsty crow drops pebbles into a pitcher until the water rises. Avianus's moral: thoughtfulness beats brute strength. Pliny the Elder already took it as real crow behaviour. This crow is the species that scientists actually tested on the fable (Jelbert 2014), so the myth and the bird meet directly. [s8] [s16]
- **Chinese.** The three-legged sun crow 三足烏 sānzúwū, the golden crow 金烏 jīnwū, lives in the sun; ten sun crows perched on the Fusang tree until the archer Hou Yi shot down all but one. Also Korean 삼족오 Samjok-o and Japanese Yatagarasu, a guide sent from heaven. [s6]
- **Chinese.** 烏鳥私情 wū niǎo sīqíng, 'the crow's private feeling': the crow that feeds its aged parent, an image of filial piety found in the Records of the Three Kingdoms and the Book of Jin. It echoes this crow's very long childhood at its parents' side. [s7] [s19]
- **Chinese classic (Analects 15).** 工欲善其事，必先利其器 gōng yù shàn qí shì, bì xiān lì qí qì: a craftsman who wants to do good work must first sharpen his tools (Confucius, answering Zigong). The page uses this proverb and labels the pairing as its own; the attribution to the Analects is real and could be added. [s9]
- **Chinese (Monkey half).** 猴 hóu sounds like 侯 hóu, marquis, so the monkey stands for promotion; in the zodiac race the monkey stopped for peaches, took a shortcut and still came ninth. [s4]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Kanak (New Caledonian Indigenous) stories about this crow: none found in a source I could open. The local name 'qua-qua' is tagged citation needed on Wikipedia. Generic 'crow spirit animal = magic and change' blog claims also left out. [s10]

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Earth ruled by Mercury meets yang Metal (Monkey). Virgo wants to understand the mechanism and do it properly; the Monkey wants to get its hands on the problem now. The New Caledonian Crow is the craftsman-trickster: it shapes a custom tool from a leaf or twig, then uses it with monkey speed. Both halves are tool people: Mercury rules craft and trade, the Monkey is the zodiac's most inventive sign.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: building the exact instrument for the job, learning by watching and then improving, planning ahead for the next problem, explaining how a thing works, quiet competence that others copy.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: sharpening past the moment (the page's own shadow, and a fair one); Virgo's nitpicking plus Monkey restlessness can mean many half-perfect projects; showing off cleverness; impatience with people who solve by trial and error.
- **Love.** Our reading: shows love through fixing and making. Cautious to commit (Virgo) yet playful once in (Monkey). Needs a partner who values the useful gift and tolerates long silences while a problem is being turned over.
- **Money.** Our reading: Virgo budgeting plus Monkey opportunism. Good at spotting the clever angle, tracking receipts, building small systems that pay. Risk: buying better tools instead of using the ones on hand.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: diagnoses the cause, often correctly, and then delivers the diagnosis too precisely. Monkey wit can turn into a sharp joke. Practice: offer the fix as a question.

## Careers
- **Engineering, product design, toolmaking, industrial design.** Our reading: the hook-tool fact; China Highlights lists science and engineering for the Monkey (s5).
- **Research science, lab work, experimental psychology.** Our reading: Virgo analysis and the crow as the subject of cause-and-effect experiments.
- **Software, automation, debugging, data analysis.** Our reading: building a tool once so the problem never needs solving by hand again.
- **Surgery, dentistry, watch or instrument repair.** Our reading: precision with a hooked instrument in a narrow space, the crow's whole foraging method.
- **Teaching a craft, apprenticeships, technical training.** Our reading: pandanus designs differ by region and are thought to pass socially (s12, s21); long juvenile learning (s19).

## Best matches
- **Archerfish** (`archerfish`): Virgo x Dragon. Same sign, and the Dragon sits in the Monkey's trine. Two precise problem-solvers.
- **Leafcutter Ant** (`leafcutter-ant`): Virgo x Rat. Rat is in the Monkey trine; same Earth sign. Both build with cut leaves.
- **Pangolin** (`pangolin`): Taurus x Snake. Snake is the Monkey's six-harmony (secret friend) partner; Taurus shares Virgo's Earth.
- **Bearded Vulture** (`bearded-vulture`): Capricorn x Dragon. Dragon trine plus Earth-sign harmony; another bird that uses a technique (dropping bones) to get at food.
- **Coconut Crab** (`coconut-crab`): Cancer x Rat. Rat trine; Cancer Water complements Virgo Earth. The live page already uses it.

## Hard matches
- **Leopard Seal** (`leopard-seal`): Pisces x Tiger. Monkey and Tiger are the traditional six-clash pair, and Pisces is Virgo's opposite sign.
- **Dragonfly** (`dragonfly`): Virgo x Tiger. Same sign, so it feels familiar, but the Monkey-Tiger clash runs underneath.

## Candidate lines
1. You trim the leaf to fit the crack before your beak goes anywhere near it, and the Monkey in you is itching the whole time to just reach in.
2. In trials, crows of your kind picked the right tool for a task they would not see for another ten minutes. Your bag gets packed the same way, for problems only you can see coming.
3. Drop the stones that sink, skip the ones that float. You sort a problem by what actually raises the water, and it makes you short with anyone who keeps throwing in corks.
4. Your sharpest tricks were learned slowly, a year and more at a parent's side watching how the leaf gets torn. When you seem to solve something in a second, that is the long apprenticeship showing.
5. Mercury hands you the analysis and the Monkey hands you the fingers. Give you a pandanus leaf and a hard question and a whole afternoon can disappear, happily.
6. The craftsman sharpens his tool first, Confucius said. You sharpen it, then sharpen it again, and some days the grub has crawled deeper by the time the edge is perfect.

| Crows make hooked tools by cutting and shaping Pandanus leaf strips, trimming the barbed edge into a hook; first documented by Hunt (1996) in Nature. | WRONG (conflation) | Hunt 1996 describes two tool types: hooked-twig tools and stepped-cut barbed pandanus-leaf tools (s11). The hook is carved from a twig; pandanus tools are torn strips whose natural edge barbs act as hooks (s10, s21). Fix: 'They carve hooks into twigs and tear barbed strips from pandanus leaves.' Hunt 1996 in Nature is correct. | [s10] [s11] [s21] |
| Crows carry tools to a new foraging site in advance; Rutz et al. (2010) in Science. | WRONG (citation) / UNVERIFIABLE (claim) | Rutz et al. 2010 Science is a stable-isotope study of how much of the diet comes from tool-caught larvae (s13), not tool transport. Klump et al. 2015 showed wild crows safekeep tools under a foot or in holes between uses (s14). Boeckle et al. 2020 showed crows choosing the right tool for a future task 10 minutes ahead in a trained sequence (s15). Replace with Boeckle 2020 and drop 'carrying across sites'. | [s13] [s14] [s15] |
| Aesop's Fable task: crows preferred sinking to floating objects, showing understanding of displacement, not association; Jelbert et al. (2014) PLOS ONE. | OK (overstated) | Sinking objects chosen 88% of the time; solid over hollow 89% (s16). But crows failed the U-tube and the narrow-vs-wide tube tasks, and the authors call their understanding 'sophisticated, but incomplete' (s16). Cut 'rather than associative learning'. | [s16] |
| Taylor et al. (2009) in PNAS: when the causal mechanism was masked, performance degraded, showing causal not associative reasoning. | WRONG (journal and description) | Taylor, Hunt, Medina and Gray 2009 is in Proc. R. Soc. B 276 (the page's own bibliography says so). Its abstract reports 3 of 6 crows solved the trap-tube and concludes they reason 'both causally and analogically'; there is no 'masked mechanism' result in it (s17). I could not open the full text (PMC blocked), so the masking detail is at best unverifiable. Rewrite from the abstract. | [s17] |
| Tool designs (hook shape, angle of cut) differ between populations; Hunt and Gray (2003) in Animal Cognition, evidence of cultural transmission. | WRONG (citation) / OK (core) | Hunt and Gray 2003 is Proc. R. Soc. B 270:867-874, doi 10.1098/rspb.2002.2302 (s12); the page's DOI link (rspb.2003.2387) does not match. The paper found three pandanus designs (wide, narrow, stepped) with overlapping regional distributions, from about 5,550 tools at 21 sites, and argues cumulative change from one origin; social transmission is proposed, not tested. 'Angle of the cut' is not in the abstract. Say 'thought to pass on socially'. | [s12] [s21] |
| One of the longest juvenile dependency periods among corvids; young stay with parents up to two years while learning tools. | WRONG (duration) | Uomini et al. 2020 (s19): young can stay with parents up to three years, and learning to make tools takes about a year. Hunt et al. 2012: parents regularly fed some juveniles up to 10 months after fledging, 'one of the longest known periods of regular extended parental provisioning in birds' (s18). 'Relative to body size among corvids' is unsourced; drop it. | [s18] [s19] |
