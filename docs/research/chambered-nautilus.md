## The Chambered Nautilus as a symbol
- **Chinese (Western Jin, 265 to 316 CE).** Two cups made from Indian Ocean nautilus shells (鹦鹉螺杯 yīngwǔluó bēi), gilded and painted, were excavated at the Jianyaomiao Western Jin cemetery in Pizhou, Jiangsu (one in 2015); ifeng says only three are known in China. Scans show more than 30 inner chambers linked by small holes, so wine fills every chamber but cannot be drunk in one go. The same article notes Li Bai used 鹦鹉杯 yīngwǔ bēi, 'parrot cup', in a verse about drinking. Strongest bridge on this page: a vessel that gives itself up one chamber at a time. [s9] [s10]
- **Chinese name.** The nautilus is 鸚鵡螺 yīngwǔluó, 'parrot snail', from the beak-like curve of the shell. The page header uses only 螺 luó, which means any spiral shell, snail or conch. [s12] [s13]
- **Palau (Micronesia).** Palauans call the nautilus kedarm and see it as a symbol of vulnerable or fragile character; a proverb compares a quick-tempered person to the nautilus. Read via Wikipedia, which cites McKnight, 'Proverbs of Palau', Journal of American Folklore 1968. I did not open McKnight; verify the wording before quoting. [s6]
- **American (1858).** Oliver Wendell Holmes, 'The Chambered Nautilus': the creature leaves each outgrown chamber for a larger one, and the poem turns this into spiritual growth, ending 'Build thee more stately mansions, O my soul'. Public domain. Note the tension: the page reads the sealed rooms as keeping; Holmes reads them as leaving. [s11]
- **European Renaissance and Baroque; Samoa.** Shells were mounted by goldsmiths as display cups for cabinets of curiosities (for example the Burghley Nef). In Samoa the shell decorates the tuiga ceremonial headdress. Both from Wikipedia only; fine as one-line context. [s5] [s6]
- **Golden ratio.** Unsourced, do not use: the popular claim that the nautilus shell is a golden spiral. Wikipedia states it is a logarithmic spiral but not a golden one. [s6]
- **Spirit-animal blogs.** Unsourced, do not use: generic 'nautilus spirit animal means growth and expansion' claims. No traditional source located. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water (Cancer, the shell-carrier ruled by the Moon) meets yin Fire (the Snake, the coiled thinker who waits). The nautilus is coiled shell plus patience: it carries its whole history, lives only in the newest room, and moves mostly in the dark. Water controls Fire in the five-phase cycle, so the Snake's heat runs banked and deep.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: steadiness under pressure that comes from finished business, a memory that keeps the record without reliving it, intuition that works on shape and mood before detail, a grip on people and projects much stronger than it looks.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: archiving instead of answering. Sealing a hurt into a chamber and calling it closed; letting people meet only the shell; Snake secrecy and Cancer guardedness agreeing too easily that the hood should stay down.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow, nocturnal, private. Comes up close only when the water feels safe, and from a partner who will not grab at the soft part. Loyal for decades once settled (a 20-year animal that matures late).
- **Money.** Our reading: Moon and Snake both favour holding. Saves in compartments, keeps separate reserves, distrusts showy spending. Risk: so much is sealed away that nothing is ever enjoyed.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: withdraws fully and seals the opening, then decides from inside. Rarely strikes first; the danger is the slow, cold distance afterwards. Practice: name the hurt before it gets its own wall.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi (rail__han and serial-han): 螺
  螺 luó means spiral shell, snail or conch, any coiled shell. The nautilus is 鸚鵡螺 yīngwǔluó (simplified 鹦鹉螺). Use the full traditional name with pinyin, or at minimum add pinyin and gloss 螺 honestly as 'spiral shell'. [s12] [s13]
- **OK**: Lives off steep reef walls, drifting between a hundred and seven hundred meters down.
  Usually below 100 m, sighted to 703 m; Dunstan 2011 extends habitable range to 700 m. Shell implodes near 800 m. [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: Grows by adding chambers in a spiral and seals each older chamber behind a wall, living only in the newest, outermost one.
  Septa seal earlier chambers; animal lives in the body chamber. [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: The siphuncle threads through every sealed chamber and slowly draws the fluid out, letting gas fill the space, so the emptied rooms keep the animal afloat.
  Siphuncle removes salt and liquid by slow osmosis; gas in chambers is below atmospheric pressure. Wording is fine. [s6]
- **WRONG**: It controls how deep it sits by adjusting that gas by tiny amounts, and rises and sinks by managing pressure rather than by swimming.
  Buoyancy changes are slow osmotic adjustments for long-term density, not a lift system. Vertical movement is jet-propelled swimming through the hyponome; Dunstan 2011 notes ascents from depth rely on energetically costly vertical swimming, and below about 250 m liquid seeps into the chambers so the animal must return shallower to regain neutral buoyancy. Rewrite: it stays neutrally buoyant and swims by jet. [s6] [s7] [s8]
- **OK**: The nautilus eye has no lens and no cornea; an open pinhole flooded with seawater, reading light and movement in blur.
  Pinhole eye open to the water, no solid lens, simple images. Wikipedia adds it relies mainly on smell for foraging and mates; worth using. [s5] [s6]
- **WRONG**: Each night it rises toward shallower reef water to feed and sinks back down before daylight, spending the bright hours deep and unseen.
  This is the old textbook model. Tracking at Osprey Reef (Dunstan 2011) found daytime split between resting at about 160 to 225 m and foraging at 489 to 700 m, and near-continuous night movement between about 130 and 700 m; patterns vary by population. Soften to 'moves up and down the reef wall through the night'. [s7] [s6]
- **WRONG**: Explores with around ninety small tentacles that grip nothing tightly.
  Count is about 50 to over 90 depending on sex and individual (OK as 'up to about ninety'). 'Grip nothing tightly' is the opposite of the record: the digital tentacles have no suckers but adhere strongly through a secretion from ridges, and release by muscle contraction. [s6] [s5]
- **OK**: At a real threat pulls its entire body inside and seals the opening with a hood.
  Leathery hood formed from two folded tentacles. [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: 'Your animal's line has waited five hundred million years' and 'the nautilus line has outlasted five mass extinctions'.
  Nautiloids appear in the Late Cambrian and have changed little over about 500 million years, so '500 million years' is defensible only for nautiloids as a group. The species N. pompilius has fossils only from the Early Pleistocene, and family Nautilidae dates from the Late Triassic. My sources confirm survival of the K-Pg event, not all five. Say 'its nautiloid ancestors go back about 500 million years' and drop 'five mass extinctions'. [s5] [s6]
- **WRONG**: Purple Star section: Tài Yīn (太陰) 'tends to sit bright in the chart' and Tiān Fǔ (天府) 'Purple Star tends to place near the Life palace' for a Cancer Snake.
  A ZWDS chart is cast from Chinese-calendar birth year, month, day and hour; Western sun signs play no role. The page's disclaimer admits the pairing is its own, but the body copy states placements as tendencies of the tradition. Relabel or cut. [s14]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Header crossing glyph 蛇 (Snake) shown without pinyin.
  Style flag, not a fact error: 蛇 shé is correct for Snake but needs pinyin per site rule. [s2]
