# Slow Loris dossier: Scorpio x Year of the Snake

Slug `slow-loris` · element Fire · researched 2026-11-08 · for the owner's rewrite, not for publishing as is.

## Strongest new findings
1. **The Snake-year animal that imitates a snake.** Researchers propose the slow loris mimics the spectacled cobra: hunched arms like a hood, a cobra-like hiss and breathing sound, a back stripe and face mask that read as a cobra from above [S5, S6]. This is the bridge for the Snake half.
2. **Gatekeeper of the heavens.** In Borneo, lorises were believed to guard heaven's gate, and each person had their own loris waiting in the afterlife [S7, S8]. A fit for Scorpio, the sign of death and rebirth.
3. **The shy one with power in its blood.** Indonesians call it malu malu, 'shy one' [S7], yet West Javanese lore says its blood could poison wounds or start a landslide [S5]. Softness and danger in one body, which is the page's whole theme, now with a cultural source behind it.

## The loris as a symbol
- **Borneo (reported in Nekaris et al. 2010)**: Slow lorises were believed to be gatekeepers of the heavens, and each person had a personal slow loris waiting for them in the afterlife. [S7, S8]
- **Indonesia**: Called malu malu, the 'shy one'. The everyday name is kukang. [S7]
- **North Sumatra and wider Indonesia**: A loris buried under a house foundation was thought to bring good luck; some buried one under a road to prevent accidents; wearing loris bones was considered lucky. A guardian of thresholds and crossings. [S7]
- **West Java (Sukabumi, Tasikmalaya, Garut)**: The loris's power was located in its blood: ancestors smeared swords with loris blood so wounds would fester; a drop of its blood touching the ground could set off a landslide; its teeth held black magic. [S5]
- **Cambodia (Mondulkiri hunters)**: Lorises were said to heal their own broken bones at once after a fall, and seeing one meant bad luck on a hunt. Loris medicine is said to cure '100 diseases'. [S7, S9]
- **Folk range-wide (Thailand, Laos, Myanmar, Indonesia, Cambodia, China, Vietnam)**: Local lore holds that the loris has a toxic bite and a bad taste. [S5]
- **Sri Lanka / India**: Folklore here concerns the slender loris (genus Loris), a different animal. Unsourced for slow loris, do not use. [S8]

## The fusion (our interpretation)
**Temperament.** Our reading: Scorpio is fixed Water, the Snake is yin Fire. Both are yin, both are the patient, inward half of their systems, so this crossing doubles down on stillness. The heat is real but kept under water. You tend to read a room long before you enter the conversation, and people often mistake your quiet for having nothing to say.

**Gifts.** Our reading: a loyalty that is slow to form and very hard to break; perception that catches what others perform rather than say; the ability to wait out a situation that would rattle faster people; and a protective streak that arrives the moment someone you love is threatened. The Borneo belief that each person has their own loris waiting at the gate fits this crossing: you tend to be the person others find at the end of the hard night.

**Shadow.** Our reading: suspicion that hardens into a guard that never stands down; testing people quietly instead of asking; holding a grudge as stored venom; and letting others underestimate you because it is safer than being seen. The Cambodian lore of the loris healing its own bones fits the shadow too: you often heal alone, without telling anyone you fell.

**Love.** Our reading: you tend to love slowly and then completely. You often watch a person for a long time before you let them close, and once you choose, you hold on with Scorpio intensity and Snake possessiveness. You need a partner who will not rush you or mock the shyness. There are times when you test loyalty in silence; saying the fear out loud costs less than the test.

**Money.** Our reading: a careful, private hoarder in the good sense. You tend to keep reserves nobody knows about and dislike talking money in public. The Snake tradition prizes calm strategy; the risk is holding so tightly that a good opportunity passes while you are still checking it.

**Conflict.** Our reading: you rarely start a fight and you often finish one. Your first move is to go still and gather information. The loris's raised-arms posture looks like surrender and is actually the moment it arms itself (S5); in conflict you can look like you have backed down while you are deciding exactly what to say. The growth edge is to warn before you bite.

## Fact audit of the live page
| Fact | Verdict | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Slow lorises are the only venomous primate; toxin from brachial glands on the inner arm mixes with saliva; can cause anaphylactic shock in humans. | **OK** | Supported by Nekaris et al. 2013 venom review. Note the gland is described as on the inside of the elbow/upper arm; venom alone was not lethal in mice, only combined with saliva. [S5] |
| Documented human fatalities have been recorded from bites, with cases described in Nekaris et al. (2013) in PLoS ONE (page links doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0060793). | **WRONG** | The 2013 Nekaris PLoS ONE paper is 'Tickled to Death' (doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0069215), about YouTube pet videos, not bites. The venom review (J Venom Anim Toxins 2013) says anaphylaxis is 'sometimes resulting in death' but gives no case count. Recommend: cite S5 and soften to 'bites can trigger anaphylactic shock, which can be fatal'. The DOI on the page (pone.0060793) could not be opened to check what it points to. [S5, S10] |
| When threatened, the loris raises both arms above its head, bringing the mouth to the brachial glands to coat its teeth before biting. | **OK** | Described in the venom review; the mix is applied to the head or held in the mouth to bite. New angle: researchers also link the hunched posture, hissing and back stripe to mimicry of the spectacled cobra. [S5, S6] |
| Like the tarsier, the slow loris has eyes fixed in their sockets that cannot rotate; it turns its whole head. | **UNVERIFIABLE** | No source found. Fixed, immobile eyes are a well-known tarsier trait; this looks like a carry-over. The Tarsier is also Scorpio's Rat animal, so the claim risks duplicating that page. Recommend cutting. |
| Locomotion speeds among the lowest recorded for any primate relative to body size. | **UNVERIFIABLE** | Slow, non-leaping movement along vines is documented (S11), but no source found for the comparative ranking. Soften to 'moves slowly and does not leap'. [S11] |
| Slow lorises are critically endangered, driven by the pet trade fuelled by viral videos. | **WRONG** | Only some species carry Critically Endangered status (the Javan slow loris, S11); the genus as a whole is not CE. Pet trade and viral videos are well supported (S10, S8). Correction: 'All slow lorises are threatened and protected under CITES Appendix I; the Javan slow loris is Critically Endangered.' Confirm each species on the IUCN Red List before publishing. [S11, S10, S8] |
| The toxin is produced continuously and replenished through regular grooming of the inner arms. | **UNVERIFIABLE** | No source found for continuous production or replenishment by grooming. Lorises do lick the gland to mix venom (S5). Recommend cutting the 'continuous replenishment' framing. [S5] |
