## The Humpback Whale as a symbol
- **Native Hawaiian.** The koholā (whale) is regarded as a form of Kanaloa, the ocean god, and some families hold whales as ʻaumakua, protective ancestral guardians. The Kumulipo creation chant records the whale's birth: "Hanau ka Palaoa noho i kai" (born is the whale living in the sea). Whales are also said to have guided Polynesians to the islands. NOAA presents this in a humpback context because the Hawaiian sanctuary exists for humpbacks; the chant itself names the whale generally. [s5]
- **Māori (Ngāti Porou, Ngāti Konohi, East Coast North Island).** Paikea, the whale rider, is the ancestor of the Ngāti Konohi sub-tribe; his carved figure rides a whale on the roof of Whitireia marae at Whāngārā (Pine Taiapa, 1937). Te Ara notes whale symbolism runs through East Coast carving and oral tradition. The species of Paikea's whale is not stated; do not call it a humpback. [s6]
- **Japanese coastal communities.** About 100 kujira-zuka (whale mounds) honor stranded or hunted whales. Some whales were called Ebisu and enshrined so they would not become angry gods; because whales arrived with the fish, the whale as Ebisu became a god of fishing and safe passage. Whales in general, not humpbacks specifically. [s7]
- **Modern global (1970 to present).** Songs of the Humpback Whale (1970), produced by Roger and Katy Payne from Frank Watlington's Navy hydrophone recordings off Bermuda, became the bestselling nature recording and helped start the worldwide Save the Whales movement; an excerpt flies on the Voyager Golden Record. [s8]
- **Classical Chinese (Dragon half, with care).** Zhuangzi opens with 鯤 Kūn, a fish of the northern sea so vast no one knows how many thousand lǐ it measures, which turns into the bird 鵬 Péng and rises 90,000 lǐ: 北冥有魚，其名為鯤 (běi míng yǒu yú, qí míng wéi kūn). Idiom 鵬程萬里 (péng chéng wàn lǐ), a boundless future. Kun is a giant fish, not a whale, in the text; Wikipedia notes no whale identification. Use only as 'giant creature of the deep sea that becomes something that fills the sky', never as Chinese whale lore. [s9] [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'humpback spirit animal means emotional depth / communication' totem-blog claims; no tradition located. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water (Cancer, Moon-ruled) meets yang Earth (Dragon). Cancer starts things from feeling; the Dragon needs scale. The Humpback is feeling expressed at full size: the song heard across a basin, the breach. In the five phases Earth dams Water, so the Dragon's Earth gives the Cancer tide a shoreline. The Moon governs tides and the humpback lives by seasonal tides of migration, cold water to warm.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: emotional range that others borrow; protection extended past the family line (the orca interference record); a voice that spreads and gets picked up; patient group strategy (the bubble net); loyalty that travels.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the breach that drowns the message; holding on (Cancer) plus pride in scale (Dragon) makes it hard to scrap the old song; hasty rescues that cost more than they save; feeling the size of an ocean and expecting others to match it.
- **Love.** Our reading: Moon-ruled devotion with Dragon visibility. Loves at volume and wants it witnessed. Needs a partner who can hold depth without being flattened by it, and who does not mind that part of the year is spent travelling toward them rather than beside them.
- **Money.** Our reading: Cancer saves for the nest; the Dragon spends on the grand gesture. Seasonal earner: long lean stretches then big feeding seasons. Risk: the expensive rescue.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: slow to anger, then all at once; will put its body between a threat and someone weaker. When hurt, goes quiet and deep before surfacing. Practice: sing at depth first, breach only if the quiet note fails.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (soften)**: Male humpbacks sing a long, structured song that changes within a single breeding season, as new phrases spread whale to whale and sometimes replace an entire population's song within a few years.
  Garland et al. 2011 documented repeated song revolutions moving east across the South Pacific over 11 years; UQ: songs usually took about two years to spread across six populations, and males 'change their tune' every year. Gradual change within a season is widely reported, but the sources reached describe yearly change. Suggest: 'changes from year to year; new songs have swept east across the South Pacific, replacing the old one population by population.' [s11] [s12]
- **OK (soften)**: A humpback can breach its entire body, up to forty tons, clear of the water; communication, parasite removal and display have been proposed, no single explanation confirmed.
  NOAA gives weight 'up to about 40 tons' and confirms breaching. Forty tons is the maximum, not a typical breach; ADW average mass about 30 t. Reasons list not verified against a primary source but 'no single explanation confirmed' is a safe statement. Suggest 'a whale that can weigh up to 40 tons'. [s13] [s14]
- **OK (narrow)**: Pectoral fins are the longest of any whale, up to a third of body length, close to five meters; genus Megaptera means 'big wing'.
  Woodward et al. 2006: humpback flippers average up to 30.8% of body length, the highest of the baleen whales studied, and longer than predicted for body size across cetaceans; ADW: 'almost a third of the body length'; NOAA: Megaptera means 'big-winged'. 'Longest of any whale' is the standard claim but the paper ranks only proportion, not absolute length among all whales. 'Close to five meters' not found in the sources reached (UNVERIFIABLE detail). Suggest 'proportionally the longest flippers of any whale, nearly a third of its body'. [s13] [s14] [s15]
- **WRONG (inflated)**: Humpbacks have been documented on more than a hundred occasions placing themselves between orcas and other species being hunted, including seals and other whales' calves, with no clear benefit to themselves.
  Pitman et al. 2017 reviewed 115 humpback-killer whale interactions in total, not 115 interventions for other species. Humpbacks harassed attacking mammal-eating orcas in at least 55% of 56 interactions; in attacks, 89% of prey were other species (pinnipeds, cetaceans, one fish). Authors: no apparent benefit when other species are attacked; 'interspecific altruism, even if unintentional, could not be ruled out.' They also suggest humpbacks may respond to orca calls without knowing the prey. Rewrite: 'Pitman and colleagues (2017) reviewed 115 encounters; humpbacks often mobbed hunting orcas even when the prey was a seal or another species.' [s16]
- **WRONG (method and outdated)**: One of the longest annual migrations of any mammal; one tracked individual logged over 9,800 km in one migration (page wording paraphrased).
  The 9,800 km record (Stevick et al., 2010-11) was not one tracked migration: one female photographed off Brazil in 1999 and off Madagascar in 2001, two sightings two years apart. That record was also beaten: Kalashnikova et al. 2024 (Royal Society Open Science) matched a male from Colombia (2013, 2017) to Zanzibar (2022), at least 13,046 km apart, by photo-ID. NOAA: some populations migrate about 5,000 miles between feeding and breeding grounds. Keep 'one of the longest migrations of any mammal'; replace the record sentence. [s13] [s17] [s18]
- **OK for the net, UNVERIFIABLE for 'learned rather than instinctive'**: Groups blow rings of bubbles beneath fish to corral them, a cooperative technique learned and passed from whale to whale rather than instinctive.
  NOAA describes group coordinated bubble-net feeding; ADW describes bubble rings and columns. The cultural-transmission evidence is for a variant, lobtail feeding, which spread through the Gulf of Maine population from 1980 to nearly 40% of whales by 2007 (Allen et al. 2013, Science). No source reached shows bubble netting itself is learned and not instinctive. Rewrite to credit the lobtail variant. [s13] [s14] [s19]
- **WRONG (generic and simplified)**: Header hanzi 鲸 (rail__han and serial-han).
  鲸 jīng is the simplified character for 'whale' in general, it carries no pinyin on the page, and other pages use traditional forms. The humpback's own name is 大翅鯨 dàchìjīng, 'big-winged whale' (TBN, Taiwan), which mirrors Megaptera; 座頭鯨 zuòtóujīng is the common alternative (TBN uses it for the genus). Recommend 大翅鯨 dàchìjīng, or at minimum traditional 鯨 jīng with pinyin. [s20]
- **OK**: Element section: Dragon Earth, Cancer Water; Earth restrains Water. Secret friend Rooster, trine Monkey.
  Consistent with the brief's system and Wikipedia's fixed elements. No Purple Star sun-sign claim found on this page; the ZWDS section correctly says charts are cast from lunar birth date and hour. [s3] [s21]
