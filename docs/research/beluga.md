## The Beluga as a symbol
- **Inuvialuit (Mackenzie Delta, western Canadian Arctic).** In a legend shown in the Canadian Museum of Nature exhibition Qilalukkat! (curated by Inuvialuk Myrna Pokiak), a young man threw his hated stepmother into the sea and she became a beluga; the sound of her complaining gives belugas their Inuvialuktun name, qilalukkat. The beluga's name is literally its voice. [s6]
- **Nunavik Inuit (Puvirnituq), the Lumaaq story.** A mother who mistreated her blind son is dragged under by a beluga after he regains his sight (with a loon's help) and harpoons one. Puvirnituq hunters also said some whales were 'part of the Lumaaq' family. Same story family as the Inuvialuit legend: belugas carry a debt about how a family treats its own. [s7]
- **Inuvialuit harvest.** Pokiak calls the beluga harvest 'a necessity for the physical, spiritual and mental health of Inuvialuit families and communities'; one whale feeds a family for a year and almost nothing is wasted. [s6]
- **Sailors and Western naturalists.** 'Canary of the sea' or 'sea canary', for the range of chirps, whistles and clicks. [s8] [s9]
- **Alaska Native languages.** Puugzaq (Siberian Yupik), Cetuaq (Central Yupik), Sisuaq (Inupiat). Names only; the source gives no meanings, so do not invent any. [s9]
- **Modern record (US Navy, San Diego).** NOC the beluga: in May 1984 a diver surfaced asking 'Who told me to get out?'. The voice was NOC, who made speech-like sounds untrained, only when alone or near his handlers, never with the other whales, and stopped after about four years. A real story that works like a legend. [s10]
- **Chinese (Dog half, year-hub material).** The honest dog admitted it needed a full basin of food while the cat lied, so the dog has chased cats ever since (Travel China Guide). True to tradition but would repeat on all 12 Dog pages. [s5]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Blog claims that the beluga spirit animal means 'joy, communication, playfulness'. No traditional source found. (no source)

Note: the Inuvialuit and Nunavik stories are told here as theirs, credited by nation and source. Keep any retelling short and separate from the 'this is you' voice. ## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Water (Pisces) meets yang Earth (Dog). Pisces absorbs every frequency in the water; the Dog decides who is pack and guards them. The Beluga is the listener with a gatekeeper: open to every voice in the estuary, loyal to a few. Earth controls Water in the five-phase cycle, so the Dog half gives the Pisces drift a shoreline. Both Inuit legends tie the beluga to how a family treats its own, which is the Dog's whole question.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: hears what is under the words; picks up other people's 'languages' fast; loyalty that survives long absences (the 1,500-mile return); honesty from the Dog that keeps Pisces empathy from turning into flattery.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the NOC problem. Learning someone's voice so well that your own goes quiet, then stopping altogether. Pisces escape plus Dog bottled worry: complains in private (qilalukkat) instead of saying it once to the person.
- **Love.** Our reading: devoted and protective, reads moods before they are spoken. Needs a partner who asks for the Beluga's own sound. Jealous only of being replaced in the pod.
- **Money.** Our reading: Dog prudence steadies Pisces looseness. Spends on the pod first. Risk: lending to anyone with a good sad story.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: absorbs, adapts, then goes quiet; when a line is crossed for someone it loves, the Dog bites and says the blunt thing. Practice: one plain sentence early, before the ice closes.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **PARTLY UNVERIFIABLE**: Hero/fact 1: belugas have imitated outboard motors, human speech and the calls of other cetaceans.
  Human-speech mimicry is real (NOC, Ridgway et al. 2012; s10). Imitation of human vocal sounds and computer sounds on cue is also shown (Murayama et al. 2014; s13). Outboard motors and 'other cetaceans' calls: no source found. Cut both. [s10] [s13]
- **OK**: The NOC whale produced human-like vocalizations unprompted; a diver surfaced thinking someone had spoken.
  May 1984, diver asked 'Who told me to get out?'. NOC was untrained, spoke only alone or near handlers, stopped after about four years. Cite Ridgway et al. 2012, Current Biology (already in page sources). [s10]
- **WRONG**: The beluga is 'the only cetacean documented to mimic sounds outside its own species by choice'.
  Killer whales have copied dolphin sounds, and the orca Wikie imitated human words such as 'hello' and 'bye-bye' (Abramson et al. 2018, Proc. R. Soc. B). Bottlenose dolphins have also been reported to mimic. Drop 'the only'. [s11]
- **OK (soften)**: The melon is a flexible acoustic organ that focuses sonar and changes shape during vocalization.
  NOAA: a 'round, flexible melon that focuses and modulates their vocalizations'. Shape change is implied by 'flexible'; keep the NOAA wording. [s8]
- **WRONG (age)**: Born grey-brown; whiten progressively, reaching white in their fifth to seventh year.
  NOAA: dark grey calves, white 'as they reach sexual maturity'. ADF&G: born dark blue-gray, usually white by age 8 or 10, only older animals truly white. Say 'dark grey at birth, white by about 8 to 10'. [s8] [s9]
- **WRONG**: Belugas live in matrilineal groups, pods organized around female lineage.
  O'Corry-Crowe et al. 2020 (Scientific Reports) found groups beyond mother-calf pairs were usually not built around close maternal kin; the results contradict the matrilineal prediction. Say 'live in shifting groups, from mother and calf to herds of hundreds' (NOAA, ADF&G). [s12] [s8] [s9]
- **OK**: Belugas follow sea ice through seasonal migrations, summering in bays and estuaries and wintering at the ice edge.
  Some travel over 1,500 miles from the Bering Sea to the Mackenzie estuary (ADF&G); summer in estuaries, swim under ice to breathing holes (SeaWorld). [s9] [s14]
- **OK**: Unfused cervical vertebrae let the beluga nod and turn its head, rare in whales.
  NOAA and SeaWorld both state it. [s8] [s14]
- **OK (style flag)**: Header hanzi 白鯨 (rail).
  白鯨 báijīng (simplified 白鲸) is the standard Chinese name for the beluga (FAO AGROVOC). Pinyin appears only in the button aria-label, not on screen; show báijīng visibly. The rail uses traditional script while the zodiac strip uses simplified (狗), so pick one. [s15]
- **OK**: Proverb 入鄉隨俗, tied to the Dog year as 'our own reading'.
  Real idiom, rù xiāng suí sú, 'when you enter a village, follow the local customs' (ChinesePod). The page labels the pairing honestly. [s16]
- **OK**: Purple Star sections.
  No sun-sign star pairing on this page; it says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. No 'Primal Zodiac' string found. (no source)
