## The Flying Fish as a symbol
- **Classical Chinese (Shanhaijing, Classic of Mountains and Seas).** At Mount Taiqi the Guan River is full of 文鰩魚 wényáo yú: shaped like a carp, fish body with bird wings, blue-patterned with a white head and red beak, it travels from the Western Sea to the Eastern Sea and flies by night; eating it cures madness, and 見則天下大穰 jiàn zé tiānxià dà ráng, when it is seen the whole land has a great harvest. 文鳐鱼 wényáo yú survives as a modern alternate name for the flying fish, so the link is real, but the classical creature is a marvel, not a zoological description. [s5] [s6]
- **Tao (Yami) people, Orchid Island (Pongso no Tao), Taiwan.** The king of the flying fish is the most important Tao cultural hero: he appeared to an old man in a dream and taught the proper Tao way of living, which was passed down the generations. Flying fish are sacred, with yearly rituals and many taboos; 'old men's fish' are forbidden to women and young men. Festival rules recorded on Wikipedia: fish are cooked whole without cutting off tail or pectoral fins, and traded only within the tribe. Attribute to the Tao by name and keep it separate from any 'you' reading. [s7] [s8] [s16]
- **Barbados.** Barbados is known as 'the land of the flying fish' and the fish is one of the country's national symbols. [s9]
- **Greek and Roman.** The family name Exocoetidae comes from Greek; flying fish were believed to leave the water to sleep ashore (Wikipedia, citing Pliny, Natural History IX.34). Use as attributed old belief only. [s9]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Flying fish 'spirit animal' meanings (freedom, rising above emotion) on totem blogs; no traditional source located. The Dragon Gate story 魚躍龍門 yú yuè lóng mén is about the carp, not the flying fish. [s15]

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Fire (Sagittarius, Jupiter) meets yin Wood (Rabbit). Wood feeds Fire, so the Rabbit's careful timing becomes the fuel for the archer's reach. The flying fish fits this exactly: a long hidden build under the surface, a burst into the open, a return to the water it knows.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: timing the exit, covering distance others think impossible, renewing momentum mid-flight, a lightness that makes hard crossings look easy.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: escape dressed as adventure. The Rabbit's habit of leaving hard realities and Jupiter's blind optimism can make every threat a reason to leap rather than to face it. Momentum gets mistaken for direction.
- **Love.** Our reading: needs a partner who is fine with brief absences and big returns. Gentle and loyal at home (Rabbit), restless for horizon (Sagittarius). Wants someone who does not read the glide as leaving.
- **Money.** Our reading: generous and optimistic, good at a sudden, well-timed opportunity, weaker at the dull stretch underwater. Needs one boring account it never touches.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: first instinct is to leave the medium where the fight is. Honest when it finally speaks, sometimes too blunt, then quick to forget.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Tail beats up to 70 times per second while partly submerged, building to about 60 km/h, 'documented by Davenport (1994) in the Journal of Experimental Biology'.
  Numbers OK: up to 70 tail beats per second and nearly 60 km/h (World Press Photo caption; National Geographic gives about 37 mph). Citation wrong: Davenport 1994 is a review in Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries 4:184-214, not the Journal of Experimental Biology, and its abstract gives no beat rate. Drop the citation or cite correctly. [s13] [s12] [s10] [s9]
- **WRONG**: Fins held rigid, not flapped; aloft up to 45 seconds and 50 to 400 m 'in a single flight, according to Davenport (1994)'.
  Rigid, non-flapping wings OK (Davenport abstract). The 45-second record was filmed by NHK in May 2008 (Guinness), so it cannot come from a 1994 paper. Typical flights are about 50 m; 400 m is reached with updrafts or consecutive glides, not one single glide (Wikipedia; National Geographic: glide up to 655 ft, consecutive glides up to 1,312 ft). [s10] [s11] [s9] [s12]
- **OK**: Chain gliding: dip the tail and beat again to extend flight without fully re-submerging.
  Wikipedia and National Geographic both describe dropping the tail into the water to push off for another glide. 'Multiple lengths of a ship' is color; unsourced but harmless. [s9] [s12]
- **WRONG**: Flight is primarily predator evasion from 'dolphins, tuna and other fast pelagic fish'; 'documented by Shenker (1984) in Environmental Biology of Fishes'.
  Claim OK: Davenport 1994 concludes flying fish most likely fly to escape predators, especially dolphinfish and ommastrephid squid. 'Dolphins' is likely a misreading of dolphinfish (mahi-mahi). No Shenker 1984 paper on flying fish could be found; the Environmental Biology of Fishes flyingfish paper found is Stevens, Bennett and Berg 2003, on spawning. Treat the Shenker citation as unverifiable and remove it. [s10] [s12]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Pectoral fins are often transparent or iridescent, hard to see in flight, noted across Exocoetidae.
  No source found. Exocoetus volitans has greyish pectoral fins (Wikipedia); many species have dark or patterned wings. Cut. [s14]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Schooling animals; many take flight together when threatened, creating a scatter effect that confuses predators.
  Group behavior OK: Exocoetus volitans 'often exits the water in shoals', and National Geographic gives the group name as school. The 'scatter effect that confuses predators' is not supported by any source found; soften to 'often leave the water together'. [s14] [s12]
- **OK**: Header hanzi 飛魚 (rail__han and serial-han).
  飛魚 fēiyú is the standard Chinese name for flying fish. The hero shows pinyin fēiyú. Fine. [s6]
- **OK**: Proverb of the year: 魚躍龍門 yú yuè lóng mén, 'a fish determined enough to leap' the Yellow River gate earns transformation.
  Labeled as our own pairing, which is honest. But the legend is about the carp (鯉魚 lǐyú), not any fish and not the flying fish; say carp if kept. [s15]
- **OK**: Page copy style
  Not a fact issue: the live page uses many em dashes in the fact and reading blocks, which the writing rules ban. No Purple Star star pairing on this page (good). Moon dates show January 2026, stale.
