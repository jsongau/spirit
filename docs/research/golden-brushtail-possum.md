## The Golden Brushtail Possum as a symbol
- **First Peoples of south-eastern Australia (Gunditjmara and Yorta Yorta cloaks held at Melbourne Museum).** Possum-skin cloaks were made for a person from infancy, with pelts added as they grew; designs cut into the skin side with a bone awl or shell and coloured with ochre recorded the wearer's Country, family and totem, and people were traditionally buried in their cloaks. Curator Kimberley Moulton calls the markings "iconography of Country, of totem and of place." The 1872 Lake Condah cloak was made by six Gunditjmara men, one of them Vicki Couzens's great-grandfather; cloak making has since been revived. The brushtail is the animal that carried a person's identity and home on their back. Tell this as theirs, attributed, never as a "you" reading. [s5]
- **Aboriginal Australia, food and names.** The brushtail is a traditional food for some Indigenous groups. Western Australian names include nunguin (Kimberley), walambari (Pilbara), wayurta (desert) and bilda (Nullarbor). Use only as attributed names; no meanings are given in the source. [s6]
- **Aotearoa New Zealand (introduced animal).** Brought to New Zealand in the 1850s to start a fur trade, the brushtail became a major agricultural and conservation pest, peaking at an estimated 60 to 70 million in the 1980s and now about 30 million. A real shadow story for this crossing: an animal so good at making any place home that it took over one. Te Ara could not be reached for a Māori view, so do not claim one. [s6]
- **The golden form itself.** Golden is one of four colour forms of the common brushtail (silver-grey, brown, black, gold). Australian Geographic says golden brushtails are rare in the wild, found mostly in small pockets around Tasmania, coloured by a mutation that lowers melanin, and easy for predators to spot, which helps explain the rarity. Gold that costs safety is the honest Leo bridge. [s6] [s7]
- **Chinese naming.** Chinese places possums in 袋貂科 dàidiāo kē, literally the "pouched marten" family; the common brushtail is 帚尾袋貂 zhǒuwěi dàidiāo, broom-tailed pouched marten. There is no traditional Chinese lore for this Australian animal; the Goat half carries the Chinese material (the page's 亡羊補牢 wáng yáng bǔ láo, mend the pen after losing the sheep). [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** "Playing possum" and opossum trickster lore belong to the American Virginia opossum, a different animal (order Didelphimorphia), not the Australian brushtail. Generic "possum spirit animal" meanings from totem blogs. No source for any golden-possum folklore. (no source)

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 貂 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin diāo.
  貂 diāo means marten or sable (genus Martes), a different animal. The possum family is 袋貂 dàidiāo, pouched marten; the common brushtail possum is 帚尾袋貂 zhǒuwěi dàidiāo (same in traditional and simplified). If one glyph is required, 袋貂 dàidiāo is the shortest correct form. [s10] [s11]
- **OK (narrow)**: Both sexes carry a chest scent gland and rub it on surfaces; in breeding males the secretion often stains the fur a visible reddish-brown.
  Wikipedia: "The chest of both sexes has a scent gland that emits a reddish secretion which stains the fur around it." ADW: used to mark territories. The staining is not limited to breeding males in either source; drop "in breeding males". [s6] [s8]
- **OK**: Nocturnal and largely solitary; rests by day in a hollow log, tree cavity or, in built-up areas, a roof space.
  ADW: nocturnal, typically solitary, rests in hollow logs or trees, in urban areas "in peoples' attics". [s8]
- **OK**: Home ranges overlap with several neighbors; clear dominance hierarchies form; direct aggression is rare; co-dominants of the same sex avoid each other.
  ADW, nearly word for word. "Several neighbors" is a paraphrase of "many individuals' home ranges overlap". [s8]
- **OK**: Deep, guttural vocalizations signal territory location and, in the breeding season, attract mates (trait titled "The hiss that ends the argument").
  ADW, close to verbatim. Wikipedia lists hisses among its calls (clicks, grunts, hisses, alarm chatters, guttural coughs, screeching), so the title is fair. [s8] [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (soften)**: Widest distribution of any Australian mammal; wet eucalypt forest to mangrove to suburban ceilings; eats garden fruit and eucalyptus leaves too toxic for most other mammals.
  ADW does say "the widest distribution of any Australian mammal", but Wikipedia says only "one of the most widespread marsupials of Australia", and introduced mammals and the dingo make the superlative shaky. Say "one of Australia's most widespread marsupials". Mangrove not found in either source. ADW mentions "highly toxic flowers and leaves" in the diet; "too toxic for most other mammals" is not in any source opened. Eucalyptus leaves are a major food (Wikipedia). [s8] [s6]
- **WRONG (second half)**: Grey and black base coats trace to variation at a single gene; rarer individuals in isolated populations carry an additional pale gold variant, a colour morph within the same species.
  First half OK: Bond et al. 2024 show an ASIP missense variant (p.Arg115Cys) makes homozygous possums black. Second half misreads the cited paper: it does not identify a gold variant at that gene and says red fur is "largely independent of the variation at ASIP". Australian Geographic attributes the golden form to a melanin-lowering mutation, found mostly in small pockets around Tasmania; no gene named. "Same species, not a separate one" is correct. Fix: "Golden possums are a rare colour form of the same species, found mostly in small pockets of Tasmania." [s9] [s7]
- **OK (minor)**: Intro: gray or black through most of its range and, in a few rare populations, gold; dens in a hollow tree or a stranger's roof.
  Four colour forms exist (silver-grey, brown, black, gold), so "gray or black" leaves out brown. Black predominates in Tasmania (Bond et al.). [s6] [s7] [s9]
- **OK**: Element shown as Earth.
  Goat fixed element is Earth. [s3]
