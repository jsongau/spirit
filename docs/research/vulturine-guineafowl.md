## The Vulturine Guineafowl as a symbol
- **Swahili (East African coast).** The kanga, the printed cloth worn across East Africa, takes its name from kanga, Swahili for guinea fowl. The Illinois Swahili Proverbs project says early black-and-white spotted designs were called KANGA 'after the noisy, sociable guinea-fowl'. Wikipedia notes a competing verb etymology tagged citation needed. The bird the spots point to is the helmeted guineafowl, not this species; frame it as guinea fowl in general. [v12] [v13]
- **Swahili folktale.** How the Guinea Fowl Got Her Spots, a Swahili tale retold by Barbara Knutson: Guinea Fowl twice saves her friend Cow from Lion, and Cow repays her with spotted camouflage 'just the thing for hiding in the shadows and grass'. A small bird and a big cattle friend: an unusually neat echo of the Ox year. Credit as Knutson's retelling of a Swahili story. [v14] [v15]
- **Ancient Greek.** The Meleagrids, sisters of the hero Meleager, wept so long at his death that Artemis turned them into guinea fowl and moved them to Leros, where the birds were kept at her shrine and not eaten (Antoninus Liberalis). The genus of the helmeted guineafowl, Numida meleagris, carries the name. Grief kept in company, a flock of sisters. [v11]
- **Chinese naming.** Guinea fowl are 珠雞 zhūjī, 'pearl fowl', for their spots; this species is 鷲珠雞 jiù zhūjī (simplified 鹫珠鸡), 'vulture pearl fowl', and Chinese Wikipedia calls it the largest of the guineafowl family 珠雞科 zhūjī kē. [v10]
- **Science as story (Kenya).** Papageorgiou et al. 2019 tracked over 400 birds in 18 stable groups of 13 to 65; groups meet into crowds of hundreds and then split 'perfectly into completely stable groups every single day'. Until then multilevel societies were known in large-brained mammals (humans, primates, elephants, giraffes, dolphins). Senior author Farine called the system 'more comparable to a primate than to other birds'. [v6] [v7]
- **Vulturine-specific folklore.** No traditional story specific to the vulturine guineafowl found in the searches run for this dossier (general guinea fowl and Swahili searches only; Samburu or Borana sources not checked). Unsourced, do not invent one. (no source)

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 珠 (rail__han and serial-han)
  珠 zhū means pearl, not guineafowl. Guineafowl is 珠雞 zhūjī ('pearl fowl'); this species is 鷲珠雞 jiù zhūjī (traditional; simplified 鹫珠鸡). If one glyph is needed, 珠 alone reads as 'pearl'; use 珠雞 zhūjī at minimum, with pinyin. [v10]
- **WRONG**: Research published in 2021 found stable groups that persist when merged; the first non-primate species documented with this multilevel structure
  The paper is Papageorgiou et al. 2019, Current Biology, not 2021. It is described as the first multilevel society in a bird ('first time ... described for birds'); multilevel societies were already known in non-primate mammals (elephants, giraffes, dolphins). The page's citation link (Science Advances, doi 10.1126/sciadv.abf8803) would not open; it is not the 2019 paper, so the citation should be replaced. The stability-when-merged finding itself is correct. [v6] [v7]
- **OK (colors) / UNVERIFIABLE (function)**: Bare blue-grey head, red eye, black-and-white hackles, cobalt-blue breast make individuals distinguishable, functioning as identification rather than display
  Wikipedia: bare blue face, long glossy blue and white hackles, cobalt-blue breast. Hackles are blue and white, not black and white. The red eye is not in any source I opened. No source says the plumage serves individual identification; that is invented. The proverb panel repeats it ('the cobalt calls to the cobalt'). Cut the function claim. [v5]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (likely inflated)**: Strong walkers that cover 20 to 40 kilometres per day
  Papageorgiou and Farine 2020 (eLife) measured daily travel distance by GPS, but the paper and PDF did not load; the eLife insight and ScienceDaily give no number. Wikipedia says it runs rather than flies. Do not print any km figure until someone reads the paper. [v8] [v9] [v5]
- **WRONG**: Three-tier hierarchy: supergroups of 20 to 60 birds containing stable subgroups of 10 to 15
  The stable social groups are 13 to 65 birds (18 groups, over 400 adults). When groups merge, 'the number of birds moving together can reach into the hundreds' and they split back into the original groups. The page puts the 13-to-65 range on the supergroup and invents a 10-to-15 subgroup. Wikipedia separately gives non-breeding flocks of about 25. [v6] [v7] [v5]
- **OK (loud) / UNVERIFIABLE (functions)**: Loud, multi-call vocal repertoire used to coordinate movement, signal alarm and keep contact across the supergroup
  Wikipedia: 'loud chink-chink-chink-chink-chink calls'. No source I opened describes a repertoire or its uses. Keep 'loud chinking calls'. [v5]
- **OK**: Broad omnivore: seeds, tubers, insects, small reptiles; persists in arid conditions
  Wikipedia: seeds, roots, tubers, grubs, rodents, small reptiles and insects; dry open habitat. [v5]
- **OK**: Lunar New Year note: born January or early February may belong to the Rat
  Aquarius runs about 20 January to 18 February, so a birthday before lunar New Year falls in the previous year, which before an Ox year is the Rat. Correctly applied. [v1]
- **OK (with note)**: Proverb 物以類聚 (wù yǐ lèi jù)
  Traces to the Zhouyi Xici I line 方以類聚，物以群分 (fāng yǐ lèi jù, wù yǐ qún fēn); the popular form is 物以類聚，人以群分. Page labels the pairing as its own, which is honest. [v16]
- **OK (none)**: Purple Star star claim
  No 'What Purple Star already suspects' block; Purple Star appears only in nav.
