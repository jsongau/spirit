## The Penduline Tit as a symbol
- **The names (Latin, Polish, Chinese).** 'Penduline' means hanging, from the bag nests hung from trees, usually over water (Remizidae). The genus name Remiz is simply the Polish word for this bird (Jarocki, 1819). The Chinese family name 攀雀 pānquè is 攀 pān 'to climb by pulling oneself up' plus 雀 què 'small bird': the climbing bird. Three languages name it for what it does: hang, and climb. [s8] [s9] [s18] [s19]
- **Central Europe, household use.** The felted nests were formerly used in Central Europe as children's slippers (Wikipedia, citing Harrap and Quinn 1996). A real, homely image: the bird's work warm enough to wear. Keep it to one sentence; I found no further folklore source. [s6]
- **Science as story: the open marriage of the reed beds.** Eurasian penduline tits are one of the best-studied cases of sexual conflict over parental care. Only one parent raises a clutch; about one in three clutches is abandoned by both parents (28 to 40 percent across Sweden, Germany, Austria and Hungary); each bird may have up to seven partners a season; some females hide eggs so they can leave first (Székely's group, Bath). This is the honest, strange core of the animal and a strong shadow theme. [s10] [s11] [s12]
- **The male's nest as his reputation.** The male chooses the site and starts the nest, calling for a female; if none joins, he abandons it and starts again (Oiseaux.net). Males that spend more time building and build larger nests have higher success, and females are more likely to stay and care for their young (Szentirmai et al. 2005). An experiment found females chose males by mask width, not nest size (Pogány and Székely 2007). Use: the work matters, but it is not the only thing she reads. [s15] [s13] [s14]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Any 'penduline tit spirit animal' meaning; any Chinese folklore for 攀雀 pānquè (none found); the false-entrance 'trickster nest' lore, which belongs to the African Cape penduline tit (Anthoscopus), not this bird. [s7]

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Earth (Virgo, Mercury) meets yin Wood (Rabbit). Virgo is the craftsman; the Rabbit is gentle, alert and slow to show its thoughts. The Penduline Tit is the quiet builder who works with its hands, hangs at odd angles to reach things, and keeps its own counsel about who gets to stay. Wood controls Earth, so the Rabbit's caution keeps checking Virgo's need to finish.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: patient, skilled making; a sense for which materials (and people) will hold; adaptability, since both halves bend rather than break; gentleness that lets others relax.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the conflict the biology names. Two careful creatures each waiting to see who will commit first, and sometimes both leaving. Virgo's perfectionism plus the Rabbit's avoidance can mean a fine nest that nobody moves into, or a quiet exit before the other person knows there was a choice.
- **Love.** Our reading: shows love through made things and practical care. Shy about saying it (Rabbit reluctance to share thoughts; Virgo slow to open). Needs a partner who will state commitment out loud, early, so neither has to guess. Rabbit and Dog's six harmony suggests a steady, loyal partner suits.
- **Money.** Our reading: frugal, handy, prefers to repair and reuse; good at building something slowly that holds value. Risk: over-investing time in one project and walking away from it before it pays.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids open fights (Rabbit) and keeps a quiet list of flaws (Virgo). The risk is leaving instead of saying. Practice: name the small thing while you are still in the nest.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 攀雀 (rail__han and serial-han), glossed pānquè
  攀雀 pānquè is the Chinese genus and family name (攀雀屬 Remiz, 攀雀科 Remizidae); the East Asian species is 中華攀雀 Zhōnghuá pānquè, the Chinese Penduline Tit. 攀 pān 'climb', 雀 què 'small bird'. Same form in traditional and simplified. Correct for this animal. [s18] [s17] [s19]
- **WRONG**: The nest has a false entrance tube at the base and a functional entrance at the top; predators investigate the false entry (Trait 01 and the whole intro, hero line and archetype)
  Wrong species. The false entrance and false chamber belong to the African Cape penduline tit (Anthoscopus minutus) and its genus, not Remiz pendulinus. The Eurasian nest has one entrance tube near the top. This is the page's central conceit; the hero line, intro, dial text and Mirror copy all depend on it and must be rebuilt. [s7] [s8] [s6] [s16] [s15]
- **WRONG (overstated)**: The male builds the outer shell before the female arrives; the female assesses nest quality before lining it, and construction quality is the selection criterion
  The male starts the nest and calls for a female (Oiseaux.net); both sexes then help build (Wikipedia, BirdSpot). Nest size and building time predict male success and female care (Szentirmai et al. 2005), but in a mate-choice experiment females preferred wider masks and showed no preference for large nests (Pogány and Székely 2007). Say: 'the male starts the nest and advertises from it; bigger builders tend to do better'. [s15] [s6] [s16] [s13] [s14]
- **WRONG (in part)**: Nest woven from willow and poplar catkins, plant fibers and spider silk over two to three weeks; male selects materials for structural and thermal properties, not appearance
  Sources give plant down, seed fluff, plant fibres, grass, hair and wool; about 20 days (Wikipedia) or 12 to 14 days (Oiseaux.net). Spider silk is the Cape penduline tit's material (Stegodyphus webs), not documented here. The 'selects for thermal properties, not appearance' clause is UNVERIFIABLE. Say 'felted from plant down, fibres and animal hair over about two to three weeks'. [s6] [s15] [s7]
- **WRONG (framing)**: Either parent may abandon the clutch if the other deserts first; each parent waits to confirm the other's commitment before fully committing (game-theory problem)
  The research shows the reverse: each parent tends to try to desert first. Care is by one parent only; about 28 to 40 percent of clutches are deserted by both; females may hide eggs so they can leave first; birds may have up to seven mates a season. Rewrite as sexual conflict, not mutual confirmation. [s10] [s11] [s12]
- **OK (soften)**: About ten grams; forages hanging inverted from reeds and thin branches, reaching food unavailable to larger or less agile birds
  About 10 g (BirdSpot), 9.3 g (Oiseaux.net); strong legs let it grip stems and hang upside down (Oiseaux.net); one foot clings to the reed while the other pulls seed from the head (Wikipedia). 'Unavailable to larger birds' is UNVERIFIABLE; cut. [s16] [s15] [s6]
- **OK (soften)**: Vocalisation is a thin, descending whistle used as a close-range contact call
  Oiseaux.net: a drawn-out, high, sharp 'tsiiiiih', very characteristic. 'Descending' and 'used within its group' not confirmed in what I opened. Say 'a long, thin, high call'. [s15]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Citation: Barta Z, Székely T, 'The optimal escape path of a parent deserting its clutch', Behavioral Ecology 1997
  Could not find this paper; title looks invented. Replace with Pogány et al. 2008 (BMC Evol Biol 8:242) or Szentirmai et al. 2007 (J Evol Biol). [s10] [s11]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Citation: Schulze-Hagen K et al. 1996, 'Nest architecture and nest materials in the European Penduline Tit', Journal für Ornithologie
  Search did not surface this record. A person should confirm it or remove it.
