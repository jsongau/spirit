## The Albatross as a symbol
- **English Romantic poetry (Coleridge, 1798).** In The Rime of the Ancient Mariner the albatross comes 'Thorough the fog' and the crew hail it 'As if it had been a Christian soul'. After the mariner shoots it, 'Instead of the cross, the Albatross / About my neck was hung.' The poem gave English the albatross-round-the-neck idiom for a burden of guilt, and ends on 'He prayeth best, who loveth best / All things both great and small.' Usable angle: the bird as a gift that turns into a weight only when it is betrayed. [gutenberg] [wiki-alb]
- **Māori (Aotearoa New Zealand).** Te Ara: the toroa's prized white feathers were worn on important occasions by leading men; plumes are called raukura or kaiwharawhara; soft belly feathers became pōhoi toroa, feather-ball earrings. A headdress of albatross and other feathers was called te rau o Tītapu, after an island in Cook Strait said to be visited by albatrosses and since sunk. Heaphy painted Te Rangihaeata in 1840 wearing pōhoi toroa beside huia feathers as marks of mana tapu. Māori also carved flutes from albatross wing bones (Wikipedia). [teara] [teara-pohoi] [wiki-alb]
- **Taranaki Māori, Parihaka (Te Whiti o Rongomai and Tohu Kākahi).** The raukura, a white feather or plume, became the emblem of Parihaka's passive resistance and of peaceful co-existence. One account told by Taranaki Whānui says an albatross landed in a Parihaka courtyard and dropped a single feather, which became the Raukura. Tell it as their account and attribute it; do not lay a 'this is you' reading over it. [pnbst]
- **Hawaiian.** Laysan albatrosses (mōlī) are considered ʻaumakua, sacred ancestor forms, and may be the sacred bird of Kāne (Wikipedia). Kīlauea Point refuge on Kauaʻi holds a 'Welcome Back' Mōlī Blessing each November with hula and chant as the birds return to nest (Pacific Birds, 2019). Note: this is the Laysan albatross of the North Pacific, not the Wandering Albatross; name the species when using it. [wiki-alb] [pacificbirds]
- **Japanese.** The short-tailed albatross is called ahōdori, 'fool bird', because it walked clumsily on land, did not fear people and was easily caught by feather hunters. 信天翁 (Japanese shintenō, Mandarin xìntiānwēng) appears as an alternate name in the Yamato Honzō of 1709. Species is the short-tailed albatross; name it. [kotobank] [wiki-alb]
- **French poetry (Baudelaire, L'Albatros).** The poet is likened to the albatross: a prince of the clouds who is mocked and exiled on the deck, where the giant wings that rule the sky keep him from walking. [wiki-alb]
- **Sailors' lore (correction).** The popular idea that sailors thought harming an albatross brought disaster is largely untrue: Cook reported in 1772 that sailors regularly killed and ate them. Some released captured birds, possibly believing they were the souls of lost sailors. Use the soul belief only with 'some sailors' and 'possibly'. [wiki-alb]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Any story that 信天翁 was named because the bird 'trusts heaven' to bring it food. The characters literally read trust (信 xìn), sky or heaven (天 tiān), old man (翁 wēng), but no dictionary or classical source I could open explains the naming. Also: generic 'albatross spirit animal' meanings on totem blogs.

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: doubled Fire, but slow Fire. Sagittarius (mutable, Jupiter) wants the far side of every horizon; the Horse (yang Fire) supplies stamina and a hunger for open ground. In the albatross the restless archer and the running horse settle into one long glide: huge range, low visible effort, and a fixed point of return. Mutable plus Fire plus Fire leaves no Earth or Water in the mix, which is why the landing is the hard part.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: range without panic, reading weak signals over a wide field, loyalty that survives distance, timing (waiting for the right wind instead of flapping), a long memory for places and people.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: motion mistaken for freedom; the Sagittarius habit of dropping plans meets the Horse habit of giving up halfway, so the danger is circling forever. Silence during the long crossing reads to others as absence. The Coleridge image fits the shadow: the bond is light to carry until it is broken, then it hangs heavy.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow courtship, then a bond meant for life, kept across long gaps. Needs a partner who measures commitment by the return, not by daily contact. Sagittarius restlessness inside monogamy (mindbodygreen) is answered by the albatross model: freedom in the season apart, fidelity at the colony.
- **Money.** Our reading: the Horse spends on impulse (Travel China Guide) and Jupiter likes big bets. The albatross version is a feast-and-glide economy: long stretches of low spending, then large outlays for the trip that matters. Needs a fixed 'nest' account that the restless side cannot touch.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: leaves for altitude when a fight needs presence. Blunt when cornered (both halves are known for bluntness). Does best naming a return date before going quiet.

## Careers
- **Long-haul aviation, ocean navigation, maritime work.** Our reading: wind-reading and range are the job; the return to port is built in.
- **Field science on remote stations, polar or island research.** Our reading: Sagittarius's search for meaning plus the Horse's stamina in months-long postings.
- **Foreign correspondent, travel writing, documentary.** Our reading: the Horse's fit for journalism and tourism (Travel China Guide) and Jupiter's wide frame.
- **Long-horizon strategy, global logistics, supply routes.** Our reading: sees the whole route before committing; good at plans measured in seasons.
- **Teaching, lecturing, expedition leading.** Our reading: Sagittarius's teacher streak (mindbodygreen) with a guide's ability to bring people home.

## Best matches
- **Wolverine** (`wolverine`): Aries Tiger. Tiger is in the Horse's trine and Aries is a Fire sign like Sagittarius. Two stamina animals who respect range.
- **Sunbittern** (`sunbittern`): Leo Dog. Dog is in the Horse's trine; Leo completes the Fire trigon with Sagittarius. Warmth meets warmth, and the Dog keeps the nest lit.
- **Rock Wren** (`rock-wren`): Gemini Goat. Goat is the Horse's secret friend; Gemini is Sagittarius's opposite sign and Air feeds Fire. The classic pull of opposites, steadied by the six-harmony bond.
- **Wallcreeper** (`wallcreeper`): Aries Goat. Secret friend (Horse and Goat) plus a shared Fire element in the Western signs.
- **Lanner Falcon** (`lanner-falcon`): Sagittarius Tiger. Same sign, same trine: another Sagittarius air-rider who understands the need to roam.

## Hard matches
- **Coconut Crab** (`coconut-crab`): Cancer Rat. Rat and Horse are a six-clash pair, and Cancer's Water, home-bound nature checks Sagittarian Fire. The crab holds the shore; the albatross leaves it.
- **Sandgrouse** (`sandgrouse`): Sagittarius Rat. Same sign, but the Rat-Horse clash: two travellers who want different things from the road.

## Candidate lines
1. A whole year on a different ocean, and you still land on the same patch of tussock grass and pick up the dance with your partner as if you had left that morning.
2. Calm days are your hard days. You sit on the swell and wait for a wind worth riding, and only someone who has never flown on a gradient would call that laziness.
3. Somewhere in you is a map of one island you have not seen in years, and it is sharper than the map of where you are right now.
4. The Horse in you wants open water and the archer wants the far side of it, so you can circle a whole ocean three times in a year and call it an ordinary season.
5. Your courtship takes years of practice dances before it means anything. Once you finally choose, the choice is built to last the rest of your life.
6. Your loyalty weighs nothing while it is kept. The Mariner learned what it weighs around the neck of the one who breaks it.

Fix: 'some circle the Southern Ocean three times in a year'. | [wiki-wand] [nature1990] |
| Return to the same colony, often the same nest site, after years at sea | **OK** | Albatrosses are highly philopatric; in a Laysan study the average distance from hatching site to own territory was 22 m. The 22 m figure is Laysan, not Wandering; do not transfer the number. | [wiki-alb] |
| Monogamous for life; reunion display of synchronized calls and movements; contact at sea essentially zero | **OK** | Pair bonds last for the life of the pair, confirmed with ritualised dances; divorce is rare and usually follows repeated breeding failure. | Navigate partly by smell: detect dimethyl sulfide from phytoplankton across hundreds of kilometres | **WRONG** | In Nevitt, Veit and Kareiva 1995 (Nature), albatrosses ignored DMS-scented slicks and black-browed albatrosses did not track DMS plumes; prions, storm petrels and white-chinned petrels were the DMS responders. No source gives 'hundreds of kilometres'. What is true: wandering albatrosses use smell to find prey; olfactory search preceded 46.8% of flown approaches to prey (Nevitt et al. 2008). | [ucd1995] [nevitt2008] |
