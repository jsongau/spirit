## The Arabian Oryx as a symbol
- **Arabic (classical poetry).** Arabic mahā (مها) names the wild oryx (some sources say a wild deer), and 'eyes of the mahā' was a stock image in verse for a beloved's beauty; medieval Arab poets compared a sweetheart's eyes to the oryx's large dark eyes. The girl's name Maha comes from it. Venus-ruled Libra and the beauty of the gaze meet here. [s12] [s13]
- **Greek and Roman natural history; the King James Bible.** Aristotle and Pliny the Elder regarded the oryx as the prototype of the unicorn; seen in profile, the two straight horns can look like one. The King James Bible translates Hebrew re'em as 'unicorn'. Note: this is Greco-Roman and biblical, not a 'desert cultures' legend as the live page says. [s6]
- **Modern conservation record (IUCN).** The last wild Arabian oryx is believed to have been shot in 1972. After captive breeding from the Phoenix Zoo 'World Herd' (started 1963) and a first release in Oman on 31 January 1982, IUCN moved it to Vulnerable in June 2011, the first species once Extinct in the Wild to improve by three categories. A dated, quotable fact that is the backbone of the page's reading. [s6] [s7] [s8] [s9]
- **Modern conservation record (UNESCO), the shadow story.** Oman's Arabian Oryx Sanctuary was the first site ever deleted from the World Heritage List (2007), after Oman cut the protected area by 90 per cent; the herd there had fallen from about 450 in 1996 to about 65, with roughly four breeding pairs, from poaching and habitat loss. A return is not a guarantee: protection can be withdrawn. [s10]
- **Gulf national identity.** National animal of Jordan, Oman, the UAE, Bahrain and Qatar; 'Orry' the oryx was mascot of the 2006 Asian Games in Doha; it is on Qatar Airways tailfins. Low citable value; one line at most. [s6]
- **Ancient Egypt (different species, do not borrow).** The 16th nome of Upper Egypt, the Oryx nome, was named for the scimitar oryx, not the Arabian oryx. Do not use as Arabian oryx lore. [s15]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'oryx spirit animal means endurance, purity, resilience' blog lines; and the famous Abbasid 'eyes of the mahā between Rusafa and the bridge' verse by Ali ibn al-Jahm, which I could not open in a reliable source in this session. 

## Header hanzi verdict
Live page shows 羚 líng in rail__han and serial-han, with pinyin líng. Verdict: NOT the animal's name. 羚 líng is the generic character for antelope or gazelle (as in 羚羊 língyáng). Chinese Wikipedia titles the species 阿拉伯大羚羊 ālābó dà língyáng, with alternatives 阿拉伯羚 ālābó líng, 阿拉伯直角羚羊 ālābó zhíjiǎo língyáng and 阿拉伯劍羚 ālābó jiànlíng [s14]. Recommended single glyph pair: 劍羚 jiànlíng, 'sword antelope', the oryx name (fits the straight horns). If the slot only takes one character, keep 羚 líng but caption it 'antelope', not as the oryx's name.

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Air (Libra) on yang Wood (Tiger). The Tiger walks alone into hard ground; Libra insists on grace once there. The Oryx is dignity under scarcity: white coat, straight horns, and a gaze poets wrote about, in a place with almost no water. Air moves through Wood like wind through acacia: it tests the shape without breaking it.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: endurance that does not look strained; the ability to read distant conditions (the oryx moves toward rain it cannot see) and go there; composure in public; beauty that is also function, like the coat that throws back the sun.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Tiger's mistrust plus Libra's wish to look fine. Help reads as a verdict on your strength. The sanctuary story is the warning: what was restored can be lost again when protection is quietly withdrawn, and pride can be the thing that cuts the reserve by 90 per cent. The page's proverb 虎落平陽 hǔ luò píngyáng fits: a tiger off its mountain is easy prey for dogs.
- **Love.** Our reading: Venus-led courtship, Tiger-led independence. Falls for beauty and good manners, then needs space and a long range. Shows love by being reliable and never needy, which can leave a partner with nothing to give. Best with someone who will walk the long way to the next green patch and not ask why.
- **Money.** Our reading: conserve hard in lean seasons (the oryx lets its body run hot rather than spend water), then move decisively when rain falls somewhere. Risk: a Tiger gamble dressed up as a Libra 'investment in quality'.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: goes still and precise first, diplomatic second, and only then lowers the horns. Fair in argument, unforgiving of being underestimated.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (soften 'declared')**: Declared extinct in the wild in 1972, surviving only in captive and private herds, before reintroduction programs returned it to protected desert reserves beginning in the 1980s.
  The last wild individual is believed to have been shot in 1972 [s8] [s9]; I found no source for a formal 1972 declaration. Captive 'World Herd' at Phoenix from 1963 [s7]. First release Oman 31 January 1982; later Saudi Arabia (1989), Israel (program from 1978), UAE, Jordan [s7] [s9]. Suggest 'The last wild Arabian oryx was shot in 1972' and add the 2011 IUCN downlisting, the strongest fact this animal has [s8]. [s7] [s8] [s9]
- **OK**: Its nearly white coat reflects solar radiation, helping it stay cooler than a darker coat would.
  Ultimate Ungulate: the bright white coat is extremely reflective and helps keep it cool [s11]. Wikipedia describes the coat but not the function [s6]. [s6] [s11]
- **WRONG (attribution)**: Viewed from the side, the two horns appear to merge into a single spike, a likely source of the unicorn legend in desert cultures.
  Wikipedia says the unicorn myth may derive from oryxes seen in profile or that lost a horn, and that Aristotle and Pliny regarded the oryx as the unicorn's prototype [s6]. That is Greco-Roman natural history, not 'desert cultures'. Rewrite: 'Aristotle and Pliny the Elder treated the oryx as the model for the unicorn'. Keep 'may', not 'likely'. [s6]
- **OK**: Can survive long stretches without drinking, getting most of its water from desert vegetation and morning dew.
  Can go several weeks without drinking; in summer it lowers metabolism and lets body temperature rise, cutting water loss by at least 50% [s6]. Moisture from plants and morning dew [s13]. The water-saving body-heat fact is better than the page's version. [s6] [s13]
- **OK**: Moves in small herds that travel long distances between grazing areas, tracking rainfall across open desert rather than holding one fixed range.
  Groups of 2 to 15; one Omani herd ranged over 3,000 km²; detects distant rainfall and moves toward fresh growth [s6]; one animal reportedly covered 90 km in 18 hours [s11]. [s6] [s11]
- **WRONG**: Stands with a notably straight back and level head carriage, a posture maintained even during long periods of rest in open, exposed terrain.
  No source for the posture claim, and the resting part contradicts the record: in the hottest part of the day it rests in shallow scrapes dug under shrubs and trees, and in summer lies inactive in shade [s6] [s11]. Cut, or replace with the scrape-digging fact. [s6] [s11]
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 羚 (líng) as the animal's Chinese name.
  羚 líng is generic antelope. Species names: 阿拉伯大羚羊 ālābó dà língyáng; also 阿拉伯劍羚 ālābó jiànlíng [s14]. Use 劍羚 jiànlíng, or caption 羚 as 'antelope'. [s14]
- **OK**: Proverb 虎落平阳 (hǔ luò píngyáng), tiger fallen to the plain, tied to the Year of the Tiger as our own pairing.
  Real idiom; usually the first half of 虎落平陽被犬欺 hǔ luò píngyáng bèi quǎn qī, 'a tiger on the plain is bullied by dogs'. Gloss on the page is right; the pairing is honestly labeled. Not separately sourced in this session.
