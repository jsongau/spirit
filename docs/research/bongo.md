## The Bongo as a symbol
- **Ogiek (Mau Forest and Kenyan highlands).** In a 2024 study gathering hunters' and forest-dwellers' knowledge of mountain bongo, an Ogiek informant said Ogiek tradition tabooed hunting bongo for meat, that the animal 'was considered lucky for colonizing traditional beehives', and that its strong skin made ropes for hanging hives. Single informant; present it as one recorded account, credit the Ogiek by name, and do not lay a 'you' reading over it. [s9]
- **Forest peoples of the bongo's range (unnamed in sources).** Wikipedia and SeaWorld record a belief that eating or touching a bongo brings spasms like epileptic seizures, which helped protect the animal; Wikipedia says the taboo is reportedly no longer widely held. Africa Geographic adds that in Gabon the bongo is held to carry sasa, a dangerous power tied to witchcraft, and that hunters who kill one risk seizures and madness. Africa Geographic gives no primary citation and names no nation; use the general taboo with care, and the sasa detail only if a stronger source turns up. [s4] [s7] [s10]
- **Kele (Gabon), the name.** Wikipedia says the English name bongo probably comes from the Kele language of Gabon. Bridge only, not a hook. [s4]
- **Kenya, the homecoming.** Mountain bongos bred in US zoos have been flown back to Kenya: a 2004 group to the Mount Kenya area (VOA reports 18; KWS, quoted by Mongabay, says 25 in the first group), and 17 more from Florida's Rare Species Conservatory Foundation in February 2025. KWS put the wild population at roughly 100, down from about 700 in the 1960s and 1970s. A modern story, not folklore, but a real one. [s11] [s12]
- **Greek scientific name.** Tragelaphus eurycerus: tragos 'he-goat' plus elaphos 'deer', eurus 'broad', keras 'horn'. The goat-deer with broad horns. Bridge only. [s6] [s5]
- **Generic 'bongo spirit animal' meanings.** Unsourced, do not use. Nothing with a traditional source found. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Earth (Taurus) holding yin Water (Pig). Earth banks Water, so the Pig's warmth and appetite are kept inside firm walls. Taurus and Pig both love comfort, food and good things; doubled, they make an animal that lives in the richest cover it can find and rarely needs to leave. The vivid coat says Venus; the hiding says yin.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: steadiness, generosity to the few let close, a deep reserve of patience, beauty carried without performance, a gift for knowing exactly where safety is.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Pig trusts too easily and the Taurus holds on too long, so this crossing can stay in cover past the point where cover helps. Withholding reads as warmth to friends and as absence to partners. The Ogiek hive story is the mirror: good fortune arrives where you settle, which can tempt you never to move.
- **Love.** Our reading: Venus and yin Water make a slow, sensual, loyal lover who shows itself in pieces. Needs a patient partner who is content with glimpses at first and earns the full view. Hates being chased into the open.
- **Money.** Our reading: a natural keeper. Taurus saves; the Pig spends generously on the people it loves. Comfortable, steady, a little private about it. Risk: lending to the wrong person because the Pig rarely suspects a trick.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: turns away first and watches in glances, the way a startled bongo faces its hindquarters toward the noise. Rarely attacks; ends things by being gone. Taurus memory means the retreat can be permanent.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Beekeeping, forestry, agroforestry, native-plant nurseries.** Our reading: the Ogiek tie between bongo and hives; Earth and Water feeding growing things.
- **Conservation, captive breeding, wildlife veterinary work.** Our reading: the zoo-to-forest homecoming of the mountain bongo; patient, long-horizon care.
- **Craft and luxury trades: textiles, leather, woodwork, interior design.** Our reading: Venus-ruled taste and the richest coat in the forest; beauty made by hand, out of view.
- **Camera-trap research, wildlife photography, archival or identification work.** Our reading: the flank stripes that let researchers tell one shy animal from another; patience for the unseen.
- **Hospitality behind the scenes: chef, sommelier, private host.** Our reading: Taurus and Pig share a love of good food and comfort; generous to guests while staying out of the spotlight.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 羚 (rail__han and serial-han)
  羚 líng is the generic word element for antelope or gazelle, not the bongo. The Chinese name of the bongo is 紫羚 zǐlíng ('purple antelope', same in traditional), also 斑哥羚羊 bāngē língyáng. Use 紫羚 with pinyin. [s13]
- **WRONG**: 10 to 15 vertical white stripes; count and layout rarely match side to side; researchers use flank pattern, facial markings and horn shape to identify wild bongos from camera-trap photos.
  Stripes 10 to 15 and side-to-side difference: OK (Wikipedia, ADW, Ultimate Ungulate). Camera-trap ID by flank pattern: OK (Gibbon et al. 2015, PeerJ, which used camera-trap and captive photos). But that study tested flank stripes and the full side profile only; horns appear only as something zookeepers say they use, and facial markings were not tested. Cut 'alongside facial markings and horn shape' or say 'mainly the flank stripes'. [s4] [s5] [s6] [s8]
- **OK**: Chestnut-to-mahogany coat broken by white stripes works as disruptive camouflage under a closed canopy.
  Wikipedia says the stripes help camouflage; coat colors per ADW (chestnut red, males darkening to nearly black-brown). The specific term 'disruptive camouflage' and the vines detail are interpretation; soften to 'the stripes are thought to help it blend in'. [s4] [s5]
- **OK**: Startled at full speed, the bongo runs through dense tangles with its spiral horns laid back along its spine.
  Wikipedia, SeaWorld (chin up, horns flat against the back) and Ultimate Ungulate (horns held against the back of the neck; old animals show bare patches where they rest). [s4] [s7] [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: As a bongo matures, horn tips lose pigment and pale to ivory, a visible mark of age.
  Ultimate Ungulate describes the horns as yellow-tipped. I found no source that ties pale tips to age. Say 'the horns end in pale, yellowish tips' and drop the age claim. [s6]
- **OK**: Among the hardest African antelope to observe; once alarmed it retreats into cover, angles its hindquarters toward the disturbance, and checks in glances.
  Wikipedia: flees, then stands hidden facing away, hindquarters less conspicuous, ready to flee. PeerJ: predominantly nocturnal and extremely shy. ADW: behavior hard to study. 'Checks in glances' is interpretation. [s4] [s8] [s5]
- **OK**: Eastern mountain bongo Critically Endangered, about 100 to under 150 left in fragmented Kenyan forests; more in captivity than in the wild.
  PeerJ: 100 to 150 in seven populations, more than 700 in captivity. Wikipedia: fewer in the wild than in captivity; estimates 100 to 140. KWS (Mongabay 2025): roughly 100. Consider adding that zoo-bred bongos have been flown back to Kenya (2004 and 2025). [s8] [s4] [s11]
- **WRONG**: Purple Star section: a Taurus born in the Pig year 'tends to seat' 太陰 Tài Yīn, the Moon star.
  No traditional basis. Zi Wei Dou Shu charts come from the lunar birth date and hour, not sun sign plus year animal. Relabel as our own image or cut. [s14]
- **OK**: Page implies the bongo moves at night ('the same hour the Bongo actually moves').
  Wikipedia: mostly nocturnal, sometimes crepuscular or diurnal. ADW says both diurnal and nocturnal, so avoid 'only at night'. [s4] [s5]
- **OK**: Proverb 人不可貌相，海水不可斗量 rén bù kě mào xiàng, hǎishuǐ bù kě dǒu liáng tied to the Pig year
  Labelled on the page as our own pairing, with pinyin. Honest as written.
