## The Steller's Sea Eagle as a symbol
- **Chinese naming.** In Chinese the bird is 虎頭海鵰 hǔtóu hǎidiāo (simplified 虎头海雕), the 'tiger-headed sea eagle'. For the Capricorn born in a Tiger year this is the strongest bridge on the page: the Chinese name itself puts a tiger's head on this bird. It breeds in Heilongjiang and is a Class I national protected animal in China. [s6] [s12]
- **Japan (natural monument).** Called 大鷲 ōwashi, 'great eagle'. Designated a national natural monument (天然記念物 tiānrán jìniànwù in Chinese reading; tennen kinenbutsu) on 23 January 1970 and a nationally rare species in 1993. Wikipedia's 'national treasure' wording is loose; the legal status is natural monument. [s6] [s9] [s10]
- **Japan (arrow feathers).** Nipponica: the tail feathers of this species were prized since ancient times for arrow fletching (矢羽 shǐ yǔ; Japanese yabane). A Saturnine image: what the bird drops becomes what flies true. [s9]
- **Ainu and Edo trade.** Eagle and hawk tail feathers were among the karumono luxury goods whose trade the Matsumae domain monopolized, presented to the Shogun, and among the main goods of the Santan trade. Tezuka does not name the species, so say 'eagle feathers', not 'Steller's feathers'. [s11]
- **Western naming.** Named for Georg Wilhelm Steller, the German naturalist. Usable as a one-clause aside only; trivia, not a hook. [s6]
- **Ainu (borrowed species, do not use for this bird).** NOVA retells an Ainu legend of an eagle that saved a starving village by bringing a dolphin ashore, the reason given for honoring the eagle as a god. The story names the WHITE-TAILED eagle, not Steller's, and gives no teller or collection. Do not use it on this page. [s14]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** An Ainu kamuy name or ritual specific to Steller's sea eagle could not be found in any source I could open (one photography blog only). Do not claim one. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Earth (Capricorn, Saturn) under yang Wood (Tiger). Saturn waits out the season; the Tiger takes the best claim the moment it opens. Wood controls Earth, so the Tiger's push keeps Capricorn's patience from turning into paralysis. The Chinese name, tiger-headed sea eagle, says it in one phrase.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: strategic patience; reading a crowded field and taking the best position without a fight; keeping backups (alternate nests); presence that ends arguments; long memory.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: taking what others caught (kleptoparasitism) and calling it efficiency; Capricorn status-hunger plus Tiger high-handedness; the easy meal that poisons, as with the lead in abandoned deer carcasses. Coldness mistaken for strength.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow, watchful, then decisive. Shows love through provision and loyalty in action (Capricorn) more than romance (China Highlights says Tigers struggle to show deep affection). Needs a partner who is active and self-sufficient and does not need the watching narrated.
- **Money.** Our reading: builds reserves like spare nests; a Capricorn long game with Tiger timing. Takes concentrated positions when the run is strong. Risk: helping itself to returns others generated, or staying with a 'free' source after it turns costly.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids small fights, wins the big one by size and position. Tends to settle disputes by taking the spot. Practice: name the claim out loud before taking it.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (soften superlative)**: One of the largest bills of any living eagle; body can reach roughly 9 kg in females, the heaviest of any eagle species.
  Bill 'probably the largest of any living eagle' (s6). Females 6.2 to 9.5 kg (s6); ADW gives average 9 kg females (s7). 'Typically the heaviest eagle in the world' but ranked below harpy and Philippine eagles in some measurements (s6): write 'usually ranked the heaviest eagle'. Note the Harpy Eagle page calls the harpy the largest eagle; the two pages contradict each other. [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: Breeds almost entirely along sea coasts and large rivers, favoring estuaries and river mouths with mature trees or rocky outcroppings; rarely found far inland.
  Nests on rocky outcroppings or tall trees on coasts and along large rivers (s6); 'sightings very far inland are rare', prefers estuaries and river mouths (s7). It also breeds on the lower Amur River (s6), so 'almost entirely coastal' is slightly strong; 'coasts and big rivers' is accurate. [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: Largely solitary outside breeding, but congregates in large numbers on a single productive salmon river when the run is strong.
  Gathers on productive spawning rivers August to September; up to 700 eagles reported on Kamchatka (s6, s7). [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: When fish are abundant, practices kleptoparasitism, stealing from each other and from other birds; adults benefit the most.
  Adults appear to benefit most; immatures steal as much but are attacked more by adults (s6). Kleptoparasitism in group feeding (s7). 'From other birds' was not confirmed in the sources I opened; keep 'from each other'. [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: A breeding pair maintains two to four nests within one territory, some over two meters across, using a different one from year to year.
  ADW: 'two to four nests in one breeding territory and use alternate nests from year to year'; nests two meters across (s7). Wikipedia gives up to 2.5 m diameter, alternates within 900 m (s6). [s6] [s7]
- **OK (add context)**: Listed as Vulnerable; studies in Hokkaido traced lead poisoning to fragments of lead ammunition left in carrion the eagles feed on.
  IUCN Vulnerable, about 5,000 and decreasing (s6). Saito 2009: 129 Steller's and White-tailed eagles diagnosed as lead-poisoning deaths by 2007, from lead fragments in sika deer carcasses left by hunters (s8). Fair to add that Hokkaido has regulated lead rifle bullets since 2000 and slugs since 2001 (s8), so the page does not imply nothing was done. [s6] [s8]
- **WRONG (incomplete)**: Header hanzi 鵰 (rail__han and serial-han).
  鵰 diāo is a generic word for a large eagle, not this species. The Chinese name is 虎頭海鵰 hǔtóu hǎidiāo, simplified 虎头海雕, 'tiger-headed sea eagle' (s6, s12). Use 虎頭海鵰 hǔtóu hǎidiāo, or at least 海鵰 hǎidiāo (sea eagle); the 虎 hǔ is a gift for a Tiger-year page. Pinyin for 鵰 was not visible in the extracted header text; check it is shown. [s6] [s12]
- **OK (meaning only)**: Proverb 如虎添翼 rú hǔ tiān yì: like a tiger given wings, someone formidable gaining one more advantage.
  Meaning confirmed (s13). The classical origin was not verified from any source I could open, so do not state one. Labelled on the page as our own pairing. A tiger given wings is a near-literal picture of a tiger-headed eagle, worth keeping. [s13]
- **OK**: Lunar New Year note: a January or early February birthday can belong to the Ox.
  Correct for Capricorn (22 Dec to 19 Jan): a Capricorn born in January before lunar New Year of a Tiger Gregorian year belongs to the Ox year. Per brief this note applies to Capricorn. [s15]
