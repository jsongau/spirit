## The Spotted Cuscus as a symbol
- **New Guinea (general ethnographic record).** Common spotted cuscus are hunted for food and for bags and hats, and in New Guinea the hunting is often done for rituals: birthdays, birthing ceremonies and male initiation. The animal sits at the thresholds of a life: being born, becoming a man. [s6]
- **Austronesian seafarers (Selayar, Indonesia).** The world's most isolated spotted cuscus population, on Selayar off Sulawesi, was probably brought there by Austronesian praus in the late Holocene. Heinsohn suggests it survives partly because the island's Muslim majority does not hunt it. A carried animal that people chose to bring home across the sea; a strong Cancer bridge (home, shell, carrying), stated as fact, read by us. [s7]
- **Melanesia, prehistoric translocation (related cuscus).** The northern common cuscus, a close relative, was introduced to New Ireland between 10,000 and 20,000 years ago and reached the Solomons after 6,000 years ago, carried by people. Use only as 'cuscuses are among the animals people carried island to island', and name the species honestly. [s8]
- **Seram Island, Maluku (Phalanger spp., not Spilocuscus).** Cuscuses are among the animals used in traditional ceremonies: killed and the meat shared during the ceremony, under the Sasi system of resource protection. Different genus; use only if framed as cuscuses in general. [s10]
- **Moluccan languages, via Dutch and French.** The word cuscus probably comes from a local Moluccan word for these marsupials, passed through Dutch koeskoes and French into New Latin. Wiktionary marks the route as probable. Trivia, not a hook. [s9]
- **Kalam (Papua New Guinea) and Tok Pisin.** Kalam names: aklang or gabi (Pawley and Bulmer, A Dictionary of Kalam, cited by Wikipedia). In Tok Pisin the white form is waitpela kuskus, and kapul covers possums, cuscuses and tree kangaroos. Naming only; no story attached. [s5] [s12]
- **Monkey bridge (Western observers).** The common spotted cuscus is sometimes mistaken for sloths, other possums or even monkeys (Wikipedia citing Grzimek). A sourced, honest link to the Monkey year: the animal people take for a monkey. [s5]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Any 'cuscus spirit animal' or totem meaning (patience, slowness, etc.). No traditional myth about the spotted cuscus was found in a source I could open. No Indigenous Australian story for the Cape York cuscus was located either. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water (Cancer, ruled by the Moon) meets yang Metal (Monkey). Metal feeds Water, so the Monkey's quick, assessing mind serves Cancer's need to protect. The Spotted Cuscus is the night watcher who builds a sleeping platform in the branches by day and moves when the light drops. It is the creature outsiders mistake for a monkey: the cleverness is real, but it is used for keeping, not for showing off.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: patience that reads people before committing; memory for what someone needed; a mind that tests the branch before trusting it; a private pattern (only the male carries the spots) that shows at close range.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the platform becomes a fortress. Cancer's withdrawal plus the Monkey's cleverness can turn into quiet maneuvering instead of plain asking. The live page's 'solitary, avoids confrontation' picture is half wrong biologically: male cuscuses bark, snarl, hiss and fight over territory. That is the real shadow: soft-looking, fiercely defended.
- **Love.** Our reading: Moon-ruled and slow to let anyone onto the platform. Once someone is there, devotion is practical (food, shelter, noticing). Needs a partner who does not take nocturnal privacy as rejection; the Monkey side needs play or it drifts.
- **Money.** Our reading: Cancer saves for the household; the Monkey sees the clever angle. A good steward who keeps reserves high up and out of reach, with a risk of hoarding against storms that never come.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: retreats first, then defends hard if the territory is crossed (the barking, upright male). Rarely starts it, and the counter-attack can surprise people who read the softness as weakness.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 徐 (xú) shown in rail__han and serial-han as the animal's character.
  徐 xú means slow, gentle; it is not the animal's name. The common spotted cuscus is 斑袋貂 bān dài diāo (same characters in traditional and simplified); alternate name 短尾斑袋貂 duǎn wěi bān dài diāo. If a mood glyph is kept, label it as a quality, not the name. [s11]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (and contradicted in part)**: Moves so slowly that it is frequently mistaken for part of the vegetation by people passing beneath; movements register as a branch shifting.
  No source found for 'mistaken for vegetation'. Sources say it is slow and sluggish (s5) but also that it has been documented walking between trees and is capable of galloping (s6). What is sourced: it is sometimes mistaken for sloths, other possums or monkeys (s5). Replace with that. [s5] [s6]
- **PARTLY OK / UNVERIFIABLE**: Male spotted cuscuses display bold chestnut spots unique to each individual on a cream-white coat.
  Only males are spotted; females are usually white or grey and unspotted (s5, s6). Spot colour varies (red/brown per ADW; grey or brown males per Wikipedia), so 'chestnut' is too narrow. 'Unique to each individual' has no source; cut it. [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: Prehensile tail partially naked on the underside for grip, used as a fifth limb.
  ADW: tails long, prehensile, lack fur, with rough papillae on the ventral surface. Wikipedia: lower half has rough scales on its inner surface for gripping. 'Fifth limb' is fair shorthand. [s5] [s6]
- **OK (soften)**: Large, forward-facing orange-red eyes adapted for low-light vision.
  Eye colour ranges from yellow and orange to red, with slit pupils (Wikipedia citing Walker's). ADW describes a blue-green eye ringed by yellow-red skin. It is nocturnal (s5). Say 'yellow to red eyes with slit pupils'. 'Adapted for low-light vision' is reasonable but no source states it for this species. [s5] [s6]
- **WRONG (second half)**: Extremely solitary outside mating season; marks territory with musky scent from glands, marking the boundary without direct confrontation.
  Solitary: OK (s5, s6). Males mark with musk and saliva (Wikipedia citing Macdonald 1984). But males also bark, snarl, hiss and stand upright to defend territory, and encounters between males can be aggressive (s5); captive males cannot share an enclosure (s6). 'Without requiring direct confrontation' is false. [s5] [s6]
- **WRONG (overstated)**: Almost never descends to the forest floor; spends its entire life in the mid-to-upper canopy.
  Arboreal and forages in understory, subcanopy and canopy (s6), but it has been documented walking between trees (s6) and resting in tree hollows, under tree roots or among rocks (s5). 'Entire life in the mid-to-upper canopy' is false. [s5] [s6]
- **WRONG (incomplete)**: Intro: lives in the rainforest canopy of Papua New Guinea and northern Australia.
  Range: New Guinea (both halves, not only PNG), Cape York, and nearby islands, west to Selayar near Sulawesi and east to the Solomons (s5, s7). Also lives in mangroves and eucalyptus forest, not only rainforest (s5). [s5] [s7]
- **OK (fix attribution)**: Proverb 厚積薄發 (hòu jī bó fā) 'attributed to the Su Shi aesthetic'.
  The phrase is from Su Shi's essay 稼說送張琥 (Jià shuō sòng Zhāng Hǔ): 博觀而約取，厚積而薄發 (bó guān ér yuē qǔ, hòu jī ér bó fā). Say 'from an essay by Su Shi', not 'the Su Shi aesthetic'. The pairing with the cuscus is labelled our own, which is honest. [s13]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Page sources: Helgen and Jackson (2015) Family Phalangeridae in Handbook of the Mammals of the World; Flannery (1995) Mammals of New Guinea.
  Could not open either book. Wikipedia cites Flannery's 1990 edition. The HMW chapter authorship was not confirmed. A person should check before keeping these citations. The IUCN link resolves to the right species id per page markup but I did not open it. 
- **OK**: Purple Star sections.
  Both Purple Star blocks say a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour and call the crossing reading 'our outline'. No star is assigned from the sign pair.
