## The raven as a symbol
- **Norse.** Odin's two ravens, Huginn (from hugr, 'thought') and Muninn (usually glossed 'memory', more exactly 'mind' or 'desire'), fly over the world each day and return to whisper what they saw. In Grimnismal Odin says he fears for Huginn, and fears more for Muninn. A ready image for a mind that sends itself out to gather the world and worries what it will lose. [s6]
- **Haida (Pacific Northwest).** Raven Steals Light: Raven, a trickster and shape-shifter, turns into a pine needle, is born as a child in the house of the old man who hoards the light, coaxes the box open and flings the sun into the sky, scattering the stars. Cleverness and disguise used to bring light to everyone. Raven is both world-maker and trickster across many Northwest Coast nations. [s7, s13]
- **Irish / Celtic.** The Morrigan, goddess of war and prophecy, appears as a raven over battlefields and foretells outcomes. At Cu Chulainn's death his enemies only believed he had died when a raven landed on his shoulder. The raven as the one who sees how things will end. [s8]
- **Greek / Roman.** Apollo's bird, once white, reported Coronis's unfaithfulness and was turned black for its 'chattering tongue' (Ovid, Metamorphoses 2). The cost of telling the truth too fast is built into the raven's very color. [s9, s13]
- **Hebrew Bible / Christian.** Noah's raven flew back and forth until the waters dried (Gen 8:7). Ravens fed the prophet Elijah bread and meat morning and evening (1 Kings 17:4-6). Jesus: 'Consider the ravens: they don't sow, they don't reap, they have no warehouse or barn, and God feeds them' (Luke 12:24). Our note: a lovely irony, since real ravens are expert hoarders. [s10]
- **English legend.** If the ravens leave the Tower of London, the Tower and the kingdom will fall; at least six are kept (seven today, one spare). The Charles II origin story is legend, and nobody knows when ravens first came to the Tower. The raven as guardian whose staying power holds a house together. [s11]
- **Chinese / East Asian (corvid, not strictly raven).** The sun crow, 金乌 (jīnwū, 'golden crow'), lives in the sun; ten sun crows once rose together and the archer Hou Yi shot down all but one. Japan's Yatagarasu, a raven or jungle crow, guided Emperor Jimmu. USE WITH CARE: Chinese folklore speaks of crows (乌鸦 wūyā), so frame it as 'the raven's cousin in the sun', not as a raven myth. [s12]
- **Modern spirit-animal / totem meanings (messenger, magic, shadow work).** Unsourced, do not use. Only blogs and New Age sites found; nothing traceable to a living tradition.

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** (Our reading.) Two fixed points meet here. Aquarius is fixed Air: it holds an idea of how people and systems should work and does not let go easily. The Monkey is yang Metal: quick, dexterous, sharp at the edges. Together they make the watcher who is also a player. You tend to stand a little apart, read the whole room, and then do something clever that nobody else saw coming. Saturn, the old ruler, gives the long memory; Uranus gives the sudden swerve.
- **Gifts.** (Our reading.) Foresight that comes from paying attention, not from worry. A sense of humor that lives right next to the serious mind (the raven that slides down a snowy roof on its back). Loyalty to a small circle, honesty that does not flatter, and a knack for solving problems in ways that look obvious only afterward.
- **Shadow.** (Our reading.) The Aquarius distance plus the Metal edge. You can keep people at the length of an arm while seeming warm. Records of who let you down are filed carefully and rarely reopened. Monkey restlessness can read the room so fast that you act on a half-finished picture. Like Apollo's raven, you can say the true thing at the wrong moment and pay for it.
- **Love.** (Our reading, built on the Monkey's 'slow to settle, fully committed once chosen' and the raven's lifelong pair bond.) You often test a partner quietly for a long time. Once someone passes, you are unusually steady: private rituals, inside jokes, a pair that defends its corner of the world together. What you need back is honesty and room. What wounds you is being watched without being trusted.
- **Money.** (Our reading, anchored by the cache.) You tend to keep reserves nobody knows about and to plan for a version of your life that has not arrived yet. Traditional Monkey career lists lean toward finance, trading and numbers, so the head for it is there. The risk is the clever bet made because it is clever.
- **Conflict.** (Our reading.) You rarely fight in the open. You go quiet, rearrange things, and move your real plans somewhere the other person cannot see. Your Metal side can end an argument with one exact sentence. The work is to say what you saw out loud before you quietly move the cache.

## Careers
- **Strategy, forecasting, intelligence or risk analysis.** Reading many players at once and predicting the next move is the raven's whole method; China Highlights lists stock trading and air traffic control for Monkeys.
- **Research science and engineering.** Aquarius inventiveness plus Monkey quick learning; science and engineering both appear on traditional Monkey career lists (s3).
- **Finance, accounting, trading.** The cache mindset and the Monkey's traditional association with banking and accounting (s3).
- **Film direction, game design, puzzle or escape-room design.** Play and planning on the same system; directing is on the Monkey list (s3). Building traps and tricks for an audience is a raven-shaped craft.
- **Investigative journalism or ethics and fraud work.** Long memory for who did what, honesty that does not flatter, and comfort standing outside the group (Aquarius).
- **Community organizing, open-source and civic tech.** Aquarius humanitarian streak plus a Monkey's skill at moving among people; ravens are social birds that share news of food.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: 01 The cache: ravens show episodic-like memory for cache location, content and time elapsed, adjusting for perishability.
  The what-where-when, perishability result is from western scrub jays (Clayton & Dickinson 1998), not ravens. Ravens do cache and plan: Kabadayi & Osvath 2017 showed ravens choose and save tools and tokens for future use. Rewrite around that study. [s19, s14]
- **WRONG**: 02 The re-cache: observed ravens move caches; ravens that never stole are less likely to re-cache (experience projection).
  The 'thieves re-cache more' finding is Emery & Clayton 2001 on scrub jays. For ravens, what is sourced: they cache farther away or wait when competitors watch (Heinrich & Pepper 1998) and they treat knowledgeable and ignorant competitors differently (Bugnyar & Heinrich 2005). Drop the pilfering-experience sentence. [s18, s17, s16]
- **OK**: 03 The play: sliding down snow-covered roofs on their backs, stick play, aerial acrobatics, pranks.
  Heinrich & Smolker report ravens sliding down steep snow-covered roofs and snowy slopes, including on their backs holding sticks. Audubon confirms stick-drop games and aerial rolls. 'Pranks on other animals' is anecdotal; soften or drop. Note the famous roof-snowboarding video is a hooded crow, not a raven. [s20, s21, s13]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: 04 The pair bond: mate for life, mutual preening, grief after a partner's death.
  Lifelong pairing and mutual preening: OK (Audubon). Grief after a partner's death: no raven-specific source found; documented anecdotes are for crows. Remove the grief clause or label it anecdotal. [s13]
- **WRONG**: 05 The grudge: ravens recognize and hold negative bias toward humans who deceived them, avoid them, alert others, threaten them months later.
  Partly right. Ravens preferred a fair human trader over an unfair one after a month (Mueller, Massen, Bugnyar et al. 2017). 'Alerting others' and multi-month mobbing come from Marzluff's American crow mask studies; NatGeo notes observer ravens did not change their choices. Keep the one-month memory, cut the rest. [s22, s23]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: 06 The culture: regional vocal dialects and distinct behavioral repertoires that affect problem-solving.
  Regional call dialects are documented (Enggist 1997: 37 pairs split into two dialect regions). 'Behavioral repertoires that affect how they solve problems' found no source. Keep dialects, cut the problem-solving claim. [s24]
- **WRONG**: Reference list: 'Osvath & Osvath (2008), Great ape and raven future planning, Animal Cognition'.
  Osvath & Osvath 2008 studied chimpanzees and orangutans only. The raven planning paper is Kabadayi & Osvath 2017, Science. [s15, s14]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Reference list: 'Bugnyar & Heinrich (2005), Animal Cognition' linked to DOI 10.1007/s10071-004-0239-6.
  Bugnyar & Heinrich 2005 was published in Proceedings of the Royal Society B, not Animal Cognition. Could not open the linked Springer DOI to see what it actually is (fetch blocked). Relink to the PMC copy. [s16]
- **WRONG**: Reference list: Marzluff & Angell, 'Gifts of the Crow: How Perception, Emotion, and Thought Allow Ravens to Behave Like Humans'.
  Real subtitle ends 'Allow Smart Birds to Behave Like Humans'. The book is mostly about crows. [s25]
- **OK**: Page note: an Aquarius born in late January or early February can belong to the Goat year.
  Correct: Aquarius starts January 20 and lunar New Year falls late January to mid February, so early-Aquarius birthdays can fall in the previous animal year. Monkey years also include 1980 and 1968 if the page wants more. [s1, s3]
