## The Bar-headed Goose as a symbol
- **Chinese, Han dynasty (Book of Han, Biography of Su Wu).** Su Wu, a Han envoy held by the Xiongnu, was made to herd sheep by Lake Baikal for 19 years. When the chanyu claimed he was dead, Chang Hui told him the Han emperor had shot a wild goose in Shanglin Park with Su Wu's letter tied to its foot. The ruse freed him (81 BC) and gave the idiom 雁足傳書 yàn zú chuán shū, a goose carries the letter. Goose and sheep in one story: the Goat year and the goose meet here. [s7] [s8]
- **Chinese marriage rites (Western Han onward).** The wild goose 雁 yàn was the betrothal gift because it migrates at the same time every year, a sign the couple would stay faithful. Cui Yin's Western Han essay on marriage uses 委禽奠雁 wěi qín diàn yàn; by the Tang the goose went with the proposal. [s9]
- **India: hamsa, ornithologist's identification.** Salim Ali (1979) argued that Kalidasa's hamsa should be read as the bar-headed goose, which really crosses the Himalayas from Tibet; Vogel (1952) noted bar-headed geese are common in India while swans are rare. Scholarly, but contested (others read hamsa as swan). [s5] [s15]
- **Hindu and Buddhist.** The hamsa is the mount (vahana) of Saraswati, goddess of knowledge and the arts, and of Brahma; later literature says it can separate milk from water, a picture of discernment; ascetics are called paramahamsa, 'supreme hamsa'. In Buddhism it is a symbol of wisdom. [s5] [s6]
- **Sanskrit poetry.** Hamsa-sandesha, 'the goose messenger': Rama sends a hamsa to carry his message to Sita (Pattanaik). India and China both made the goose a carrier of letters between people kept apart. [s6]
- **Song dynasty poem used on the page.** 不畏浮雲遮望眼 bù wèi fú yún zhē wàng yǎn is a line from Wang Anshi's poem 登飛來峰 Dēng Fēilái Fēng; the next line is 自緣身在最高層 zì yuán shēn zài zuì gāo céng ('because I stand on the highest level'). A poem line, not a folk proverb. [s17]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Totem-blog claims that the goose spirit animal means 'teamwork, loyalty, the hero's quest'; Celtic wild goose as Holy Spirit (not researched, no primary source opened). (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Fire (Sagittarius) meets yin Earth (Goat). Sagittarius aims at the far side of the mountain; the Goat wants a soft field and the flock fed. The Bar-headed Goose is the gentle long-hauler: soft manners, a huge range, and a pull home. Fire feeds Earth in the five-phase cycle, so Sagittarius restlessness becomes the Goat's quiet stamina instead of burning out.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: climbs what others assume is impossible and then plays it down; carries messages between worlds (Su Wu's goose, the hamsa messenger); discernment, the milk drawn out of water; keeps faith with distant people.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Everest myth. Letting people believe the bigger story about your feats; Sagittarius boasting dressed in Goat modesty. Also the Goat's clinging: a vow kept to a lake long after it stopped feeding you.
- **Love.** Our reading: the betrothal goose. Slow and serious to commit, then faithful on a seasonal clock. Needs freedom to fly far and a partner who is there when it lands. Long-distance love suits this crossing better than most.
- **Money.** Our reading: Jupiter generosity checked by Goat caution. Spends on travel and on other people; saves like a goose banking fat before migration. Risk: funding a far-off dream on faith.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids the fight, then says the tactless true thing at altitude. Prefers to leave and come back with a clear head. Practice: hug the terrain; take the low pass when it gets you there.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (oversimplified)**: Fact 1: a unique alpha-hemoglobin subunit with a single amino acid substitution raises oxygen affinity.
  Natarajan et al. 2018: bar-headed and greylag hemoglobins differ at five sites, three alpha-chain changes on the bar-headed lineage. Pro-to-Ala at alpha 119 (Perutz's prediction, tested by Jessen et al. 1991) has the largest effect, but the others contribute. Say 'a handful of changes, led by one at position 119'. [s14] [s13]
- **WRONG**: Fact 1: at 29,000 feet, near the summit of Everest, its blood still oxygenates tissue; oxygen about a third of sea level.
  No tracked goose has come near 29,000 ft. Highest record is 7,290 m (about 23,900 ft), one brief flight; 98% of logger records were below 6,000 m (Bishop et al. 2015). The Everest sighting is a second-hand report traced to Swan (1961). The 'a third of sea level' figure is right for Everest's summit pressure, but the geese are not there. [s12] [s15] [s11]
- **OK (narrow it)**: Fact 2: GPS tracking confirmed a single non-stop Himalayan crossing of seven to eight hours, with ascents above 21,000 feet, rather than staging at altitude.
  Hawkes et al. 2011 used satellite Argos-GPS tags: climbs of 4,000 to 6,000 m in 7 to 8 hours northbound (median 8 h), southbound median 4.5 h. A 2011 peak near 6,400 m (about 21,000 ft) is reported (s15). Not every bird was non-stop: one landed and finished next morning. Also many geese stage at places like Qinghai Lake (s18) and in western Tibet for 20 to 30 days (s15), so drop 'rather than staging'. [s10] [s15] [s18]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Fact 3: V formation, with lead position rotating so each bird takes a turn at the front.
  Hawkes et al. 2011 only suggest calm air 'may also facilitate the use of formation flight'. Rotating leaders has been measured in other birds (northern bald ibis), not this species in any source I opened. Cut or say 'like many geese, may fly in V formation'. [s10]
- **OK / UNVERIFIABLE tail**: Fact 4: two parallel dark bars across a white head; individuals distinguishable from far away at altitude.
  Black bars on a white head, paler than other grey geese: OK (s15). 'The individual can be distinguished from far away' at altitude is embellishment; cut. [s15]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Fact 5: strong site fidelity, returning to the same breeding lakes every spring.
  Wikipedia says only that some birds may show high wintering-site fidelity. Zheng et al. 2018 did not test year-to-year fidelity. No source for breeding-lake fidelity. [s15] [s18]
- **WRONG**: Fact 6: radar tracking recorded altitudes exceeding 23,000 feet, 'higher than most commercial aircraft fly in turbulence avoidance protocols'; used routinely.
  The 7,290 m record came from a body-worn data logger, not radar, and was a single brief flight; most flights hug terrain at about 4,500 to 5,500 m (s11, s12). Airliners cruise far higher. Drop 'routinely' and the aircraft comparison. [s11] [s12]
- **WRONG**: Repeated '29,000 feet over Everest' lines (dossier strapline, chakra card, Awakened section, share line, proverb gloss 'above most clouds').
  Same error as fact 1, repeated at least five times. Replace with the terrain-hugging truth, which is the better story. [s11] [s12] [s15]
- **OK / UNVERIFIABLE (Kazakhstan)**: Meet the Animal: breeds in Tibet, Mongolia, Kazakhstan; migrates twice yearly across the Himalayas to the Indian subcontinent.
  Breeds mostly on the Tibetan Plateau north to Mongolia, winters Assam to Tamil Nadu (s15). Kazakhstan not confirmed in what I opened. Not every population crosses the Himalayas; some winter in Tibet (s12: Mongolia to Tibet or India). Say 'many populations'. [s15] [s12]
- **OK (style flag)**: Header hanzi 斑頭雁 (rail).
  斑头雁 bān tóu yàn is the standard Chinese name for Anser indicus (ChinesePod). Pinyin is only in the aria-label, not on screen; show it. Traditional script in the rail, simplified elsewhere; pick one. [s16]
- **WRONG (label)**: 'The proverb of your year': 不畏浮雲遮望眼.
  It is a line by the Song poet Wang Anshi from 登飛來峰 (s17), not a proverb. The page does say the pairing is its own. Relabel 'a line we pair with the Goat year' and credit Wang Anshi. The gloss relies on the false 29,000 ft altitude. [s17]

  No sun-sign star pairing; the page says charts come from lunar birth date and hour. No 'Primal Zodiac' string found. (no source)
