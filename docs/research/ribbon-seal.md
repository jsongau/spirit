## The Ribbon Seal as a symbol
- **Japanese (common name).** The Japanese name is 鞍掛海豹 (kurakake azarashi; Chinese reading ānguà hǎibào), the 'saddle-draped seal': on the male's dark coat the white bands look like a horse's saddle thrown over its back. A strong image for a page about a marked animal. [s13]
- **Chinese (common name).** Chinese names are 環海豹 huán hǎibào ('ring seal') and 帶紋海豹 dàiwén hǎibào ('banded-pattern seal'). The page's header 紋 wén is the 'pattern' syllable of the second name. Caution: 環斑海豹 huánbān hǎibào is the ringed seal, a different species. [s14]
- **Central Yup'ik, Nunivak Cup'iq, St. Lawrence Island Yupik, Inupiaq.** Alaska Native names: qasruliq (Central Yup'ik), qasruleq (Nunivak Cup'iq), kukupak (St. Lawrence Island Yupik), qaigullik (Inupiaq). Hunters take fewer than 200 a year, the fewest of any ice seal, because the seals rarely come near coastal villages. Neither source glosses the names; do not invent meanings. [s8] [s10]
- **St. Lawrence Island (Inupiaq or Yupik makers), early 20th century.** A 140 cm clothing bag sewn from a whole ribbon seal skin with sinew is held at the Hood Museum. Elim elders told ELOKA that 'nobody hardly hunts the ribbon seal' and that the skins are now used for decoration. The striking hide is what people kept. [s11] [s10]
- **Yup'ik (seals in general, not ribbon-specific).** In the Bladder Festival, the seal's spirit (yua) was held to live in its bladder; inflated bladders of the year's seals were honored in the qasgiq for five or six days, then returned to the sea so the seals could be reborn. Use only as seal lore in general, clearly labelled. [s12]
- **Chinese (Dog half).** The page's proverb 狗不嫌家貧 gǒu bù xián jiā pín is the second half of the folk saying 兒不嫌母醜，狗不嫌家貧 ér bù xián mǔ chǒu, gǒu bù xián jiā pín: a child does not reject a plain mother, a dog does not reject a poor home. Its origin text is not given by the source; call it a folk saying. [s15]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Selkie legends belong to grey and harbour seals in Scottish, Irish and Faroese lore, not the North Pacific ribbon seal. Generic 'seal spirit animal' meanings found on totem blogs have no traditional source. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water (Cancer, Moon-ruled) meets yang Earth (Dog). In the five-phase control cycle Earth contains Water, so the Dog gives Cancer's feeling a bank to run between. Cancer keeps a shell and retreats sideways; the Dog stands guard at the door at nightfall. The Ribbon Seal is the guardian who leaves: calm and almost careless on the ice, then gone for months into open water where nobody can follow. Its markings arrive slowly over three or four molts, so its identity is earned, never given.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: loyalty that does not depend on conditions (the poor-home proverb), memory for what people need, concentrated care in a short window, composure among strangers, the ability to work far from any audience.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Cancer's grudge plus the Dog's difficulty saying what it thinks. Retreats to open water instead of naming the hurt. Can mistake being unreachable for being safe, and can give a short burst of total care then disappear before the other person is ready.
- **Love.** Our reading: Moon-ruled devotion with a Dog's fidelity. Gives everything for a season, the way a mother seal stays with her pup on the floe for three to four weeks, then trusts the bond to hold across distance. Needs a partner who reads long silences as a swim, not a leaving, and who is still there when the ice comes back.
- **Money.** Our reading: Cancer saves for the home, the Dog is cautious and prudent; a steady saver who spends on shelter and on the few people inside it. Risk: holding back from good bets out of fear of losing the floe.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: goes sideways like the crab, then outward like the seal heading south. Rarely starts a fight; rarely forgets one. When the Dog's guard is triggered it defends hard and fast for someone else, far more readily than for itself.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (not the animal's name)**: Header hanzi 紋 (rail__han and serial-han) with pinyin wén.
  紋 wén means pattern or stripe. It is one syllable of the Chinese name 帶紋海豹 dàiwén hǎibào but does not name the animal. Correct traditional names: 環海豹 huán hǎibào (zh.wikipedia title) or 帶紋海豹 dàiwén hǎibào. Suggest 帶紋海豹 because it keeps 紋. Do not use 環斑海豹 (ringed seal). [s14]
- **WRONG (pup colour; band placement)**: Adult males carry four bold white bands: around the neck, around each flipper insertion, and across the torso; no other pinniped has this banding; females muted; pups are born grey and develop the pattern over years.
  Pups are born in a white woolly lanugo, shed at about 3 to 5 weeks, then turn blue-black above and silver below with no bands (NOAA, ADF&G). Bands: neck, each FORE flipper, and hips (NOAA); Wikipedia says one around the neck, one around the tail end and a ring on each side enclosing the front flipper. 'Across the torso' should be 'around the hips'. Bands develop over about 3 years (NOAA) or emerge at about 4 (Wikipedia): 'over years' is OK. Female muted: OK. Unique banding: OK. [s6] [s7] [s8]
- **OK (soften)**: Pelagic for most of the year, non-breeding season entirely at open sea, rarely hauling out outside breeding and pupping.
  NOAA: spends most time in open ocean, uses pack ice in spring to pup, nurse and molt, does not remain on ice after molting. But NOAA also says significant numbers return to the ice when it reforms in winter, so 'entirely at open sea' is too absolute. Add moulting to the haul-out reasons. [s6] [s9]
- **WRONG**: Actively avoids human approach, among the most difficult pinnipeds to observe, typically submerges rather than investigates when researchers approach.
  Sources say the opposite. NOAA: 'relatively unwary of their surroundings while hauled out.' ADF&G: 'Ribbon seals are not afraid of passing boats or humans.' Wikipedia: people in boats can approach closely before disturbing them. They are hard to study because they live far offshore, not because they flee. The 'submerges rather than investigates' line has no source. Rewrite or cut, and the 'difficult to approach' reading built on it must go. [s6] [s7] [s8]
- **OK**: Mothers nurse for approximately four weeks on sea ice, then the pup is weaned and independent.
  ADF&G: 3 to 4 weeks, pup weight more than doubles. Wikipedia: four to six weeks of care on pack ice. NOAA: most pups weaned by mid-May. 'About three to four weeks' is the safest wording. [s8] [s7] [s6]
- **OK (reword)**: One of the least-studied pinnipeds in its range; behavioural data sparse; pelagic habits make observation difficult.
  NOAA AFSC 2005 calls it 'this poorly-known species', with 'relatively little' known of abundance, distribution, migration and food; the 2005 Kamchatka tagging was the first live capture and instrumentation. ADF&G: summer life little known, 'rarely seen'. 'Least-studied' as a ranking is not stated; use 'poorly known'. [s9] [s8]
- **OK (soften)**: IUCN Least Concern, yet highly vulnerable to Bering Sea ice loss; depends on ice for pupping and moulting.
  Least Concern (IUCN 3.1) per Wikipedia. NOAA's 2013 review expected sea-ice change to cause a gradual decline but found ESA listing 'not warranted at this time'. 'Highly vulnerable' overstates; say 'depends on sea ice, which a 2013 NOAA review expects to shrink'. Breeding also happens in the Sea of Okhotsk, not only the Bering Sea (the intro mentions only the Bering). [s7] [s6]
- **OK**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) section.
  This page assigns no star to the crossing and says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. No overreach found. (no source)
- **OK**: Proverb 狗不嫌家貧 gǒu bù xián jiā pín, labelled as our own pairing.
  Real folk saying (second half of 兒不嫌母醜，狗不嫌家貧). Pinyin present on the page. [s15]
