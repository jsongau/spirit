## The Snowy Owl as a symbol
- **Shang China (c. 1200 BC).** The owl was a major emblem of the Shang: Queen and general Fu Hao was buried at Yinxu (excavated 1976) with a pair of bronze owl-shaped wine vessels, now in the Henan Museum and National Museum of China. Wikipedia's article says the owl as bad omen largely took hold in the Zhou dynasty. Owl in general, not the snowy owl; frame it that way. [s5]
- **Shijing (Book of Songs), Bin Feng.** 鴟鴞 (chī xiāo): a mother bird pleads 'O owl, O owl, you have taken my young ones; do not also destroy my nest.' The owl as the one who takes. Shows the later, darker Chinese view. Generic owl, not snowy owl. [s6]
- **Inuit, eastern Arctic.** Raven and Owl, both once white, paint each other with lamp-black; Raven fidgets, Owl throws the paint over him, and each has worn its colours since (Rachel Qitsualik's telling, the most recent, mainly eastern Arctic version). The owl comes out pale and spotted. Her text does not name the species; do not call it a snowy owl story without a second source. [s7]
- **Inuit, Fort Chimo (Kuujjuaq) co-operative, 1960s.** Ookpik: Jeannie Snowball made sealskin owl dolls, by her granddaughter's account to honour an owl she hunted that kept her family from near-starvation. Ookpik was chosen to represent Canada at a 1963 Philadelphia trade show and became a national icon. The IAF piece does not state the species; check before calling it a snowy owl. [s8]
- **Quebec (1987).** The National Assembly chose the snowy owl as Quebec's avian emblem in 1987 'as a symbol of the province's support for wildlife protection'. [s9]
- **Buddhist parable (Monkey half).** Monkeys grasping the moon (猿猴捉月 yuán hóu zhuō yuè; the page uses 猿猴取月 yuán hóu qǔ yuè): a monkey chief sees the moon in the water, thinks it has fallen, and the troop links tails to pull it out until the branch breaks. Moral: do not chase appearances. Belongs to all 12 Monkey pages. [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'snowy owl spirit animal means wisdom/magic/transition' claims from totem blogs; no traditional source found. Ainu owl deity Kotan-kor-kamuy is Blakiston's fish owl, not the snowy owl: do not borrow. Hedwig (Harry Potter) is pop culture, not folklore. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: Saturn's patience (Capricorn, cardinal Earth) with the Monkey's quick yang Metal mind. Capricorn counts the cost; the Monkey counts the angles. The snowy owl lives this: it skips breeding in lean lemming years and lays big clutches in rich ones. Earth feeds Metal in the generating cycle, so Capricorn's endurance becomes raw material for the Monkey's sharp timing.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: reads a season correctly and commits fully when the numbers are real; comfortable alone in open, exposed places; works at odd hours (daylight hunter in summer, night hunter on winter coasts per Project SNOWstorm); cool under scarcity; a dry wit that surprises people who took the stillness for coldness.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the recheck that never ends; withholding effort so long the season passes; leaving without a word when a place stops paying; the Monkey's restlessness dressed up as Capricorn prudence. The moon-in-the-well risk turned inward: trusting a bright surface in people when the count is about feelings, not lemmings.
- **Love.** Our reading: cautious, assessing, slow to settle (both halves share this per s2 and s4), then intensely provisioning once committed: in boom years a snowy owl pair raises a big brood. Needs a partner who does not take the waiting for indifference and who can laugh at the dry jokes.
- **Money.** Our reading: boom-and-bust aware. Saves hard in lean years and spends decisively in rich ones; good at timing. Risk: sitting on cash after the market has turned, or one dramatic move south when restless.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: goes still and silent, watches, then acts once and precisely. Rarely shouts. Can disappear rather than argue, which the other side experiences as abandonment. Practice: say the number in the room, before leaving it.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Risk analysis, actuarial work, commodities or cyclical investing.** Our reading: the lemming-cycle bookkeeper; Capricorn structure plus Monkey quickness with numbers.
- **Polar, wildlife or field research; remote postings.** Our reading: at home in open, exposed, empty country; long solitary watches.
- **Night or shift work: emergency medicine, air traffic, security, logistics.** Our reading: hunts by day in summer and by night on winter coasts (s13); attention that does not need the usual hours.
- **Strategy, turnaround or crisis management.** Our reading: knows when to hold back and when to go all in; the Monkey's invention inside Capricorn discipline.
- **Wildlife protection and conservation policy.** Our reading: Quebec chose this bird to stand for wildlife protection (s9).

## Candidate lines
1. In a lean lemming summer you skip the nest and keep your strength for yourself. The spring the burrows fill again, you lay a clutch so large it looks reckless to anyone who did not see the count.
2. Your biggest moves come after the fat years, never the hungry ones. Plenty is what sends you south, toward a frozen shoreline nobody on the tundra expected you to reach.
3. Saturn gave you the long white patience and the Monkey gave you the angles, so you sit on one snow hummock for an hour and already know three ways the lemming could run.
4. By day in the endless summer, by night on a winter beach far to the south: you hunt in whatever hours the place requires, and the owls who keep to the dark think you strange.
5. Each moult takes a few more dark bars off the old males until the snow can barely see them. Less is written on you every year, and every year you have decided more.
6. You can see the moon floating in the meltwater and you do not reach for it. The Monkey in you wants to; the Capricorn in you checks the sky first.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (generic)**: Header hanzi: 鸮 (xiāo) shown in the hero/rail.
  鸮 xiāo means owl, so it is not wrong, but it is the family word. The species name is 雪鸮 (xuě xiāo), snow owl (Chinese Wikipedia; also 白鸮 bái xiāo). Recommend 雪鸮 xuě xiāo with pinyin. Hero glyph shown without pinyin. [s15]
- **OK (with softening)**: Unlike almost every other owl, the Snowy Owl is largely diurnal, hunting by daylight rather than at night, an adaptation to the near-continuous light of the Arctic summer.
  ADW: 'Unlike most owls, snowy owls are largely diurnal.' 'Almost every other owl' overstates it (ADW says 'most'). Project SNOWstorm tracking found a wintering owl roosting by day and hunting at night over Delaware Bay, so the daylight habit is a summer/Arctic pattern, not a rule. 'Adaptation to Arctic light' is a reasonable inference but not stated in the sources opened. [s11] [s13]
- **OK**: A single Snowy Owl can eat more than 1,600 lemmings in a year; clutch size swings from 3 to 16 eggs depending on prey.
  ADW: 'one owl may consume more than 1,600 lemmings in a year'; clutch 'usually 3 to 11', up to 16 when prey is extremely abundant. Suggest 'usually 3 to 11, up to 16 in boom years'. [s11]
- **WRONG (scope and citation)**: In years when lemmings are scarce, Snowy Owls may forgo breeding entirely, and after a documented collapse in lemming cycles, fledgling production across the species fell by roughly 98 percent.
  Forgoing breeding is OK (ADW). The 98 percent is from Schmidt et al. 2012 (Proc. R. Soc. B), two sites in high-arctic NE Greenland, 1988 to 2010, not 'across the species'. The page's source list credits it to 'Therrien et al.'; the paper's lead author is Niels M. Schmidt (PMC3479802 is the right paper, wrong author). Fix: 'in northeast Greenland, fledgling production fell 98 percent after the lemming cycle collapsed (Schmidt et al. 2012)'. [s11] [s12]
- **WRONG (cause)**: Roughly every four years, Snowy Owls undertake irruptive migrations far south, driven by scarcity on the tundra rather than by season or weather.
  The four-year rhythm is supported (ADW; Audubon: 'every four years or so there's a modest irruption'). The cause is outdated: ADW says 'presumably because prey is scarce', but Project SNOWstorm research reported by Audubon finds 'privation isn't what drives them south; it's plenty': a lemming boom produces many young, and 'the vast majority' of irruptive birds in 2013 were juveniles. Rewrite to credit the boom. [s11] [s13]
- **OK**: Snowy Owls show variable dark barring; females and juveniles carry more barring than adult males, some of whom near-whiten with age.
  ADW: 'Females tend to have more markings than males, which may become nearly completely white as they age'; young owls are darker and more heavily marked. [s11]
- **UNVERIFIABLE for this species (borrowed)**: Owls hunting through snow rely on directional hearing sensitive enough to locate prey beneath a snow layer, with low frequencies carrying furthest through it.
  This is the Great Gray Owl study (Clark and Duncan 2022, Proc. R. Soc. B): snow muffles high frequencies more than low, and refraction makes an 'acoustic mirage' of up to about 5 degrees; only six trials. Nothing opened shows it for snowy owls, and ADW describes snowy owls hunting from a perch by scanning. Either name the Great Gray Owl or cut it. [s14] [s11]
- **OK**: Crossing note: born in January or early February, a birthday in those weeks can belong to the Goat.
  Correct for Capricorn (22 Dec to 19 Jan): a January Capricorn born before lunar New Year belongs to the previous year, which before a Monkey year is the Goat. [s16]
- **OK**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) claims on the page.
  No star is assigned from the sign pair; page says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. No 'Primal Zodiac' string found. 
- **OK**: Proverb 猿猴取月 (yuán hóu qǔ yuè), told as monkeys linking tails down a well for the moon's reflection, the branch giving way; labelled as our own pairing.
  Real Buddhist parable; JAANUS gives the variant 猿猴捉月 with the same plot (troop joins tails, branch breaks). The page says 'the branch above them starts to give'; in the parable it breaks and they drown. Fine as softened. Pairing honestly labelled. [s10]
