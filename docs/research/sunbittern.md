## The Sunbittern as a symbol
- **Spanish-speaking Latin America (common names).** Among its Spanish names are ave sol (sun bird) and garza del sol (sun heron), plus tigana and pavito de agua. The English 'sunbittern' likewise comes from the sunburst wing pattern (Grzimek). Every naming tradition we found reads the hidden wing as a sun, which lines up with Leo's ruler, the Sun. [s9] [s7]
- **Rural Neotropics (recorded practice).** Grzimek records that local people sometimes take young sunbitterns from the nest and tame them for their skill at catching flies and spiders. A rare case of the bird living as a quiet, useful household guardian, which suits the Dog's service instinct. Present as recorded practice, not as advice. [s7]
- **Scientific name (Greek).** Eurypyga is Greek eurys (wide) plus pyge (rump, tail), a name for the fanned display. Wiktionary mirror only; the meaning of 'helias' (often read as Greek helios, sun) is NOT sourced, do not state it. [s12]
- **Chinese naming.** Its Chinese name is 日鳽 rì jiān, literally 'sun heron'. 鳽 jiān on its own is a general word for small herons and pond herons (Handian). Chinese also files it as 南美擬鷺 nán měi nǐ lù, 'South American false heron'. [s10] [s11]
- **Evolutionary lineage (science, usable as story).** The sunbittern is the only member of its family; molecular studies make the kagu of New Caledonia its closest living relative, a small Gondwanan lineage split across an ocean. [s6] [s7]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** No Indigenous Amazonian, Mesoamerican or other folklore about the sunbittern could be found in any source I could open. Spirit-animal meanings online are blog inventions. Do not write a 'sunbittern totem' meaning. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Fire (Leo) held inside yang Earth (Dog). Leo is ruled by the Sun; the bird's every name calls its hidden wing a sun. The Dog keeps it folded at its post. The result is a showpiece that guards before it shines: brilliant on demand, ordinary by choice. Fire feeds Earth, so the brightness becomes steadiness instead of burning off.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: timing; the defensive display that ends a threat in one move; loyalty that needs no audience; patience that makes the strike exact; willingness to look foolish to protect someone (the broken-wing act).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the sun that never opens. Leo's need to be seen meets the Dog's habit of staying in the background, and the compromise can harden into waiting forever for a bank worthy of the display. Also pride: Leo's touchiness plus the Dog's slow trust can make one slight last for years.
- **Love.** Our reading: Dog-slow to commit, Leo-generous once committed. Shows love by guarding and noticing. Needs a partner who values the folded version and is patient for the full one. Fears being taken for granted more than being rejected.
- **Money.** Our reading: Dog caution with a Leo streak for one beautiful, visible purchase. Saves quietly, spends rarely, spends big. Keeps resources for the people under its wing.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: freezes and reads first. If pushed, opens all at once in a way the other side does not forget. Rarely starts the fight; the frontal display is defense, not attack. Risk: going straight from silence to full display with nothing in between.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (soften)**: At rest the Sunbittern is a close, fine barring of black, brown and grey, and the bird is often overlooked even where it is common, standing quietly along a stream or swamp edge.
  Plumage: 'fine linear patterns of black, grey and brown' (s6); 'cryptic... perfectly camouflaged... in dappled sunlight' (s7). 'Often overlooked even where it is common' was not found in any source I opened; UNVERIFIABLE as worded. Suggest 'its fine black, grey and brown barring blends into dappled stream light'. [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: When alarmed, it spreads both wings and its tail into a wide, flat display of chestnut and gold false eyespots, invisible when folded.
  Wings and tail are fanned toward an intruder; two large chestnut patches edged black and white on a yellowish, grey area (s8). Wikipedia gives eyespots of red, yellow and black, used in threat, courtship and to startle predators (s6). Grzimek calls it the frontal display, a defensive response once mistaken for courtship (s7). [s6] [s7] [s8]
- **OK**: Hunts by walking a stream edge in slow, deliberate steps and striking suddenly with its long neck and spear-shaped bill to take fish, amphibians, crustaceans and insects.
  'Stalks prey deliberately, with neck retracted... spears prey with quick jabs' (s7); walks slowly, heron-like jab (s8). Diet: insects are an important part, plus crabs, shrimps, fish, tadpoles, frogs, eels, lizards (s6). Consider putting insects first. [s6] [s7] [s8]
- **OK**: Typically found singly or in pairs, holding a stretch of stream or forest water rather than gathering in flocks.
  Grzimek: typically seen alone or in pairs (s7). 'Holding a stretch' (territoriality) is not stated in sources opened; sedentary per s8. Keep 'singly or in pairs', drop 'holding a stretch' or soften. [s7] [s8]
- **OK (minor embellishment)**: If a nest is approached, a parent will often perform a broken-wing display, feigning injury to draw a predator away before flying off unharmed.
  Grzimek: one wing is dragged along the ground to distract predators from the nest (s7); s8 describes ruffled back and open wing dragged with hisses. 'Often' and 'flying off unharmed' are not in the sources; cut those words. [s7] [s8]
- **OK (one word)**: Sole member of its family, Eurypygidae; genetic study places its nearest living relative as the kagu, a flightless bird found only in New Caledonia.
  Sole member of Eurypygidae; molecular studies indicate kagu and sunbittern are each other's closest living relatives, a minor Gondwanan lineage (s6, s7, s10). The kagu is usually called 'nearly flightless'; prefer that. [s6] [s7] [s10]
- **WRONG (incomplete)**: Header hanzi 鳽 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin jiān.
  鳽 jiān alone is a generic word for small herons and pond herons (Handian, s11), so on its own it names a heron, not this bird. The sunbittern's Chinese name is 日鳽 rì jiān, 'sun heron' (s10). Use 日鳽 rì jiān; the sun character also carries the Leo link. [s10] [s11]
- **OK**: Proverb 犬馬之勞 quǎn mǎ zhī láo: modest offer of devoted service.
  Dictionary gloss: to serve like a dog or a horse, humble service (s13). Labelled on the page as our own pairing, which is honest. [s13]
