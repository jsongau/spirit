## The Rock Wren as a symbol
- **Hopi (Oraibi), recorded by H.R. Voth 1905.** In 'Coyote Woman and Wren', told by Tangákhoyoma, Wren (Tû'chvo) sings and hops from rock to rock, making Coyote Woman laugh and spill the water she carries to her pups. Warned she will eat him, Wren slips out of his skin, dresses a stone in it, and sings from under a rock. Coyote bites the stone, breaks her teeth and flees. A second tale, 'The Bull-snake and the Tû'chvo (Wren)', puts the wren's nest on a bluff. A small singer who wins with a stone and a hiding place under rock is an uncanny echo of the real bird's stone pavement. Caveat: Voth says only 'Wren'; on the Hopi mesas that could be the rock wren or the canyon wren. Present as Hopi wren story, nation and teller named, without a 'you' reading laid over it. [s8] [s9]
- **Chinese classical (Zhuangzi ch. 1).** Xu You refuses Yao's throne: 鷦鷯巢於深林，不過一枝 (jiāoliáo cháo yú shēn lín, bùguò yì zhī), 'the wren nests in the deep forest, yet needs no more than one branch.' The idiom 鷦鷯一枝 (jiāoliáo yì zhī) means wanting very little. This is about the Eurasian wren, the wren family generally, not Salpinctes. Warning: the second half of the same sentence (偃鼠飲河 yǎn shǔ yǐn hé) is already used in the star-nosed mole dossier; do not let both pages lean on one line. [s10] [s11] [s12]
- **Chinese classical (Shuoyuan ch. 11).** 臣見鷦鷯巢於葦苕 (chén jiàn jiāoliáo cháo yú wěi tiáo), 'I have seen a wren nest on a reed tassel' (Xunzi ch. 1 has a matching line). The usual reading, a well-made nest lost because of what it is tied to, was NOT confirmed from an opened source: ctext and Wikisource were unreachable. Verify the full passage before using the moral. If it holds, our note: the rock wren is the opposite case, it ties everything to stone. [s12]
- **Scientific naming.** Salpinctes is Greek for 'trumpeter'; obsoletus is Latin for 'worn out' or dull, for the plumage. True and on point (loud voice, drab coat), but it is name trivia, so keep it out of the hook. [s5]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** European wren lore (King of the Birds, Hunting the Wren on St Stephen's Day) is about the Eurasian wren, a different species and continent; do not transfer it. New Zealand rock wren material (piwauwau) belongs to Xenicus, not this bird. 'Rock wren spirit animal' blog meanings: none sourced. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Air (Gemini) meets yin Earth (Goat). Gemini broadcasts, the Goat builds a private shelter. The rock wren does both at once: a trumpet of a voice with a body the color of the rock, and a nest set deep in a crevice with a paved approach. Air gives the reach; Earth gives the stones.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: range of voice (a male rotates through 69 to 119 song syllables), quick reading of a terrain, the patience to place hundreds of small stones, self-sufficiency (no rock wren has ever been recorded drinking water), and a home that is hard to find and easy to defend.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: being heard everywhere and found nowhere. Gemini scatter plus Goat privacy can turn into an endless approach with no welcome at the end. The Goat's quiet stubbornness hides behind Gemini charm.
- **Love.** Our reading: courts out loud and lets people in slowly. Needs a partner who enjoys the conversation and does not demand the front door on the first day; the right one gets walked up the paved path.
- **Money.** Our reading: frugal and resourceful (needs no water source, will nest in quarries and rubble piles); earns through talk and many small jobs, then puts it into the home. Risk: over-building the defenses.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: sings at the border instead of fighting at the door; Kroodsma's study ties song variety to territory defense. Chases off what wrecks the nest (rock wrens drive away egg-destroying house wrens). Rarely starts a fight, holds ground once it does.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **PARTLY OK (fragment, simplified)**: Header hanzi 鹪 (rail__han and serial-han), shown with pinyin jiāo.
  鹪 jiāo (traditional 鷦) is the first half of 鷦鷯 jiāoliáo, wren; Dong Chinese lists 鹪鹩 as its only common word, so alone it is a fragment. The species name is 岩鷦鷯 yán jiāoliáo (Taiwan: 普通岩鷦鷯 pǔtōng yán jiāoliáo). Recommend traditional 鷦 jiāo at minimum, or 岩鷦鷯 yán jiāoliáo. The page also mixes simplified (鹪) with traditional proverb tiles (補). [s13] [s12] [s14] [s15]
- **OK (understated) / UNVERIFIABLE superlative**: Rock wrens build a pavement of small flat stones leading up to and sometimes lining the nest entrance; highly unusual among birds; can involve dozens of stones.
  Pavement confirmed (All About Birds, Audubon). Warning and Benedict: nests average about 230 stones, each about 3.1 g, together about 20% of body mass; pavement helps keep nest dry and warm. 'Dozens' understates it: use 'hundreds'. 'Highly unusual among birds' has no source; cut or soften. [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **OK (plumage) / UNVERIFIABLE (detection claim)**: Finely speckled grey-brown plumage, patterned like grains of sand, blends with the rock, so the bird is usually noticed first by its loud ringing song.
  Grey-brown upperparts with small black and white spots: confirmed. No source opened discusses camouflage function or 'heard before seen'; label it as observation, not fact. [s5]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (overstated)**: Among the passerines most tightly associated with cliff and rock habitat, nesting almost exclusively on canyon walls, boulder fields, talus and exposed rocky ground few other songbirds use.
  Rocky, sparsely vegetated habitat from below sea level to alpine: confirmed. But it also nests in construction debris, stone quarries, rock piles and abandoned mines, and the canyon wren is arguably more cliff-bound. The ranking 'among the most tightly associated' and 'few other songbirds use' are unsourced. The cited PMC climbing paper was not checked for this ranking. [s6] [s7] [s5]
- **OK (soften)**: Forages with a long, slightly downcurved bill, probing cracks for insects and spiders it cannot see into before it commits its bill to the gap.
  Long bill probing crevices among rocks: confirmed. Diet mainly insects; spiders occasional. 'Cannot see into before it commits' is embellishment; cut. [s6] [s7] [s5]
- **WRONG (framing)**: High-elevation and northern breeders largely withdraw south or lower for winter while milder populations stay, so the species is neither a true migrant nor a true resident.
  Audubon: 'strongly migratory, departing from northern part of range for the winter'; southern populations are permanent residents; winter birds use low-elevation rocky areas. That makes it a partial migrant: northern birds are true migrants. Rewrite as 'northern birds migrate south; southern birds stay all year'. [s7] [s5]
- **OK (needs precision)**: Males sing an unusually large and varied repertoire, cycling through many distinct phrases rather than repeating just one before switching, often in long bouts from an exposed perch.
  Repertoires of 69 to 119 syllables (All About Birds and Birds of the World via Wikipedia). Kroodsma 1975: each syllable is usually sung 3 to 4 times within a burst of 10 to 15 before moving on, so it does repeat before switching. Larger repertoires deter rivals better (Pitt 2018 thesis). Suggest: 'a male carries 69 to 119 song syllables and rarely stays on one for long'. [s5]
- **OK**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) claims.
  No star assignment from sign plus year on this page; the Purple Star block says charts are cast from lunar birth date and hour.
