## The Golden Snub-nosed Monkey as a symbol
- **Chinese, classical lexicon (Erya, c. 3rd century BCE; Shuowen Jiezi, 121 CE).** The Erya glosses the 蜼 wèi: 蜼卬鼻而長尾 (wèi áng bí ér cháng wěi), "the wei has an upturned nose and a long tail." The Shuowen says it is like a macaque, with upturned nose and long tail. The sinologist Robert van Gulik identified the wei as the golden snub-nosed monkey. So this animal sits in China's oldest dictionary, defined by the very face the live page leads with. [s5] [s6]
- **Chinese, Guo Pu commentary (4th century CE, quoted in the Kangxi entry).** Guo Pu describes the wei as monkey-like with an upturned nose and a tail four or five chi long, greyish-yellow; when it rains it hangs from a tree and plugs its nostrils with its tail, or with two fingers. Real old natural-history lore about the upturned nose, and a usable image: the animal that waits out weather it cannot change. [s6]
- **Chinese, Song dynasty sumptuary rule.** The 狨 róng, a long-haired golden monkey whose fur was highly prized (Luo identifies it as the golden snub-nosed monkey; Read suggested a gibbon), gave its hide to seats and bedding that under Song rule only civil and military officials of the third rank and above could use. Yang Yi's Tan Yuan calls it 金絲狨 jīnsī róng, golden-thread monkey, from Sichuan and Shaanxi. For a Leo: gold that marked rank. The identification is disputed; say "a golden monkey, most likely this one". [s5]
- **Chinese, ritual vessels (Zhouli).** The Zhouli lists 蜼彝 wèi yí, wei vessels, beside tiger vessels for libations: 祼用虎彝蜼彝 (guàn yòng hǔ yí wèi yí). Present only as "the wei appears on ancient ritual vessels"; I did not find a source tying it to specific virtues. [s6]
- **Chinese, natural-history chronicles.** Lu Dian's 11th-century encyclopedia describes a monkey with a fluffy golden tail in the mountains of Sichuan. Zhao Xumao and Paul Garber (Diversity and Distributions, 2018) used chronicles back to the Erya to show snub-nosed monkeys once ranged across eastern and central China and shrank to the western mountains. The cold-mountain life is partly a retreat, not only an origin. [s9]
- **Chinese, folk belief and naming.** Chinese Wikipedia records other names (仰鼻猴 yǎngbí hóu, upturned-nose monkey; 金線猴 jīnxiàn hóu; 金絲狨 jīnsī róng) and says some ethnic minority groups treat the animal as a 避邪 bìxié object that wards off evil; the groups are not named, so use lightly or not at all. The Latin name roxellana honors Roxelana, wife of Suleiman the Magnificent, for the monkey's beauty (trivia, never the hook). [s7]
- **Generic monkey lore (not specific to this species).** 猴 hóu puns on 侯 hóu, marquis; a monkey on a horse, 馬上封侯 mǎ shàng fēng hóu, wishes an immediate promotion. True of all monkeys, so use only as a small aside if at all. [s5]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Claims that Sun Wukong (孫悟空 Sūn Wùkōng) was modeled on the golden snub-nosed monkey, and blog "golden monkey spirit animal" meanings. No traditional or scholarly source located. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Fire (Leo, ruled by the Sun) meets yin Water (Pig). Leo wants the whole ridge warm and watching; the Pig wants real company and is generous to a fault. Water controls Fire, so the Pig half quietly limits how far the Leo light is spent. The animal gives the shape: a summer band of hundreds that the cold breaks back into troops of 60 to 70, and inside every troop a one-male unit. Big public warmth, small counted circle.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: warmth that makes strangers feel included; real calm in trouble (Pig, s4); loyalty that is generous once given (both halves); a knack for gathering crowds that stay friendly; a face that cannot fake it, which is why people trust it.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Pig is easily fooled (s4) and the Leo craves attention (s2), so this crossing can spend its warmth on the crowd and arrive home with little left. Leo pride hides the hurt. The Pig's weak hand with money can meet Leo's lavish gestures. The page's own proverb, 人怕出名豬怕壯 (rén pà chū míng, zhū pà zhuàng), fits: the brightest gold on the ridge draws the wrong attention too.
- **Love.** Our reading: Sun-ruled and yin. Loves out loud, with gifts and gatherings, but trusts slowly in private. Needs one partner who is plainly first, ahead of the crowd. The grooming study is the honest bridge: females groomed males more and traded it for access, and males groomed back less as their units grew (s11). The risk for this crossing is the same imbalance: the bigger the circle, the thinner the care at its center.
- **Money.** Our reading: China Highlights says the Pig is not good at managing wealth and warns it off finance jobs (s4); Leo spends to show love (s2). Likely pattern: generous host, vague budget. The fix is structure, not willpower: set aside the winter store before the summer gathering.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: slow to suspect, slow to fight, then hot (Leo temper). Prefers to let a big group split quietly rather than have a public row, the way the band breaks into smaller troops in winter. Watch for staying friendly with someone who has already shown their hand.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (generic, recommend change)**: Header hanzi 猴 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin hóu.
  猴 hóu means monkey, so it is not wrong, but it is not this animal's name. The species is 金絲猴 jīnsīhóu (golden-thread monkey; simplified 金丝猴), formally 川金絲猴 chuān jīnsīhóu, Sichuan golden snub-nosed monkey. Recommend 金絲猴 jīnsīhóu. The hero also shows 猪 (simplified); the traditional form is 豬 zhū. Pick one script sitewide. [s7] [s8]
- **OK**: Flattened nose with forward-facing nostrils; two flaps of skin at the wide nostrils rise into peaks that almost reach the forehead, leaving almost no bridge.
  ADW: "Two flaps of skin on the widely opened nostrils form peaks that almost touch the forehead." "Almost no bridge" paraphrases "flattened"; acceptable. [s10]
- **OK (soften coat)**: Golden-orange coat thickest through shoulders and back; among the very few primates living in a temperate zone.
  ADW: "among the few primates who live in temperate zones", 1,600 to 4,000 m. Wikipedia: withstands colder average temperatures than any other non-human primate. Coat: belly, forehead and neck are golden in both sexes; the back is greyish black (males) or brownish black (females), with long golden guard hairs over the back and cape in adult males. Say "long golden guard hairs over the shoulders" rather than a golden-orange back. [s10] [s8]
- **WRONG (superlative)**: One-male units merge with bachelor males into bands of up to 600 in warm months, an extraordinary size for any primate except humans, before cold splits them into subgroups of 60 to 70.
  600 and 60 to 70 are correct (ADW). The "except humans" superlative is wrong: mandrill hordes at Lopé averaged 620 and reached 845, described as possibly the largest cohesive groups of wild primates. Cut it or say "one of the largest groups any primate forms". [s10] [s13]
- **WRONG**: Qinling study: only about three in ten grooming exchanges between males and females were reciprocated; more grooming offered as a one-male unit grew larger; more access earned by whoever groomed most.
  Wei et al. 2012: 29.7% of ALL grooming bouts (855) were reciprocated, not male-female bouts specifically. Male reciprocation DECREASED as the number of females in the unit rose (rs = -0.85), the opposite of the page. Females groomed males more, and this tracked copulation rate (r = 0.76) and less food aggression (r = -0.63). Suggested fix: "About 30 percent of grooming bouts were returned in kind; females groomed the male more than he groomed them, and the more females a male had, the less he groomed back." [s11]
- **OK (minor)**: Warm months: broadleaf leaves, conifer needles, buds and fruit; winter narrows to bark and lichen; band descends to graze grass and wild onion.
  ADW: winter "bark and lichen"; descends "to feed on grasses and wild onions". Wikipedia: at Shennongjia mainly lichen November to April; at Qingmuchuan leaves, bark and buds in winter, dogwood fruit in summer. "Conifer needles" not found in either source; drop or source it. [s10] [s8]
- **OK**: Winter nights: band beds down in the lower canopy, not the exposed upper branches; one-male units closer together after dark.
  Zhang et al. 2011 abstract: "often slept in the lower stratum of the tree canopy, avoiding the upper canopy where it is cold and windy"; band "more spatially cohesive at night than in daytime, with shorter distances between one-male units." The study calls it the Sichuan snub-nosed monkey, the same species. [s12]
- **OK**: Element shown as Water.
  Pig fixed element is Water. [s3]
