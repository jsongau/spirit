## The Dragonfly as a symbol
- **Japan, samurai.** The dragonfly was a favourite samurai emblem, kachi-mushi, the 'victorious insect', admired because it approaches prey flying straight ahead and does not veer off course (The Met, 18th-century dragonfly helmet). Manggha adds that a dragonfly 'never moves back when flying', read as persistence in battle. This is the strongest bridge on the page: the warrior lore praises the exact thing the interception studies measure. Note the biology: dragonflies can fly backward; the lore is about the hunt, not the anatomy. [s7] [s8] [s6]
- **Japan, the name of the country.** The Kojiki records Akitsushima, read as 'Island of Dragonflies', as an early name of Japan (Manggha). Wikipedia frames it more cautiously ('may mean') and ties it to a legend in which a dragonfly eats an insect that bit the founder Emperor Jimmu. Use 'is often read as'. [s8] [s6]
- **China, classical poetry.** Yang Wanli (Song), 小池 Xiǎo Chí 'Little Pond': 小荷才露尖尖角，早有蜻蜓立上頭 (xiǎo hé cái lù jiān jiān jiǎo, zǎo yǒu qīngtíng lì shàng tóu), 'the young lotus has barely shown its sharp tip, and a dragonfly already stands on top of it.' A perch taken first, before anyone else has seen the bud. Du Fu (Tang), 曲江 Qūjiāng: 點水蜻蜓款款飛 (diǎn shuǐ qīngtíng kuǎnkuǎn fēi), 'touching the water, dragonflies fly unhurried.' The Beijing News reading: stillness and motion in balance, ease, lightness. [s9]
- **Navajo and Pueblo.** For the Navajo, dragonflies symbolize pure water; the dragonfly is a common motif in Zuni pottery, Hopi rock art and Pueblo necklaces (Wikipedia, citing its own references). Tell it as theirs, short, no 'you' reading laid over it. [s6]
- **Europe, folk names.** Europe often saw dragonflies as sinister: English 'devil's darning needle'; Swedish folklore says the devil uses dragonflies to weigh souls; Welsh gwas-y-neidr, 'adder's servant'. A useful shadow note: the watcher read as a judge. [s6]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'dragonfly spirit animal means change and illusion' lines from totem blogs, and Chinese 'dragonfly brings prosperity' feng shui claims. No traditional source opened. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Earth (Virgo, Mercury) meets yang Wood (Tiger). Virgo measures; the Tiger commits and does not back down. In the Dragonfly the two become one motion: the reading is done on the perch, and the strike comes from a decision already made. Wood controls Earth in the five-phase cycle, so the Tiger's push is what keeps Virgo's checking from becoming the whole life.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: an eye that picks the one thing worth chasing out of a busy field; follow-through once committed (the kachi-mushi that does not veer); calm under pressure; a track record people learn to trust.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the hover that does not end (Virgo perfectionism), then a sudden hard stop when someone pushes (Tiger stubbornness, 'rarely backs down'). The European folk names point at the other shadow: the watcher who becomes the judge, weighing people instead of meeting them.
- **Love.** Our reading: Mercury makes the Dragonfly attentive to small words and changes; the Tiger brings courage once the choice is made. Slow to open (Virgo), then direct and loyal. Needs a partner who does not take the quiet watching as coldness, and who will say when the waiting has gone on long enough.
- **Money.** Our reading: researches before buying, then buys decisively and holds. Risk is all-or-nothing timing: long stretches of waiting, then one big committed move. Works best with a written rule for when the research is finished.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: sees where the argument is going before it starts and may jump straight to the end of it. The Tiger half does not retreat once it has spoken. Practice: say the first observation while it is still small, before it becomes a verdict.

- **Air traffic control, flight operations, logistics dispatch.** Our reading: tracking many moving objects and calling the one that matters; the interception studies (Mischiati 2015) are literally about steering to where a target will be.
- **Quality assurance, auditing, fraud and anomaly detection.** Our reading: Virgo's eye for the flaw, with the Tiger's nerve to report it and stand by it.
- **Emergency medicine, triage, surgery.** Our reading: fast reading of a crowded scene, then one committed action.
- **Field ecology, wetland survey, water-quality work.** Our reading: the Navajo pure-water association and the dragonfly's own pond edge; patient watching with a clear purpose.
- **Sports analysis, scouting, competitive strategy.** Our reading: Tiger competitiveness plus Virgo measurement; predicting a trajectory is the job.
- **Investigative journalism or intelligence analysis.** Our reading: long quiet preparation, then a piece that lands once and does not need to be repeated.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 準 (rail__han and serial-han), glossed zhǔn
  準 zhǔn means accurate, standard, to permit (MDBG). It does not mean dragonfly; it looks chosen for the 'precision' theme. The animal's Chinese name is 蜻蜓 qīngtíng (same form in traditional and simplified). Replace with 蜻蜓 qīngtíng, or a single 蜓 tíng only if the design needs one glyph (less standard). [s15] [s14]
- **OK (soften)**: Hunting success about 95 percent, the highest of any studied predator; sharks about half, lions about one in four
  Combes lab trials on Libellula cyanea: 92.1% average over 3,000+ trials (SICB abstract); 'up to 95%' and 'more than 95 percent' in Wikipedia and the NYT summary. Say 'more than 90 percent in lab trials'. The 'highest of any studied predator' superlative and the shark and lion figures have no source on the page: UNVERIFIABLE, cut or source them. [s11] [s6] [s10]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Compound eyes cover roughly 97 percent of the visual field; a dragonfly can select and track one target within a swarm of thousands
  No source for 97%. Wikipedia: nearly 24,000 ommatidia per eye, complete vision in the frontal hemisphere, and elsewhere 'can see in 360 degrees' (internally inconsistent). Selective attention to one target is real (single-neuron work summarised in s10) but 'swarm of thousands' is an inflated number. Suggest: 'about 24,000 lenses per eye, and neurons that lock onto a single target and ignore the rest'. [s6] [s10]
- **OK**: Four wings that operate independently; flight in six directions plus sustained hover
  Wikipedia: can propel itself in six directions, hovers by counter-stroking, flies backward at three body lengths a second. 'Independently' is widely stated but not in the source I opened; 'each wing pair can beat out of phase' is safer. [s6]
- **OK (narrow it)**: Nymph stage lasts one to five years underwater; adult phase weeks to months
  Wikipedia: up to five years in large species, two months to three years in smaller ones; most adults live five weeks or less, some only days. Say 'from two months to five years' and 'usually a few weeks'. [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Adults spend more than 90 percent of their time perched and motionless (also the hero line 'motionless ninety percent of the time')
  No source found. It is also not true across the order: many dragonflies (darners, hawkers) hunt continuously on the wing, and Wikipedia says adults 'hunt on the wing'. Perching applies to skimmers such as Libellula, the genus in the capture-success studies. Narrow to 'many skimmers hunt from a perch' or cut the number. [s6]
- **WRONG**: Fossil record 325 million years ago with wing architecture nearly identical to modern species; intro: 'more than three hundred million years, wing architecture essentially unchanged'
  The 325-million-year fossils are Meganisoptera (griffenflies), which Wikipedia calls 'only distant relatives'. True dragonflies first appear in the Early Jurassic, roughly 200 to 175 million years ago. Rewrite: 'dragonfly-like griffenflies flew 325 million years ago; true dragonflies date to the Early Jurassic'. [s6]
- **WRONG**: Citation: 'Combes, S. A. et al. (2012). Dragonflies predict prey trajectory, PNAS 109(18)'
  I found no such PNAS paper. The 2012 Combes paper is 'Linking biomechanics and ecology through predator-prey interactions: flight performance of dragonflies and their prey', Journal of Experimental Biology 215:903. Replace the citation. [s12]
- **OK**: Citation: Mischiati, M. et al. (2015). Internal models direct dragonfly interception steering, Nature 517
  Confirmed: Nature 517 (7534), doi 10.1038/nature14045. This is the paper that supports the 'predicts where prey will be' claim. [s13]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Citation: Olberg, R. M. et al. (2000). Prey pursuit and interception in dragonflies, J Comp Physiol A
  Did not open a record this session. Widely cited and probably correct (vol. 186), but a person should check it before it stays. 
- **OK**: Proverb 磨刀不誤砍柴工 (mó dāo bù wù kǎn chái gōng), labeled 'our own reading, not tradition'
  Honest label; pinyin present. No source checked for the proverb itself; it is a common saying. 
- **OK**: Matches on page: Bongo (secret friend), Green Sea Turtle (same trine), Giant Centipede (same year)
  Pig-Tiger is a six harmony; Horse is in the Tiger trine (Wikipedia grid). Same-year pairing is the site's own rule, not a tradition. [s5]
- **OK**: Purple Star mentions
  The page only points to casting a chart from lunar birth date and hour. No star is assigned from the sign pair.
