## The Tibetan Blue Bear as a symbol
- **Tibetan and Himalayan (yeti lore).** The bear sits under the yeti legend. Swami Pranavananda derived the 'teh/ti' in yeti from Tibetan dred (spoken 'tre'), bear; Dzu-teh is glossed 'cattle bear'. Messner concluded the yeti is a bear. Two scraps of 'yeti fur' from Hillary's 1960 expedition were identified as blue bear pelt. Lindqvist et al. 2017 (Proc. R. Soc. B) found a femur from a 'yeti' in a Tibetan Plateau cave and a 1950s Nepal hair sample were Tibetan brown bear. The bear is the real animal behind a legend people preferred to the animal. [s6] [s7] [s8] [s9]
- **Tibetan Buddhism (yeti, via the same lore).** The yeti was adopted into Tibetan Buddhism as a nonhuman animal that can sometimes follow the Dharma; Bön held the blood of the mi rgod ('wild man') useful in ceremonies. This is yeti belief, not bear belief: use only as context for item 1, never as 'bear medicine'. [s7]
- **Chinese, Shijing (Book of Songs), ode Si Gan 斯干 Sī Gān.** A lucky dream of bears, 維熊維羆 wéi xióng wéi pí ('bears and grizzly bears'), is read by the diviner as 男子之祥 nánzǐ zhī xiáng, an omen of a son. Generic bears, not this subspecies: a bear in a dream as strength arriving. [s10]
- **Chinese common name.** In Chinese it is 藏馬熊 zàng mǎxióng (simplified 藏马熊), the Tibetan horse bear, or 西藏棕熊 Xīzàng zōngxióng; English sources also list 'horse bear'. Our reading: a Dog-year animal carrying the Horse in its name, and the Horse is in the Dog's trine. [s6] [s12] [s3]
- **Modern record.** Long known in the West only from a few furs and bones; 'one of the rarest subspecies of bear in the world, rarely sighted in the wild'. First documented evidence in India came from North Sikkim above 4,000 m in 2024. [s6] [s11]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Blue bear spirit animal' or 'blue bear totem' meanings: none found in any traditional source. The coat is not blue (see audit). (no source)

- **Conflict.** Our reading: climbs away and goes quiet rather than argues; returns only when the ground feels safe. Rarely starts a fight, defends its few people hard. The Dragon-year types who storm in loudly are the hardest for it.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK but generic**: Header hanzi 熊 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin xióng
  熊 xióng means 'bear' in general. Not wrong, but it does not name this animal. Specific traditional name: 藏馬熊 zàng mǎxióng (Tibetan horse bear); alternative 西藏棕熊 Xīzàng zōngxióng (Tibetan brown bear). Recommend 藏馬熊 if the rail allows three glyphs, otherwise keep 熊. [s12] [s6]
- **WRONG**: Intro: 'dark blue-grey in coat with a pale patch at the throat'
  Coat ranges from dark brown to creamy white, with lighter areas on head, neck and back in some individuals (s12); the distinctive mark is a 'yellowish scarf-like collar that broadens from the shoulders to the chest' (s11), not a throat patch. 'Blue' is an old Western name, not the coat colour. Also fixes the page's recurring 'pale mark at your throat' imagery: say collar across shoulders and chest. [s11] [s12]
- **OK (narrow), framing misleading**: Fact 1: a recent species-wide review of brown bear subspecies by the IBA names grizzly, Kodiak and tentative Gobi, and does not list the Tibetan population
  The IBA brown bear species profile names U. a. horribilis, U. a. middendorffi, northwest Eurasian U. a. arctos and a tentative Gobi population, and does not mention pruinosus (s13). But it is a species web profile, not a 'review of subspecies'. And U. a. pruinosus (Blyth 1854) is a named, widely used subspecies (s6). Rewrite as 'the IBA's brown bear profile does not mention it'. [s13] [s6]
- **OK**: Fact 2: brown bears range from sea level to about 5,000 m; Tibetan population at the uppermost edge, alpine meadows and scree above treeline
  IBA: 'any altitude between sea level and 5,000 m' (s13). Tibetan brown bear habitat: alpine forests, meadows and steppe above 4,000 m (s11). [s13] [s11]
- **OK (species-level)**: Fact 3: excellent smell over long distances, human-level hearing, comparatively poor eyesight
  ADW: excellent smell (carcass scent over two miles), human-level hearing, 'relatively poor eyesight' (s14). These are Ursus arctos generalities, not Tibetan-specific findings; say 'brown bears'. Bear eyesight being poor is contested in popular sources; keep 'relatively'. [s14]
- **OK**: Fact 4: winter dormancy lowers heart rate and metabolism, not true deep torpor, can be roused readily
  IBA: no deep torpor bouts typical of rodents; easily active if disturbed (s13). ADW: 'not true hibernation', 'aroused readily' (s14). Species-level, not studied in the Tibetan population. [s13] [s14]
- **OK**: Fact 5: largely solitary outside mating and mother-cub groups; overlapping home ranges
  ADW: generally solitary, only female-young bonds; home ranges overlap extensively, no territorial defence (s14). Species-level. [s14]
- **OK (species), Tibetan clause UNVERIFIABLE**: Fact 6: gall bladders sold on traditional medicine markets with no documented benefit; illegal trade a threat across the range, Tibetan Plateau included
  ADW supports the gall bladder trade and the lack of documented medicinal value (s14); IBA lists illegal trade in bear parts (s13). For the Tibetan population specifically, Wikipedia's bile-threat sentence carries 'citation needed' (s6). Drop 'the Tibetan Plateau included' or source it. [s14] [s13] [s6]
- **OK**: Hero: rarely photographed, real sightings still notable events
  'One of the rarest subspecies of bear in the world... rarely sighted in the wild' (s6, s11). [s6] [s11]
- **OK**: Crossing: January or early February birthdays can belong to the Rooster; Dog years 1994, 2006, 2018
  Applies to Capricorn (late Dec to 20 Jan). Rooster precedes Dog. 2018 Dog year ran 16 Feb 2018 to 4 Feb 2019 (s4). [s4] [s1]
- **OK**: Proverb 犬馬之勞 quǎn mǎ zhī láo, labelled as our own pairing
  Real idiom; the page honestly labels the pairing as its own. Note it also carries the Horse, the Dog's trine partner. 
- **OK**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) content
  No star is assigned from sign plus year on this page; it correctly says charts are cast from lunar birth date and hour.
