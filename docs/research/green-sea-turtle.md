## The Green Sea Turtle as a symbol
- **Chinese, Liezi (湯問 Tāng Wèn chapter).** When the five immortal mountains of the eastern sea kept drifting on the tides, the emperor had Yuqiang (禺彊 Yúqiáng) order fifteen giant sea turtles, 巨鼇 jù áo, to lift them on their heads, and the mountains held still. Primary line: 乃命禺彊使巨鼇十五舉首而戴之 (nǎi mìng Yúqiáng shǐ jù áo shíwǔ jǔ shǒu ér dài zhī). The ao is a sea turtle that carries a fixed place on its back through moving water. [t10] [t11]
- **Modern Chinese wordplay.** 海归 hǎiguī, 'returned from overseas', is a homophone pun on 海龟 hǎiguī, sea turtle. People who study or work abroad and come home are called sea turtles. This is the strongest fit on either page: a living Chinese word that already means the one who leaves for the ocean and comes back. [t12] [t13]
- **Japanese folk tale, Urashima Tarō.** A fisherman saves a sea turtle; it carries him to the Dragon Palace under the sea. When he goes home, his village and parents are gone: days below were three hundred years above. He opens the forbidden box and ages at once. A turtle tale about the cost of staying away too long. [t16]
- **Hawaiian, Ka'u, Punalu'u Bay.** Kauila, a mystical turtle who could take the form of a girl, watched over children playing at Punalu'u and kept a spring of fresh drinking water. The 1995 marker notes green turtles (honu) feed in the bay today. The marker does not say what species Kauila was, so do not call her a green turtle. [t17]
- **Hawaiian, Kumulipo creation chant.** Honu, the sea turtle, is born in the fourth wā of the Kumulipo, with lobster, lizards and jellyfish. [t18]
- **Chinese, Four Symbols.** The Black Tortoise, 玄武 Xuánwǔ, guards the North and winter, has affinities with water, and stands for long life. Use with care: it is a tortoise wound with a snake, not a sea turtle. Our reading only: the Horse is the noon and south of the wheel, so this crossing carries the North creature on a South-born animal. [t19] [t3]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'turtle spirit animal means patience, grounding, Mother Earth' lines and Turtle Island creation stories applied to sea turtles. Turtle Island stories concern turtles generally, not Chelonia mydas, and I did not source them here. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water under the Moon meets yang Fire. Cancer is the most homeward sign; the Horse is the most leave-taking animal. The Green Sea Turtle holds both by doing them in sequence: years of open current, then a straight line back to one beach. Water restrains Fire in the five phases, so home is the brake on the Horse's run, never its cage.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: a long memory for places and people; loyalty that survives silence; the nerve to go far alone; the ability to become someone new (carnivore young, grazer grown) and still be recognised at home.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Urashima's box. Staying gone past the point where return is easy, then arriving to find the village changed. Cancer's grudge plus the Horse's pride can turn a long absence into a reason never to explain it.
- **Love.** Our reading: Moon-ruled and devoted, yet Horse-free. Needs a partner who reads a long quiet as trust. Comes back fully, and expects to find the bond exactly as it was. Best with someone who keeps their own shore and their own sea.
- **Money.** Our reading: Cancer saves for the nest; the Horse spends on the trip. Does best with one untouchable home fund and a separate travel purse. Risk: big, sudden spends on freedom, then a careful rebuild.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: swims off rather than fights. Comes back later with the matter settled privately. Practice: say how long you will be gone before you go.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Marine and field science, expedition work, ranger and conservation posts.** Our reading: long seasons away, a return to base, and care for one protected place (Cancer); real distance and autonomy (Horse).
- **Foreign correspondent, international development, diplomatic postings.** Our reading: the 海归 hǎiguī pattern made into a job: years abroad, then bringing the whole of it home.
- **Midwifery, paediatric nursing, school counselling.** Our reading: Kauila the child guardian and Cancer's nurture, with the Horse's stamina for long shifts.
- **Genealogy, oral history, archives, heritage tourism.** Our reading: Cancer's pull toward the past and the turtle's return to its birthplace.
- **Long-cycle independent work: books, research, software you ship in one finished piece.** Our reading: disappears into the work and surfaces with something whole.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (borrowed species)**: Green turtles use geomagnetic imprinting on their natal beach to navigate back to nest, sometimes decades on (cites Current Biology 2015).
  The cited study (Brothers and Lohmann 2015) analysed 19 years of loggerhead nesting in Florida, not green turtles (t7). Natal-beach fidelity in green turtles is real (t8: 'often returning to the very beach where they were born'), but magnetic imprinting is a hypothesis with its best evidence in loggerheads. Rewrite: 'Green turtles often return to nest on the beach where they hatched; studies of loggerheads suggest they find it by its magnetic signature.' [t7] [t8]
- **OK (cut the superlative)**: Hatchlings and juveniles eat jellyfish, crustaceans and small fish, then shift almost entirely to seagrass and algae; one of very few reptiles to make that switch.
  Juveniles are carnivores in the oceanic stage for 3 to 5 years (t5); adults eat almost only seagrass, seaweed and algae, juveniles more omnivorous (t8). 'One of very few reptiles to make that switch' is UNVERIFIABLE; diet shifts with age are common in reptiles. Safer: adults are the only largely herbivorous sea turtle, but I could not open a source saying that either, so cut it. [t5] [t8]
- **OK**: Sex is set by sand temperature; warmer nests produce mostly females, cooler mostly males.
  Jensen et al. 2018: 'the proportion of female hatchlings increases with the incubation temperature'; northern Great Barrier Reef green turtles were 99.1% female as juveniles (t21). The page's own SWOT link could not be opened from here. [t21]
- **WRONG (inflated)**: Juveniles vanish into an offshore 'lost years' phase, often five to ten years, before returning to coastal waters.
  Reich et al. 2007 estimate 3 to 5 years in the oceanic stage (t5); Mongabay's report on Mansfield et al. 2021 says two or three years (t6). Use '3 to 5 years'. [t5] [t6]
- **OK (soften)**: About one in a thousand hatchlings survives to adulthood.
  The figure is a population-balance estimate, built mainly on loggerheads. Hays et al. 2026 call it a fair global average, with real rates from about 1 in 400 to 1 in 2,000 (t9). Say 'roughly 1 in 1,000, by a common estimate'. [t9]
- **OK**: Green sea turtles can live an estimated fifty to seventy years.
  BOEM: 'can live up to 70 years or more in the wild', mature at 25 to 35 (t8). NOAA page cited on the live page could not be opened from here. [t8]
- **OK but weak**: Header hanzi 龜 in the rail and serial mark.
  龜 guī means tortoise or turtle in general (t14), and the page shows it with no pinyin. MDBG also lists a colloquial insult sense ('cuckold'). Better: 海龜 hǎiguī, sea turtle (t13), which also carries the 海歸 'returnee' pun (t12). The species name is 綠蠵龜 lǜxīguī in Taiwan (t15). [t12] [t13] [t14] [t15]
- **WRONG (no traditional basis)**: Purple Star section: the Cancer Horse crossing 'keeps landing on' Tiān Jī 天機 and Tiān Liáng 天梁.
  Zi Wei Dou Shu stars are placed from the lunar birth date and hour, not sun sign plus year animal. The page does label it 'our own reading', but 'keeps landing on the same two' and 'Near the Life palace, Purple Star tends to place' read as claims about the tradition. Cut or relabel plainly. [t22]
- **OK**: Proverb 老馬識途 lǎo mǎ shí tú, the old horse knows the way.
  Recorded in the Han Feizi: Guan Zhong let old horses lead Duke Huan of Qi's lost army home (t20). Page correctly labels the pairing as its own. [t20]
