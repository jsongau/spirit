## The Wallcreeper as a symbol
- **Chinese (the bird's own name).** The standard name is 紅翅旋壁雀 hóng chì xuán bì què (simplified 红翅旋壁雀), literally 'red-winged, wall-circling sparrow'. Recorded folk names include 石花兒 shí huā'r, 'little stone flower', 爬樹鳥 pá shù niǎo, 'tree-climbing bird', and 爬岩樹 pá yán shù. The stone-flower name is the best single image for this page: a bloom of colour that opens on bare rock. The literal glosses are ours from the characters. [s9] [s10]
- **Ancient Greek (scientific name).** Tichodroma means 'wall runner', from the Greek for wall and runner; muraria repeats the wall in Latin. Linnaeus first filed it with the treecreepers as Certhia muraria (1766). [s5] [s9]
- **French birding culture.** The bird is so often compared to a butterfly that a French film about it is titled 'L'Oiseau Papillon' ('the butterfly bird', Frank Neveu), and an English Pyrenees guide calls it the 'butterfly of the cliffs'. Treat this as a modern birders' nickname, not folklore. The page's exact phrase 'butterfly of the crags' was not found in any source. [s12] [s13]
- **Spain, 2025.** SEO/BirdLife's public vote named the wallcreeper Spain's Bird of the Year 2025 (37% of nearly 6,000 votes) to draw attention to it; Spain holds roughly 1,200 to 1,800 birds, mostly in the Picos de Europa and central Pyrenees, and climate change is named the main threat. [s11]
- **Europe (modern civic symbol).** The European Central Bank's Governing Council shortlisted a wallcreeper beside a mountain landscape as a possible front motif for the redesigned 5 euro banknote. [s5]
- **Hungarian name.** Its Hungarian name is Hajnalmadár; hajnal means dawn, so 'dawn bird'. No source found for why it carries that name, so use only the plain name, not a story. [s8] [s9]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** No Alpine, Himalayan, Tibetan or Chinese folk tale or spirit-animal meaning for the wallcreeper was found in any reliable source. Totem-blog meanings, if they appear, are invented. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Fire (Aries) meets yin Earth (Goat). In the five phases Fire feeds Earth, so the Aries push is what keeps the Goat's ground from going cold. Aries wants to go first and alone; the Goat wants beauty and a safe ledge to come back to. The Wallcreeper does both at once: it takes the steepest, least wanted rock, and it works that rock with an unexpected flash of red. A stone flower.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: nerve for exposed, unglamorous work; a precise, delicate touch inside rough conditions; self-sufficiency that does not need applause; loyalty to a chosen patch, returned to year after year.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the colour shows only while working, so rest feels like disappearing. Aries impatience plus Goat privacy can harden into isolation that looks like independence. The Goat's stubbornness can make the wall a habit long after it stopped being the right wall.
- **Love.** Our reading: Mars-ruled and yin. Shows love by doing the hard practical thing, rarely by saying it. Needs a partner who notices the red in the wing and does not demand a constant show of it. The Horse (secret friend) and Air signs give it room to fly off and come back.
- **Money.** Our reading: earns through a narrow, hard-won skill few others can do; spends little on display. Risk is underpricing work nobody else wanted to take on.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: holds its ground, like the winter territory both sexes defend alone. Quick Aries flare, then Goat withdrawal to its own ledge. Practice: name the boundary before defending it.

2. Give you the crack in the rock that everyone else flew past and you will work it all winter, alone, and come back to the same stone next year.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Forages almost entirely on sheer, near-vertical cliff faces and gorge walls, moving in short hops and flutters rather than settling to feed.
  Wikipedia: moves across cliff faces in short flights and quick hops, wings often partly spread; Naturalis: searches the shade of vertical cliffs. Add that in winter it also works buildings and quarries, and sometimes sallies for flying insects. [s5] [s7]
- **OK (wing-flick) / UNVERIFIABLE (nickname wording)**: Flicks its wings half-open in near-constant motion, exposing crimson-and-black patches otherwise hidden against grey plumage; nicknamed 'the butterfly of the crags'.
  Constant wing-flicking: Naturalis and Britannica. 'Otherwise hidden' slightly overstates; some red shows on the closed wing. The exact phrase 'butterfly of the crags' was not found anywhere. Sourced alternatives: 'butterfly of the cliffs' (Hike Pyrenees blog) or the French film title 'L'Oiseau Papillon'. Use one of those or drop the quote marks. [s6] [s7] [s12] [s13]
- **OK (with softening)**: Slender, downward-curved bill probes cracks in bare rock for insects and spiders, a food source almost no other bird in its range can reach; only living member of the family Tichodromidae.
  Long, thin, down-curved bill and insect and spider diet: Britannica, Wikipedia. Sole living member of Tichodromidae: Wikipedia, but note some authorities (Vaurie; Britannica) treat it as a subfamily of the nuthatches, Sittidae; a 2016 study puts it as sister to Sittidae. 'Almost no other bird can reach' is UNVERIFIABLE; cut it. [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: Largely solitary outside breeding; males and females each defend an individual feeding territory; birds return to the same winter stretch of rock in consecutive years.
  Wikipedia states all three, including that females sing mainly while defending winter territories (a usable detail). [s5] [s13]
- **WRONG (winter floor) / incomplete (breeding range)**: Breeds at roughly 1,000 to 3,000 m at or above the treeline, and descends no further than about 400 to 800 m even in winter.
  1,000 to 3,000 m is the European figure only; it breeds at 2,800 to 4,000 m in the Tien Shan and 3,600 to 5,100 m in the Himalaya. The winter claim is false: birds regularly winter on cathedrals and viaducts in Brittany and Normandy, have wintered in England and the Netherlands (one spent two winters, 1989 to 1991, on a Vrije Universiteit building in Amsterdam), and visit Bangladesh. Britannica: in winter it sometimes descends to lowlands and climbs city buildings. Rewrite: 'in winter many move down to foothills, and a few turn up on lowland cathedrals and quarries'. [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: Adult measures about 15.5 to 17 cm and weighs 15 to 20 g.
  Length 15.5 to 17 cm (Wikipedia); 16 to 17 cm and 15 to 20 g (MME BirdLife Hungary). Wikipedia gives a narrower 17 to 19 g, inside the page's range. 'Rock faces hundreds of metres unbroken' is scene-setting, not a cited fact. [s5] [s8]
- **WRONG (generic, not the bird's name)**: Header hanzi (rail__han and serial-han): 雀 què.
  雀 què alone means sparrow (especially the Eurasian tree sparrow) or any small bird, and in Cantonese slang it is also vulgar. It is only the last character of the wallcreeper's name. Correct traditional name: 紅翅旋壁雀 hóng chì xuán bì què (simplified 红翅旋壁雀). If the slot fits only a short form, use 旋壁雀 xuán bì què, 'wall-circling bird'. 石花兒 shí huā'r ('stone flower') is a recorded folk name and could appear in copy with pinyin, but not as the header. [s9] [s10] [s14]
- **WRONG (no traditional basis)**: Purple Star section: 'read for the Aries Goat crossing it keeps landing on the same two' stars, 七殺 Qī Shā and 武曲 Wǔ Qū.
  Zi Wei Dou Shu stars are placed from the lunar birth date and hour, not the Western sign. The page does add a disclaimer lower down ('Zodi Animal's own reading ... not a traditional one'), but the lead sentence says the system itself 'keeps landing' on these stars, which it does not. Relabel the whole block as our own pairing from the first line, or cut it (MOJO review item 3). [s15]
