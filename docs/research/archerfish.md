## The Archerfish as a symbol
- **Chinese, classical (Guliang Zhuan commentary by Fan Ning, Jin dynasty).** The 蜮 yù, also called 短狐 duǎnhú or 射工 shègōng, is a creature in the water said to hold sand in its mouth and shoot it at people: 蜮，短狐也，蓋含沙射人. From it comes the idiom 含沙射影 hán shā shè yǐng, 'hold sand and shoot at the shadow', meaning to attack someone by insinuation. It is a myth, not a description of the archerfish, but it is the Chinese imagination of a hidden water-shooter, and the idiom maps exactly onto the Virgo critic's shadow. Use with that framing. [s5] [s6]
- **Chinese name.** 射水魚 shèshuǐyú, 'water-shooting fish', is the Chinese name of Toxotes jaculatrix (genus 射水魚屬 shèshuǐyú shǔ). [s7]
- **European natural history (18th to 19th century).** After Schlosser's report to the Royal Society, the shooting trick was pinned on the wrong fish, the copperband butterflyfish (Chelmon rostratus). Philip Henry Gosse repeated the mistake in 1857 and even put a spitting butterflyfish on his book cover. The real shooter's work was credited to a prettier fish for decades. A story about misattribution, which suits a sign that cares who did what. [s8]
- **Scientific name.** Toxotes is usually glossed as 'archer' and jaculatrix as 'dart thrower'; the species was described by Pallas in 1767 (FishBase lists the authority). I did not open a dictionary source for the Greek and Latin glosses, so check before use. [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** No Indigenous Australian, Thai or Malay folklore about the archerfish could be found in any source I could open. Spirit-animal meanings for the archerfish on blogs have no traditional source.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (not the animal's name)**: Header hanzi 射 (rail__han and serial-han).
  射 shè means 'to shoot'. It is part of the name but does not mean archerfish. The Chinese name is 射水魚 shèshuǐyú, 'water-shooting fish' (traditional; simplified 射水鱼). If the design needs one glyph, keep 射 but label it 'shoot, from 射水魚 shèshuǐyú'; otherwise use the full three characters with pinyin. [s7]
- **OK (first half); UNVERIFIABLE (second half)**: Toxotes jaculatrix compensates for refraction when targeting prey, a perceptual correction most vertebrates cannot perform.
  Refraction compensation is supported by FishBase and Wikipedia (s10, s9). 'Most vertebrates cannot perform' has no source; plunge-hunting birds also catch prey through the water surface, and little egrets showed no loss of success from refraction (Lotem et al. 1991, s16). Cut the comparison. [s10] [s9] [s16]
- **WRONG (mechanism)**: The jet varies in volume and hydrodynamics; the fish concentrates impact by rotating the stream as it leaves the mouth, adjusting for distance and size.
  The jet is not rotated. The fish modulates the jet's velocity so the rear travels faster than the front and the water gathers into one blob just before impact (Vailati et al. 2012, s12; Wikipedia s9). Scaling shot force to prey size is the subject of the paper the page cites (Schlegel et al. 2006, 'matched to prey adhesion'); keep that, replace 'rotating'. [s12] [s9]
- **PARTLY OK; details UNVERIFIABLE**: Schuster et al. (2006): naive archerfish acquire the correct shooting angle by observing experienced individuals, correcting their aim within a single session of watching, not through trial and error.
  Fish that had failed at moving targets watched an expert shoot and could then hit moving targets when retested (Nautilus account of Schuster's work, s13; Wikipedia s9). The task was hitting moving targets, not a 'shooting angle', and I could not open the abstract to confirm 'single session'. Rewrite as: 'fish that watched a skilled shooter could then hit moving targets they had failed at before'. [s13] [s9]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (threshold)**: When prey is within about 20 cm of the surface, archerfish abandon the jet and leap.
  Archerfish do leap for prey within reach; Shih et al. 2017 measured jumps up to about 2.5 body lengths (s14, s9, s11). No source I opened gives a 20 cm switch point. Say 'when prey hangs within reach, it jumps instead of shooting'. [s14] [s9] [s11]
- **OK (soften 'full marine')**: Lives in estuaries, mangroves and freshwater rivers across Southeast Asia and northern Australia, tolerating fresh water to full marine salinity.
  FishBase: freshwater and brackish, mainly mangrove estuaries, also rivers and streams, reported on reefs near overhanging vegetation; India east to the Philippines, south to Indonesia, Vanuatu, Solomons, New Guinea, northern Australia (s10). ADW: fresh water to salt water (s11). Add India and the Pacific islands; 'brackish to near-marine' is safer than 'full marine'. [s10] [s11]
- **WRONG (as framed)**: When several archerfish see the same prey, the one that first identifies and shoots takes precedence; others orient to the first shot rather than re-targeting.
  No source gives the shooter precedence. The opposite risk is documented: other archerfish steal the dislodged prey (kleptoparasitism), the shooter's success depends on the competition present when it shoots (der Weduwen et al. 2024, s15), and archerfish change their shooting when others are visible, probably because of that theft risk (Wikipedia s9). Group members do race to where the prey will land (Nautilus, s13). Rewrite around the race and the theft. [s15] [s9] [s13]
- **OK**: Purple Star section.
  Unlike the elephant page, this page assigns no star and says a full chart is cast from the lunar birth date and hour. Nothing to fix. [s17]
- **OK as labeled**: Proverb 精益求精 jīng yì qiú jīng, tied to the Year of the Dragon.
  Labeled as our own pairing, not tradition. The page's gloss uses an em dash and an 'is not a destination, it is' shape; fix both in the rewrite.
