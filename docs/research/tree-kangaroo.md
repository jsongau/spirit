## The Tree Kangaroo as a symbol
- **Moni people, Western New Guinea (Dingiso, Dendrolagus mbaiso).** When Tim Flannery's team described this mostly ground-living tree kangaroo in 1995 they named it mbaiso, which means 'the forbidden animal' in Moni. Many Moni hold it to be an ancestor that must never be harmed, and the species stays common in the west because of that protection. Tell this as the Moni's belief, about that one species, and keep it out of the 'you' voice. [s10]
- **Dyirbal and Yidiny (Wet Tropics, Queensland).** Mabi is the Dyirbal and Yidiny name for Lumholtz's tree-kangaroo, and Mabi forest, a rare Atherton Tableland rainforest type, carries that name. [s11]
- **Yopno, Uruwa and Som communities, Huon Peninsula, Papua New Guinea.** In 2009 the YUS Conservation Area, named for three rivers, became Papua New Guinea's first conservation area; people from 35 villages pledged land on which hunting, logging and mining are banned, largely for the Matschie's tree-kangaroo. [s12] [s9]
- **Torricelli Mountains, Papua New Guinea.** From 1999 the Tenkile Conservation Alliance persuaded 13 villages to stop hunting the Tenkile (Dendrolagus scottae), an animal that had been a common source of meat; the alliance is named after it. [s13]
- **Greek-Latin scientific name.** Dendrolagus means 'tree hare'. Chinese calls it 樹袋鼠 shù dàishǔ, literally 'tree pouch-rat', since 袋鼠 dàishǔ (kangaroo) is 'pouch rat'. Gloss of the characters is ours. [s7] [s15]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** No general 'tree kangaroo spirit animal' meaning and no Pig-year folklore link were found in a reliable source. No PNG clan story beyond the Moni dingiso belief was sourced. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Fire (Sagittarius) meets yin Water (Pig). Water checks Fire, so the explorer's urge is cooled by the Pig's appetite for comfort and home. The Tree Kangaroo is a ground animal's lineage that went back up into the canopy: a far-ranging spirit that chose one high, leafy home and settled into it. Jupiter's wide horizon plus the Pig's love of rest gives a seeker who travels for meaning, then sleeps long in the branches it found.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: courage to reverse a path when the reasoning says so; generosity and warmth; a talent for surviving falls and climbing back; a big view from a quiet perch.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Pig's trust plus Sagittarian optimism can mean leaping before checking the branch. Long rest can slide into hiding, the way a shy tree kangaroo crashes away through the leaves rather than be seen. Restlessness can make it leave a good tree too soon.
- **Love.** Our reading: generous, affectionate, loyal once settled; needs real freedom and a partner who does not read a solo wander as rejection. Not a sharer of personal space: these animals keep their own home range, so love works best with a clear 'my tree, your tree' and lots of visiting.
- **Money.** Our reading: the Pig is traditionally loose with money and Jupiter likes to expand, so spending on travel, food and learning comes easily. Strength: generosity that builds goodwill. Risk: no reserve for the drop.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: blunt at first (Sagittarius), then soft and forgiving (Pig). Rarely holds a grudge, but will hold a boundary around its own patch.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (with one UNVERIFIABLE detail)**: Descended from arboreal possum-like ancestors that came to the ground, then returned to the trees, developing grip pads, longer forelegs and rotating ankles.
  Ancestry and return: Wikipedia (rainforest-floor ancestor descended from an arboreal, possum-like animal) and AWS ('macropods used to dwell in trees ... came down'). Grip pads: ADW. 'Rotating ankles' is UNVERIFIABLE; the sourced trait is that, unlike other kangaroos, they can move paired hindlimbs independently in trees (ADW). Swap it in. [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **WRONG (hind feet, as worded)**: Re-evolved shorter hind feet, longer forelegs and padded soles, less efficient on the ground than their relatives.
  Wikipedia and the Australian Wildlife Society both say tree-kangaroos have LONGER and broader hind feet with long curved nails compared with ground kangaroos. ADW says the hindlimbs are smaller relative to body size and the forelimbs and tail proportionally larger. Safe wording: 'broad hind feet with long curved claws, smaller hind legs, stronger forelimbs and spongy gripping soles'. Clumsier on the ground is fair (ADW: on the ground they only hop). [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **OK (tail clause UNVERIFIABLE)**: Can drop sixty feet (eighteen metres) to the ground without apparent injury, using the tail for balance during descent.
  AWS and Bennett's tree-kangaroo article: drops of about 18 m without injury; Bennett's can also leap 9 m down to another branch. Wikipedia marks the 18 m line as needing a citation, so attribute it to AWS. The long tail helps balance in the trees (Wikipedia); its use 'during descent' is not stated anywhere I found. Cut that clause. [s6] [s14] [s5]
- **OK (understated)**: Young are born extremely underdeveloped and complete development in the pouch over several months.
  Pouch life is long: 246 to 275 days (Wikipedia), about nine months (AWS), about a year in Lumholtz's (ADW). 'Several months' undersells it; say 'about nine months to a year'. One joey a year (Wikipedia). [s5] [s6] [s7]
- **WRONG**: Large home ranges that overlap significantly between individuals, particularly among females; not territorial in the fighting sense.
  Reversed. In Lumholtz's tree-kangaroo, female ranges do not overlap with other females (ADW: about 2 ha; JCU Rainforest CRC: 'exclusive and nonoverlapping ranges of around 0.7 ha'); it is MALE ranges that overlap several females and males. JCU calls them 'highly territorial', and Matschie's tree-kangaroos defend and mark territory boundaries (Wikipedia). ADW does say Lumholtz's do not appear to defend territories, so sources split on fighting, but the female-overlap claim is wrong in every source. [s7] [s8] [s9]
- **OK (species-specific)**: Sleep up to fifteen hours a day in the canopy.
  Wikipedia on Matschie's tree-kangaroo: 14 to 15 hours a day sleeping and resting. ADW on Lumholtz's: inactive (resting or alert) about 90% of the time, over 99% of time in trees. Say 'sleep and rest' and name the species, or use the ADW figure. [s9] [s7]
- **OK**: Header hanzi (rail__han and serial-han): 樹袋鼠, with pinyin shùdàishǔ on the serial.
  樹袋鼠 shù dàishǔ (simplified 树袋鼠) is the standard Chinese name; the genus is 樹袋鼠屬. Minor: the page mixes traditional 樹袋鼠 with simplified 猪 in the hero; pick one script per page. Pinyin should be spaced shù dàishǔ. [s15]
- **OK**: Purple Star section.
  This page names no star; it says a full chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. No overreach. [s16]
