## The Roadrunner as a symbol
- **Hopi and other Pueblo peoples.** Roadrunners were medicine birds that guard against evil spirits. The X-shaped track, two toes forward and two back, hides which way the bird went and was used as a sacred sign to confuse evil. Pueblo people of New Mexico drew the track near the dead to mislead spirits following the departed soul (TPWD). Roadrunner feathers decorated cradleboards to protect babies. [s3] [s4] [s5]
- **Zuni.** The Zuni have a Roadrunner Clan, Poye-kwe. Secondary source only; confirm with a Zuni or academic source before printing the clan name. [s5]
- **Tarahumara (Rarámuri) and Plains peoples.** Some Tarahumara believed eating roadrunner meat gave running skill; Plains peoples hung its skin over lodge doors against evil (TPWD). Strong, sourced bridge to Sagittarius speed. Present as recorded belief, attributed. [s4]
- **Tohono O'odham / Akimel O'odham (Pima).** In O'odham tradition the roadrunner (taḏai) is credited with bringing fire to the people; among the Pima, seeing one is good luck. Fire-bringer is a striking fit for a Fire sign, but tell it as theirs, with no 'you' reading laid over it. [s3]
- **Maya (Ch'orti' and Ch'ol).** The Ch'orti' keep taboos against harming the bird; the Ch'ol call it ajkumtz'u' and believe its call makes listeners tired. Mexican folk belief also had it bringing babies, as the stork did in Europe. [s3] [s2]
- **Frontier and state lore.** Anglo frontier belief that a roadrunner leads lost travelers to a trail; names chaparral cock, ground cuckoo, paisano; state bird of New Mexico. Usable as color; the cartoon is commodity. [s2] [s4]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** The Apache 'Leader of the Birds' and Maya 'roadrunner gives its plumage to Quetzal' stories appear only as one-line listings on a secondary site; no text located. Also generic 'roadrunner spirit animal' meanings from totem blogs. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Fire (Sagittarius) meets yin Metal (Rooster). Sagittarius wants open country and the fastest line through it; the Rooster wants order, routine and a sharp eye on detail. The roadrunner holds both: up to about 32 km/h on the ground (s2), yet patrolling the same half-mile territory with a pair bond kept for life (s1). Fire checks Metal in the five-phase cycle, so the speed keeps the precision from going stiff, and the precision keeps the speed from scattering.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: nerve around what others avoid (it takes small rattlesnakes, s4), fast decisions on known ground, honesty that lands, a morning ritual that sets up the whole day, loyalty to one partner and one place.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Sagittarian bluntness sharpened by Rooster pride. The crest goes up and the orange skin behind the eye shows when it feels threatened (s1); you are readable before you mean to be. Also over-specialization: brilliant on home ground, impatient off it.
- **Love.** Our reading: lifelong pairs that renew the bond each spring with courtship steps and calls (s1), and split the work, with the male taking the night shift on the eggs (s3). Love here is a working partnership with a little ceremony kept alive every year.
- **Money.** Our reading: Rooster bookkeeping plus Sagittarian appetite for a big swing. Good at knowing exactly what is on its patch; risk is impulsive spending on the next horizon.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: direct and fast. Circles, dodges, then strikes (s4). Rarely sulks; says the thing. Practice: check whether the threat is real before the crest goes up.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 走鵑 (rail__han and serial-han), voiced zǒujuān
  走鵑 zǒujuān ('running cuckoo') is the Chinese name of the greater roadrunner; Chinese Wikipedia lists 走鹃 (simplified) for Geococcyx californianus, also called 大走鵑 dà zǒujuān (traditional form on Oiseaux.net). Pinyin is present in the aria label; make sure it is visible next to the glyph. [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: Rarely becomes airborne; can run over 30 km/h
  Up to 32 km/h (20 mph); reports up to 42 km/h (Wikipedia). Flight limited to short bursts and glides (Cornell). TPWD gives a lower clocked figure of about 15 mph, so 'up to about 30 km/h' is the safe line. The sentence 'Flight is slower on the ground it occupies' is meaningless; cut it. [s2] [s1] [s4]
- **PARTLY WRONG**: Regularly preys on rattlesnakes and other venomous snakes, holding the head and slamming the body on a rock, then swallowing headfirst
  Sources say small or young rattlesnakes (TPWD, Wikipedia), not 'regularly' venomous snakes in general. Cornell: kills rattlesnakes by pecking the head repeatedly; it beats larger prey such as lizards and rodents against a rock. TPWD describes stabbing then slamming snakes. 'Headfirst' unsourced. Rewrite: 'takes small rattlesnakes, dodging strikes, then stabbing and beating them against the ground'. [s1] [s4] [s2]
- **OK**: Exposes a patch of dark skin between the shoulder blades to the morning sun to warm up
  Cornell: faces the sun and raises back and wing feathers to show heat-absorbing black skin; Wikipedia: may hold the posture two or three hours early in the morning. 'Costs no calories' is our gloss; soften to 'saves energy'. Wikipedia also says it drops its body temperature from 40 to 34 C at night, cutting energy use by more than 30 percent. [s1] [s2]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (wording)**: Crest rises automatically, an involuntary signal of alarm, excitement or arousal
  Cornell: raises its crest and shows an orange skin patch behind the eye when threatened or displaying to a rival. 'Involuntary' is not supported; say 'raises its crest when threatened or displaying'. [s1]
- **OK**: Pairs monogamous and territorial year-round; one bird incubates by day, one during the cooler night
  Cornell: lifelong bonds renewed each spring. Wikipedia (Roadrunner): monogamous, mate for life, may hold territory all year; both sexes incubate, males at night. Incubation 19 to 20 days. [s1] [s3]
- **WRONG**: Permanent territories of up to one square mile, announced by a low cooing call and scent marking
  Cornell: territories up to about half a mile in diameter (roughly 0.2 square miles), not one square mile. Cooing call from a perch is right. Scent marking is unsourced and very unlikely for a bird; cut it. [s1] [s2]
- **OK**: 'The partner it keeps for life' (intro and Awakened section)
  Cornell: lifelong pair bonds. [s1]
- **OK (labeled)**: Proverb 一步一個腳印 (yī bù yī gè jiǎoyìn), one step one footprint, tied to the Rooster year as our own pairing
  Common modern idiom; the page honestly labels the pairing as its own. Good image tie: Pueblo lore about the roadrunner's X track (s4, s5) would make the footprint link sourced rather than generic. [s4]
