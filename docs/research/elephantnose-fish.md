## The Elephantnose Fish as a symbol
- **Ancient Egypt (relative, not this species).** The Oxyrhynchus fish, a downturned-snout elephantfish of the genus Mormyrus in the same family as the elephantnose, was sacred at Per-Medjed, which the Greeks named Oxyrhynchus, 'sharp-nosed'. In the Osiris myth it ate the one part of Osiris that Isis never recovered. It was linked to Hathor and shown wearing her horned sun-disc crown; bronze figures with rings were worn as amulets. Use only if the page says plainly that this is a cousin fish, not Gnathonemus. [s11] [s12]
- **Chinese name.** FishBase lists the Mandarin names 彼氏錐頜象鼻魚 (bǐ shì zhuī hé xiàng bí yú, 'Peters' awl-jaw elephant-nose fish') and 鸛嘴長頜魚 (guàn zuǐ cháng hé yú, 'stork-beak long-jaw fish'). The short everyday form is 象鼻魚 xiàng bí yú, 'elephant-nose fish'. Note the Chinese name, like the English, also mistakes the chin for a nose. [s13]
- **Chinese (the page's own proverb).** 心有靈犀一點通 xīn yǒu líng xī yì diǎn tōng is a line from Li Shangyin's Tang poem 無題 (Untitled), not a folk proverb. 靈犀 líng xī is the rhinoceros horn said to carry a white thread linking its two ends, later a figure for wordless mutual understanding. A fitting image for a fish that reads others through an unseen channel; the pairing stays labeled as ours. [s14]
- **Science as image (bridge, not folklore).** The fish does not rely on electricity alone: its cones sit in reflective photonic-crystal cups that help it see in muddy, dim water (Kreysing et al. 2012, Science). Two senses tuned for the murk. [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** No West or Central African folklore, totem or spirit-animal tradition for the elephantnose fish was found in any source I could open. Spirit-animal meanings on totem blogs are unsourced. (no source)

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 电 (rail__han and serial-han), shown with pinyin diàn
  电 diàn (traditional 電) means electricity, not this animal. The species' Mandarin name per FishBase is 彼氏錐頜象鼻魚 bǐ shì zhuī hé xiàng bí yú; the everyday traditional form is 象鼻魚 xiàng bí yú, 'elephant-nose fish'. Use 象鼻魚 xiàngbíyú. [s13]
- **WRONG**: Intro: navigates 'through an organ in the elongated lower jaw that is often mistaken for a nose'
  The electric organ that makes the field is in the tail. The chin extension (Schnauzenorgan) is a receptor covered in electroreceptors, not the generating organ. Fact card 01 gets this right; the intro sentence does not. [s5]
- **WRONG (one word)**: Fact 01: generates a weak field with an electric organ, reads distortions; the 'trunk' (chin) is the primary receptor; navigates, finds prey and communicates 'entirely' through this medium
  Field, receptor and uses are OK per Wikipedia. 'Entirely' is wrong: the fish also sees, with retinal photonic-crystal cups that improve vision in turbid water (Kreysing et al. 2012). Drop 'entirely'. [s5] [s10]
- **WRONG**: Fact 02: highest brain-to-body-weight ratio of any non-human vertebrate, about 3.1% vs about 2% in humans; cerebellum dedicated almost entirely to electric signals
  3.1% is right (Nilsson 1996, via Guinness). The superlative is wrong: tree shrews and small birds have higher brain-to-body ratios (Wikipedia). The real record is energy: the brain uses over half the body's oxygen (Guinness; about 0.6 per Wikipedia). 'Almost entirely' electric for the cerebellum is UNVERIFIABLE. Rewrite as the oxygen record. [s6] [s7] [s5]
- **WRONG**: Fact 03: distinguishes objects by shape and electrical conductivity simultaneously, a dual-channel integration no other known animal performs
  Telling resistive from capacitive objects is real, but von der Emde and Ringer 1992 found all four mormyrid species tested could do it. Drop 'no other known animal'. Shape recognition is likely real (von der Emde and Fetz 2007) but I could not open that paper. [s8]
- **OK (soften)**: Fact 04: discharge rate varies from about 1 per second to more than 80 per second, firing faster when the field is more complex
  Grant et al. 1999 give a mormyrid range from under 1 Hz to over 100 Hz, and mention novelty responses. The range is understated, not wrong. 'Fires faster when more ambiguous' is a simplification; say 'speeds up when something new appears'. [s9]
- **WRONG**: Fact 05: Gnathonemus petersii is non-aggressive
  Wikipedia: unless kept in a very large tank it is aggressively territorial toward its own species; keepers are told to keep only one. It is not a danger to other species, which is the true part. [s5]
- **WRONG (last clause)**: Fact 06: turbid rivers and lakes of West and Central Africa; visibility in centimeters; sight effectively useless
  Range OK (lower Niger, Ogun, upper Chari; muddy slow rivers per Wikipedia). 'Sight is effectively useless' is contradicted by Kreysing et al. 2012, which shows a retina built for turbid water. Intro also says 'West Africa' only; use 'West and Central Africa'. [s5] [s10]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Cited source: Szabo, T. et al. (2004), The mormyrid cerebellum: Functional anatomy and electrosensory processing, J Comp Physiol A
  No paper with this title found. Likely a fabricated citation; remove unless a human locates it. 
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Cited source: Amey-Özel, M. et al. (2015), More neurons but not larger brains..., Frontiers in Neuroanatomy
  No paper with this title found. Amey-Özel did publish on the Schnauzenorgan, but not under this title as far as I could find. Likely fabricated; remove. 
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Cited source: von der Emde and Fetz (2007), Distance, shape and more, J Exp Biol
  Plausibly real (J Exp Biol 2007) but I could not open it; full title is longer than shown. Check before keeping. 
- **OK (relabel)**: Proverb card: 心有灵犀一点通 labeled 'The proverb of your year', gloss of 犀 as rhinoceros horn said to respond to spirit
  Gloss matches Zdic. It is a line of Li Shangyin's Tang poem 無題, not a folk proverb; credit the poet. The page already says the Monkey pairing is ours. [s14]
