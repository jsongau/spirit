## The Shoebill as a symbol
- **Uganda (Lake Victoria fishing communities).** A Uganda Wildlife Authority officer reports that fishermen believe meeting a shoebill while setting nets means a very small catch that night, and some kill the birds for it. The stillness read as a bad omen. Handle with care on the page: it is a conservation threat, not a charm. [s9]
- **Arabic / Sudan.** Abu markub, 'father of the shoe'. The scientific name Balaeniceps rex reads as 'whale-headed king'. A king's name for the Capricorn (authority) animal. Note: name trivia must not be the hook line. [s10] [s7]
- **Chinese name.** 鲸头鹳 jīngtóuguàn, 'whale-head stork'. Chinese Wikipedia notes it now sits in its own family (鲸头鹳科) and is closer to pelicans than to storks; captive birds are nicknamed the 'husky of birds'. Zambian name Mapumambao glossed as 'the one who knocks on wood' (single source, zh Wikipedia; treat as unconfirmed). [s11]
- **Chinese (Ox half).** Yu the Great was said to throw iron oxen (buffalo) into rivers and lakes to hold back floods; the custom became setting iron ox statues beside water as guardians. An Ox standing guard at the water's edge is the closest Chinese image to the shoebill in its swamp. [s6]
- **Chinese (Ox half).** 孺子牛 rú zǐ niú, the willing ox that serves children (Lu Xun's self-description): quiet service. 执牛耳 zhí niú ěr, 'hold the ox's ear', from an oath ritual: to hold leadership. Service and authority together, very Capricorn. [s6]
- **Ancient Egypt.** Wikipedia says the shoebill 'may have been known' to the ancient Egyptians (Houlihan 1988). Too weak to build on; mention only as 'may'. [s7]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Shoebill spirit animal' meanings and the claim that zoo shoebills bow to keepers as a greeting. Neither Wikipedia nor ADW mentions bowing; I found no source I could open. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Earth (Capricorn, Saturn) meets yin Earth (Ox). Same element twice, so nothing feeds or checks it from inside. Capricorn is the sea-goat, half land half water; the shoebill is a land bird that lives standing in water. The Ox guards the water like Yu's iron oxen. The result is the guardian who waits at the edge of the swamp: Saturn's patience plus the Ox's endurance.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: stillness that is work (磨刀不误砍柴工), one decisive act after long preparation, loyalty to one territory and one partner, the ability to thrive in conditions others avoid.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: Capricorn pessimism plus Ox stubbornness. Waiting becomes the whole plan; distance gets called loyalty; poor communication (Ox, s4) means others read the stare as threat or absence, as the fishermen read the bird as bad luck.
- **Love.** Our reading: Capricorn is devoted but slow to open (s2); the Ox values long relationships (s4). Courtship is proximity held for a long time. The bill-clatter greeting sounds like a warning to outsiders and like hello to the mate.
- **Money.** Our reading: the most conservative money in the wheel. Long holds, real estate and land (both in the Ox career list, s4), no speculation. Risk: holding a position after it stopped paying.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: no threat display, a held stare (ADW, s8). Silent first, then one blunt statement. Afterward needs time to find its footing, like the bird after the collapse.

## Careers
- **Research, long builds, engineering.** Our reading: long wind-ups reward the stand-and-wait hunter; engineering and mechanics are in the Ox list (s4).
- **Real estate, land, agriculture.** Our reading: Earth doubled; China Highlights lists real estate and agriculture for the Ox (s4).
- **Wetland conservation, fisheries, water management.** Our reading: Yu's iron ox by the water (s6) and the bird's own habitat.
- **Negotiation, law, audit.** Our reading: the held stare and one decisive move; Capricorn discipline (s2).
- **Leadership in slow institutions.** Our reading: 执牛耳 zhí niú ěr (s6) and Saturn rule; authority earned by staying.

## Candidate lines
1. You can stand in the papyrus long enough for the lungfish to forget you, and you count that hour as work done, not time lost.
2. You get one strike, then you have to find your feet again in the mud. That is why you will not move for anything short of certain.
3. When someone walks up to what you are guarding, you neither hiss nor leave. You hold their eyes until they decide the visit is over.
4. The water you pick is the water other hunters skip, thin on air and slow. Sooner or later what you want has to come up there to breathe.
5. Some fishermen on the lake call you bad luck for the nets. Stillness this complete unsettles anyone who came for a quick haul.
6. At the nest your hello is a clatter of bill on bill. To outsiders it sounds like a warning. The one you chose heard it as a greeting from the first season.
