## The Deep Sea Anglerfish as a symbol
- **Science history (Iceland and Britain, 1922 to 1925).** For a century every deep-sea anglerfish caught was female. In 1922 Icelandic biologist Bjarni Saemundsson found two small fish fixed to a female's belly and took them for her young. C. Tate Regan dissected one and showed it was her mate, 'merely an appendage of the female' (Regan, Proc. Royal Society, 1925). The hidden partner who was there all along is a usable image for this crossing. [s11] [s12]
- **Modern genetics (Swann et al. 2020, Science).** Species with permanent attachment have largely lost the MHC genes the immune system uses to recognise foreign tissue, and killer T cell function is greatly reduced. They gave up the tools of rejection so a mate could become part of the body. Strong bridge to the Scorpio love reading. [s7]
- **Modern microbiology (Baker et al. 2019, eLife).** The glowing bacteria are Candidatus Enterovibrio escacola (and Ca. E. luxaltus in Cryptopsaras). They are picked up from the surrounding seawater, not passed from parent to young; the fish grows its lure only as it nears maturity and descends to bathypelagic depth. The light is gathered from the deep, at the point of growing up. [s6]
- **Japan (shallow-water relative, the ankō monkfish, Lophius).** Not a deep-sea species, but the same order. A winter delicacy in Ibaraki, where the saying sets 'blowfish of the West, anglerfish of the East'; the fish is cut while hanging (tsurushi-giri) and every part but the bones is eaten. Use only with the species named honestly. [s15]
- **Japanese language.** デジタル大辞泉 records 鮟鱇 (ankō) used figuratively for a foolish person, from the fish's sluggish movement; slang also uses it for 'mouth', from its huge jaw. Stillness read from outside as dullness, which the deep-sea reading can answer. Again this is the Lophius relative. [s16]
- **Chinese (the page's proverb).** 厚積薄發 hòu jī bó fā, 'store thickly, release thinly', from Su Shi's essay 稼說送張琥 (Jià shuō sòng Zhāng Hǔ): 博觀而約取，厚積而薄發 (bó guān ér yuē qǔ, hòu jī ér bó fā). Verified; the page's use stands. [s18]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Anglerfish spirit animal' and totem meanings (illumination, hidden truth, patience) found only on spirit-animal blogs. No traditional or Indigenous lore exists for ceratioid anglerfishes, which were unknown to science until the 19th century and live below any fishery. Pig folklore (Zhu Bajie, the Great Race) belongs on the year hub, not here. (no source)

- **Conflict.** Our reading: goes still and dark rather than loud. Scorpio remembers; the Pig forgives more than you would expect, but only once. When it does strike it is fast and final, like jaws closing on prey that came too near.

| Header hanzi 光 (guāng) in rail__han and serial-han | **WRONG** | 光 means 'light', not anglerfish. Anglerfish is 鮟鱇 ānkāng; the deep-sea anglerfish is 深海鮟鱇 shēnhǎi ānkāng (TaiBIF uses 深海鮟鱇 as the common name for Ceratias holboelli, 霍氏角鮟鱇). Use 深海鮟鱇 with pinyin, or 鮟鱇 if space is tight. | [s17] |
| The esca's light comes from symbiotic bacteria, primarily Photobacterium mandapamensis, living in the lure tip; the fish does not make the light itself | **WRONG** | Bacterial light is right; the species is wrong. Deep-sea anglerfish symbionts are Candidatus Enterovibrio escacola (most genera sampled) and Ca. Enterovibrio luxaltus (Cryptopsaras couesii), acquired from seawater (Baker et al. 2019, eLife). P. mandapamensis does not appear in that study; it is a Photobacterium known from other, shallow-water light-organ fishes (our recollection, not checked here, so do not print it). | [s6] |
| Hunts by remaining nearly motionless, using passive drag to hold depth while the lure dangles; documented in Pietsch (2009) | **OK** | Ambush hunting and passive floating are supported (Wikipedia, Grzimek). 'Passive drag to maintain depth' is odd wording and was not found in an opened source; Pietsch 2009 (Oceanic Anglerfishes, UC Press) is a real book but its page was not checked. Keep the stillness, drop the drag mechanism. | [s13] [s14] |
| In ceratioid anglerfishes the male is roughly 6 to 7% of the female's body mass; he bites in, circulatory systems fuse, body degenerates except testes (Pietsch 2005) | **WRONG** | Overgeneralised. Pietsch 2005 finds permanent attachment in only 5 of 11 ceratioid families, 10 of 35 genera, 23 of 160 species; other species use facultative parasitism or temporary non-parasitic attachment, and Live Science quotes Pietsch that 'many other ceratioid anglerfish are nonparasitic'. The 6 to 7% mass figure is unsourced; Photocorynus spiniceps males are 6.2 mm against a 46 mm female (length), and Grzimek gives females 3 to 13 times male length. Rewrite as 'in some species'. | [s8] [s9] [s10] [s14] |
| Live in mesopelagic and bathypelagic zones, 200 to 2,000 m; pressure 20 to 200 atmospheres; 2 to 4 C | **OK** | Zones confirmed (Grzimek). The range is narrow: the seadevil Cryptopsaras couesii is recorded from 75 to 4,000 m, most often 500 to 1,250 m. Pressure follows from depth (about 1 atm per 10 m). The temperature figure was not checked against a source; drop it or soften. | [s14] |
| Can control the movement of the esca to mimic smaller prey | **OK** | Wikipedia: anglerfish 'can wiggle the esca to make it resemble a prey animal'; Grzimek describes the illicium thrashing the bait. | [s13] [s14] |
| After fusion the male loses his eyes, digestive system and most internal organs; only the gonads remain; the female's immune system does not reject him | **WRONG** | Partly right. No rejection is supported: permanently attaching species largely lack MHC genes and have reduced killer T cell function (Swann et al. 2020). The organ claim is overstated: popular accounts say eyes, fins and some internal organs atrophy (Mental Floss), Pietsch says the exact nature of fusion has never been studied, and it applies only to the minority of species with permanent attachment. 'Only the gonads remain' is not supported; say the male becomes largely a sperm source fed by her blood. | [s7] [s9] [s11] |
| Proverb section: 'The bacteria accumulate for years in the esca' | **UNVERIFIABLE** | No source found for a years-long accumulation; the eLife study says symbionts are acquired from the environment as the fish matures. The proverb itself (厚積薄發, Su Shi) is verified. Cut the bacterial timeline. | [s6] [s18] |

Say 'deep-sea anglerfishes (suborder Ceratioidei)'. Depth caveat as above. | [s14] |
