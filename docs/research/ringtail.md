## The Ringtail as a symbol
- **Nahuatl (central Mexico).** In Mexico the ringtail is called cacomixtle, from Nahuatl tlahcomiztli: tlahco 'half' plus miztli 'cougar, wild cat', so roughly 'half mountain lion'. Miztli is attested in Molina's 1571 dictionary and in Sahagun. Dictionary.com applies the word to Bassariscus astutus. Note: cacomistle is also the name of B. sumichrasti, which has its own Zodi page (Gemini Pig); keep the two pages from sharing this item word for word. [s9] [s10] [s8] [s4]
- **Mexico, popular name.** Texas Monthly reports the ringtail is sometimes called mico de noche, 'night monkey', in Mexico. A real folk name that ties the animal to the Monkey half of this crossing. One secondary source only; attribute it. [s8]
- **American Southwest mining camps (1800s).** Settlers and prospectors kept ringtails around cabins and camps as companions and mousers, which gave the name miner's cat. The Desert Museum notes the name is a misnomer: the ringtail is a raccoon relative, not a cat. [s4] [s6] [s8]
- **Arizona (state symbol).** The ringtail has been Arizona's official state mammal since 1986 (Desert Museum: August 1986). ADOT later worked it into the background of the Arizona driver license as a security feature and ran a contest to nickname it. [s6] [s13]
- **Greek scientific name.** Bassariscus astutus breaks down as bassar 'fox', isc 'little', astut 'cunning': the cunning little fox. ADW also notes the genus is sometimes called a living fossil because living species are hard to tell from Neogene forms. Use only as a bridge line, never as the hook. [s4]
- **Indigenous North American traditions.** Unsourced, do not use. Searches found no ringtail story or totem meaning from a named nation with a traceable source. Do not invent one. (no source)
- **Generic 'ringtail spirit animal' meanings.** Unsourced, do not use. Only blog and generated-encyclopedia content found. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Earth (Taurus) under yang Metal (Monkey). Earth feeds Metal, so the Taurus steadiness becomes the ground the Monkey's cleverness stands on. The result is a quick mind that likes a home base: a canyon animal that keeps one den range for years and still solves every ledge fresh. Taurus wants to stay; the Monkey wants the next problem. The ringtail does both by living in rock that is all problem.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: grace under pressure in tight, vertical places; a knack for turning a fall into a controlled descent; practical cleverness that earns its keep (the miner's cat hunting the camp's mice); self-sufficiency in lean seasons; a sense of humor that stays mostly private.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the half-something problem the Nahuatl name hints at. Half cougar, half cat, never fully either; hard to pin down, slow to belong. Taurus stubbornness plus Monkey restlessness can make someone who will not leave and will not fully settle. Monkey slyness can read as untrustworthy to people who only meet the night shift.
- **Love.** Our reading: Venus rules Taurus, so this crossing is tactile and loyal once it chooses; the Monkey keeps it playful and a little elusive. Shows care through useful acts at odd hours rather than speeches. Needs a partner who lets it keep its own den and does not read solitude as rejection.
- **Money.** Our reading: lean-season thinking. The Taurus half saves; the Monkey half spots the clever angle. Good at living on little and making resources stretch, the way a desert ringtail gets much of its water from food. Risk: under-asking for pay because getting by feels normal.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids the head-on fight and changes the angle instead. Will hold ground on its own territory (Taurus) and outmaneuver rather than overpower (Monkey). When cornered it leaves a sharp signal, then disappears.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 貍 (rail__han and serial-han)
  貍 lí is a variant of 狸, glossed 'fox; raccoon dog; fox-like animal'. It does not name the ringtail, which is a raccoon relative. The Chinese name is 蓬尾浣熊 péngwěi huànxióng ('bushy-tailed raccoon'; same in traditional), also 環尾貓 huánwěi māo ('ring-tailed cat'). Suggest 蓬尾浣熊 with pinyin, or drop the glyph. Also check the cacomistle page, which may use the same glyph. [s14] [s15]
- **WRONG**: The hind ankle can rotate more than 180 degrees, keeping pads and claws on the surface in a headfirst descent.
  Overstated. ADW, the Desert Museum, University of Arizona Extension and Texas Monthly all say the hind foot rotates 180 degrees. Only Wikipedia says 'over 180'. Say 'can turn its hind feet around 180 degrees'. The cited Liu et al. 2016 paper gives no degree figure in its abstract and concluded that no special muscles are needed for foot reversal. [s4] [s6] [s7] [s8] [s5] [s11]
- **OK**: Tail ringed in fourteen to sixteen bands, about body length, a counterbalance; can reverse direction by cartwheeling on a slim foothold.
  14 to 16 rings: UA Extension and Wikipedia. Tail about body length: ADW gives head-body 305 to 420 mm and tail 310 to 441 mm. The cartwheel turn appears only in Wikipedia; treat as secondary and keep it soft or cut it. [s7] [s5] [s4]
- **WRONG**: Ringtails stem up narrow crevices by pressing their feet against opposite walls, or ricochet between walls in wider gaps.
  Mechanism wrong. Chimney stemming as described by the Desert Museum and Wikipedia is all four feet on one wall and the back against the other, not feet on opposite walls. Ricocheting between walls is correct (ADW, Desert Museum). [s6] [s5] [s4]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: In the 19th-century mining boom, ringtails moved into cabins and mine shafts to hunt rodents; miners sometimes left them a warm box near the stove, hence miner's cat; never domesticated.
  Core is OK: settlers and prospectors kept ringtails as companions and mousers, hence miner's cat (ADW, Texas Monthly, Desert Museum). The warm box near the stove detail was not found in any source I opened; cut it. Sources say miners kept them, which is closer to pets than to animals that 'moved in'. [s4] [s8] [s6]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Arid-habitat ringtails can survive largely on water from food, aided by urine more concentrated than almost any other mammal studied.
  Only Wikipedia states it ('more concentrated than any other mammal studied') and I could not open a primary study. UA Extension only says prickly pear and other fruit 'may reduce its need for water'. Soften to that, or cut until a primary source is found. [s5] [s7]
- **WRONG**: Strictly nocturnal, largely solitary outside the spring breeding season; communicates through scent marks at shared latrine sites.
  'Strictly' is wrong: ADW says activity is mostly at night and occasionally at dusk; ADOT says almost entirely nocturnal. Solitary except when mating: OK (ADW). Latrines: OK, Barja and List 2006 found ringtails reuse latrines of 2 to 19 faeces, placed non-randomly on raised objects and road margins (Mexico City study). 'Shared' latrines is not stated in the abstract; say 'reused'. [s4] [s13] [s12]
- **WRONG**: Purple Star section: the Taurus Monkey crossing 'keeps landing' on 天府 Tiān Fǔ in the Life palace.
  No traditional basis. Zi Wei Dou Shu charts are cast from the lunar birth date and hour, not from Western sun sign plus year animal. Relabel plainly as our own image or cut (see REVIEW-mojo section 0.3). [s16]
- **OK**: Proverb 山高皇帝遠 shān gāo huángdì yuǎn tied to the Monkey year
  Labelled on the page as our own pairing, not tradition. Honest as written. Traditional characters carry pinyin.
