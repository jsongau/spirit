## The Moose as a symbol
- **Evenk (Siberia).** In the Evenk cosmic hunt the great elk-moose Kheglen (also Keglun) steals the sun at the autumn equinox; the hunter Main (in the oldest version the bear-man Mangi) chases it across the sky and kills it at the spring equinox, restoring the sun. Kheglen is the Big Dipper, the Milky Way is the hunters' ski tracks, and the story was acted out in spring rites; the shared 'kill' mirrors the nimat custom of a hunter sharing his catch. The best fit on the wheel: a Fire (sun) year animal whose real crown falls in winter and returns in spring. [s15]
- **Finnish (Kalevala).** Rune XIII: Louhi, mistress of Pohjola, will give her daughter to Lemminkäinen only if he catches the moose of Hiisi. The demon's moose is built from the forest: head of punk-wood, horns of bare willow branches, legs of reeds, skin of fir bark. He chases it on new skis, catches it, loses it, and breaks his skis, spears and staff. The moose as the quest that cannot be owned. [s18]
- **Anishinaabe (Ojibwe) clans.** Mooz (moose) is a clan in the hoof group of the Anishinaabe clan system; Wikipedia states 'the moose are mediators and exemplify peace', citing Flocken 2013 (ambiguous citation). Use as 'Wikipedia, citing Flocken' or confirm with an Anishinaabe source before printing. [s16]
- **Anishinaabe star knowledge (as told by an Anishinaabe artist).** Mooz is also an autumn constellation: the Great Square of Pegasus is its body and faint Lacerta its antlers, high in the sky in the hunting season. Hunters offered tobacco and hung the moose's bell in a tree to honor its spirit. Quoted saying: 'when there are no more moozoog, there will be no more Anishinaabeg.' Source is a personal blog by Zhaawano Giizhik; attribute it to him by name. [s17]
- **Chinese and Manchu (names).** Chinese name 駝鹿 tuólù, 'camel deer'. Other names 堪達罕 kāndáhǎn, 犴達罕 hāndáhǎn and 犴 hān all come from Manchu (zh.wikipedia). The live page's 鹿 lù is the generic word for deer. [s19]
- **Algonquian (word origin).** English 'moose' entered by 1606 from the Algonquian languages; Proto-Algonquian *mo·swa. Trivia only; Wikipedia gives no gloss, and the common 'twig eater' gloss was not verified. Do not use as a hook. [s6]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Moose spirit animal means self-esteem / headstrong wisdom' claims on totem sites; no traditional source located. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Earth (Taurus, Venus) meets yang Fire (Horse). Fire feeds Earth in the five-phase generating cycle: the Horse's heat becomes the Taurus building season. Taurus wants to hold one place; the Horse wants open ground and will not be penned. The Moose resolves it by holding a wide range alone. It builds the largest crown of any deer at up to an inch a day, wears it through one autumn, and lets it go. In Evenk story the moose carries the sun away at the autumn equinox and the sun comes back with spring, which is exactly the season of the antler.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: building at scale without an audience, stamina in hard ground (wades and dives where other deer stop), reading danger by ear and nose, independence that does not need permission.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Horse's stubbornness hardened by fixed Earth. Disappears into the willows after a big effort and calls it self-sufficiency. Lets a finished thing fall without telling anyone it was finished. Pushes on into deep snow a little past what is wise.
- **Love.** Our reading: Venus makes it sensual and generous, the Horse makes it free-ranging. Courts in a single intense season, then needs space that a partner may read as withdrawal. Best with someone who has their own range and enjoys the reunion more than the daily check-in.
- **Money.** Our reading: Taurus accumulates, the Horse spends on movement and freedom. Makes big seasonal investments (the yearly crown) and is willing to write them off when the season ends. Risk: rebuilding from zero each time instead of carrying anything forward.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids conflict by distance and size, not by talk. Goes silent and moves off; if cornered, the Horse's temper and the Taurus refusal to yield make it immovable. Practice: say what is ending before you walk into the trees.

2. When the lake looks closed to everyone else, you wade in to the shoulders and put your head under for what grows on the bottom.
3. You hear trouble long before you see it, one ear turned each way, and you trust the sound over the view.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (generic deer, not moose)**: Header hanzi 鹿 (rail__han and serial-han) with pinyin lù.
  鹿 lù means deer in general. The moose is 駝鹿 tuólù (traditional; simplified 驼鹿), also 犴 hān or 堪達罕 kāndáhǎn from Manchu. Do not use 麋鹿 mílù, which is Père David's deer. Suggest 駝鹿 tuólù. [s19]
- **OK**: Largest antlers of any living deer, grown from bare bone each spring, up to roughly 2 cm a day, among the fastest tissue growth in any mammal.
  Wikipedia: moose antlers average over 30 cm higher than the next-largest deer (wapiti). NPS and Idaho Fish and Game: up to about an inch a day; Guinness lists deer antlers as the fastest-growing mammalian tissue at 2.5 cm a day, using the moose as its example. '2 cm' is conservative; 'about an inch (2.5 cm)' is the sourced figure. [s6] [s7] [s8] [s14]
- **OK**: After the rut a testosterone drop weakens the antler-skull joint until the rack falls, usually by early winter; bare pedicles regrow the next spring.
  USFWS: shorter days lower testosterone, which weakens the antler-skull connection. ADF&G: mature bulls shed as early as November, mostly December and January. Minnesota DNR: growth begins in April. 'Early winter' fine; 'by midwinter' more exact. [s9] [s12] [s11]
- **OK (understated)**: Strong swimmers that submerge completely and dive several meters to graze lake-bottom plants; one of the few deer species known to feed this way.
  Wikipedia: known to dive over 5.5 m (18 ft) and 'the only deer that are capable of feeding underwater'; nostrils close under water pressure. Change 'one of the few deer species' to 'the only deer'. Minnesota DNR: dives to the bottom of shallow ponds and lakes. [s6] [s11]
- **WRONG (overstated)**: Long legs and tall shoulder height let moose wade through snow up to roughly a meter deep and water past the belly that would stop most other deer.
  Snow studies cited in the 2023 Eur J Wildl Res paper: movement impeded at about 70 cm and almost ceasing beyond 90 cm (Kelsall and Prescott 1967); 60 to 70 cm impedes Eurasian moose and 100 cm is critical (Nasimovich 1955); over 90 cm can raise mortality (Coady 1974). A metre is the danger line, not a depth moose wade through. Say 'moves through snow up to about 70 cm before it slows them'. Shoulder height 1.4 to 2.1 m (Wikipedia), tallest of any deer: OK. Water past the belly: plausible, not specifically sourced. [s13] [s6]
- **OK**: Poor eyesight but excellent hearing and smell; large ears rotate independently to track sound from any direction.
  Minnesota DNR: 'rather poor eyesight, but acute senses of smell and hearing'. NYSDEC: nearsighted; 'can move each ear and each eye independently'. 'From any direction' is unsourced phrasing; drop it. Wikipedia adds that the palmate antler can act as a parabolic reflector for sound, a stronger and sourced bridge. [s11] [s10] [s6]
- **OK (soften)**: Unlike most deer, largely solitary outside the mother-calf bond and the rut, holding wide individual ranges.
  Wikipedia: unlike most other deer, moose do not form herds and are solitary. NYSDEC and Idaho: several may gather near water, and winter 'yards' form when food is scarce. 'Wide individual ranges' not sourced here; drop or source. [s6] [s10] [s14]
- **WRONG (no traditional basis)**: Purple Star section: 'Purple Star tends to seat the vault star (天府 Tiānfǔ) close to the Life palace, 命宮, for a Taurus born in the Horse year', with 破軍 Pòjūn close by.
  Zi Wei Dou Shu places stars from lunar birth date and hour; it does not seat Tiānfǔ or Pòjūn by Western sun sign and year animal. The body text states it as what 'Purple Star tends to' do, which the disclaimer at the end contradicts. Relabel the whole block as our own reading or cut it. Also 命宮 appears with no pinyin (mìnggōng). [s20]
