## The Leafcutter Ant as a symbol
- **K'iche' Maya (Popol Vuh).** Set an impossible task by the lords of Xibalba, the Hero Twins send leafcutter ants to bring back flowers from the lords' own guarded gardens; the guardian birds never notice the ants trimming them. Small, many, unnoticed, and the ones who win the test. Source here is a Wikipedia summary; check a translation (Christenson) before quoting. [s5]
- **Guatemala (zompopos, local name).** Leafcutters are called zompopos; Hellmuth notes the Popol Vuh also has them cutting birds' tail feathers. Use only as the local name. [s6]
- **Guane and Santander, Colombia.** Queens of Atta laevigata (hormigas culonas) eaten since pre-Columbian times by the Guane; gathered in the rainy-season nuptial flight, around May. A sauce made from them (ommai) is a traditional wedding gift believed to be an aphrodisiac. [s7]
- **Chinese proverb.** 千里之堤，潰於蟻穴 qiān lǐ zhī dī, kuì yú yǐ xué: a thousand-li dike is breached by an ant hole; small carelessness, huge damage. Here the ant is the threat, which suits the colony's own obsession with keeping rot out of the garden. Traditional attribution to Han Feizi not confirmed from a source I could open; present without the attribution. [s8]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Brazilian saúva sayings ('Pouca saúde e muita saúva', the Saint-Hilaire line) could not be opened at a source; Proverbs 6:6 'Go to the ant' page blocked and concerns ants generally. Generic 'ant spirit animal means teamwork and patience' blog claims. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Earth (Virgo) and yang Water (Rat). In the five-phase cycle Earth checks Water: Virgo banks the Rat's quick current into channels. The leafcutter is that channel made real: a trail, a chamber, a garden, millions of quick bodies held to one plan.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: tests before trusting and remembers the result for weeks; keeps rot away from what matters; carries more than its share; builds routes others follow without asking; feeds people it will never meet.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: eats last and calls it virtue; treats every small leak as the ant hole in the dike; Rat impatience turned into scolding whoever slows the column; can quarantine people as coldly as waste.
- **Love.** Our reading: Mercury talk and Rat wit up front, a slow test underneath. Takes the leaf home before deciding, and the verdict may come hours later. Loves through service: the garden runs, the partner may not notice who runs it.
- **Money.** Our reading: Rat thrift plus Virgo budgeting. Builds reserves, spreads the harvest over many sources, avoids a single big bet.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: removes the problem rather than shouting at it; withdraws trust quietly and does not easily restore it, the way a rejected plant stays rejected for weeks.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 蟻 (rail__han and serial-han)
  蟻 yǐ means ant (traditional form of 蚁), so it is the right animal, but generic. The leafcutter's own name is 切葉蟻 qiēyèyǐ (Atta and Acromyrmex). Keep 蟻 yǐ if one glyph is needed; add pinyin. [s15] [s8]
- **WRONG**: Intro: colonies of up to eight million workers, farming a fungus that no member of the colony has ever eaten directly.
  Adults feed on fluid from the gongylidia (fungal swellings) and on plant sap; larvae eat the fungus. 'No member has ever eaten it' is false. Size: SDZWA gives more than 5 million workers per queen; 'eight million' not confirmed in a source I opened, so say 'millions'. [s9] [s11]
- **OK**: Ants do not eat the leaves; they farm Leucoagaricus gongylophorus; the mutualism is obligate.
  Wikipedia: L. gongylophorus is the most commonly documented cultivar of higher attines; mutualism obligatory. Note the 'do not eat the leaves' point is fine, but adults do take leaf sap. [s11]
- **WRONG**: Before cutting, a worker presses its forelegs on the leaf and antennal receptors sample for compounds that would harm the fungus; failed leaves are abandoned.
  Rejection of leaves harmful to the fungus is learned after the fact: workers first accept the material and stop collecting it about 10 hours after it reaches the garden; the avoidance lasts at least 9 weeks (Herz et al. 2008). The foreleg-press test is not supported by anything I found. [s12] [s11]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Trails extend more than 200 m; workers lay pheromone; they rotate trees to prevent over-harvesting any single source.
  Trail length and pheromone marking OK (SDZWA: some trails longer than 500 m, in use for months or years). The rotation 'to prevent over-harvesting' is an intent claim with no source found; cut it. [s10]
- **WRONG**: Workers carry fragments up to 50 times their own body weight; mediae cut and carry, smaller workers tend the garden.
  Measured maximum in Atta cephalotes is 8.78 times body mass (JEB 2019, vol. 222, jeb199240), close to a predicted 6.9. '50 times' is a popular figure repeated on Wikipedia without a study. Caste split is broadly right. [s13] [s11]
- **OK**: Minims ride on leaf fragments and defend carriers against parasitic phorid flies.
  SDZWA: minims riding on leaves help deter flies that lay eggs in ants' heads. [s10] [s11]
- **WRONG**: Dedicated waste chambers away from the garden, often vented; waste workers separated from garden tenders until they have groomed; contaminated material removed within hours.
  Chambers and separation are right (Hart and Ratnieks 2001: internal heap in A. cephalotes; waste workers hardly ever leave it; contaminated workers meet aggression). There is no groom-then-return protocol: they stay out. 'Vented' and 'within hours' unsourced. Note A. colombica uses an external dump. [s14] [s10] [s11]
- **WRONG**: Sources list: 'IUCN Red List · Atta cephalotes'
  SDZWA lists leafcutter ants as not assessed by IUCN. An IUCN citation for a species without an assessment looks fabricated; remove it. [s9]
- **WRONG**: Style: em dashes in the 'Our reading' lines (for example in the leaf-testing 'Our reading' line), and 'not X, it is Y' shapes
  Breaks the house writing rules; rewrite in the rewrite pass. [s16]
