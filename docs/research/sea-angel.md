## The Sea Angel as a symbol
- **Japan, Hokkaido drift ice.** クリオネ kurione is called 流氷の天使 ryūhyō no tenshi, angel of the drift ice, or 氷の妖精 kōri no yōsei, ice fairy; it arrives with the Okhotsk drift ice, a symbol of the Hokkaido winter, and its wild feeding has also earned it a devil-like reputation. It is a star exhibit at the Okhotsk Ryuhyo Museum in Abashiri. Species caveat: the Okhotsk animal is now assigned to Clione elegantissima, not C. limacina, so frame this as the sea angel of Japan, not this exact species. [s11] [s12] [s13] [s7]
- **Greek (scientific name).** The genus Clione is named for Cleio (Clio), a sea nymph in Greek myth; limacina is Latin for slug-like. The group name Gymnosomata means naked body, for the lost shell. [s7] [s8]
- **Chinese (names).** The species is 裸海蝶 luǒ hǎi dié, naked sea butterfly; 海天使 hǎi tiānshǐ, sea angel, is the wider descriptive term used for it. [s10]
- **Modern Japanese pop culture.** The Pokémon Manaphy and Phione are based on the clione; Wikipedia calls the animal extremely well-known in Japanese culture. Use lightly, as a share hook, not as tradition. [s6]
- **Biology bridge (visible heart of the hunt).** The body is transparent and the red-orange glowing part people see in the middle is the digestive organ; it is also a simultaneous hermaphrodite. A clean, true image for 'what you carry shows through'. [s12] [s8]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Spirit-animal or totem meanings for sea angels (purity, grace, guardian angel) appear only on blogs. No Indigenous Arctic (Inuit, Sámi) tradition about Clione was found. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Water meets yang Fire. In Wu Xing Water controls Fire, and in the Western pairing Water and Fire do not mix, so this crossing lives with a real tension: a soft, drifting Pisces surface carrying a Horse's single-minded chase. The sea angel is that tension made visible: a glassy drifter that hunts one prey and seizes it in a fraction of a second.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: patience that can outlast scarcity (a year without food in the lab), precise focus on the one thing that matters, a gentleness people trust, and the speed to act when the moment comes.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: specialization without a backup; being so clear and gentle on the surface that people are shocked by the edge; the Pisces drift used as cover for avoiding a decision the Horse has already made.
- **Love.** Our reading: devoted to one person with unusual specificity; transparent about feeling; needs a partner who can hold both the softness and the intensity. The hermaphrodite sea angel suggests a love that does not sort roles by script.
- **Money.** Our reading: lives off reserves well (lipid stores, the long fast); a saver in lean times who spends fast and all at once on the one thing wanted.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: drifts, says little, then acts all at once. Practice: show the cones a little earlier, so people are not blindsided.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (general term)**: Header hanzi 海天使 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin hǎitiānshǐ.
  海天使 hǎi tiānshǐ, sea angel, is used in Chinese for this animal and has pinyin on the page. The species name is 裸海蝶 luǒ hǎi dié, naked sea butterfly; consider it as a secondary label. Traditional and simplified forms are identical. Minor: the page writes 马 (simplified) in 'Horse year' but 馬 in the proverb. [s10]
- **WRONG**: Lede: Clione limacina swims through Arctic and Antarctic waters.
  C. limacina lives in the Arctic Ocean and North Atlantic (south to at least the Sargasso Sea). Antarctic sea angels are a separate species, C. antarctica, and North Pacific and Okhotsk animals are now C. elegantissima. [s6] [s7]
- **OK**: Fact 1: a sea slug, shell-less gastropod; wing-like appendages are modified foot tissue; 'sea angel' is common usage.
  Parapodia are the modified foot; sea angels are the gymnosome clade. 'Sea slug' is informal but acceptable. [s8] [s9]
- **WRONG (detail)**: Fact 2: feeds with buccal cones, six hook-bearing tentacle-like structures, everted only during predation.
  Six buccal cones (three pairs), adhesive, everted to seize the prey's shell: right. The hooks are separate: chitinous hooks everted from hook sacs pull the body out of the shell. Rewrite: 'six adhesive buccal cones grab the shell, then chitinous hooks pull the snail out'. [s6] [s7] [s9]
- **OK (soften)**: Fact 3: feeds almost exclusively on Limacina sea butterflies; one hunting only the other.
  Adults feed almost exclusively on Limacina helicina and L. retroversa; when adult Limacina are scarce, stomachs held amphipods and calanoids, so 'only' is too strong. [s6] [s7]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (likely wrong; cut)**: Fact 4: sea angels produce bioluminescent light, a faint blue-violet glow.
  No source found that Clione limacina is bioluminescent; Wikipedia, SeaLifeBase, Sea Slug World and Walla Walla describe it without any light organ. The likely origin is the red-orange gut that seems to glow through the clear body. The Wu Xing card ('the bioluminescent glow in the dark column') repeats the claim and should go too. [s6] [s9] [s12]
- **WRONG (detail)**: Fact 5: veliger larvae briefly bear a shell; the shell is absorbed as the animal develops.
  The shelled veliger loses its shell at metamorphosis into a polytroch larva; Wikipedia says larvae discard their embryonic shells a few days after hatching. Shed, not absorbed. [s8] [s9]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (overstated)**: Fact 6: as temperatures rise, Limacina populations are declining due to acidification dissolving shells; sea angels face direct starvation risk.
  Live Limacina shells are dissolving where water is undersaturated in aragonite (Bednaršek et al. 2012, Southern Ocean, L. helicina antarctica), so the threat is real. A measured population decline I could not source, and warming and acidification are conflated. 'Direct starvation' ignores that C. limacina survived 356 days without food in the lab (Böer et al. 2007). Rewrite as a risk, not a fact. [s15] [s14]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Sources block: 'IUCN Red List, Clione limacina'.
  SeaLifeBase points to an IUCN entry but shows no category; CITES and CMS list it as not evaluated. Do not imply a conservation status. Lalli and Gilmer 1989, Pelagic Snails, is a real reference. [s9]
- **OK**: Purple Star sections.
  No star assigned from the sign pair; chart described as cast from lunar birth date and hour.
