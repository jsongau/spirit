## The animal as a symbol
- **British Army naming.** 'Riflebird' comes from the likeness of the male's black velvety plumage to the dark uniform of the British Army's Rifle Brigade (Wikipedia; the Australian Museum says 'allegedly'). The species name paradiseus is Latin for paradise. A soldier's coat on a dancer: usable for a Leo Horse who looks severe until the display opens. [s5, s7]
- **Bundjalung (northeast NSW and Gold Coast/Tweed).** The Bundjalung dictionary records yahdj (Gold Coast Tweed yaheh) for catbird, satin bowerbird and rifle bird. One word for this bird and the Satin Bowerbird, the other animal in this batch, from the country where both live. Dictionary entry only, no story; do not build a myth on it. [s12]
- **Bird-of-paradise family lore (Europe, 16th century).** Magellan's crew brought back skins traders had stripped of feet and wings; Pigafetta recorded the local name bolon diuata, 'birds of God', said to come from paradise. Europeans believed the birds never landed. That is the root of 'paradise' in the family and species names. Caution: the riflebird is Australian and was not part of that trade, so frame it as the family's story, not this bird's. [s13, s7]
- **Nest lore (natural history).** Paradise Riflebirds drape cast-off snake skins on the rims of their nests; the purpose is unknown, perhaps decoration or a deterrent to nest robbers (Birds in Backyards). Only the female builds and attends the nest (Australian Museum), so this belongs to her, not the displaying male. [s8, s7]
- **Horse-year classical text (page proverb).** 塞翁失馬 sài wēng shī mǎ, from Huainanzi ch. 18 人間訓 Rénjiān xùn: the frontier man's lost horse returns with a herd, his son breaks a leg riding one, and the injury spares him from war. Fortune and misfortune turn into each other. Year-animal lore that would sit on all twelve Horse pages; use sparingly. [s16]
- **Aboriginal story specifically about the riflebird.** Unsourced, do not use. None found in a source I could open.

## The fusion (our reading unless cited)
- **Temperament:** Our reading: double Fire. Leo wants to be witnessed; the Horse wants open ground and no orders. In the riflebird that becomes one exact, all-out display from a traditional perch, then back to foraging alone along trunks and branches. The heat is real and it is spent in bursts.
- **Gifts:** Total commitment when the moment is right; presence that reorganizes attention; honesty and loyalty (both traditions name them); stamina for distance and seasons (moves from above 500 m in summer to below 200 m in winter).
- **Shadow:** Leo vanity plus Fire Horse stubbornness. Can perform for anyone who lands on the branch, or refuse to perform at all because standing still feels like a fence. The polygynous male who leaves the nesting entirely to the female is a fair warning image about showing up for the courtship and not the rest.
- **Love:** Courts in a single dazzling, physical gesture, wings curved overhead, head thrown back. Romance needs freedom built in: a partner with her own branch. Six Harmonies with the Goat suggests a gentler, home-making partner steadies it.
- **Money:** Our reading: big generous spender in bursts (Leo extravagance), restless with long commitments (Horse); does best with a few bold bets and an automatic floor of savings.
- **Conflict:** Flares fast and loud, a single harsh call, then moves on. Fixed Leo holds the grudge longer than the Horse would; the Horse's dislike of orders means ultimatums backfire.

## Candidate lines
1. One thick branch high in the canopy is yours, used season after season, and the rest of the forest is just the ground you cross between showings.
2. From most angles you pass for a soldier in black; tilt toward the sun and the throat flashes green-blue, and you know almost to the inch where a watcher has to stand.
3. When you finally open, you open past what the joint is meant to allow, wings curved over your head like a cape, and then it is over and you are gone down the ridge.
4. Your call is one long harsh yaaass across the gully. You say it once and do not repeat it for anyone who was not listening.
5. Fire from the Leo sky and Fire again from the Horse year, and those velvet black feathers are there to hold that much heat in until the branch is right.
6. Summer up above five hundred metres, winter down in the gullies, and every spring you come back to the same perch as though you never left.
