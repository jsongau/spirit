# Harpy Eagle dossier: Leo x Year of the Tiger

## The Harpy Eagle as a symbol
- **Ancient Greek (the name)**: Both the English name and the genus Harpia come from Greek harpyia, the harpies: wind spirits who carried the dead away to Hades or Tartarus. The bird was named for a soul-snatcher of the air. [s1]
- **Chinese art**: The character 鹰 yīng covers eagles, hawks and falcons. An eagle stands for strength, boldness and keen sight; eagle tail feathers were a protective charm; an eagle on a rock reads as a lonely struggle, an eagle in a pine as strength into old age. Painted together, eagle (yīng) and bear (xióng) make the rebus 英雄 yīngxióng, 'hero'. [s8] [s9]
- **Amazonian (Hixkaryana, Brazil)**: Hixkaryana men hold that spiritual power lives in the hair; they hang harpy eagle feathers from feathered hair tubes to strengthen that power and connect to the spirit world. [s10]
- **Indigenous South America, general**: Harpy feathers and talons were coveted as symbols of power, and the bird was held in a mix of fear and awe. ADW notes occasional use in ceremonial rituals by Indigenous hunters. In many South American cultures cutting down the kapok tree, a favored nest tree, is considered bad luck. [s11] [s2] [s1]
- **Panama (national emblem)**: National bird of Panama and shown on its coat of arms; ADW adds the state bird of Paraná, Brazil. [s1] [s2]
- **Aztec**: Wikipedia states the harpy eagle was sacred to Quetzalcoatl. Single secondary claim with no primary source checked: verify before use. [s1]
- **Amazonian shamanic 'sky messenger' / trance lore**: Unsourced, do not use. Found only on a lore blog with no claim-level citation (hitchhikersguidetoreligion.com). (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament**: Leo is a fixed Fire sign ruled by the Sun, made to be seen; the Tiger is yang Wood, brave, solitary and quick to strike. Wood feeds Fire, so the Tiger half literally fuels the Leo half. The Harpy Eagle person carries a large presence they choose not to spend. They watch first, decide once, and then commit with their whole weight. The crest is the tell: when they care, it shows, even if they say nothing.
- **Gifts**: patience that is actually attention; loyalty that compounds over years; the nerve to act alone once sure; a steadying effect on a room or a team once they decide to stay.
- **Shadow**: pride that hides as stillness. Leo needs recognition and the Tiger refuses to ask for it, so this person can wait for years to be noticed rather than say what they want. Tiger impetuousness shows up as the rare, sudden, too-big reaction after a long silence. Territory can harden into control.
- **Love**: slow to choose, then for keeps. They court by protecting and providing, not by talking. They need a partner who can tell quiet from cold, and who says out loud that they see them. Jealousy is about territory more than about feelings. Two eggs, one chick: they would rather give one person everything than share themselves thinly.
- **Money**: a nest builder. Tends to hold one big asset (a home, a business, a single long position) and add to it for years instead of chasing many small bets. Risk: Tiger impulse and Leo generosity produce occasional large splurges after long restraint.
- **Conflict**: goes silent and watches, which others misread as calm. When they finally move, it is one decisive act, and it can land harder than intended. Best practice for them: name the issue while it is small. Metal (a clear deadline, a plain boundary) helps them in the site's own Wu Xing frame.

## Careers
- **Founder or head of a small, tight operation**: Leo wants the center; the Tiger is traditionally suited to leading (China Highlights). They do best owning one territory outright.
- **Surgeon, pilot, emergency or tactical roles**: Long scanning, then one precise act under pressure. China Highlights lists pilot among Tiger careers.
- **Investigator, analyst, trader with long holding periods**: Working stillness: watching a field for a long time before committing capital or a conclusion.
- **Conservation, ranger or land steward**: Holding and defending ground; the throne-tree loyalty to one place.
- **Performer, director or writer who works in long cycles**: Leo visibility plus Tiger artistry (actor, writer, artist all appear on the Tiger career list), released rarely and big.

| Headline: 'The largest eagle in the world' | WRONG | Contested. ADW calls it the largest species, but Wikipedia says only that it is 'sometimes cited as the largest eagle alongside the Philippine eagle', and BBC Wildlife says the title is often claimed for Steller's sea eagle, with harpy and Philippine eagles roughly as heavy (up to about 10 kg). The page also contradicts itself on the next line ('largest eagle in the Americas'). Fix: 'the largest eagle in the Americas' or 'among the heaviest eagles alive'. | [s1] [s2] [s3] |

| 02 The talons: up to 12.5 cm, among the largest of any living eagle; legs subdue prey 'many times heavier' | OK / WRONG on last clause | 12.5 cm is ADW's figure; Wikipedia gives mean 12.3 cm in females and 'the largest talons of any living eagle'. The 'many times heavier' prey phrasing is unsupported: typical prey (sloths, monkeys) is roughly the eagle's own weight range. Cut that clause. | [s1] [s2] |
| 03 The waiting: perches motionless up to 23 hours scanning for sloths and monkeys | OK (single source) | ADW: 'can potentially perch for up to 23 hours in search of prey.' Wikipedia only says it perches for long periods. Keep the hedge 'reportedly'. | [s2] [s1] |

| 05 The one chick: up to two eggs, one chick raised, fledges 6 to 7 months, about 10 months of care, breeds every 2 to 3 years | OK | Matches ADW. Wikipedia: fledges at 6 months, parents keep feeding for another 6 to 10 months, so 'about ten months total' is the low end. | [s2] [s1] |
