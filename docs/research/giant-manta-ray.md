## The Giant Manta Ray as a symbol
- **Hawaiian (Roxanne Kapuaimohalaikalani Stewart, Kai Palaoa, 2015).** The manta is hāhālua, which Stewart says can be read as 'two breaths' (hā breath, lua two). Mantas appear in the second wā of the Kumulipo creation chant and ranked among the higher sea forms, 'the deep divers who go where we cannot go'. Seeing one can let a person huli i ke au hou, begin anew in a new time. [m10]
- **Chinese names.** Mandarin calls mantas 蝠鱝 fú fèn, 'bat ray' (m13). Taiwan uses 鬼蝠魟 guǐ fú hóng, 'ghost bat ray', and the folk name 魔鬼魚 mógǔ yú, 'devil fish'; the oceanic manta is 雙吻前口蝠鱝 shuāng wěn qián kǒu fú fèn (m11). Our reading: a Dragon-year animal that Chinese speakers already name a ghost and a devil of the sea. [m11] [m13]
- **Chinese, Dragon year proverb (already on the page).** 画龙点睛 huà lóng diǎn jīng: Zhang Sengyao painted four dragons at Anle Temple without eyes; when made to dot two, they flew off in thunder. Recorded in Zhang Yanyuan's Tang dynasty Lidai Minghua Ji. Year-hub folklore, not manta lore. [m14]
- **Science as symbol (Burgess et al. 2016).** Stable isotopes from Ecuador mantas showed most of the diet comes from mesopelagic depths, roughly 200 to 1,000 m, not surface plankton. The animal everyone watches skimming the surface is living mostly on what it finds in the dark. [m6] [m7]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Manta as Hawaiian 'aumakua, Caroline Islands manta goddess, Yap manta dances, Indonesian souls-of-the-drowned, Polynesian protective tattoos, Moche fear of anchor-dragging mantas, 'angels of the sea'. The only page collecting these says itself they come from blogs and social media and are unverified (m15). The Moche museum objects I found show stingrays, not mantas. [m15]

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Air ruled by Venus meets yang Earth with imperial scale. Libra wants proportion; the Dragon wants the whole sea. The Manta resolves it in motion: an enormous body that glides, never thrashes, and never quite stops. Air over Earth in this system is wind over open water, so vision gets reach before it gets weight.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: makes very large plans feel graceful and joinable; fair-minded in a crowd; keeps the peace by moving, not by standing guard; feeds on depth others never see.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the eyes left undotted. Circling the big idea, weighing it one more time, and never landing the small finishing stroke. Libra's need to please plus the Dragon's need for recognition can make the pitch more polished than the work.
- **Love.** Our reading: Venus-ruled and generous at scale. Courts with the whole future, then forgets Tuesday. Needs one partner who is its cleaning station: a fixed, trusted place where the glider finally holds still and lets itself be tended.
- **Money.** Our reading: thinks in large flows, filters at volume. Good at seeing where money pools; less good at the small leak. Needs a partner or system for the line items.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: glides off and comes back around at a wider arc. Rarely strikes. Under pressure Libra stalls and the Dragon pulls rank; practice naming the one decision needed today.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Disc can reach roughly seven meters (23 ft) across; largest ray alive.
  Wikipedia: 'a disc size of 7 m (23 ft) across' (m5). The IUCN page cited on the live page was not opened. [m5]
- **OK**: Cephalic fins unroll from a spiral into paddles that funnel plankton and small prey into the mouth while it keeps moving.
  Wikipedia: rolled in a spiral for swimming, flared to channel water into the mouth when feeding (m5). [m5]
- **OK (name the study)**: A 2016 study found about 73 percent of the diet comes from deep mesopelagic water, not surface plankton.
  Burgess et al. 2016, Royal Society Open Science, stable isotopes, Ecuador: 'the majority of dietary intake is of mesopelagic origin' (m6). The 73/27 split is from co-author Anthony Richardson in the UQ release (m7). Say it is an estimate from one population. [m6] [m7]
- **OK**: Cannot stop swimming; relies on forward motion to pass water over the gills.
  Wikipedia: 'needs to swim continuously to channel water over its gills for respiration' (m5). [m5]
- **OK for brain; mirror needs a caveat**: Largest brain of any fish; may show mirror self-recognition. Intro also says it 'appears to recognize its own reflection'.
  Brain: Wikipedia, up to 200 g, the largest of any fish (m5). Mirror: one study, Ari and D'Agostino 2016, two captive giant mantas at Atlantis, Bahamas, mirror test only, no mark test (m8); Stewart et al. 2016 argue the behaviours are ordinary social behaviour (m9). 'May' in the fact block is fine; the intro's 'appears to recognize' overstates. Name the study and the dispute, or cut. [m5] [m8] [m9]
- **OK**: Holds a near-stationary position for several minutes at a reef cleaning station.
  Wikipedia: 'adopts a near-stationary position for several minutes' at cleaning stations (m5). Only an encyclopedia source; a primary study would be better. [m5]
- **WRONG (imprecise)**: Header hanzi 鰩 in the rail and serial mark.
  鰩 yáo means ray in general, including skates (m12); it is not the manta name. Use 蝠鱝 fú fèn, manta ray (m13); Taiwan writes 鬼蝠魟 guǐ fú hóng (m11). Page also shows no pinyin for the glyph. [m11] [m12] [m13]
- **Weak**: Source list: only Wikipedia and IUCN for all six facts.
  Swap in Burgess et al. 2016 (m6) and Ari and D'Agostino 2016 with the Stewart response (m8, m9). [m6] [m8] [m9]
- **OK**: Proverb 画龙点睛 huà lóng diǎn jīng.
  Zhang Sengyao story from the Lidai Minghua Ji (m14). Pairing is correctly labelled as the page's own. [m14]
