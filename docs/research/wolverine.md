# Wolverine dossier: Aries x Year of the Tiger

Slug `wolverine`. Element: Wood (from the Tiger); Aries adds Fire, cardinal. Research date 2026-11-08. Everything marked 'Our reading' is interpretation, not tradition.

## Western half: Aries
- Temperament: Pioneering, enterprising, courageous, fast and direct ('takes the straight line'), independent and able to be alone, enthusiastic, heroic in defense of a cause. Shadow: impatient and brusque, quick anger that does not hold a grudge, 'would much rather start 10 jobs and let someone else finish them', aggression as cover for insecurity, self-focus, the sore winner, poking people for a reaction.
- Planet: Mars (the Sun is exalted in Aries)
- Element: Fire (with Leo and Sagittarius)
- Modality: Cardinal; opposite sign Libra

## Chinese half: Tiger

- Yinyang: Yang
- Element: Wood (fixed); earthly branch 寅 yín

- Lucky: null (dropped per brief)

## The Wolverine as a symbol
- **Innu (Labrador and Quebec).** Kuekuatsheu, the wolverine, is the trickster at the center of many Innu stories: sometimes a hero who saves Innu families from cannibals, sometimes a scoundrel who tricks people and animals. In the creation story he survives a great flood with the animals and the world is rebuilt from mud brought up from below (Wikipedia names a mink as the diver; Annie Picard's 2024 retelling, from her grandmother in the Mushuau dialect, has a muskrat make the sacrifice). Christine Poker of Natuashish told WCS Canada that her grandparents said 'Kuekuatsheu left with them' when families moved into larger communities. Tell it as theirs, nation named, no 'you' reading laid over it. [w7] [w8] [w5]
- **French Canada (from Innu-aimun).** The Canadian French name carcajou is borrowed from the Innu-aimun / Montagnais kuàkuàtsheu, the same name as the trickster. [w5] [w6]
- **Northern Europe (the glutton name).** The wolverine's reputation as a glutton (Latin Gulo, French glouton) may rest partly on a false etymology: Norwegian fjellfross, 'mountain cat', is thought to have been heard as German Vielfraß, 'eats a lot'. Finnish ahma does come from a word for glutton. Good honest hook: the gluttony is a story told about the animal, not a measured trait. [w5]
- **Mi'kmaq, Passamaquoddy and Dene (secondary source only).** Wikipedia says Mi'kmaq and Passamaquoddy tell of Lox, a wolverine trickster and thief often paired with the wolf, and that the Dene of northwestern Canada tell of the wolverine as a trickster who reshapes the world. Only a tertiary source reached; find a nation-led source before using. [w5]
- **Michigan, USA.** Michigan is 'the Wolverine State' and the University of Michigan mascot is the wolverine, yet the origin is obscure (fur trade at Sault Ste. Marie, or an insult to settlers) and ADW says wolverines probably did not occur there historically. A 2004 animal near Ubly was the first confirmed in Michigan in about 200 years. A reputation bigger than the presence: fits the archetype. [w5] [w6]
- **Chinese (Tiger half).** Proverb on the live page: 虎頭蛇尾 hǔ tóu shé wěi, 'tiger's head, snake's tail', a strong start and a weak finish (MDBG). A second fit for a small animal facing large ones: 初生牛犊不怕虎 chūshēng niúdú bú pà hǔ, 'a newborn calf does not fear the tiger', praise for fearlessness that is partly inexperience; The World of Chinese traces the metaphor to Zhuangzi. [w19] [w18]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'wolverine spirit animal = ferocity, tenacity, protecting your space' claims on totem blogs; no nation or text behind them. Also the Olaus Magnus gorging-between-two-trees story: I could not open a source that attributes it (the Gulon Wikipedia page gives it without a citation). (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Fire (Aries, Mars) meets yang Wood (Tiger). In the five phases Wood feeds Fire, so the Tiger's nature is fuel for the Aries flame: double ignition, the most 'go first' crossing on the wheel. The wolverine fits the size gap: roughly dog-sized, reported to take carcasses from bears, cougars and wolves (w6). Both halves are yang and both are starters.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: courage that does not wait for permission, speed to claim new ground, self-sufficiency (Aries can be alone; the wolverine travels alone), the knack of making a meal from what others left frozen, reserves spread out so one loss never ends you.
- **Shadow.** Our reading, with a sourced convergence: both traditions name the same flaw. The Tiger proverb 虎頭蛇尾 hǔ tóu shé wěi is a strong start and weak finish (w19), and the Astrology Podcast says Aries 'would much rather start 10 jobs and let someone else finish them' (w2). Add the Tiger's mistrust and reluctance to communicate (w4) and Aries brusqueness: the wolverine that wins the carcass and eats alone.
- **Love.** Our reading: Mars-ruled and yang. Loyal and protective to the point of fighting for its own, wants a partner who does not need managing and will not crowd the range. Aries anger flares and drops (w2); the Tiger's mistrust (w4) means trust is slow even when passion is fast. Needs its own territory inside the relationship.
- **Money.** Our reading: quick to claim, quick to spend on the next ground. The caching instinct (one food source split into up to six caches, w9) is the healthy version: several accounts, several income lines, none advertised. Risk: Tiger overindulgence (w4) and Aries impatience with slow returns.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: stands its ground against far bigger opponents and does not back down for show. Aries forgets fast; the Tiger does not explain itself. Practice: pick which carcass is worth the bear. Not every fight earns a second look.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Founder, first hire, turnaround or launch roles.** Our reading: cardinal Fire starts things and the Tiger leads; the 虎頭蛇尾 risk means pair it with a finisher.
- **Wilderness, search and rescue, expedition, remote field work.** Our reading: the wolverine's solitary long-range travel and cold tolerance; Aries comfort with solitude (w2).
- **Litigation, advocacy, union or tenant organizing.** Our reading: Aries as the hero defending a cause (w2) plus a small body that takes on big opponents.
- **Salvage, restoration, resale, distressed-asset work.** Our reading: the 90-degree molar that gets value from frozen leftovers others abandoned (w5, w7).
- **Combat sports, emergency medicine, firefighting.** Our reading: speed, direct action and composure under pressure; Mars as the ruler.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (soften)**: Weighing roughly 9 to 25 kilograms, the wolverine has been documented driving bears, cougars, and entire wolf packs off their own kills.
  ADW gives adults 9 to 30 kg and says wolverines are reported to take carcasses from 'bears, cougars, and even packs of wolves' (w6). Wikipedia gives males 11 to 18 kg, females 8 to 12 kg (w5); WCS Canada gives Ontario males up to 14 kg (w7). WCS Yellowstone: one male challenged a bear about 10 times his size over an elk carcass (w10). But Wikipedia also records a wolverine killed in a kill contest with a black bear, and wolves kill wolverines (w5). Use 'reported to', not 'documented driving off entire wolf packs'. [w6] [w5] [w7] [w10]
- **OK**: Upper molars rotated roughly 90 degrees, letting it shear and crush frozen meat and bone.
  Wikipedia: a special upper molar at the back of the mouth rotated 90 degrees, used to tear meat from frozen prey or carrion (w5). WCS Canada: lets them chew through bone to the marrow (w7). Note it is one molar per side, not 'molars' in general. [w5] [w7]
- **OK (fact) / WRONG (citation)**: Wolverines routinely split a single carcass into multiple food caches, hidden in snow, boulders and bog.
  van der Veen et al. 2020 tracked 38 GPS-collared wolverines and 303 caches; they 'made between 1 and 6 caches per source', year-round, in steep rugged terrain, unproductive habitat or forest (w9). 'Bog' is not in the abstract. The live page credits this paper to 'Dyck et al.'; the real authors are van der Veen, Mattisson, Zimmermann, Odden and Persson. Fix the citation. [w9]
- **OK**: Home ranges run from roughly 50 to over 1,500 square kilometres depending on sex and season, among the largest of any animal its size.
  ADW: females 50 to 350 km2, males 600 to 1,000 km2, winter ranges up to about 2,000 km2 (w6). WCS: one male's range covers 500 square miles (about 1,300 km2), larger than a grizzly's (w10). Wikipedia gives males over 620 km2 (w5). [w6] [w10] [w5]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (likely inflated)**: One dispersing juvenile male tracked crossing 585 miles of highways, mountain ranges and state lines over a few months (also in the hero intro).
  No source I could open gives 585 miles. The Wyoming to Colorado male M56 traveled about 500 miles in April and May 2009, crossing Interstate 80 (WCS, Live Science) (w11, w12); Wikipedia gives about 500 miles (800 km) to Colorado and over 800 miles in total before it died in North Dakota in 2016 (w5). A different male, M304, covered about 543 miles in 42 days across three states (w13). The cited 2017 Northwest Science paper exists but its text was behind a bot wall (w21). The page's own heading says 'Five hundred miles'. Use 'about 500 miles'. [w11] [w12] [w5] [w13] [w21]
- **WRONG (framing)**: Fur prized for parka ruffs not because it resists frost forming, but because rime can be brushed clean with a single flick.
  ADW says the fur is 'prized for its frost resistant properties' (w6); Wikipedia says it is highly hydrophobic and 'resistant to frost' (w5). ExplorersWeb says breath ice does not stick and brushes off as loose powder (w14). The brush-off part is supported; the 'not because it resists frost' contrast contradicts both sources, and 'a single flick' is embellishment. Rewrite: 'frost from breath does not bind to it and brushes off'. [w6] [w5] [w14]
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi (rail__han and serial-han): 貂 with pinyin diāo.
  貂 diāo means sable or marten, genus Martes (w15). The wolverine is 貂熊 diāoxióng ('marten-bear'); the alternative name is 狼獾 lánghuān (w16, w17). Traditional and simplified forms are identical. Use 貂熊 diāoxióng. [w15] [w16] [w17]
- **OK**: Proverb 虎頭蛇尾 hǔ tóu shé wěi: a tiger's head, a snake's tail; a fierce beginning with a weak ending. Labelled 'a proverb we tie to the Year of the Tiger'.
  MDBG: 'tiger's head, snake's tail; a strong start but weak finish' (w19). Pinyin correct. Labelling is honest. [w19]
- **OK**: Live page matches: Honey Badger (Pig, secret friend), Swift (Gemini x Horse, trine), Harpy Eagle (Leo x Tiger, same year).
  All three relations are correct per the traditional table (w3, w20). Harpy Eagle is a same-year pick; Leo Fire with Aries Fire also gives element harmony, so it holds up. [w3] [w20]
