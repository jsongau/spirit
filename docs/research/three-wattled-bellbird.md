## The Three-wattled Bellbird as a symbol
- **Honduras (rural Olancho, Cordillera de Agalta).** Geographer Mark Bonta recorded seven local names for the bird, including pajaro cafetero (coffee bird) and pimentero (allspice bird): in one village its call tells farmers the coffee is ripening, in another that allspice is ready. Also bell ringer and bell tuner. The bird is a living harvest calendar. Bonta also describes young males practising their calls in coffee farms damaged by Hurricane Mitch. [s6]
- **Costa Rica, conservation (1992).** The Bellbird Biological Corridor began in 1992 to protect this one species, and was drawn along its seasonal migration: from 1,850 m cloud forest on the Continental Divide at Monteverde, through seven life zones, down to the mangroves of the Gulf of Nicoya, about 67,000 hectares. A whole landscape plan named for a single voice. [s7]
- **Costa Rica, national (2021).** To mark 200 years of independence, Costa Rica created the Bicentennial Pajaro Campana (Bellbird) Biological Reserve on 24 August 2021 (Executive Decree 42615-MINAE), 5,075 ha in Coto Brus bordering La Amistad, with the three-wattled bellbird as its main conservation target. [s8]
- **Science as culture (Kroodsma).** Bellbird song is learned, not inherited, and it drifts: Kroodsma tracked three dialects since the 1970s; in one, a whistle fell from about 5,500 Hz to about 3,700 Hz, roughly 70 Hz a year to 2001. He says the birds are 'relearning their songs probably all the time'. Immature males spend about 6 years in subadult plumage developing song, and Monteverde birds are 'often bilingual' in the Monteverde and Talamanca dialects. A rare animal with a measurable oral tradition. [s9] [s10] [s11]
- **Chinese (Ox half).** 牛角挂书 (niú jiǎo guà shū), hanging books on the ox's horns: a herd boy who read while herding, the idiom for a diligent student. And the page's own proverb 对牛弹琴 (duì niú tán qín), Gongming Yi playing his lute to an ox that never reacts, meaning a fine message given to the wrong listener. Both are Ox idioms, so they belong to all 12 Ox pages; use the study one as a bridge to the six practice years. [s5]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** No Indigenous myth, spirit-animal or totem tradition for Procnias tricarunculatus was found. Bellbird lore online is usually about the New Zealand bellbird (korimako, Anthornis melanura) or Australian bell miner, different birds. Do not borrow it. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: Mercury's quick tongue (Gemini, mutable Air) inside the Ox's slow, yin Earth patience. Gemini wants two voices; the Ox wants to drill one until it is perfect. The bellbird does both literally: young males can hold two dialects, then spend about six years in practice plumage before they own a perch and a song. Earth here also grounds Air: the voice is fast, the commitment underneath it is slow.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: a voice that carries far when it finally speaks; long apprenticeship without needing an audience; the ability to hold two ways of speaking and switch between them; timing tied to real seasons, like the harvest farmers hear in its call; leaves useful things growing behind it (the seed gaps).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Ox's poor communication and Gemini's restlessness combine into someone heard everywhere and reachable nowhere. Holds a perch by volume rather than conversation. Can keep revising (the song keeps drifting) and never call a thing finished. Hard to find after speaking.
- **Love.** Our reading: courts by display at a distance, then wants loyalty up close. Gemini needs talk and novelty; the Ox needs proof over years. Falls for people who can hear the second dialect, the quieter voice under the public call. Needs a partner who does not take the silences between calls as rejection.
- **Money.** Our reading: Ox thrift with a Gemini side hustle. Builds one serious skill over years and lives off it; risk is spreading across too many small ventures when restless, or holding one perch too long out of stubbornness. Follows the 'fruiting trees': income that moves with seasons.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: does not argue at length. One loud, precise statement at close range (as the resident male calls at an intruder on its perch), then expects the other side to leave. Works when the line is real; wounds when used on friends. Practice: stay for the second, quieter conversation.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (not this animal's name)**: Header hanzi: 鈴 (líng) shown in the hero/rail as the animal's character.
  鈴 líng means a small bell, a nice image but not the bird's name, and it is a traditional form on a page that otherwise uses simplified characters. The Chinese name listed by Oiseaux.net is 肉垂钟伞鸟 (ròu chuí zhōng sǎn niǎo), literally 'wattled bell umbrella-bird'; the bell character there is 钟 (zhōng), not 铃/鈴. If one glyph is wanted, 钟 zhōng is closer to the real name; never show it without pinyin. [s17]
- **WRONG (superlative)**: The male's call is likely the loudest call made by any bird in the world, carrying clearly up to half a mile through dense cloud forest.
  Half a mile is OK (ADW, Wikipedia, ABC). 'Loudest of any bird' is outdated: Podos and Cohn-Haft (2019, Current Biology) measured the white bellbird (Procnias albus) at about 125 dB, the loudest known; ABC gives the three-wattled at up to about 100 dB. Say 'among the loudest bird calls on Earth' and keep the half mile. ADW's 'probably the loudest' predates 2019. [s12] [s13] [s14] [s16]
- **OK (minor)**: Adult males grow three thin black wattles, roughly 5 to 7.6 centimeters long, hanging from the base of the bill and each side of the mouth; females carry none.
  ADW: 'usually 5.1 to 7.6 cm long and a dark gray/black color'. Wikipedia adds they can reach 10 cm when extended during song. ABC: two hang from the corners of the mouth, one from the top of the bill. Females lacking wattles is consistent with the male-only description; ADW and ABC describe wattles only on males. [s12] [s13] [s16]
- **OK**: Bellbirds are notoriously secretive; most people and researchers first learn a bird is present by its call.
  ADW: 'a very secretive bird. In fact, the main way they are discovered is by their distinctive call.' [s12]
- **OK**: When a rival lands on a claimed perch, the resident male drives it toward the edge and calls at point-blank range, so the intruder is frequently knocked off the branch.
  ADW: the first bird 'will drive him toward the edge of the perch' and 'the intruder is frequently knocked off the branch'. ADW does not say the knocking-off is caused by sound alone; avoid implying the call itself is the force. [s12]
- **WRONG (two parts)**: Young males take years to develop a finished call, and roughly half stay fluent in two regional dialects at once before settling, as adults, on a single song for the rest of their lives.
  Years of development is right: about 6 years in subadult plumage (Kroodsma et al. 2013). 'Roughly half' is UNVERIFIABLE: the abstract says Monteverde birds are 'often bilingual' and gives no fraction. 'A single song for the rest of their lives' is WRONG: Kroodsma documents songs that keep changing (one whistle dropped about 70 Hz a year, 1970s to 2001) and says birds are 'relearning their songs probably all the time'. Also bilingual birds are described at Monteverde (Monteverde plus Talamanca dialects), not as a general adult stage. [s9] [s10] [s11]
- **OK (with softening)**: Feeding almost entirely on wild avocado and other Lauraceae fruit, the bellbird favors broken branches as feeding perches and disperses seeds into sunlit gaps so effectively it has been called the tree family's single most important disperser.
  ADW: favourite food wild avocado (Lauraceae); 'their perches are often broken branches'; Daniel Wenny called it 'far and away the most effective disperser of Lauraceae seeds'. Wenny and Levey (1998, PNAS) found bellbirds dropped seeds under song perches in canopy gaps, where seedlings survived better; that study was on one tree species (a shade-tolerant Ocotea) and the perches were song perches, not feeding perches. Suggest 'song perches' and 'the most effective disperser Wenny measured'. 'Almost entirely' is stronger than ADW's 'favorite food'; soften to 'mostly'. [s12] [s15]
- **OK**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) claims on the page.
  No star is assigned from the sign pair. The page states a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. No 'What Purple Star already suspects' block found. No 'Primal Zodiac' string found. 
- **OK**: Proverb 对牛弹琴 (duì niú tán qín), labelled as our own pairing with the Ox year.
  Real idiom; meaning on the page (fine words to an audience that cannot receive them) matches The World of Chinese, which tells it as Gongming Yi playing his lute to an ox. Pairing is honestly labelled. Pinyin present. [s5]
