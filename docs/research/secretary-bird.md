## The Secretary Bird as a symbol
- **South Africa (national coat of arms, adopted 2000).** The secretary bird stands at the centre of the arms with wings raised and the rising sun between them. The official reading: uplifted wings for the nation's ascendance and its protection, the snake-killing bird as protector against enemies, and 'a messenger of the heavens' that 'conducts its grace upon the Earth'. The sun stands for rebirth. Motto in |Xam: ǃke e꞉ ǀxarra ǁke, 'diverse people unite'. [s6] [s8]
- **Sudan (national emblem).** The secretary bird carries the emblem under the motto 'Victory is ours' (An-nasr lanā). It was chosen as a native Sudanese bird in place of the eagle and hawk used by other Arab states. Adoption date differs between sources: Wikipedia's Secretarybird page says 1969, the Emblem of Sudan page says 1985. [s5] [s7]
- **Maasai (East Africa).** The Maasai name ol-enbai nabo is glossed as 'one arrow', from the crest. Pairs neatly with the Latin genus Sagittarius, 'the archer', also usually read from the crest quills, and serpentarius for the snake hunting. [s5] [s14]
- **Xhosa and Zulu (South Africa).** Xhosa name inxhanxhosi; Xhosa folklore credits the bird with great intelligence. Zulu name intungunono. Only one secondary source (Wikipedia) located; use lightly and name the source. [s5]
- **European naming (18th century).** Buffon (1780) read the name from the crest feathers, like quill pens behind a clerk's ear. A rival theory (Fry 1977) traces 'secretary' to French secrétaire from Arabic saqr et-tair, 'hawk that flies'; Ian Glenn rejected it. The quill story is the popular one but contested. [s5] [s14]
- **Predynastic Egypt (weak, do not lead with it).** Wikipedia says the bird appears on an ivory knife handle from Abu Zaidan, about 3200 BC. The Brooklyn Museum catalogue entry for that handle lists rows of animals but does not name a secretary bird. Treat as unconfirmed. [s5] [s15]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'secretary bird spirit animal' meanings (truth-seeking, discernment, grounding) appear only on totem blogs. No traditional source found. Also no traditional Chinese lore exists for this African bird; any Dragon-versus-snake link is our interpretation only. 

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Fire (Aries) feeds yang Earth (Dragon). Aries wants to strike first; the Dragon wants the ground claimed before it moves. The Secretary Bird solves this with walking: it covers the grassland on foot for hours, then ends a fight in a kick that lands in about 15 milliseconds (s9). A snake-killer born in the Dragon's year carries a private joke: in Chinese lore the dragon commands rain and rank (s4), and this bird keeps the serpents in their place. That last link is our image, not a tradition.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: calm under real threat, precision over volume, stamina for long unglamorous groundwork, an authority people trust because it was earned on foot. South Africa chose this bird to stand for protection (s6), and that is the gift: other people feel safer near you.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: composure that hides how hard the walk was, so nobody offers help. Aries impatience plus Dragon dominance can make the strike land before others have been heard. The kick is fast and final; apologies are slow.
- **Love.** Our reading: Mars-ruled and yang. Courts by showing up, steadily, at the same place, and protects fiercely once committed. Secretary birds are believed monogamous and add to the same nest year after year (s10, s11): love here is built in layers. Needs a partner who does not need constant display and who will say plainly when the protectiveness becomes control.
- **Money.** Our reading: earns by covering ground others skip, then commits hard to one position. Good at the long patient accumulation (the nest) and at one decisive move. Risk: the all-in strike on a hunch that felt like certainty.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: watches, circles, says little, then ends it. Rarely starts a fight, rarely loses one, sometimes wins a fight that only needed a conversation. Practice: let the other side see you coming.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Field work and land management (rangers, ecologists, surveyors, agronomists).** Our reading: covering 20 to 30 km a day on foot (s8, s10) is the job description; Earth element plus Aries stamina.
- **Security, protection, emergency response, safety inspection.** Our reading: the national-symbol role as protector (s6) and calm precision under threat.
- **Surgery, dentistry, precision trades.** Our reading: a strike guided by vision and timing, not force alone; Portugal et al. read the 15 ms contact as evidence of precise visual targeting (s5, s9).
- **Auditing, compliance, investigations.** Our reading: the clerk's-quill name over a hunter's body; patient groundwork, then a finding that ends the matter.
- **Founding or leading a small firm.** Our reading: Aries starts, the Dragon commands; China Highlights lists law, engineering and architecture among Dragon fits (s4), LoveToKnow lists entrepreneurship for Aries (s2).

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG (not the animal's name)**: Header hanzi 鹫 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin jiù.
  鹫 jiù alone means vulture. The Chinese Wikipedia article for the secretary bird is titled 鹭鹰 lùyīng (traditional 鷺鷹) and lists 蛇鹫 shéjiù (traditional 蛇鷲), 秘书鸟 mìshū niǎo and others as alternates. Use 蛇鷲 shéjiù (snake vulture, the most widely used name, and it carries the snake) or 鷺鷹 lùyīng. Page also shows simplified 鹫 and 龙 while the brief asks for traditional forms. [s14]
- **OK (add the sample size)**: Kicks of roughly 195 newtons, about five times body weight, contact time about 15 milliseconds, studied with a force plate and high-speed cameras.
  Portugal et al. 2016: 195 plus or minus 34 N, 5.1 body weights, 15 plus or minus 4.4 ms, from 45 kicks by ONE captive male, Madeleine, at the Hawk Conservancy Trust, UK, striking a rubber snake. Say 'one trained bird' and 'a rubber snake'; the page implies a wild-population measurement. [s9] [s5]
- **OK**: Covers up to 20 to 30 km a day on foot.
  BirdLife South Africa and The Peregrine Fund give an average of 20 to 30 km a day; San Diego Zoo says more than 32 km in a day of hunting. [s8] [s10] [s11]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: One of only two raptor species that hunts mainly by walking.
  No source found naming a second species or making this count. Peregrine Fund says only 'almost strictly terrestrial hunters'. Cut the 'one of only two' clause. [s10]
- **OK (contested, soften)**: Quill-like crest likely the source of the common name.
  Buffon's quill-pen theory is the traditional one; Fry (1977) proposed an Arabic origin, saqr et-tair, which Glenn rejected. 'Often said to' is safer than 'likely'. [s5] [s14]
- **WRONG (term) / UNVERIFIABLE (function)**: Stiff thickened feathers resembling eyelashes, known as rictal bristles, ring the eyes and shield them from dust and struggling prey.
  Rictal bristles grow at the base of the beak, not around the eyes. BirdLife South Africa calls the secretary bird's 'eyelashes' small modified feathers without barbs. The shielding function during the kick has no source located; present it as a guess or cut it. [s13] [s8]
- **PARTLY WRONG**: Largely monogamous pairs reuse and add to a stick nest atop an acacia over many seasons; a long-held nest can be about 2.5 m across and weigh close to a metric ton.
  Monogamy and reuse: OK (Peregrine Fund, San Diego Zoo). Size: San Diego Zoo gives up to 2.4 m; Wikipedia gives a typical 1.0 to 1.5 m platform, 30 to 50 cm deep. Weight 'close to a metric ton': no source found; it looks borrowed from bald eagle nest records. Cut the weight; say 'up to about 2.4 m'. [s10] [s11] [s5]
- **OVERSTATED**: Flies up to roost in the same treetop nest every night at dusk.
  They return to roost in late afternoon or dusk, in Acacia or Balanites trees; a pair 'may roost together in their nest year-round, or rest on other flat-topped trees'. Drop 'the same nest every night'. [s10] [s12] [s5]
- **OK**: Hero: 'faster than a blink'.
  Contact of about 15 ms is far shorter than a human blink (well over 100 ms). Fine as stated, given the one-bird caveat. [s9]
- **NO TRADITIONAL BASIS**: Purple Star section: 'Our Purple Star reading for this crossing' assigns 武曲 Wǔ Qū (the General) and 七殺 Qī Shā (the Killings) to Aries x Dragon.
  Zi Wei Dou Shu stars are placed from the lunar birth date and hour, not the Western sun sign or the year animal. It is labeled 'Our', which helps, but lines like 'Purple Star tends to seat Wu Qu exactly where a Secretary Bird would stand' present it as the tradition's view. Relabel plainly or cut (see REVIEW-mojo section 0.3). [s16]
