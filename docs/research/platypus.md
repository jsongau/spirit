## The Platypus as a symbol
- **D'harawal (Sydney and South Coast), told by Frances Bodkin and Gawaian Bodkin-Andrews.** Gaya'dari: a Black Duck clan girl and a Water Rat clan man run away together to a cave under a waterfall. Their children, half duck and half water rat, break the clan marriage law; the man dies protecting her, and her descendants, the platypus, stay hidden. The authors frame the stories as teaching law, with layers of secrets and lessons. [s6]
- **Kullilla (Queensland), told by Michael J Connolly.** A young duck ignores warnings about Mulloka the water devil, is taken by Biggoon the lonely water rat, escapes, and later hatches children with fur, bills and spurred hind feet. Banished, her children's line becomes the Gaya-dari, 'quite a tribe apart'. [s7] [s19]
- **Upper Darling River (nation not named in source).** When the Ancestor Spirits set the totems, birds, marsupials and fish each invite the platypus to join. After consulting the echidna it declines all three, because it shares traits with each and wants to stay friends with all. WWF-Australia's author reads it as 'Not an inability to fit in, more an act of diplomacy.' [s5]
- **Djabugay, Yuin, Wiradjuri, Wadi Wadi, palawa.** Names and status: Wiradjuri biladurang; Yuin (Dhurga) Yaranbul, part of creation stories at Gulaga and Biamanga; Wadi Wadi Matakupay; Djabugay Ngunbay, 'place of platypus', now Kuranda, where Djabugay ranger Dameon Hunter says the platypus was 'too special to be hunted. It was taboo.' palawa kani name: larila. Other recorded names: mallingong, boondaburra, tambreet. [s5] [s8]
- **British science, 1799.** George Shaw at the British Museum doubted the first pelt was real; History Today says he 'attacked it with a pair of scissors in a failed attempt to detach the beak'. The suspicion was a duck's beak sewn onto a beaver-like body. The Edinburgh anatomist Robert Knox later suggested Chinese sailors might have made it. [s8] [s9]
- **Chinese name.** 鴨嘴獸 yāzuǐshòu (simplified 鸭嘴兽), literally 'duck-bill beast'. The literal reading is ours; the article does not explain the coinage. The Latin names say the same: Ornithorhynchus 'bird beak', anatinus 'duck-like'. [s17]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Platypus spirit animal' meanings on totem blogs (intuition, feminine energy, etc.); no traditional source. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Air (Aquarius) meets yin Water (Pig). Aquarius is the anomaly that refuses to be filed; the Pig is the wholehearted forager who goes in with eyes shut. Together: the one who finds things in the dark by a sense nobody else has, and who is content to look improbable while doing it. Leo, this crossing's opposite sign, is the Rhinoceros Hornbill: the platypus works by stealth what the hornbill does by display.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: perception that works without the usual channels; loyalty to more than one group at once (the totem story's diplomacy is an Indigenous teaching and stays theirs, but the Aquarius bridge-builder echoes it); generosity of the Pig; calm under pressure.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Pig's trust plus Aquarian detachment. Easily fooled up close, then retreats into a plugged burrow. Carries a spur nobody expects, and may use it in a flash when cornered.
- **Love.** Our reading: wants a partner who believes what you sensed before you can prove it. Affection arrives through odd channels (no nipples; the milk seeps through skin onto fur, s8, s12). Needs long private recovery time.
- **Money.** Our reading: Pig generosity with Aquarian indifference to status. Spends on comfort and on friends, ignores the standard playbook, sometimes too trusting with lenders and schemes.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids it underwater for as long as possible, then the spur. Better: say what you sensed early, plainly, before the venom question comes up.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 鴨嘴獸 yāzuǐshòu (rail__han and serial-han)
  Correct traditional form of 鸭嘴兽, the platypus. Pinyin shown in the rail; make sure it carries tone marks wherever the glyph appears. [s17]
- **OK**: Bill has about 40,000 electroreceptors and 60,000 mechanoreceptors; eyes, ears and nose close during a dive.
  Pettigrew 1999: electroreceptors 'approximately 40 000', mechanoreceptors 'approximately 60 000'; hunts with eyes, ears and nostrils 'tightly closed'. [s10]
- **OK**: One of five living monotremes, lays leathery eggs; lineage diverged about 166 million years ago.
  Five species: platypus plus four echidnas (s8, s19). 166 Myr is the Warren et al. 2008 estimate (published range 160 to 210); say 'an estimated'. [s8] [s11] [s19]
- **OK**: Male venomous spur on each hind leg linked to a crural gland; severe long-lasting pain in humans; used in male competition in breeding season.
  Wikipedia: only males venomous (females lose a vestigial spur sheath); severe pain, sensitivity can last months. NSW: only males have spurs, venom strong enough to kill a dog. [s8] [s12]
- **OK (detail unverified)**: No nipples; milk secreted through skin patches and pools in grooves on the belly.
  No teats and milk through skin pores confirmed (s8); NSW says young suckle the underbelly fur (s12). 'Pools in grooves' not found in sources opened; say 'seeps onto the fur'. [s8] [s12]
- **WRONG (detail)**: 1799, George Shaw suspected taxidermy fraud: 'a beaver's bill sewn onto a mammal's body'; examined it with scissors for stitches.
  The suspected fake was a duck's beak sewn onto a beaver-like body (s8). 'A beaver's bill' is a garble. Scissors confirmed: History Today says he tried to cut off the beak (s9); Wikipedia says he looked for stitches (s8). [s8] [s9]
- **OK (add context)**: Burrows up to 30 metres long; nesting burrow entrance plugged with soil from inside.
  NSW: nesting burrows up to 30 m, resting burrows 3 to 5 m (s12). Australian Platypus Conservancy: nesting burrows most often under 10 m; 2 to 9 compacted soil plugs (s13). Plugs are along the tunnel, not only the entrance. [s12] [s13]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Sleeps up to 17 hours per day.
  No source found for 17. Siegel et al. 1999 (one male, 48 hours): quiet sleep 6 to 8 h/day, REM 5.8 to 8 h/day; their table states sum to about 14 h (our arithmetic). Citable instead: more REM sleep than any adult mammal studied (s15). [s14] [s15]
- **WRONG (citation)**: Page source list: 'Proske & Gregory (2003) Electrosensory bill of the platypus. Nature 362:587'.
  No such paper found. Nature volume 362 is from 1993, not 2003, so the reference contradicts itself. A real Nature paper is Gregory, Iggo, McIntyre and Proske 1987, Electroreceptors in the platypus, Nature 326:386-387 (s16). The Pettigrew 1999 J Exp Biol 202:1447-1454 citation is correct (s10). [s16] [s10]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (no traditional basis)**: Element chapter: 'Metal-over-Water nature', Aquarius treated as Metal feeding the Pig's Water.
  Wu Xing has no Air; Aquarius is an Air sign (s1). Mapping Aquarius to Metal is the site's own construction and should be labeled as our reading or dropped. The Leo page avoids this because Fire exists in both systems. [s1] [s3]
- **OK (sharpen)**: Lunar New Year note: Recent Pig years 1995, 2007, 2019; January and early February birthdays can belong to the Dog.
  Correct and important here. In 2007 the Pig year began on 18 February (s18), so nearly every Aquarius born in early 2007 is a Dog-year person. Say so for 2007 specifically. 1995 and 2019 start dates not checked. [s18]
