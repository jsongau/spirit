## The Sailfish as a symbol
- **Chinese (name).** The common Chinese name is 旗魚 qíyú, banner fish, for the sail carried like a flag. In Taiwan the species-level name for Istiophorus platypterus is 雨傘旗魚 yǔsǎn qíyú, umbrella banner fish. The character meanings are plain; no source explains who coined the names, so do not invent an origin story. [s6]
- **Taiwan, Chenggong (Taitung) fishing tradition.** 鏢旗魚 biāo qíyú, harpooning billfish from the bow, is described as the oldest and now nearly lost way of taking 旗魚 off eastern Taiwan. The fish arrive with the northeast monsoon after October, and Chenggong holds a harpoon contest at the end of October; crews of two stand on the bow and strike fish that surface to feed, and fishermen say it depends on reading the currents. Caveat: 旗魚 in Taiwanese usage covers billfish broadly and neither article names sailfish specifically. [s7] [s8]
- **United States, Florida.** Florida named the Atlantic sailfish its official state saltwater fish in 1975. [s9]
- **Japanese (name).** Japanese calls the Indo-Pacific sailfish bashōkajiki, written 芭蕉梶木 or 芭蕉旗魚; 芭蕉 bashō is the Japanese fibre banana plant, a likely nod to the broad leaf-like sail, but the source does not explain the name, so treat the leaf reading as ours. [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'sailfish spirit animal means speed, success, self-confidence' meanings on totem and dream blogs; no Indigenous, Pacific or other traditional source located. Hemingway is usually linked to marlin, not sailfish; leave him out. (no source)

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Header hanzi 旗魚 (rail__han and serial-han).
  旗魚 qíyú, banner fish, is the standard Chinese name for sailfish (and in Taiwan a broader term for billfish). Species name in Taiwan: 雨傘旗魚 yǔsǎn qíyú. Add pinyin qíyú next to the glyph if it is not shown. [s6]
- **WRONG**: Lede and fact 5: speeds up to 110 km/h (68 mph); holds the recorded speed record for fish.
  The 110 km/h figure comes from 1940s stopwatch timings of hooked fish. Domenici's group measured maximum speed at about 36 km/h (22 mph) with high-speed video and accelerometers (Svendsen et al. 2016). Wikipedia reports burst speeds of about 7 m/s in hunts. Drop the record claim and the number; NatGeo and Oceana still repeat 68 mph, which is how it spread. [s11] [s12]
- **WRONG**: Fact 5: body heat generated by locomotion and a counter-current heat exchanger near the eye and brain keeps body temperature above the water.
  Billfish (Istiophoridae, including sailfish) warm the brain and eyes with a heater organ made from a modified eye muscle, the superior rectus, by non-shivering thermogenesis, up to about 14 C above ambient. It is not heat from swimming and it does not warm the whole body. Rewrite to 'a heater organ built from an eye muscle warms its brain and eyes'. [s17]
- **OK (soften)**: Fact 1: sail nearly as tall as the body, folds flat into a groove in under a second; raised sail adds drag; must be lowered for maximum speed.
  Sail is kept folded while swimming to reduce drag and raised during attacks (Oceana, Wikipedia; NatGeo: raised to corral prey). 'Nearly as tall as the body' and 'under a second' I could not source; cut the numbers. [s12] [s13] [s18]
- **WRONG**: Fact 2: hunts in loose groups of two to twenty; each takes a turn charging with sail raised while others herd; rotation keeps individuals from exhausting themselves.
  Turn-taking is right (they attack one at a time). Group size is understated: NatGeo reports up to 40 sailfish around one school; Herbert-Read et al. 2016 studied groups of up to 70. The stated reason is unsourced: researchers suggest turn-taking avoids injuring each other with bills, and the group gain comes from prey injured by earlier slashes being easier for later attackers. [s13] [s15] [s18]
- **OK / UNVERIFIABLE (last clause)**: Fact 3: bill used to slash and stun, not spear; lateral sweeps then circling back for stunned fish; bill adds drag and reduces maneuverability.
  Domenici et al. 2014: the bill taps individual prey or slashes through the school with powerful lateral motions, and enters schools without triggering escape. 'Adds drag and reduces maneuverability' is unsourced and sits oddly next to NatGeo's 575 degrees per second bill turn; cut it. [s13] [s14]
- **WRONG (borrowed species and overstated)**: Fact 4 and lede: sail coloring changes in under a second via chromatophores; flashes coordinate the attack sequence and signal who takes the next charge.
  Documented change is on the body: silvery to nearly black with orange spots and electric blue bars on the flanks when attacking (NatGeo). The 'signals the next attacker' idea is not shown in sailfish; the 2024 color-signal study was on striped marlin (body stripes), and even there it is read as a possible signal of motivation to attack. Wikipedia lists sailfish color communication only as 'some sources indicate'. [s13] [s16] [s12]
- **WRONG (part) / UNVERIFIABLE (part)**: Fact 6: highly migratory, no fixed territory, never return to a home range; tagged fish recovered thousands of km away within months.
  Oceana confirms great migrations. But NOAA's cooperative tagging recaptured one sailfish at the exact Islamorada wreck where it was tagged one year earlier, and another less than 40 miles away after two years, which contradicts 'do not return'. 'Thousands of km within months' I could not source. [s18] [s19]
- **WRONG**: Sources block: 'Domenici et al. (2014), Sailfish cooperative hunting behavior, Biology Letters' and 'Marlin et al., Billfish speed and thermoregulation studies'.
  Domenici et al. 2014 is in Proceedings of the Royal Society B, titled 'How sailfish use their bills to capture schooling prey'. 'Marlin et al.' is not a real citation (no author, title or year); remove. [s14]
- **OK**: Crossing section: born in January or early February, the birthday can belong to the Snake.
  Aquarius runs about 20 January to 18 February, so the lunar New Year note applies, and the year before a Horse year is the Snake. [s1] [s3]
- **OK**: Purple Star sections.
  No star is assigned from the sign pair; the page says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour.
