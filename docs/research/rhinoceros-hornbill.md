## The Rhinoceros Hornbill as a symbol
- **Iban (Sarawak, Borneo).** The carved kenyalang, a stylized rhinoceros hornbill, is the centerpiece of Gawai Kenyalang. At its climax the painted figure is raised on a tall pole. Hornbills are intermediaries between the deity Singalang Burong and the human world; the festival could once be sponsored only by a war leader or his descendants. [s7]
- **Iban (Penn Museum record).** Gawai Kenyalang was held in prosperous times; the great hornbill on its pole was sent off to fly away carrying the forces of misfortune with it. Households also made smaller anak (child) figures. [s8]
- **Dayak, Ibanic groups.** Some Ibanic Dayak hold the rhinoceros hornbill to be chief of worldly birds; its statue welcomes Sengalang Burong, god of the augural birds, to feasts. Wikipedia states the war god himself is represented by the brahminy kite, so do not call the hornbill the war god (a blog in search results does). [s6]
- **Malaysia (modern civic).** State bird of Sarawak; appears on the reverse of the 5 ringgit note. Wikipedia also calls it Malaysia's national bird; treat that line as weakly sourced. [s6]
- **Chinese name.** Hornbills are 犀鳥 xīniǎo, 'rhinoceros bird'; this species is 馬來犀鳥 mǎlái xīniǎo, the Malay hornbill. The name echoes the English one: a horn on the face. [s9] [s10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Chinese hornbill-ivory lore (鶴頂 hèdǐng carvings) belongs to the helmeted hornbill, a different species with a solid casque; poachers confusing the two is why the rhinoceros hornbill was uplisted to Vulnerable in 2018 (s6). Generic 'hornbill spirit animal' blog meanings: no traditional source. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Fire (Leo) feeds yang Earth (Dragon). Leo is the display and the voice; the Dragon is the ground and the scale. The hornbill carries both: the casque is a stage prop it repaints daily with its own oil, and the nest is a fortress it builds with mud. Big outside, sealed inside.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: loyalty that is announced in public and then kept in private; the stamina to provide on a schedule for months; presence that carries; a gift for protecting what is fragile without making a show of the protecting.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the wall that outlives the danger. Leo pride plus Dragon control can turn care into confinement. The real hornbill parents chip the dried mud away once the chicks are feathered (s6); the shadow is the one who keeps finding reasons not to.
- **Love.** Our reading: one courtship, done loudly (the pair duets, s5), then monogamy that is likely lifelong. Shows love as provision. Needs a partner who reads the feeding trips as romance and who will say when it is time to open the wall.
- **Money.** Our reading: a provider and a builder; spends generously on display and on the household, then locks the rest down. Risk: status spending (Leo) and over-control of shared money (Dragon).
- **Conflict.** Our reading: defends territory hard (pairs hold up to about 2.3 square kilometres, s5) and then goes silent. That silence works like the mud wall. Ask for the slit: one channel left open.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 犀 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin xī shown
  犀 xī means rhinoceros, not hornbill. Hornbills are 犀鳥 xīniǎo; this species is 馬來犀鳥 mǎlái xīniǎo. Use 犀鳥 xīniǎo (or the full species name). Also: the hero line shows 龙 (simplified Dragon) with no pinyin; add lóng. [s9] [s10]
- **OK**: Bill and casque naturally pale; uropygial (preen) gland oil rubbed on during preening stains them deep orange-red.
  ADW: mandibles naturally white, stained orange-yellow by uropygial oil. Wikipedia: orange and red from preen oil. 'Worked into the feathers' is not in either source; cut that clause. [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: Casque is largely hollow and researchers believe it may act as an acoustic resonating chamber.
  ADW: 'The casque is hollow' and 'believed that the casque may enhance vocalizations by functioning as an acoustic resonating chamber.' Keep the hedge. [s5]
- **OK**: Mate chosen through duet calling and a head-raised display; monogamous, typically for life.
  ADW: pairs duet; head-raised posture in courtship; 'likely to remain with their mate for life'. Keep 'likely'. [s5]
- **OK (timing unverified)**: Before laying, the female enters a hollow and the pair seals the entrance with mud, droppings and fruit pulp, leaving a slit for feeding.
  Materials and slit confirmed (ADW: mud, feces, fruit peels, female seals with male's help; Wikipedia: mud, food, feces). Wikipedia says the walling happens after the eggs are laid, so 'Before laying' conflicts; drop the timing. [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: Male alone provisions mate and chicks, up to thirteen times a day.
  ADW: 'He may bring the female food up to thirteen times in a single day.' [s5]
- **OK (softened)**: Certain large-seeded trees rely almost entirely on hornbills for dispersal, since few other animals can swallow the seed.
  ADW: 'Trees with very large seeds are almost completely reliant on hornbill dispersal.' The 'few other animals large enough' reason is the page's own gloss; unverifiable, cut or soften. [s5]
- **OK**: Dragon half: 'a territory worth two square kilometres'.
  ADW: breeding pairs hold territories up to 2.3 square kilometres. Say 'up to about two square kilometres'. [s5]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: 'The loudest bird in the canopy' (hero subtitle).
  No source ranks it loudest. Its calls are loud and far-carrying; drop the superlative. (no source)
- **OK**: Purple Star blocks
  No star is assigned from Leo plus Dragon on this page. The text correctly says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. (no source)
