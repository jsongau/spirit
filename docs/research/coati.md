## The Coati as a symbol
- **K'iche' Maya (Popol Vuh).** After a bat takes Hunahpu's head in the House of Bats, Xbalanque calls the animals to bring their food. The coati brings a squash, and Christenson's literal translation says 'She rolls it with her nose as she comes'; the squash becomes Hunahpu's new head, and later the decoy that wins the ballgame against the lords of Xibalba. The coati's nose literally carries the hero's replacement head. [k6] [k7]
- **K'iche' Maya (Popol Vuh).** In Christenson's literal translation one name of the creator grandmother is 'White Great Coati', paired with the grandfather 'White Great Peccary'. She is Xmucane, called one of the oldest diviners and daykeepers, who casts lots at the making of humankind. A coati-named seer suits Sagittarius, the sign tradition links to prophets. [k6] [k8] [k1]
- **K'iche' Maya (Popol Vuh), the Monkey link.** The same book turns the twins' older half-brothers, One Howler Monkey and One Artisan, into monkeys: their loincloths become tails and they flee when their grandmother laughs. In one text the coati is the helper and the monkeys are the jealous brothers, a direct bridge between this animal and its year sign. Use with the culture named, not as a 'you' reading. [k7]
- **Tupi (Brazil), the word.** Coati comes from Tupi kwáti (Old Tupi kûati, from Proto-Tupi-Guarani). Coatimundi is from Tupi kwatimúnde, 'older male coati not with a band', from kwáti plus múnde 'snare, trap'. Solitary males were once thought to be a separate species. The popular gloss 'belt-nose' is not supported by the sources I opened: unsourced, do not use. [k9] [k10] [k11]
- **Monkey year (use sparingly).** No real Chinese proverb is attached on the page (see fact audit). Monkey idioms exist but belong to the year hub and would repeat on 12 pages. (no source)
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'coati spirit animal means curiosity and community' totem-blog claims, and claims about coatis in Aztec or Guarani myth that I could not open in a primary or academic source. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Fire (Sagittarius, Jupiter, the archer) on yang Metal (Monkey, clever and quick). Both halves are restless and curious, which makes this one of the most outward crossings on the wheel. Fire controls Metal in Wu Xing, so the Sagittarian heat keeps working the Monkey's sharp edge; the Coati is the forager whose nose is always one crevice ahead of its plans.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: finding the thing nobody else thought to look for, under bark or under a story; bringing back what the group needs (the coati with the squash); humor and ease in a band; a sense for what is coming that reads like foresight.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: always onto the next log; investigation as a way to postpone choosing. The Monkey's pranks plus the archer's blunt tongue can wound without meaning to. The coatimundi of the Tupi name: the one who wanders off from the band and calls it freedom.
- **Love.** Our reading: falls for the person who keeps surprising them, and stays for the one who lets them roam and come back. Needs a band, not a cage, and a partner who does not read the wandering nose as wandering heart.
- **Money.** Our reading: generous and quick to spend on experiences (Jupiter); good at spotting deals (Monkey). Risk: too many open ventures, none finished.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: snorts, tail up, makes the claim loudly, then is over it. Rarely holds a grudge; can say the sharp true thing at the worst moment.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **IMPRECISE**: Header hanzi 長鼻浣熊 chángbí huànxióng (rail__han and serial-han)
  Dictionaries use 長鼻浣熊 as a general gloss for 'coati' (Youdao), so it is not wrong in plain use. But on Chinese Wikipedia 长鼻浣熊 is the mountain coati, Nasuella olivacea, and the page's species Nasua nasua is 南美浣熊 nánměi huànxióng (also 南浣熊 nán huànxióng). Recommend 南美浣熊 nánměi huànxióng for this page. [k19] [k18] [k17]
- **WRONG**: Nasua nasua, the South American Coati, inhabits tropical and subtropical forests from Arizona to Argentina.
  Nasua nasua lives in South America, Colombia and Venezuela to Uruguay and northern Argentina (ADW). The coatis of Arizona are white-nosed coatis, Nasua narica. Fix: 'South America, from Colombia and Venezuela to Argentina and Uruguay'. [k12] [k11] [k13]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: The snout can rotate up to 60 degrees in any direction.
  Repeated by zoo pages (Sedgwick County Zoo, for the white-nosed coati) and by Wikipedia without a citation. No primary measurement found. ADW says only that the nose is 'very flexible'. Soften to 'long, very flexible snout'. [k15] [k13] [k12]
- **OK (soften)**: Females and juveniles travel in bands of 10 to 30; adult males solitary except in the breeding season.
  ADW: bands 'with up to 30 individuals'; males join only in the breeding season. Wikipedia gives 15 to 30; Pro-Carnívoros gives 4 to 20. Say 'bands of up to about 30'. [k12] [k11] [k16]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: A band works a food site exhaustively, overturning every stone and probing every crevice before moving on.
  ADW confirms digging food out of rotting logs with claws and searching leaf litter with the snout, but nothing says sites are worked 'exhaustively' before abandonment. Cut the superlative. [k12]
- **OK (secondary)**: Holds its banded tail erect in dense vegetation as a signal that keeps band members in contact.
  Wikipedia (Coati): coatis often hold the tail erect, which keeps troops together in tall vegetation. ADW says only that the tail is used for balance. Acceptable with softening ('is thought to help'). [k13] [k12]
- **PARTLY UNVERIFIABLE**: After intensive morning foraging, coatis rest during the midday heat for several hours, built into the foraging cycle.
  San Diego Zoo (white-nosed coati): 'May rest for up to several hours during the day'. No source opened ties it to midday heat for Nasua nasua, and 'the system's recovery mechanism' is invented. Soften and note the species. [k14]
- **OK (soften)**: Coatis use scent-marking to map territory and communicate social information.
  Coatis rub gland secretions on objects in their home ranges (Wikipedia); white-nosed coatis wipe urine on trees and logs, most often between two males (San Diego Zoo). 'Map territory' overstates it. [k13] [k14]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Proverb 好奇心是知識的開端 hàoqíxīn shì zhīshi de kāiduān, 'the proverb of your year'
  No source shows this as a traditional Chinese proverb or chengyu; it reads as a modern sentence. The page admits the pairing is its own, but the heading 'The proverb of your year' implies tradition. Replace with a sourced idiom or relabel as a saying. (no source)
- **STYLE**: Source list: 'BirdLife International is not applicable; see: Emmons 1997'
  Template text leaked into the sources block. Gompper and Decker 1998 (Mammalian Species 580) is a real reference but I could not open it. (no source)
- **OK**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) claims
  No star is assigned from the sign pair; text says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. (no source)
- **STYLE**: Style: em dashes throughout fact and reading copy
  Remove in the rewrite. (no source)
