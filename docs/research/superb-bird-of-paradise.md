## The Superb Bird of Paradise as a symbol
- **Science as symbol (McCoy et al. 2018).** The male's display black is built to make the colour beside it look brighter: the authors describe 'an evolved optical illusion, which makes adjacent colours appear even brighter.' The bird's ordinary back feathers are normal black. The darkness exists to serve the one bright thing. This is the most usable, sourced image for the page. [s8] [s9]
- **New Guinea Highlands (birds of paradise as a family).** Bird-of-paradise plumes are wealth and relationship: Gimi men wore them at festivals and used them in bridal and funeral payments (Penn Museum); in the Wahgi Valley Marie Reay recorded feathers among the most valued goods, central to bride-price, and married women were barred from wearing them (ANU). Bell (Smithsonian) says feathers displayed kinship with birds and status. None of these sources names Lophorina, so say 'bird-of-paradise plumes', not 'superb' plumes. [s13] [s14] [s15]
- **European naming legend (family level).** Skins reached Europe from Magellan's voyage (Tidore, 1521) with feet removed; rumours said the birds came from the heavens and never landed, and Linnaeus named the greater bird of paradise Paradisaea apoda, 'footless'. Wallace (1869) records the Portuguese belief. Not about the superb species; use only as background on the family name 'paradise'. [s16]
- **Chinese (Rooster half): Han Shi Wai Zhuan.** The rooster's five virtues 五德 wǔ dé: 文 wén (culture, its crest), 武 wǔ (martial, its spurs), 勇 yǒng (courage), 仁 rén (kindness, calling others to food), 信 xìn (reliability, keeping time). Zdic confirms the five names and the source (juan 2); the body-part glosses in brackets are the usual reading of the passage but I did not open the full text, so check before quoting them. A displaying male that is also a timekeeper suits this crossing. Year-hub material, so use one line at most. [s6]
- **Chinese proverb already on the page.** 三年不飛，一飛沖天 (sān nián bù fēi, yī fēi chōng tiān), from the story of King Zhuang of Chu answering Wu Ju's riddle about a bird that neither flew nor sang for three years. The page's use is apt and honestly labelled as our pairing. [s7]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Any claim that the superb bird of paradise specifically has a named myth, totem or clan role. I found none; all New Guinea material I could open is about birds of paradise generally or other species (Raggiana, riflebird, lesser BoP). (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Earth (Taurus, Venus) meets yin Metal (Rooster). Earth generates Metal, so Taurus patience is the ore and the Rooster's polish is the blade. Taurus wants the thing slow, sensory and certain; the Rooster wants it exact, on time and witnessed. The result is a private perfectionist with one devastating reveal, a plain bird with a cape folded away.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: craft refined in private until it is effortless, exact timing, the ability to make one bright thing land by keeping everything around it quiet, steadiness in ordinary hours, loyalty once chosen.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the rehearsal that never ends. Taurus stubbornness plus Rooster pride in flawlessness can keep the cape folded for years. A second shadow from the Rooster side: wanting the audience's full attention once it finally performs, and sulking when it does not get it.
- **Love.** Our reading: Venus-ruled and yin; courtship is face to face and for one person at a time. Shows little for a long time, then everything at once. Needs a partner who is willing to come back to the log more than once, and who reads the plain months as preparation rather than distance. Ox and Snake years, and Dragon as secret friend, are natural allies.
- **Money.** Our reading: Taurus saving plus Rooster discipline: a steady accumulator who spends heavily on one quality item rather than many cheap ones. Risk: over-investing in polish (tools, gear, training) before taking the work to market.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: quiet until a line is crossed, then frank and precise (the Rooster is outspoken). Holds a grudge longer than it admits (fixed Earth). Practice: name the issue before it is perfectly phrased.

## Candidate lines
1. Most of the year you move through the understory in plain dark feathers, and the people who think they know you have never once seen the cape.
2. You will sweep leaves off the same fallen log for weeks before the one visitor comes, and you would rather keep sweeping than show the dance half learned.
3. Your black is the kind that drinks light, so the single bright thing you finally choose to show lands twice as hard.
4. The Bull in you wants it slow and certain; the Rooster in you wants it seen and called perfect. Your whole season is spent between the sweeping and the showing.
5. When you open the cape it is for one person, face to face, close enough to touch, and almost nobody else ever learns the shape exists.
6. From a branch above, your best moment looks like a smiling face bouncing in the dark. From inside it, you are counting every step.

Better: 風鳥 fēngniǎo (the Chinese family term; the genus Lophorina is 华美风鸟属 huáměi fēngniǎo shǔ, so superb bird of paradise is 华美风鸟 huáměi fēngniǎo), or the common 極樂鳥 jílèniǎo. | Lives in New Guinea's mid- and upper-montane forest; dark and easy to overlook most of the year. | OK | Australian Museum: mid to upper montane forest, 1000 to 2300 m, also disturbed forest and gardens. Note the 2017 to 2018 split: the 'superb' name now covers several Lophorina species depending on authority. | [s11] [s12] |
| Cape feathers are structurally super black: tightly packed, upward-angled barbule filaments trap light; absorb as much as 99.95 percent; among the darkest black in any bird. | WRONG (number framing) | McCoy et al. measured directional reflectance of 0.05 to 0.31% at normal (head-on) incidence. The paper itself notes directional reflectance is not absorption; total reflectance across angles was about 3.5% (vs 4.5 to 5.5% for normal black). So '99.95% of all light that strikes them' overstates. Say 'reflects as little as 0.05% of light hitting it head-on'. Structure description (microspiked barbules tilted out of plane, forming cavities) is OK. The page cites the right paper. | [s8] [s9] |
| A separate patch of ordinary black feathers on the back plays no part in courtship; super black reserved for the cape, around an iridescent blue-green breast shield raised in display. | OK | Physics World on McCoy: the superb's non-display back feathers had typical barbules and were more reflective. Breast shield display confirmed by Australian Museum and Scholes and Laman. Strictly the super black is on display plumage generally, not only the cape. | [s9] [s10] [s11] |
| At peak display the cape becomes a nearly flat oval around the body with the breast shield forward, likened to a smiling face. | OK | Scholes and Laman 2018: superba's open cape is oval, wider than tall; display likened to a black and blue bouncing 'smiley' face (citing Laman and Scholes 2012). | [s10] |
| Male performs a rapid, precise side-to-side hopping sequence in front of the female. | WRONG (wrong species) | Borrowed-species error. Scholes and Laman: the superb (superba) male bounces with shuffle-hop steps around the female; the quick side-stepping glide with no bounce is the Vogelkop species (L. niedda). Correct to 'bounces around her in shuffling hops'. | [s10] [s11] |
| Males are solitary and keep one or more small display courts, patches of forest floor cleared of leaf litter and defended, reused across seasons. | WRONG / UNVERIFIABLE | Scholes and Laman: both Lophorina species display on a fallen log in the understory; males toss leaves off the log, and the authors say it is unclear whether this is court clearing or a ritual display. Wikipedia says males clear a display area or scrub a branch smooth. 'Patches of forest floor', 'one or more', 'defended from rivals' and 'reused across seasons' I could not source. Say 'clears leaves from a fallen log where it dances'. Polygynous (Wikipedia) fits 'solitary' loosely. | [s10] [s12] |
| Split from the Vogelkop lophorina in a 2017 to 2018 revision, based specifically on the fanned display's shape (smiling oval vs frowning crescent) and motor pattern, rather than on plumage or song alone. | PARTLY WRONG | Irestedt et al. 2017 proposed splitting Lophorina into three species (niedda, superba, minor) from DNA and museum specimens; Scholes and Laman 2018 then confirmed niedda with courtship differences, including song (raspy screech vs whistled 'yiap') and wing-snap timing. So the split did not rest 'specifically' on display; display confirmed it. Also it was a three-way split. The neotype designation was disputed (Wikipedia). Retitle 'The species named by its dance'. | [s10] [s12] |
