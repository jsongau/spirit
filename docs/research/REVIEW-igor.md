# Igor's voice review: 10 research dossiers, two sample rewrites, batch voice guide

Reviewer: Igor (house voice). Date: 2026-11-08. Read: `docs/16-voice-doctrine.md`, BRIEF.md, all 10 dossiers (.md and .json), live `site/animals/koala/` and `site/animals/raven/`. No page edited. No git run.

Source ids in brackets, like [s8], point to the source list in that animal's own dossier (`koala.md`, `raven.md`). A sentence with no bracket is our reading.

---

## The headline finding

The 60 candidate lines already sound alike, and the brief caused it. BRIEF.md tells researchers to write "You tend to", "You often", "There are times when". They obeyed. **47 of 60 lines open with one of those three stems** (17, 17 and 13). Read the ten dossiers back to back and it is one voice reading one horoscope with ten animal names swapped in. Two lines are close to word-for-word duplicates across animals (koala 1 and luna-moth 1). Fix the brief before the next wave of research, or the next 134 dossiers will do the same.

The hedges themselves are fine. Doctrine 16 lists them as preferred. The problem is putting them at the front of every sentence. Hedge mid-sentence, or drop the hedge and let a plain statement carry it ("You heal alone").

### A2. The 10 weakest, rewritten

Each rewrite uses only material in that animal's own dossier.

1. **luna-moth 1** (duplicates koala 1: "know within a minute... weeks pretending")
   Rewrite: *You can find the one person in a crowded city who is thinking about you, and still ask a friend whether you imagined it.*
   Built on the male tracking a scent for miles to its exact source, plus Libra's second guess.

2. **luna-moth 4** ("You were named for the Moon..." The reader was not named for anything. A trivia line pretending to be a reading.)
   Rewrite: *Your clearest choices get made after the house goes dark, when nobody is waiting on your answer.*

3. **harpy-eagle 4** ("Your name comes from the harpies... take someone's whole heart with you." Trivia hook, and it makes a love line out of a soul-snatcher. Creepy, not uncanny.)
   Rewrite: *You can sit across from someone for a whole season without showing a thing. Then one afternoon you have chosen, and you rarely take it back.*
   Built on the long motionless perch (single source, so keep it as image, not stat).

4. **harpy-eagle 5** ("In old Chinese paintings the eagle and the bear together spell hero..." No pinyin, and it lands on a coffee-mug ending, "protecting something bigger than you".)
   Rewrite: *Old Chinese painters set the eagle (鹰 yīng) in a pine when they meant strength that holds. You are steadiest with one high place and a long view from it.*

5. **raven 5** ("The question worth asking is whether that person still exists." Therapist voice. Doctrine 16 bans it.)

   Built on the one-month fair-trader memory [raven s22].

6. **pronghorn 6** ("the bravest thing you can do is stop running" is a motivational poster.)

7. **pronghorn 4** ("fall for people who can keep up... anyone who tries to fence you in" is the "values freedom" slop doctrine names.)

10. **slow-loris 5** ("love slowly, then all the way, and you remember everything" is the shared slow-then-total love line; it fits 30 animals.)

### A3. Tics to ban (each one would make 144 pages sound alike again)

Counted across the 60 lines.

1. **Three-stem openers.** "You tend to" (17), "You often" (17), "There are times when" (13). Rule: at most one per section, never two sentences in a row. Remove the instruction from BRIEF.md.
2. **"You know first, everyone else catches up."** harpy 1, slow-loris 1, raven 1, pronghorn 1, star-nosed-mole 2, koala 1, luna-moth 1. Seven animals given the same gift. Ban the shape outright unless the animal's own sense organ carries it (the mole's star, the owl's ear).
3. **"Decide in seconds, pretend for weeks."** Literally duplicated (koala 1, luna-moth 1). Ban.
4. **"People mistake your quiet for X."** capybara 4, harpy 2, raven 3, slow-loris 2. Doctrine already says use it sparingly. Cap: one per batch of 12.
5. **"The room."** Eight lines put the reader in a room (reading it, calming it, loosening it, knowing it). A room is nobody's habitat. Use the animal's own setting: the tree, the cache, the colony, the burrow, the river.
6. **Unseen labor.** "Nobody noticed / nobody thanks you / nobody else realizes" (koala 2, capybara 2, arctic-tern 3). It flatters and it transfers to every animal. Cap: one per batch.
7. **Slow test, then love for life.** raven 6, slow-loris 5, harpy 3, luna-moth 5 (the fast version of the same line). Love sections must use a love behavior that belongs to this animal, or a named pairing tradition.
8. **Trivia as hook.** "Your name comes from...", "You were named for...", "In the old Borneo telling..." followed by the moral (harpy 4, harpy 5, luna-moth 4, slow-loris 6). Myths belong in the reading section with a credit, not squeezed into a one-line mirror.
9. **The coach's tail.** "The question worth asking", "the bravest thing you can do", "you are happiest when you let it", "the world rewards you for it". Any clause that tells the reader what to do or feel about the line. Delete on sight.
10. **"Quiet" and "exactly" as filler.** "Quiet" is in 8 of 60 lines. "Exact/exactly" turns up as an intensifier. Each page gets one "quiet", max.
11. **Forced threes.** koala 1 ("a place, a person or a plate"), koala 6, capybara 5 ("food, a spare room, a blanket"). Jay's rules ban them.

## B. Sample rewrites

### B1. KOALA (Taurus born in a Rabbit year)

Rhythm brief: long, unhurried sentences, a single Moon image, few paragraphs, settles on one quiet closing line. The koala does not hurry, so neither does the prose.

**Is this you?** (57 words)

- **Under pressure.** You go still and let the storm spend itself somewhere else. Pushed past that, the soft one bites, and nobody forgets it.
- **In love.** You choose by taste, then stay in that one tree long after others would have wandered.
- **The cost.** Some years you wait by the stump for the good thing to come back the way it came before.

Sources behind the lines: the soft one bites, 兔子急了还咬人 tù zi jí le hái yǎo rén [s6]; the stump, 守株待兔 shǒu zhū dài tù [s7]; choosing by taste [s14] [s15].

**What the Koala means** (about 330 words)

*Zodi Animal reading · Taurus born in a Rabbit year*

You were born under two signs that both look toward the Moon. Taurus is ruled by Venus, and the Moon is exalted there [s1]. The Rabbit of the Chinese zodiac lives on the Moon as the Jade Rabbit, keeping Chang'e company and pounding the elixir of long life [s6]. So you came in by soft light, and you have always done your best living slowly.

Taurus is fixed Earth, the sign that settles on the one good thing and stays [s1]. The Rabbit is yin Wood, gentle and alert, the animal that keeps watching while it rests [s3] [s4]. In you the two become a settled watcher. You can sit in one place all afternoon and still know which way you would leave.

The koala lives the same way. Even among trees of one species, it feeds less on leaves heavy with a toxic compound called sideroxylonal, and it passes over the poorest trees [s14] [s15]. It chooses by taste. So do you, and once a tree has fed you, a whole forest of better offers has trouble moving you.

In love you open late and close slowly. You need someone who reads your stillness as care. The old Chinese pairings give you three easy matches. The Clark's Nutcracker (Virgo, Goat year) shares your trine of Rabbit, Goat and Pig, and Virgo is Earth like you [s3] [s19]. The Tibetan Blue Bear (Capricorn, Dog year) is your secret friend, since the Dog is the Rabbit's hidden ally, and Capricorn is the third Earth sign [s3]. The Capybara (Cancer, Pig year) is trine kin, with Cancer's Water to soften your Earth [s3] [s1]. The hard one is the Devil's Flower Mantis: the Rooster clashes with the Rabbit, and Scorpio sits opposite Taurus [s3] [s1].

At work, give yourself one deep thing and no hurry. Tasting, curating and editing all reward a difference only you can taste.

The Gumbaynggirr tell of the Koala Brothers, who made a bridge home out of their own long insides [s8].

You have always carried the way back inside you.

### B2. RAVEN (Aquarius born in a Monkey year)

Rhythm brief: short, clipped, built in two halves around Odin's two ravens. Fragments allowed. Bold lead-ins. The section moves the way a raven checks a cache: look, move, look again. Different paragraph lengths, a different way in (myth first, reader second), a different way out (a turn, not a settling).

**Is this you?** (52 words)

- **Under pressure.** You go quiet. Then you move your plans somewhere nobody watched you put them.
- **In love.** A long, private test comes first. Pass it, and you get a partner for life who defends your corner with you.
- **The cost.** You remember who cheated you long after they forget the trade. Being right about people gets lonely.

Sources behind the lines: caching away from watchers [s17]; pairing for life [s13]; Monkey slow to settle, then committed [s3]; remembering an unfair trader a month on [s22] [s23].

**What the Raven knows** (about 330 words)

*Zodi Animal reading · Aquarius born in a Monkey year*

Odin kept two ravens. Huginn means thought. Muninn is usually read as memory [s6]. Every day they flew the world and came back to whisper what they had seen. Odin said he feared for Thought. He feared more for Memory [s6].

You were built with both birds.

**Thought** is your Aquarius half. Fixed Air, ruled by Uranus and, in the older books, Saturn [s1]. Inventive and at ease being the odd one out [s1]. Add the Monkey: yang Metal, quick to learn, quicker to play [s2] [s3]. You stand at the edge of the group and see how it fits. Then you do the clever thing nobody saw coming.

**Memory** is the half you guard. A raven that sees a rival watching will cache its food farther off, or wait [s17]. In a 2017 study, ravens still chose a fair trader over a cheat a month later [s22] [s23]. You keep things the same way. Your real plans sit in a second place. Your list of who did not play fair is short, and accurate.

Love comes late and stays. The Monkey is slow to settle and fully committed once it chooses [s3]. Ravens pair for life [s13]. You test someone for a long time without telling them. Pass, and you have them for good.

Your easy matches run through the Monkey's trine of Rat, Dragon and Monkey [s4]. The Meerkat (Gemini, Rat year) brings the trine and shared Air [s4] [s1]. The Giant Manta Ray (Libra, Dragon year) closes the trine, and Libra is the third Air sign [s4] [s1]. The Cuttlefish (Gemini, Snake year) is your secret friend, Snake and Monkey being a Six Harmonies pair [s4]. The hard one is the Harpy Eagle. Tiger clashes with Monkey, and Leo stands opposite Aquarius [s4] [s1].

Work that pays you to read many players at once suits you. Strategy and forecasting fit. So does fraud work, where a long memory is the job.

The Greeks said the raven was white until it told Apollo a hard truth too fast [s9].

You still tell the truth. You just check who is watching first.

## C. Voice guide for the batch writers (one paragraph)

Three rules keep 144 pages from collapsing into one. **First, build the animal's world before you write a sentence about the reader.** Make the private lexicon doctrine 16 asks for (eight to twelve nouns from the creature's habitat and its sourced behaviors), put every line in that place and never in "a room", and open no section with "You tend to", "You often" or "There are times when". **Second, give every page its own shape and rhythm, chosen before drafting and logged in a batch ledger.** Pick a way in (myth first, proverb first, a single image, a list of matches, a direct address) and a cadence (long and settled, clipped and staccato, call and answer). No two pages in a wave of 12 share an opening move, a closing move or a cadence. **Third, every line that claims to know the reader must hold something only this crossing has**: a sourced behavior, a named tradition, or a named pairing reason, cited. Run the swap test on each line. The recurring shapes in A3 (know-first, people-mistake, slow-then-total love, unseen labor, the coach's tail) are capped at one use per wave, across all 12 pages, and the ledger records who spent it.
