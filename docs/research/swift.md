## The Swift as a symbol
- **English heraldry (the martlet).** The martlet, 'little martin', is a footless heraldic bird modelled on swifts and martins, said never to roost from birth to death. It stands for continuous effort and the quest for knowledge, and was said to mark a fourth son who had to leave and make his own way (Wikipedia calls that last explanation implausible). Six martlets are on the arms of Sussex; also Pembroke College Cambridge, University College Oxford, McGill. [s7]
- **Ancient Greek and Latin naming.** Apus comes from Greek a-pous, 'without foot'; the ancients took the swift for a footless kind of swallow. [s6]
- **Beijing (北京雨燕 Běijīng yǔyàn).** The Beijing swift (subspecies pekinensis) has nested on the Zhengyang Gate 正陽門 Zhèngyáng Mén for over 600 years by local account; old Beijingers called it 樓燕 lóuyàn, 'tower swallow', and also 無腳鳥 wú jiǎo niǎo, 'footless bird', because it cannot walk on flat ground. It was the prototype for Nini 妮妮 Nīni, a 2008 Beijing Olympic mascot, and the first digital image for the Central Axis World Heritage bid. [s8] [s9]
- **Beijing to Africa (modern tracking).** 2014 tracking showed Beijing swifts leave in mid July, loop through Mongolia and Central Asia, and winter on the southern African plateau, returning around February. China Daily gives about 14,000 km each way; China News gives about 30,000 km and 37 countries (likely the round trip). Cite one and say which. [s8] [s9]
- **British and Scots folk names.** Devil bird, from its shrill screaming in summer (Sussex Wildlife Trust); the Scots dictionary records 'deviling' for the swift in Berwickshire and several English counties. [s10] [s11]
- **Chinese cuisine, related birds (use with species named).** 燕窩 yànwō, 'bird's nest' soup, is made from the solidified saliva nests of swiftlets (Aerodramus), close relatives in the swift order, not from the common swift. Only usable as 'the swift family glues its homes with saliva'. [s15]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Swift spirit animal' meanings on totem blogs; a Ted Hughes 'Swifts' quotation (poem not opened this session); any claim that 燕 yàn means swift. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: Mercury's quick mind on a Horse's legs, Air feeding Fire. Moves constantly, talks fast, covers ground no one else sees it cover. Yet the swift is a homing animal: one crevice, often one partner, a return each spring. Restless in method, faithful in direction.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: range, speed of thought, reading the weather of a situation and changing altitude before anyone speaks, and loyalty that survives long absence.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the footless-bird story taken too literally. Treating every landing as danger, letting motion stand in for closeness, and skipping the rest stop the real bird takes. Gemini scatter plus Horse dislike of orders can leave people unsure when you will be back.
- **Love.** Our reading: pairs for years and returns to the same ledge, but courtship and closeness happen on the move. Needs a partner who trusts the return and does not ask for daily landings. Goat-year partners (the secret friend) and Air-sign partners read this best.
- **Money.** Our reading: earns by covering ground; can be loose with spending while in motion. The nest is built fast from what passes by and then fixed hard: good at turning many small, gathered sources into one solid base once it decides to settle.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: flies low and fast in bad weather, then climbs out. Will talk circles around a fight or simply leave the sky above it. Rat-year types who want to pin things down tend to collide with it.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 燕 (rail__han and serial-han), pinyin yàn
  燕 yàn on its own means swallow (家燕 jiāyàn, barn swallow, a songbird). China News stresses swifts are not swallows (s8). The swift is 雨燕 yǔyàn (same in traditional characters); common swift 普通雨燕 pǔtōng yǔyàn; Beijing swift 北京雨燕 Běijīng yǔyàn; old Beijing name 樓燕 lóuyàn. Use 雨燕 yǔyàn. Same mistake class as 鷗 on the Arctic Tern page. [s8] [s6]
- **OK**: Fact 1: named from the Greek for 'without feet'; tiny clawed feet built to cling to vertical surfaces; cannot perch like most birds
  Wikipedia: Apus from Greek 'without foot'; very short legs used for clinging to vertical surfaces; never settle voluntarily on the ground (s6). ADW: grasping foot with toes opposed in pairs for vertical surfaces (s14). Strictly the Latin genus name is Apus, from Greek apous. [s6] [s14]
- **WRONG (outdated, understated)**: Fact 2: Common Swifts are 'unique among most birds' in staying aloft; an individual may spend an entire day on the wing, touching down only to feed young or roost at night
  Copied almost word for word from ADW (s14), which describes the breeding season. Hedenström et al. 2016 (Current Biology) showed swifts stay airborne about ten months outside breeding, over 99.5% of the time aloft, some never landing (s12). The page's own hero line says ten months, so the fact contradicts the page. 'Unique among most birds' is meaningless. Rewrite with the ten-month finding. [s12] [s14]
- **OK**: Fact 3: fly low in cold, wet or windy conditions, climb higher when weather is favourable
  ADW states exactly this (s14). [s14]
- **OK**: Fact 4: documented mating in flight, as well as on the nest
  ADW and Wikipedia ('often mate and sleep on the wing') (s14, s6). [s14] [s6]
- **OK, one clause UNVERIFIABLE**: Fact 5: saliva from enlarged breeding-season salivary glands glues feathers and plant material, much gathered on the wing, into a nest in a vertical crevice
  Nests of airborne material caught in flight, bonded with saliva (s6). 'Enlarged breeding-season salivary glands' is documented for other swifts and swiftlets but I found no opened source for the common swift; cut 'enlarged breeding-season'. [s6]
- **OK**: Fact 6: monogamous and faithful to the same site, same crevice and partner year after year; banded bird recaptured at 17; wild lifespan recorded up to 21
  ADW: a swift banded in Sweden re-trapped at 17; lifespan high 21 years; faithful to sites each year and mates during a breeding season (s14). Wikipedia: often return to the same site and partner year after year (s6). Say 'often the same partner'. [s14] [s6]
- **WRONG**: Hero 'The cost': 'the same instinct that lets you cross a continent without resting'; also 'cover enormous distance' copy
  The page's own cited study (Åkesson et al. 2012) found spring migrants used 'a restricted stopover area in West Africa in all but one individual' and autumn birds used sub-Saharan stopovers (s13). Change 'without resting' to 'without landing' or drop it. [s13]
- **OK**: Hero: 'the sky keeps the rest of you for ten months'
  Hedenström et al. 2016 (s12). [s12]
- **OK with softening**: Intro: 'returns, without fail, to one crevice and often one partner'
  Site fidelity is strong (s6, s14) but 'without fail' is an absolute; prefer 'year after year'. [s6] [s14]
- **OK**: Crossing: Horse years 2002, 2014, 2026; no January note
  Correct, and the lunar New Year note is rightly absent for Gemini. Fire Horse 2026 runs 17 Feb 2026 to 5 Feb 2027 (s4). [s4]
- **OK**: Proverb 老馬識途 lǎo mǎ shí tú, 'an old horse knows the road'
  Real idiom; fits homing. No source opened for its origin this session, so do not add an attribution on the page. 
- **OK**: Element section: 'Wu Xing has no slot for Air'
  Accurate framing; Air is a Western element, Wu Xing has five phases (s3, s1). [s3] [s1]
