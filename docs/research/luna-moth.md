## The moth as a symbol
- **Roman / Western naming**: Linnaeus named the species luna in 1758 after Luna, the Roman moon goddess, for the moon-like spots on its wings. A moth literally named for the Moon: good hook for a night creature whose page already lists New Moon as its power phase. [S1, S2]
- **Chinese (Book of Songs)**: 螓首蛾眉 qín shǒu é méi, 'cicada forehead, moth eyebrows', from the ode 碩人 Shuò Rén in the 詩經 Shījīng. The moth's feathered antennae became the classic image of a beautiful brow. In Chinese, the moth is first a sign of beauty. Strong fit for Venus-ruled Libra. [S11]
- **Chinese (idiom)**: 飞蛾扑火 fēi é pū huǒ, 'the moth throws itself into the flame': courting your own ruin. Used in Water Margin. Maps almost exactly onto the Luna Moth's shadow on the live page (spending everything on whatever is nearest). Use as the shadow image, framed gently. [S12]
- **Appalachian (US)**: A white moth is taken as a sign that ancestors are present. The luna moth is pale green, so use only as 'pale moths at night as visitors from the other side', not as a luna-specific belief. Secondary source citing a 2019 web source. [S13]
- **Hawaii / Central America**: The black witch moth stands for death but also the soul; in Hawaii, one seen soon after a loss may be the soul returning to say goodbye. A different species, so use only as general 'moth as soul messenger' lore. Secondary source (Quinn 2008). [S13]
- **Indigenous North American luna-moth meanings, Greek psyche as moth-soul**: unsourced, do not use. Many spirit-animal sites claim specific Native American luna moth teachings; I found no reliable source.

## The fusion (our interpretation)
- **Temperament**: (Our interpretation) Venus Air gives the scales: the urge to weigh, compare and choose well. Yin Earth Goat gives the soft heart that wants to settle and give. Together: a night creature that reads the air for the one right signal, then gives itself completely. Cardinal Libra starts things; the Goat wants peace once it lands. The tension is speed of feeling against the need to be sure.
- **Gifts**: (Ours) Taste that works as radar; reading people early; gentle presence that makes rooms calmer; devotion with no rationing once trust is earned; the ability to make an ordinary night feel like an occasion.
- **Shadow**: (Ours, anchored in the 飞蛾扑火 fēi é pū huǒ image and Libra's traditional indecision) Two opposite failures: hovering too long at the edge of a choice, then flying straight into the nearest flame when the waiting gets lonely. Goat's tendency to cling plus Libra's dislike of conflict can keep it in a bad bond to avoid a hard talk.
- **Love**: (Ours) Romantic, courtly, attentive to beauty and small rituals. Falls through atmosphere: scent, voice, a well-set table. Needs a partner who is the real signal and not just the nearest light. Once committed, loyal and warm, and quietly hurt by coarse behavior.
- **Money**: (Ours) Spends on beauty and on other people; can let a pleasant mood stand in for a budget. Does best with money that is set aside before the evening starts, so generosity has a ceiling it did not have to argue with.
- **Conflict**: (Ours) Avoids open fights, smooths, waits, then withdraws in silence. A grievance can grow in the dark for weeks. Best repair: a calm, private, scheduled talk where both sides are weighed fairly, which speaks to Libra's sense of fairness.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK**: Adults have vestigial mouthparts and no functional digestive system; they never eat and live about seven to ten days on caterpillar fat reserves.  
  Vestigial mouthparts, do not feed, 7 to 10 day adult life all confirmed. 'No functional digestive system' was not stated in the sources I read; safer wording: 'cannot feed'. [S1, S2]
- **OK**: Males detect a female's pheromones from several miles away, then fly upwind through obstacles to the exact source.  
  'Several miles' appears in Wikipedia; UF only says 'from a distance'. The upwind-through-obstacles detail is general moth behavior, not confirmed for luna specifically. Keep the distance, soften the rest. [S1, S2]
- **OK**: Spinning hindwing tails divert bat echolocation attacks; intact moths were caught far less often than tailless ones.  
  Barber et al. 2015: bats caught about 35% of intact moths vs about 81% with tails removed; about half of attacks hit the tails. These numbers would strengthen the page. [S3, S4]
- **WRONG**: A female typically mates with the first male that reaches her, after which egg-laying begins the same night and continues for several nights running.  
  First-male part is OK. But mating starts after midnight and egg-laying begins the EVENING AFTER mating, not the same night (Wikipedia and UF agree). Correction: 'egg-laying starts the following evening'. [S1, S2]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: A newly emerged moth spends two to three hours pumping fluid into its wings before it can fly.  
  Sources say wings enlarge 'over several hours' and that morning emergence leaves time for wings to dry before evening flight. The specific 'two to three hours' was not found. Suggest: 'several hours, from morning emergence to its first evening flight'. [S1, S2]
- **OK**: Each wing carries a single large eyespot, thought to startle or confuse a predator.  
  UF: discal eyespots on both fore and hind wings. Wikipedia: eyespots thought to confuse predators. [S1, S2]
