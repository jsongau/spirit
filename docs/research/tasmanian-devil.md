## The Tasmanian Devil as a symbol
- **Mainland Aboriginal Australia (Lake Nitchie, western NSW).** A man buried about 6,820 years ago (plus or minus 200) at Lake Nitchie wore a necklace of 178 pierced Tasmanian devil canine teeth (Wentworth Shire heritage listing). Wikipedia gives 49 animals and quotes archaeologist Josephine Flood that the devil was hunted for its teeth. A strong, quotable, non-commodity item: the devil's bite worn as an honour by a person, thousands of years before the species vanished from the mainland. [wentworth] [wiki-td]
- **Aboriginal Western Australia (Jukkan 2 shelter, Pilbara).** Devil teeth and jaw fragments 7,000 to 47,000 years old lie among human occupation debris; dozens of bones carry devil chew marks, suggesting devils scavenged at human camps for tens of thousands of years (Archaeology magazine, 2026). The devil as the camp's night neighbour. No cultural use of these teeth is reported; do not claim one. [arch2026]
- **Tasmanian Aboriginal (palawa).** The palawa kani name is purinina. Recorded names include tarrabah, poirinnah and par-loo-mer-rer (Wikipedia); the Tasmanian Government gives poorininah and poirinna. Indigenous people and devils sheltered in the same caves in Tasmania. No story or totem meaning could be sourced; give names only, attributed. [wiki-td] [nre-about] [nre-faq]
- **Colonial Tasmania.** European settlers heard screams, coughs and growls in the bush at night; some thought of hellhounds, and the name 'devil' stuck (Tasmanian Government). Explorers called it 'Beelzebub's pup' after hearing its far-carrying night calls (Wikipedia). The government adds that the snarls are mostly bluff, a ritual to minimise harmful fighting. [nre-faq] [wiki-td] [nre-about]
- **Modern Tasmania and popular culture.** Emblem of the Tasmanian Parks and Wildlife Service; named Tasmania's state emblem in 2015; inspired the Looney Tunes character Taz in 1954 (Wikipedia). The Tasmanian Government calls it a Tasmanian icon. The Chinese name of the cartoon is also 袋獾 dài huān. [wiki-td] [nre-about]
- **Chinese name.** 袋獾 dài huān, literally pouch (袋 dài) badger (獾 huān); also called 塔斯馬尼亞惡魔 tǎsīmǎníyà èmó, Tasmanian devil. Same characters in traditional script. [zhwiki-td]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'Tasmanian devil spirit animal / totem' meanings on blogs, and any Tasmanian Aboriginal Dreaming story about the devil. None could be traced to a named nation or source.

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: fixed Water (Scorpio) over yang Earth (Dog). Both halves are about staying: Scorpio stays with what it knows, including what would be easier to drop; the Dog stays with its people and its ground. Earth holds Water, so the feeling has walls around it: a small territory, a few loyalties, all defended. The devil's own biology fits: one island, a loud ritual at the carcass that mostly stops real fights, and a bite that finishes the job.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: finishes what others abandon, says the hard thing out loud, reads mood shifts early (the Dog's perceptiveness plus Scorpio depth), loyal for decades, unafraid of the messy part of any job.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the strength is the wound. The bite that defines the devil is also how its cancer spreads, and the fierce loyalty can become grudge, suspicion of strangers and stress carried alone. Bluntness from both halves can hurt people who were already on side.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow to let anyone into the den, then all in. Loves through presence and protection more than words (Scorpio feels more than it says; the Dog is not built for romantic gestures). Needs a partner who can hear the snarl as bluff and stay at the table.
- **Money.** Our reading: fixed signs and an Earth year animal favour holding over trading. Waste offends this nature (the devil eats bone and fur). Good at salvaging value others left behind; risk is hoarding resources the way it hoards hurts.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: loud first, physical rarely. The display is meant to settle who gets what without blood. The danger is the fight that has gone past display, because neither Scorpio nor the Dog backs down easily.

## Best matches
- **Pallas's Cat** (`pallass-cat`): Scorpio Rabbit. Rabbit is the Dog's secret friend, and the same Scorpio sky: a quiet ally who understands depth without needing it explained.
- **Sea Otter** (`sea-otter`): Cancer Rabbit. Secret friend (Dog and Rabbit) plus a fellow Water sign: soft where the devil is loud, and just as loyal to its raft.
- **Orca** (`orca`): Scorpio Horse. Horse is in the Dog's trine; same sign. Two pack animals with strong family loyalty and a reputation bigger than the truth.
- **Moon Bear** (`moon-bear`): Cancer Tiger. Tiger is in the Dog's trine and Cancer is a Water sign that shares Scorpio's protectiveness.
- **Moose** (`moose`): Taurus Horse. Trine (Horse and Dog) plus the Taurus and Scorpio opposition, Earth holding Water: steady, stubborn, devoted.

## Hard matches
- **Gila Monster** (`gila-monster`): Scorpio Dragon. Dragon and Dog are a six-clash pair. Same sign, same bite: two animals who hold on and will not let go, aimed at each other.
- **Rhinoceros Hornbill** (`rhinoceros-hornbill`): Leo Dragon. The Dragon-Dog clash plus a Leo-Scorpio square, Fire against Water: spotlight against the den (Travel China Guide notes the Dragon's craving for the spotlight against the Dog's quiet).

## Candidate lines
1. Your screech at the carcass is mostly theatre. The people who know you learn that the noise is how you keep the real bite from happening.
2. Others take the soft cuts and wander off. You stay for the bone and the fur, and the job is finished only when there is nothing left of it.
3. Your island is small and you know every gully of it by smell. Leaving it would cost you the map, and the map is half of who you are.
4. The Dog in you guards a handful of people, and the Scorpio in you guards what they told you in the dark. Both sleep in the same den.
5. What you do hardest, the bite and the showing up at every fight that matters, is also the place you get hurt. You know that, and you still go.
6. Thousands of years before anyone called you a devil, someone wore your teeth on a string to their grave. Your bite has been mistaken for menace for a long time.
