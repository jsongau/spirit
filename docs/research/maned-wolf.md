# Maned Wolf: research dossier

Aquarius born in a Tiger year. Slug `maned-wolf`. Element: Wood (from the Tiger), with Aquarius's Air as the second note. Researched 2026-11-08. Read only; no site file changed, no git run.

Brackets like [s4] point to the source list at the end. Anything marked "Our reading" is interpretation, not tradition.

## Top findings

1. **Wrong date windows.** The crossing note lists Tiger years as 1998, 2010, 2022 and only warns that early-year births can be Ox. Tiger years run into the next January or February, so Aquarians born 20 Jan to 15 Feb 1999, 20 Jan to 2 Feb 2011 and 20 to 21 Jan 2023 are Maned Wolves and are never told. Most 2010 Aquarians (20 Jan to 13 Feb) are Ox [s16].
2. **Header character is wrong.** 狼 láng means wolf, and carries greed and cruelty in classical Chinese [s11]. The maned wolf is 鬃狼 zōngláng, 'mane wolf' [s8] [s19]. Show it with pinyin.
3. **Three Animal facts are wrong.** It is not strictly solitary (monogamous pairs share one territory and walk it apart) [s3] [s4]; lobeira has at least four other main seed dispersers [s5]; and its closest relative is the bush dog [s2]. The pair-apart behavior is better material for the love reading than the 'solitary' framing was.
4. **Best new folklore:** Tupi guará means 'red' [s12]; Brazil hunted it for its eyes as luck charms [s2]; Bolivian riders used its leather saddle against bad luck [s2]; it rides the R$200 note [s2]. On the Chinese side, 一山不容二虎 yī shān bù róng èr hǔ and 调虎离山 diào hǔ lí shān are real tiger idioms that fit this animal [s9] [s10]. The page's own proverb, 虎行千山, could not be found anywhere.

## The animal as a symbol

- **Brazil, Tupi and Guarani names.** The Brazilian name guará comes from Tupi and is glossed as 'red', for the coat. The Guarani name aguara guasu means 'large fox'. Neither name calls it a wolf: the people who lived beside it named its color and its size. [s12] [s2] [s8]
- **Brazil, folk belief.** In a few parts of Brazil maned wolves were hunted for body parts, above all the eyes, which were believed to be good-luck charms. Other parts were thought to treat bronchitis, kidney disease and snakebite. The Smithsonian notes some are still killed for parts believed to be magical; Brazilian conservationists call these beliefs unfounded and a cause of decline. [s2] [s1] [s4] [s12]
- **Bolivia, folk belief.** Riding on a saddle made of maned wolf leather was believed to protect the rider from bad luck. [s2]
- **Brazil, national emblem.** Known as the symbol of the Cerrado. Chosen for the R$200 banknote, released by Brazil's Central Bank in 2020: the animal now rides on the country's highest note. [s2] [s13] [s12]
- **Chinese, the tiger's mountain.** 一山不容二虎 yī shān bù róng èr hǔ, 'one mountain cannot hold two tigers': two rivals cannot share one place. A second real idiom, 调虎离山 diào hǔ lí shān, 'lure the tiger off its mountain', means drawing an opponent out of its stronghold to beat it. Both fit an animal defined by its own held ground. [s9] [s10]
- **Chinese, the wolf character.** The wolf 狼 láng is a mostly pejorative figure in classical Chinese writing, standing for harm, greed and cruelty (狼狈为奸 láng bèi wéi jiān, 'wolf and bei plotting together'; 豺狼虎豹 chái láng hǔ bào, 'jackals, wolves, tigers and leopards' for vicious people). This matters for the page header, which uses 狼 alone. [s11] [s10]
- **Online 'maned wolf spirit animal / totem' meanings.** Unsourced, do not use. Only generic spirit-animal blogs with no tradition or citation behind them were found.

## The fusion (our reading)

**Temperament.** Aquarius is fixed Air, the sign that thinks its own way and keeps thinking it; the Tiger is yang Wood, brave, territorial and happy alone. The Maned Wolf person holds ground that nobody else would have thought to claim, and holds it calmly. People rarely know what to call them, the way the animal is neither wolf nor fox, and they long ago stopped needing a label. They read the field from height before they move.

**Gifts.** original sight that comes from standing a little apart; a steadiness under pressure that does not depend on anyone agreeing; appetite for the overlooked thing (the fruit a predator is not supposed to want); and a habit of leaving places richer than they found them.

**Shadow.** Aquarius detachment plus Tiger pride. When misnamed or misread, this person withdraws to height rather than explain, and the distance can harden into a fixed position. The Tiger's impetuous streak shows up as a sudden, loud refusal after a long silence. Their weak spot is the old trick of 调虎离山 diào hǔ lí shān: pull them off their own ground (a fight about labels, a role that is not theirs) and they lose what made them strong.

**Love.** a partnership of two territories. Maned wolf mates share and defend one range but travel and rest apart for most of the year [s3] [s4]. This person loves the same way: loyal for the long haul, but needing whole stretches of their own path. They court with attention, not display. The right partner hears the evening call and answers it, and does not read separate days as a cooling.

**Money.** earns best from a niche nobody else has mapped. Tends to back unusual bets that look odd to others and pay off slowly. Risk: Aquarius contrariness and Tiger impulse can mean refusing good, ordinary advice simply because it is ordinary.

**Conflict.** first move is retreat to altitude, out of reach, to decide what is true. Then one clear statement, like the single bark after dusk. Rarely shouts, rarely repeats. Most conflicts end with them quietly leaving the mountain rather than sharing it, which looks peaceful and can be costly.

## Careers (our reading)

- **Field research, ecology, conservation.** Long solo stretches in open country and patient reading of a landscape suit fixed Air's independent mind and the Tiger's comfort alone.
- **Independent strategy or consulting.** Paid to see the whole field from above and say it once, without joining the group they advise.
- **Urban or landscape planning, design of public spaces.** Shaping ground that other people will use for decades, a working version of the seed-disperser habit. Aquarius's wide social view meets Wood's growth.
- **Investigative journalism or long-form writing.** Tiger nerve plus Aquarian contrarian instinct: following the story nobody else picked up, and publishing one call that carries.
- **Founding a small, unusual business.** A niche of one, like a genus of one. Works when they own the territory outright; struggles under rules with no clear purpose (Kerykeion notes Aquarius strains where conformity is prized) [s15].

## Fact audit of the live page

| Fact on page | Verdict | Note | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| The maned wolf has the longest legs relative to body size of any canid, an adaptation for visibility over tall grass, not for pursuit speed. | **UNVERIFIABLE** | No source found for 'longest legs relative to body size of any canid'. Wikipedia says 'exceptionally long legs' and 'the tallest of the wild canids'; use that. The visibility function is a hypothesis (ADW: legs 'may serve' to see above tall grass), so 'not for pursuit speed' is stated too firmly. | [s2] [s3] [s1] |
| Roughly 50% of the diet is plant material; primary food is the lobeira (Solanum lycocarpum), sometimes called the wolf apple. | **OK** | Smithsonian gives about 50 percent fruit and vegetables. Wikipedia puts wolf apple at 40 to 90 percent of the diet depending on place and season. | [s1] [s2] |
| Sole living species in the genus Chrysocyon, belonging to no broader canid group within its subfamily. | **WRONG** | First half OK. Second half wrong: molecular studies consistently place its closest living relative as the bush dog (Speothos), with a split about 3 million years ago. Correct line: the only species in its genus, with the bush dog as its nearest kin. 'Largest canid in South America' (Meet section) is OK. | [s1] [s2] |
| Strictly solitary; adults maintain overlapping home ranges of 25 to 30 square kilometers but actively avoid contact with conspecifics outside a brief breeding period. | **WRONG** | Maned wolves live as monogamous pairs that share and defend one territory; mates hunt, travel and rest apart and rarely meet outside the breeding season. Boundaries between neighboring pairs are strictly observed, so ranges of different pairs do not overlap much. Size varies a lot: about 27 square km average in Dietz's study (ADW), 26 square km (Smithsonian), average 44 and range 5 to 105 square km (Pró-Carnívoros). Suggested fix: 'Mated pairs share a territory of roughly 25 to 45 square kilometers yet spend most of the year walking it separately.' | [s3] [s1] [s4] |
| Primary territorial call is a deep roar-bark, audible several kilometers away, lower and more powerful than calls of related canids. | **UNVERIFIABLE** | The roar-bark as a long-range call used to locate the mate and keep other wolves away is OK. No source found giving a distance in kilometers, and none comparing its pitch or power with other canids. Say 'one of its only long-range calls' instead. | [s1] [s6] [s7] |
| Maned wolves spread lobeira seeds ... making them key dispersal agents for plants that rely on no other animal for this function. | **WRONG** | A USP thesis on lobeira names five main seed dispersers: maned wolf, bush dog, crab-eating fox, tapir and the saúva leafcutter ant.
