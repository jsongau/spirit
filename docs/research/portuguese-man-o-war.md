## The Portuguese Man o' War as a symbol
- **English (sailing era).** The name comes from the man-of-war, an armed sailing warship: the float read as a ship under sail. 'Portuguese' is commonly linked to a Portuguese ship type; Wikipedia gives only the warship origin, so do not assert more. [s6]
- **Portuguese and Brazilian.** Caravela Portuguesa, the Portuguese caravel: the same ship image in another language. [s6]
- **Japanese.** カツオノエボシ katsuo-no-eboshi, written 鰹の烏帽子, 'the bonito's eboshi', a tall court cap. Japanese Wikipedia says the name arose on the Miura and Izu peninsulas because it rides in on the Kuroshio current when the bonito arrive (tagged citation needed). Also called デンキクラゲ denki kurage, 'electric jellyfish', for the shock-like sting. [s7]
- **Chinese.** 僧帽水母 sēngmào shuǐmǔ, 'monk's-cap jellyfish', also 葡萄牙戰艦 Pútáoyá zhànjiàn, 'Portuguese warship'. One culture sees a cap of renunciation, another a warship. [s8]
- **Australian English.** Bluebottle, for the blue float. (2025 genetics also separated several Physalia lineages, so an Australian bluebottle may not be Physalia physalis.) [s6] [s7]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** 'Man o' war spirit animal / jellyfish totem' meanings exist only on blogs; no traditional myth about Physalia was found in any culture. The symbol here is its names, not a folklore.

- **Conflict.** Our reading: does not argue, does not move toward you; the defense is already deployed below the surface. Practice: name the boundary before someone swims into it.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (nuance)**: A siphonophore of four functionally distinct zooid types (pneumatophore, dactylozooids, gonozooids, gastrozooids), each unable to survive independently.
  NOAA: 'each of the four specialized parts'. Wikipedia: 'seven different types of zooids have been described' and they 'all depend on each other for survival'. Four is the common simplification; say 'four main kinds of parts'. [s5] [s6]
- **WRONG (mechanism overstated)**: Asymmetric float; right- and left-handed individuals; this determines whether an animal drifts toward shore or away under the same wind.
  Handedness is real, but it sets the sailing angle, about 45 degrees to the left or right of the wind; left-handed animals sail to the right of the wind, right-handed to the left. Whether that is toward or away from a shore depends on the coast and wind; the proposed benefit is that only about half a population is pushed ashore. Rewrite as 'sails about 45 degrees to one side of the wind'. [s6]
- **WRONG (length)**: Tentacles can extend up to 165 feet below the surface; nematocysts fire on contact and keep firing after death and after detachment.
  The page's own source, NOAA, now says tentacles average 10 m (about 30 ft) and 'may extend by as much as 30 meters (about 100 feet)'; Wikipedia agrees. 165 ft (50 m) is an old figure. The sting after death and detachment is OK: 'detached tentacles and dead specimens... can sting just as painfully', potent for hours or days. [s5] [s6]
- **WRONG (overstated)**: Nematocysts fire regardless of species, size or context and cannot distinguish prey from predator from bystander.
  The man-of-war fish lives among the tentacles, has at least one antigen to the toxin and avoids being stung by swimming around them. 'Fires the same on everything' is not supported. Also, the intro's 'post-death sting persistence' is fine. Cut the absolute. [s6]
- **WRONG (half)**: Large concentrations appear after storms; individual colonies drift alone and aggregation is circumstantial.
  Winds do drive them into bays and onto beaches (Wikipedia), but 'individual colonies drift alone' contradicts NOAA, which says they 'sometimes float in legions of 1,000 or more'. 'After storms' specifically is UNVERIFIABLE from what I opened; say 'onshore winds'. [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: All zooids are genetically identical clones of a single founding polyp; one individual in several forms.
  Wikipedia: 'All zooids... develop from the same single fertilized egg and are therefore genetically identical'; the first structures are the float and a protozooid. NOAA: zooids are clones working as one. [s6] [s5]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (taxonomy changed)**: Intro: Physalia physalis is found in the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific oceans.
  NOAA says 'mostly in tropical and subtropical seas'. A 2025 study identified five lineages (P. utriculus worldwide, P. megalista in the southern oceans, P. mikazuki in Japan), so the three-ocean range for P. physalis alone is now uncertain. Say 'warm seas worldwide' for the genus. [s5] [s6]
- **OK**: Header hanzi (rail__han and serial-han): 僧帽水母, pinyin sēngmào shuǐmǔ.
  Chinese Wikipedia's lead name for Physalia physalis is 僧帽水母, also called 葡萄牙戰艦 Pútáoyá zhànjiàn. Same characters in traditional and simplified. Pinyin is shown on the page. [s8]
