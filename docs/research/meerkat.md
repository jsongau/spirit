## The Meerkat as a symbol
- **Kalahari field science (the sentinel).** A meerkat climbs a mound or branch and keeps watch while the group digs. Britannica: members take turns, but only once they have eaten their fill, so sentinels are 'not really the altruists they were once thought to be' (after Clutton-Brock et al. 1999, 'Selfish sentinels'). Manser (1999) showed the guard's quiet surveillance calls tell foragers a sentinel is on duty. The watcher who keeps talking while it watches is the clearest Gemini-Rat image the animal offers. [m2] [m3] [m8]
- **Kalahari field science (the teacher).** Thornton and McAuliffe (2006, Science), Kalahari Meerkat Project: helpers kill or disable prey for young pups, pulling the scorpion's sting, then move older pups gradually to live prey and nudge it back if the pup hesitates. A documented case of teaching in a wild mammal; a strong Mercury bridge. [m5]
- **Alarm language.** Meerkat alarm calls differ by predator type and urgency: aerial alarms make listeners crouch, terrestrial alarms make them scan and move, and a separate recruitment call gathers the group to a snake (Manser, Bell and Fletcher 2001). A vocabulary for danger. [m1] [m3]
- **Afrikaans / Dutch (name).** 'Meerkat' came into English from Afrikaans. Proposed origins: mier 'ant' plus kat; or an older Dutch name for a monkey, linked to Old High German mericazza and Sanskrit markaṭa. Meer 'lake' plus kat is considered unlikely. Usable only as a light aside. [m1]
- **Chinese (name).** The Chinese name is 狐獴 hú měng, 'fox mongoose'. 獴 měng alone means mongoose in general. [m9] [m10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** The widely repeated claim that southern African peoples called meerkats 'sun angels' who guard villages from a 'moon devil' appears only on blogs and craft sites; no ethnographic source was found (SANBI, Britannica, Wikipedia and the Namibian Red List account have nothing). Do not use. No San, Khoekhoe or Tswana meerkat story could be sourced either.

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Air (Gemini) meets yang Water (Rat). Air sits outside the five-phase wheel, so it does not feed or check the Water; it rides beside it. Gemini brings the chatter and the two-way attention, the Rat brings the nose for danger and the eye for the next meal. The meerkat does both at once: digs with the group, keeps one ear on the guard's calls, and takes its turn on the mound.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: early warning, a precise vocabulary for different kinds of trouble, the instinct to teach in small steps, sociability that holds a group together, quick recovery after a scare.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Rat's reputation for small nerve, the Gemini habit of spreading attention thin. Every shadow on the sand gets a bark. The mob can come to rely on you for the watch and forget to offer you a shift.
- **Love.** Our reading: shows love by watching over and by feeding. Talks a lot and listens hard. Needs a partner who takes a turn on the mound and who does not mistake an alarm call for panic. Rat starts romances easily; Gemini gets bored; the fix is a shared project, a burrow worth keeping.
- **Money.** Our reading: Rat thrift plus Gemini side projects. Keeps several bolt-holes: savings, a second income, a backup plan. Risk: too many small digs, none deep enough.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: calls it out early and loud, then gathers allies before facing it. Alone, the meerkat retreats to the burrow and barks from the entrance; with its group, it closes in. In people: avoids one-on-one showdowns, wins as a coalition.

2. One call for the hawk, another for the jackal: in the Kalahari you would never waste the same bark on both, and the people near you learn your two voices.
