## The Caracara as a symbol
- **Rioplatense Spanish (Argentina, Uruguay).** Carancho is everyday slang for an opportunist, usually a predatory lawyer or union boss; in Argentina an ambulance-chasing lawyer is called a carancho, the premise of Pablo Trapero's film Carancho (2010, Cannes Un Certain Regard). Sayings: 'carancho que comió, voló' (the carancho that has eaten flies off: someone who loses interest once fed); 'cada carancho en su rancho' (every carancho in its own hut); 'desordenado como nido de carancho' (messy as a carancho nest). The opportunist shadow of this crossing comes straight from folk speech. [s6] [s8]
- **Andean (Quechua-speaking Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador).** Called Aqchi and held sacred: in founding myths it travels with Wiraqocha (Viracocha) as he travels to bring order to the world, black-bodied, golden-beaked, with magical knowledge of kunan pacha, the present time. Only source is Spanish Wikipedia with no primary citation opened; treat as 'recorded in', verify before use. [s6]
- **Mapuche (Chile).** The Mapudungun name for the bird is traru. The war leader Lautaro (Lef-Traru, c. 1534 to 1557), who beat Pedro de Valdivia at Tucapel in 1553, carried it in his name; English Wikipedia glosses Lef-Traru as 'swift hawk', Spanish Wikipedia ties traru to the caracara. Name the man and the nation, keep it factual. [s6] [s9]
- **Guarani and Tupi (South America).** The name caracara is onomatopoeic, from Old Tupi karakará; Félix de Azara recorded that the Guarani called it Caracará because the bird keeps calling it. The Carcarañá river's name is said to mean 'devil carancho' in Guarani. [s5] [s6]
- **Darwin, Voyage of the Beagle (1839).** Darwin's 'Carrancha' on the La Plata savannahs: it 'runs (in contradistinction to hopping)', waits while 'the Gallinazo commences the feast', pursues the vulture 'till that bird is compelled to vomit up the carrion', is 'said to be very crafty', and calls with its head thrown back until 'the crown almost touches the lower part of the back'. Darwin also called it 'inactive, tame, and cowardly', which cuts against a heroic reading. [s7]
- **Mexico (Aztec eagle debate).** Ornithologist Rafael Martín del Campo proposed that the sacred 'eagle' of the Aztec codices was the caracara; the golden eagle is Mexico's national bird and the one on the flag. Use only as a scholarly proposal, never as fact. [s5]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Caracara 'spirit animal' or totem meanings (resourcefulness, rebirth, etc.) appear only on totem blogs; no traditional source found. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Fire (Sagittarius) wants the whole open field; yang Earth Dog wants one territory and one loyalty. The caracara holds both: a bird that keeps a year-round home range with its mate and still walks the roadside every morning to see what the night left. Fire feeds Earth in the five-phase cycle, so the ranging half keeps the loyal half fed.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: reads ground others overfly; eats from any channel without pride; loyalty that shows as presence at the right carcass, not speeches; a face that cannot hide real engagement.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the carancho of the pampas sayings. Arriving where someone else did the work, then flying off once fed ('carancho que comió, voló'). Sagittarian bluntness plus Dog stubbornness can make the harsh call land harder than meant.
- **Love.** Our reading: slow to pair (Dog caution), then a long, territorial bond; preens and is preened. Needs a partner who lets it range by day and trusts it home by night. Fear: being fenced into a single field.
- **Money.** Our reading: a scavenger's economy. Good at salvage, second markets and finding value others discard; risk is living permanently on leftovers instead of building one thing of its own.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: assesses on foot, then pushes in hard and takes the carcass, the way caracaras dominate vultures. Rarely starts the fight, rarely loses it. Watch the head-thrown-back loudness once committed.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **OK (imprecise)**: Header hanzi 卡拉卡拉鷹 (kǎlākǎlā yīng) on rail and serial
  卡拉卡拉鷹屬 is the Chinese Wikipedia name for the genus Caracara, a phonetic loan plus 鷹 yīng (hawk/eagle). Acceptable. The species on the page, the crested caracara, is 鳳頭巨隼 fèngtóu jùsǔn ('crested giant falcon'), also seen as 鳳頭卡拉鷹 fèngtóu kǎlākǎlā yīng. 鳳頭巨隼 is more precise and avoids calling a falcon a hawk; pinyin on page is correct. [s15]
- **WRONG**: Intro: 'a large, falcon-shaped hawk of the Americas, technically a falcon'
  Backwards. It is a falcon (family Falconidae) that looks like a long-legged hawk. Rewrite: 'a falcon built like a long-legged hawk'. [s10] [s11] [s5]
- **OK (cut the hyperbole)**: Regularly walks and runs on the ground to investigate terrain and chase prey, 'spending more time on foot than most falcons spend in a lifetime'
  HawkWatch: 'it walks as much as it flies'; Wikipedia: often seen on foot searching for food; Darwin 1839: 'It runs (in contradistinction to hopping)'. The lifetime comparison is unsourced; cut it. [s11] [s5] [s7]
- **OK (superlative unverifiable)**: Eats live prey, carrion, eggs, insects and plant material; one of the most generalist diets in the falcon family
  All About Birds and Wikipedia list insects, small vertebrates, fish, crabs, eggs, carrion and fruit. 'One of the most generalist in the falcon family' has no source found; soften or cut. Same for the Mirror line 'No other raptor maintains this range simultaneously' (UNVERIFIABLE, cut). [s10] [s5]
- **OK (mechanism unverifiable)**: Associates with vultures at carcasses and can displace them, using the vulture's detection ability to locate food
  Dominance over black and turkey vultures is documented (Wikipedia; Darwin saw it chase a vulture until it vomited its meal). Sierra Club notes caracaras wait for vultures to open a carcass. That caracaras use vultures' detection to find food was not found in any source opened; reword to 'feeds beside vultures and often drives them off'. [s5] [s7] [s13]
- **WRONG (direction)**: Bare orange-red face 'flushes deeper red during arousal, excitement, or alarm'
  Color change with mood is real (Wikipedia; FNAI ties it to blood flow), but HawkWatch says adult facial skin is orange-red 'becoming yellowish when excited', and Sierra Club also says it goes to yellow when excited. Rewrite: 'the bare face shifts color with mood, from orange-red toward yellow when excited, by most accounts'. [s11] [s13] [s5] [s12]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (likely borrowed)**: Some populations observed using sticks and other objects to dig out prey or move obstacles
  No record of crested caracara stick tool use found. The caracara cognition result that exists is the striated caracara (Phalcoboenus australis), a different species, solving puzzle boxes on the Falklands without tools (Harrington et al. 2023). Cut the trait, or swap to a sourced crested caracara behavior. [s14]
- **UNVERIFIABLE (framing misleading)**: Forms loose, flexible groups of two to ten birds that gather at food and disperse
  HawkWatch: 'often occurs in small groups concentrating around carrion'; Wikipedia: 'typically solitary', several may gather at a big food source; FNAI: adult pairs hold territory year-round, often for years. No source gives 'two to ten'. Rewrite around territorial pairs that tolerate gatherings at carrion. [s11] [s5] [s12]
- **OK (taxonomy dated)**: Source list cites Caracara cheriway (Morrison 1996) and IUCN 'Caracara plancus (Southern Crested Caracara)'
  Northern and southern forms are now one species, Caracara plancus, with subspecies cheriway and plancus. Update the label. [s5] [s15]
- **OK**: Proverb 隨機應變 (suí jī yìng biàn), labeled as Zodi Animal's own pairing with the Dog year
  Real idiom, honestly labeled. Pinyin present on tiles.
