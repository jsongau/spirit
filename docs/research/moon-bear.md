## The animal as a symbol
- **Japan, matagi hunters of Tohoku and upland Niigata, Akita, Nagano.** The moon bear (tsukinowaguma) was a messenger (otsukai) of the mountain deity, yama no kami. Niigata folklore says the white crescent was left by a silk-wrapped amulet the mountain god gave the bear. Bears with no mark (minaguro, 'all black') were the most sacred; a hunter who shot one by accident offered it to the god and stopped hunting. After a kill, hunters prayed for the bear's soul (kebokai) so its spirit would rest. [s5]
- **Korea, Tangun (Dangun) founding myth.** A bear and a tiger asked to become human and were sent into a cave on mugwort and garlic, out of the sun. The tiger grew impatient and left; the bear stayed and became a woman, mother of Tangun, founder of Korea. A bear and a tiger in one story is a direct mirror of this crossing (a Tiger year inside a patient, cave-keeping Cancer). Britannica says 'bear'; it does not name the species, so do not claim it is the moon bear. [s6]
- **China, Mencius.** 魚與熊掌 yú yǔ xióngzhǎng: 'I like fish, and I also like bear's paw; if I cannot have both I let the fish go.' Mencius uses it to say he would give up life for righteousness (舍生取義 shě shēng qǔ yì). The bear's paw stands for the harder, higher choice. [s7]
- **China, folk names.** The animal is called 月熊 yuè xióng and 月牙熊 yuèyá xióng (moon bear, crescent-moon bear) for the chest mark, and colloquially 黑瞎子 hēixiāzi, 'the black blind one'. [s9]
- **Taiwan, Atayal, Taroko, Bunun and others.** Hunting the Formosan black bear (a subspecies of the moon bear) is taboo in 10 Indigenous communities. The source gives the taboo only, not its story; do not add a reason. [s8]
- **China, proverb (Tiger half).** 一山不容二虎 yī shān bù róng èr hǔ, one mountain cannot hold two tigers: two strong people cannot share one place. Already on the live page. [s15]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Ainu iyomante bear ceremony (concerns the brown bear in Hokkaido, not the moon bear). Shijing 斯干 Si Gan dream line 維熊維羆 (bear dream as omen of a son): source page would not open, so not verified here. Generic 'moon bear spirit animal' blog meanings: none found with a traditional source.

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: cardinal Water (Cancer) feeding yang Wood (Tiger). Cancer guards a soft core; the Tiger guards ground. The Moon Bear is the guardian who climbs: brave enough to face a threat, but its first move is upward, into a nest of bent branches with a view. The Moon rules Cancer and the moon is printed on this bear's chest, so the sign's planet is literally worn on the body. Water feeds Wood, so feeling is the fuel for the courage.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: protection that does not ask to be thanked; nerve in a crisis; long memory for kindness; a readable warmth (the crescent) that makes strangers trust fast; patience in the dark season (the bear who stayed in the cave).
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the Tiger's suspicion plus the crab's shell. Retreating from care the same way it retreats from danger; keeping its own den off-limits (one mountain, two tigers); carrying everything alone until it has to den for a whole winter. The Tangun story names the split: the tiger half wants to walk out of the cave.
- **Love.** Our reading: devoted and provider-minded; loves by shielding. Opens slowly, then is a keeper for life. Needs a partner who does not chase it up the tree and does not read the retreat as rejection.
- **Money.** Our reading: Cancer stores for winter, the Tiger takes bold swings. Tends to build a deep reserve (fat for the den), then make one brave, large move for the people it protects. Risk: overspending on others while hiding its own needs.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: climbs first, stands tall if cornered (the mane), rarely fights for nothing. Goes quiet and distant, then can flare in Tiger temper. Helpful habit: name the retreat out loud so it is not read as a verdict.

## Careers
- **Emergency medicine, firefighting, search and rescue in mountains.** Our reading: Tiger nerve plus Cancer's urge to shelter; the bear that climbs to see the whole slope.
- **Child protection, family law, social work.** Our reading: guarding the soft thing is the whole crossing; the Moon is the sign of home and family.
- **Wildlife ranger, forest guard, conservation enforcement.** Our reading: Tiger holds territory, Wood is the forest; echoes the Taiwanese taboo on harming the bear.
- **Security, risk and safety leadership.** Our reading: reads threat early, builds high vantage points, plans for winter.
- **Hospitality with a private base: innkeeper, chef-owner, retreat host.** Our reading: Cancer's home instinct with a den to retreat to between guests.
- **Ethics, compliance, ombudsman roles.** Our reading: Mencius's bear's paw, the harder right choice, held with Tiger stubbornness.

## Candidate lines
1. Somewhere in the canopy there is a platform of bent branches that only you have seen, and you are on it the minute a voice below changes pitch.
2. Your crescent shows before you say a word. Strangers trust it on sight, and you spend the next year deciding whether they were right to.
3. In the Korean story the bear stayed in the dark cave on mugwort and garlic while the tiger walked out. You carry both of them, and in a hard winter the bear usually wins.
4. The Tohoku hunters said the bear with no moon on its chest belonged to the mountain god. Part of you wants to be that unmarked bear, unreadable, and part of you knows the moon is why anyone climbs up to find you.
5. One mountain, two tigers: you will share almost anything except the den, and whoever earns a place in it gets a keeper for life.
6. Mencius let the fish go and took the bear's paw. Offer you a choice between safe and loyal and you take loyal, then climb a tree to recover from it.
