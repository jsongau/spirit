# Whale Shark: research dossier

Pisces x Year of the Dragon. Element: Earth (Dragon), with Pisces Water. Slug `whale-shark`. Researched 2026-11-08. Lines marked 'Ours' are our interpretation; everything else carries a source id (listed at the end).

## Headline findings

- The Dragon is the ruler of seas and rain in Chinese myth, and the carp that leaps the Dragon Gate becomes a dragon. This is the strongest traditional bridge between a fish sign and a Dragon year. [cn-dragon, longmen]
- Ningaloo coral spawning happens just over a week after the full moon, which is waning gibbous to last quarter: a sourced reason for the page's Waning Gibbous power moon. [wa-spawning]
- Japanese fishermen called it the Ebisu-shark, a luck-bringer that drives fish to the boats; Kenyan legend says angels dropped coins on its back. Fortune-teller gold. [ebisu, natgeo-kenya]

## The whale shark as a symbol

- **Japan.** Fishermen tied whale sharks to Ebisu, god of fishermen and luck; the animal was called the Ebisu-shark because it brings in masses of fish and was believed to protect fishermen. Our reading: the big quiet presence that brings abundance to everyone near it. [ebisu, whale-worship]
- **Kenya (Diani coast).** Locals call it papa shillingi, the shark covered in shillings. The legend says God so admired its beauty that he had angels drop gold and silver coins on its back, which is why the spots glitter when it surfaces. Our reading: a blessing you wear on your back without asking for it. [natgeo-kenya]
- **Madagascar, Indonesia.** Malagasy marokintana means many stars; in Sulawesi and Papua it is gurano bintang and in East Java geger lintang, star-backed. Several unrelated coastal peoples looked at the spots and saw a night sky. Our reading: a fish that carries the stars, which suits a Water sign tied to dreams and the heavens. [wiki-ws]
- **Vietnam, Philippines, Taiwan.** Vietnamese ca ong, literally sir fish (an honorific); the Philippine butanding appears on the 100-peso note; in Taiwan a folk name is 豆腐鲨 (dòufu shā), tofu shark, for its soft white flesh. Respect, national pride and plain affection. Note: the Vietnamese whale-deity cult of Ca Ong is mainly about whales; we could not source whale-shark worship specifically, so do not claim it. [wiki-ws, zh-ws]
- **China (Zhuangzi).** The opening of the Zhuangzi tells of 鯤 (kūn), a fish so huge no one knows how many thousand li long it is, which turns into the giant bird 鵬 (péng) and rises on the wind. A giant fish that becomes something that flies. Our reading: the Pisces fish with Dragon-sized ambition hidden in it. [peng]
- **China (Dragon Gate).** In the legend 鯉魚跳龍門 (lǐyú tiào lóngmén), the carp that leaps the waterfall at the Dragon Gate becomes a dragon; the idiom means a sudden rise in standing earned by effort. The cleanest traditional bridge between a fish (Pisces) and a dragon. Our reading: the slow swimmer who, at the right season, rises. [longmen]
- **Spirit-animal / totem meanings for whale shark.** Unsourced, do not use. Online whale-shark spirit-animal pages are uncited modern content.

## The fusion (all ours)

- **Temperament.** Ours: Pisces water inside a yang Earth year. In Chinese myth the dragon is the lord of seas and rain, so this crossing is the one place on the wheel where the Dragon is fully at home in Pisces water. You tend to carry a big, quiet presence: people feel you before you say anything. Mutable Pisces gives you the give and the drift; the Dragon gives you a spine and a sense of scale. Wu Xing reading: Earth contains Water, like banks giving a river its course, so your Dragon side tends to give your feelings a shape.
- **Gifts.** Ours: you take in a lot without getting flooded. You often sense where the good season or the right people are before others do, and you turn up there. Like the Ebisu-shark, you tend to bring luck to whoever swims near you, often without noticing. Patience reads as calm authority.
- **Shadow.** Ours: the Pisces drift plus Dragon pride. You can assume your size protects you and miss fast-moving trouble. You can disappear for long stretches and expect people to still be at the reef when you come back. The two fish pull opposite ways, and the Dragon hates admitting it is torn, so the indecision can show up as grand silence.
- **Love.** Ours: you love by being steady and by returning. You are drawn to people who feel safe enough to rest near. You need a partner who does not take your slow replies as coldness, and you do best when you say out loud when you will be back. Venus exalted in Pisces: generous, romantic, sometimes too forgiving.
- **Money.** Ours: Jupiter's luck and the Dragon's fortune symbolism, filtered through a feeder that waits for the bloom. You tend to do well with slow, compounding money (long holds, steady clients, the same good waters each year) and badly with chasing. Watch for being so generous that everyone near you eats first.
- **Conflict.** Ours: you keep swimming. Your first move is to stay calm and carry on at your own pace, which can feel serene to you and dismissive to the other person. The Dragon can flare late and big after a long Pisces silence. Naming the problem early, while it is small, saves you the eruption.

## Fact audit of the live page

| Fact on page | Verdict | Note | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Largest living fish, reaching up to 12 m | OK | Largest living fish is correct. 12 m is a common figure but understates the record: the largest reliably recorded was 18.8 m. Suggest 'commonly 12 m, recorded to about 18 m'. | wiki-ws |
| Processes approximately 3,000 liters of water per hour through its gill rakers | WRONG | Far too low. Motta et al. 2010 estimated about 600 cubic meters (roughly 600,000 liters) per hour for a 6 m shark during surface ram feeding; even the popular low figure is 'over 6,000 liters per hour'. Also the filter is the gill pads/filtering pads; fix the number in trait 01 and the Capacity gate. | motta2010, sharksorg, wiki-ws |
| Feeds by active suction feeding or passive ram feeding | OK | Both modes are described in the literature (Motta et al. 2010 studied ram filter feeding; suction feeding is also documented). | motta2010, wiki-ws |
| Aggregates at Ningaloo, Isla Mujeres, the Maldives with seasonal timing tied to coral spawning and plankton blooms; individuals return across years | OK | Ningaloo coral spawning is March to April, just over a week after the full moon; whale sharks are there March to August. About 35% of individuals re-sighted at the same site in more than one year; some Ningaloo sharks seen for 19 years. 'Near-clockwork' is fair framing. | wa-spawning, ningaloo-fidelity, wiki-ws |
| Unique pattern of white spots and stripes used for photo-ID, like a fingerprint | OK | Pattern is unique to each individual and used for photo identification. | wiki-ws |
| Tracked to depths of 1,900 meters | OK | Deepest recorded dive 1,928 m (Tyminski et al. 2015, Gulf of Mexico). Could say 'nearly 2,000 m'. | tyminski, wiki-ws |
| Estimated to live 70 to 150 years; sexual maturity at about 25 to 30 years | UNVERIFIABLE | No source found for 70 to 150. Wikipedia gives an estimate of 80 to 130 years; the oldest validated age is 50 years (Ong et al. 2020, which confirmed bands are annual). Male maturity about 25 years (Perry et al. 2018); females later and less certain. Suggest '80 to 130 years by estimate; individuals of 50 confirmed'. The page's '150-year fish' line should go. | wiki-ws, ong2020 |
