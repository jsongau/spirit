## The Fork-tailed Drongo as a symbol
- **Chinese (Rooster half), Hanshi Waizhuan.** The rooster's virtue of 仁 rén is calling the flock when it finds food, and of 信 xìn is a call that never lies about the hour. The drongo inverts both: it calls to make others drop their food. It keeps the third virtue, 勇 yǒng, attacking much larger raptors at the nest. This inversion is the sharpest new idea for the page; label it as our reading built on s5. [s5]
- **Greek (Mercury, Gemini's ruler, is the Roman Hermes).** The Homeric Hymn to Hermes calls the newborn god 'a robber, a cattle driver, a bringer of dreams, a watcher by night, a thief at the gates'. On his first day he steals Apollo's cattle and 'reversed the marks of their hoofs, making the front behind and the hind before', then lies about it; he also makes the tortoise 'a singer' by inventing the lyre. Trickster thief with a borrowed voice, under the planet that rules Gemini. [s6] [s1]
- **Kalahari field science, as story.** Pied babblers forage better with a drongo sentinel on watch, even though the same drongo steals from them (Radford et al. 2011). The liar is also the lookout: drongos give true alarms too, and false ones about three times as often (s9). [s11] [s9]
- **Malagasy (word origin).** The English word drongo comes from Malagasy, Betsimisaraka dialect, first used for the crested drongo of Madagascar (Malagasy railovy). Trivia only; do not use as a hook, and note it is a different species. [s13]
- **Southern African names.** Zulu iNtengu; Afrikaans mikstertbyvanger, literally fork-tail bee-catcher (the literal gloss is ours from the Afrikaans words; the source gives only the name). The bird mainly hawks bees and insects stirred up by herds. [s12] [s15]
- **Taiwanese (relative species, black drongo).** The black drongo, Dicrurus macrocercus, is known in Taiwan as 烏鶖 or 烏秋 (wūqiū); it chases kites and crows out of its territory and sits on the backs of cattle. This is a related Asian species, not the fork-tailed drongo, and the source is a Wikipedia mirror. Use only if named honestly as the drongo's Asian cousin. [s14]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Claims that the drongo is an African 'king of birds', a messenger of the ancestors, or a fire-bringer. I found nothing traditional for the fork-tailed drongo; Malagasy tales found online concern other birds or could not be opened. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Air (Gemini, ruled by Mercury the messenger and thief) meets yin Metal (the Rooster, the bird of the honest call). Two talkers in one body, one trickster and one herald. The drongo lives in the gap between them: a voice that announces danger, sometimes truly, sometimes for profit. Metal gives the edge and the nerve; Air gives the range of voices.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: an ear for exactly the call a listener will obey; fast reading of when a line has gone stale; real courage when its own nest is threatened, the Rooster's 勇 kept intact; usefulness as a lookout even to people it teases.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the inverted virtues. A call meant to share food becomes a call that takes it; the trustworthy crow at dawn becomes the false alarm. Afterwards comes the Gemini twin's question: which voice was really mine? Rooster boastfulness can make the cleverness need an audience.
- **Love.** Our reading: quick, funny, a flirt who imitates your laugh back to you. Trust grows slowly on the other side because this bird can do any voice. Once committed, it defends the nest against anything, hawk or human. Needs a partner who enjoys the wit and asks for the plain voice now and then.
- **Money.** Our reading: lives by opportunity and timing, earning about a quarter of its keep from other people's effort (thefts are about a quarter of daily calories, s9, s10). Good at commission, negotiation and side income. Risk: short-term wins that cost reputation over time, the babblers that stop listening.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: talks its way around most fights and changes tactic the moment one stops working. Over what is truly its own, it stops talking and dives, size be damned. Hard lesson: not every argument needs a new voice; sometimes the first true call was enough.

## Careers (our reading)
- **Sales, negotiation, fundraising.** Our reading: the right call at the right second moves people; Flower's drongos switch calls after a failed attempt (s10).
- **Voice acting, impressions, comedy, podcasting.** Our reading: at least 51 alarm calls, most of them borrowed (s10); Mercury rules speech.
- **Security, risk analysis, fraud investigation.** Our reading: the drongo sentinel that raises real alarms for the group (s11); it takes a deceiver to catch one.

- **Field guide, ranger, wildlife tracker.** Our reading: a bird that rides on the backs of large animals and reads the whole savanna from its perch (s8, s12).

## Candidate lines
1. A drongo carries at least 45 borrowed alarms and only six of its own. Your best line might be someone else's, picked up years ago and delivered at the second it will land.
2. The old rooster earned its virtue by calling the flock when it found food. Your call can run the other way, and some part of you keeps count of which one you used today.
3. When the babblers stop jumping at your call, you switch to a starling's. Few people see how fast you read a face going bored, or how lightly you change the tune.
4. Under fifty grams, and still diving at a hawk near the nest. The talk is for the open savanna. Around your own people there is no talk, only the dive.
5. Babblers feed better with a drongo on watch, even one that steals from them. Friends keep you near for the same reason: your warnings are real more often than your mischief.
6. In love you ride on the back of something larger and catch what it stirs up, light and quick. The season you finally hang a nest in the fork, you bind it with spider silk and mean it.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 鸟 (rail__han and serial-han).
  鸟 is the simplified generic character for 'bird' (traditional 鳥 niǎo), not this animal, and it is simplified where the frigatebird page uses traditional. The fork-tailed drongo's Chinese name is 叉尾卷尾 chā wěi juǎn wěi (s8); 卷尾 juǎnwěi, 'curled tail', is the drongo family name and is written the same in traditional script, as in Taiwan's 大卷尾 dà juǎnwěi for the black drongo (s14). Short header option: 卷尾 juǎnwěi. Add pinyin either way. [s8] [s14]
- **OK (warthog UNVERIFIABLE)**: Hero: small enough to perch on a warthog's back, bold enough to dive at a hawk twice its size.
  Perching on the backs of large mammals is sourced (s8); s7 names elephants and giraffes, s15 buffalo and elephants. No source opened names warthogs specifically. Mobbing larger raptors is sourced (s12, s7). [s7] [s8] [s12] [s15]
- **OK (reword)**: False alarm: drongos stole food from meerkats and pied babblers in roughly half of their kleptoparasitic attempts by giving a false alarm call.
  Flower 2011: 'half of these thefts involved a false alarm' (s9). The source counts thefts, not attempts; say 'about half of their thefts used a false alarm'. Drongo thefts make up about a quarter of daily calories (s9, s10). [s9] [s10]
- **WRONG (misattributed)**: Borrowed voice: drongos frequently mimic the alarm calls of the very species they target; mimics are structurally indistinguishable from the real thing; fooled meerkats and babblers in playbacks.
  In Flower 2011 the 'indistinguishable' finding compared the drongo's FALSE alarms with its own TRUE alarms, not mimics with their models; drongos mimicked glossy starling, crowned plover and pied babbler alarms, and mimicry of meerkat alarms was unconfirmed at that point (s9). Playbacks did make both meerkats and babblers abandon food (s9). Later work lists meerkat alarm mimicry (s7, s8) and at least 51 alarm types, 45 borrowed (s10). [s9] [s10] [s7] [s8]
- **OK**: The switch: targets habituate to a repeated false alarm; drongos change to a different or rarer mimicked alarm to restore the effect.
  Flower, Gribble and Ridley 2014 (Science): babblers respond less to repeated alarms; swapping the call resets vigilance; drongos switch on about three quarters of repeat attempts, more often after a failure (s10). 'Rarer' is not in the source; cut it. [s10]
- **WRONG (weight inflated) / OK (mobbing)**: The mob: weighing around fifty grams, attacks and mobs far larger predators including hawks, especially near the nest.
  Weight is 34 to 48 g (s8); say 'under fifty grams'. Mobbing larger raptors and fierce nest defence, even against people, are sourced (s12, s7). [s8] [s12] [s7]
- **OK**: The forked tail: red-eyed, all-black bird with a narrow tail that splays into a deep fork.
  s7: narrow fork-shaped tail, black plumage, red-brownish eyes; s8: adults have an intense red iris, juveniles brown. [s7] [s8]
- **OK**: The flush: found near wild and domestic mammals and other foragers, hawking insects their movement flushes.
  s7 (elephants, giraffes), s15 (Cape buffalo, elephant herds), s8 (perches on their backs). [s7] [s8] [s15]
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Reference list: NCBI 'Aggressive hosts are undeterred by a cuckoo's hawk mimicry, but probably make good foster parents'.
  Listed as a source but no visible fact on the page uses it, and PMC blocked my fetch. Cut it from the list unless a fact cites it. 
- **OK**: Dragon is the Rooster's secret friend; Earth feeds Metal.
  Six-harmony pair and generating cycle correct (s3). No Purple Star star assignment and no January/February lunar note on the page, both correctly absent for Gemini. [s3]
