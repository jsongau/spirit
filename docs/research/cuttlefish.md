## The Cuttlefish as a symbol
- **Chinese, name lore (Nanyue Zhi 南越志, quoted in Bencao Gangmu 本草綱目).** The name 烏賊 wūzéi, 'crow's bane', is explained by a story: the cuttlefish loves to eat crows, so it floats on the surface as if dead; a crow lands to peck it, and the cuttlefish wraps it up and pulls it under. Text: 其性嗜烏，每自浮水上，飛烏見之以為死而啄之，乃卷取入水而食之，因名烏賊 (qí xìng shì wū, měi zì fú shuǐ shàng, fēi wū jiàn zhī yǐ wéi sǐ ér zhuó zhī, nǎi juǎn qǔ rù shuǐ ér shí zhī, yīn míng wūzéi). Folk etymology, not biology: cuttlefish do not hunt crows. [c6]
- **Chinese, Tang dynasty (Duan Chengshi, Youyang Zazu 酉陽雜俎, 'Guang Dong Zhi').** Swindlers east of the Yangzi wrote contracts in cuttlefish ink to cheat people out of goods; the writing looked like pale ink and after a year the characters vanished, leaving blank paper. Text: 江東人或取墨書契，以脫人財物。書跡如淡墨，逾年字消，唯空紙耳 (Jiāngdōng rén huò qǔ mò shū qì, yǐ tuō rén cáiwù. Shū jì rú dàn mò, yú nián zì xiāo, wéi kōng zhǐ ěr). Later notes (Guixin Zazhi 癸辛雜識) say half a year. A promise written in disappearing ink is the sharpest symbol this animal has. [c6]
- **Chinese, Tang dynasty (Youyang Zazu).** Origin legend: the First Emperor of Qin, touring the east, threw his counting bag (算袋 suàndài, a bag for tallies and writing tools) into the sea, and it became the cuttlefish, shaped like the bag with two very long straps. Text: 昔秦皇東遊，棄算袋於海，化為此魚 (xī Qín Huáng dōng yóu, qì suàndài yú hǎi, huà wéi cǐ yú). Ties the animal to counting, records and the official's kit, which suits Mercury-ruled Gemini. [c6]
- **Ancient Greek (Aristotle, History of Animals, Book IX).** 'Of molluscs the sepia is the most cunning, and is the only species that employs its dark liquid for the sake of concealment.' Aristotle also records it showing itself in front of its ink and then retreating back into it, and hunting fish, even mullets, with its long tentacles. [c7]
- **Greco-Roman and European.** The Greek and Latin word for cuttlefish, sepia, became the name of the brown pigment made from its ink, valued in the Greco-Roman world and later as a writing and drawing ink. The animal literally gave a color to written records. [c8]
- **Chinese idiom (year animal, use sparingly).** 打草驚蛇 dǎ cǎo jīng shé, 'beat the grass and startle the snake' (the page's proverb): from Zheng Wenbao's Nantang Jinshi, the corrupt magistrate Wang Lu who saw his own guilt in a complaint against a subordinate; 13th of the Thirty-Six Stratagems. Real and correctly glossed on the page, but it is Snake lore shared by all 12 Snake animals. [c10]
- **Unsourced, do not use.** Generic 'cuttlefish spirit animal means adaptability and shape-shifting' claims on totem blogs. No traditional source found. No Indigenous cuttlefish tradition located in a source I could open. (no source)

## The fusion (our reading)
- **Temperament.** Our reading: mutable Air (Gemini, the twins, Mercury) on yin Fire (Snake, still and private). Gemini supplies the two faces; the Snake supplies the stillness and the timing. The Cuttlefish is the double signal held perfectly still until the strike. Fire is fed by Air in the Western pairing, so the Snake's slow heat gets oxygen from Gemini's speed; the risk is that it flares.
- **Gifts.** Our reading: reading a room and answering it with the right surface; speaking to two people at once and being honest with both; patience before a move (the Nanyue Zhi cuttlefish floats 'dead' until the crow lands); a counting, record-keeping mind, the emperor's tally bag of the Youyang Zazu.
- **Shadow.** Our reading: the vanishing-ink contract. Promises made in a color that fades after a year; agreeing in the moment and being gone when it comes due. Gemini's noncommittal streak plus the Snake's secrecy can turn fluency into evasion. The ink cloud as a habit: when cornered, you show yourself, then retreat into the cloud (Aristotle).
- **Love.** Our reading: courts by matching, mirrors the partner fast, and keeps the fixed color hidden. Needs someone who notices which pattern stays when no audience is there, and who asks for the promise in permanent ink without making it a trap.
- **Money.** Our reading: sharp with numbers and deals (Mercury, the tally bag), drawn to quality (the Snake's materialist streak). Risk: too many side bets kept quiet; contracts and commitments that are vague on purpose.
- **Conflict.** Our reading: avoids the open fight (Gemini) and prefers to wait it out unseen (Snake). When pressed, shows a strong display, then vanishes. Growth edge: say the plain thing once, in a color that does not change.

## Fact audit of the live page
- **WRONG**: Header hanzi 墨 (rail__han and serial-han), shown with pinyin mò
  墨 mò means ink, not cuttlefish. The animal's name is 烏賊 wūzéi (traditional; simplified 乌贼), also 墨魚 mòyú (simplified 墨鱼). Use 烏賊 wūzéi, or 墨魚 mòyú if the ink link is wanted. [c9]
- **OK**: Color change is produced by chromatophores, pigment organs controlled directly by the nervous system, shifting appearance within seconds to match the sea floor.
  ADW: changes color and texture 'within seconds' via chromatophores. Wikipedia describes three chromatophore layers. Neural control is standard cephalopod biology. [c11] [c8]
- **OK (soften)**: Mourning cuttlefish males show courtship to a female on one side and a female pattern to a rival male on the other, 'letting him court without provoking a fight he would likely lose'.
  Brown, Garwood and Williamson 2012, Biology Letters, Sepia plangon. Species named correctly. Add the real limits: seen only when a single rival male was present, used about 39% of the time in that context. 'A fight he would likely lose' is not in the source; cut it. [c12]
- **WRONG**: Before striking, cuttlefish can produce a 'Passing Cloud' display ... shown to coincide with prey freezing in place just before the strike. Cited as 'Passing Cloud displays during hunting in Sepia officinalis (Adamo et al., 2006)'.
  Wrong title and wrong finding. The real paper is 'Signaling to the enemy? Body pattern expression and its response to external cues during hunting in the cuttlefish Sepia officinalis' (Biol Bull 2006). It reports that Passing Cloud displays and arm darkening dropped after the cuttlefish saw a model bird; it does not show prey freezing. Closest real finding: in broadclub cuttlefish (Sepia latimanus), a downward-moving stripe display made crabs less likely to react to an approaching predator (Santon et al. 2025). Rewrite or cut. [c13] [c14]
- **OK**: Two long tentacles tipped with suckered clubs shoot out to seize prey before the shorter arms draw it in.
  ADW: eight arms and two longer tentacles, all with suckers; shoots out the two longer tentacles to grab prey. Aristotle records the same. [c11] [c7]
- **PARTLY UNVERIFIABLE**: One of the largest brain-to-body ratios of any invertebrate; lab studies show it will wait for preferred food, 'a form of self-control not previously documented outside vertebrates'.
  Brain ratio: Wikipedia says 'one of the largest ... of all invertebrates' (OK). Self-control: Schnell et al. 2021, Proc R Soc B, Sepia officinalis, waits of 50 to 130 seconds (OK). The novelty claim is not what the press releases say: Cambridge and Phys.org call it the first link between self-control and learning ability in a non-primate. Replace 'not previously documented outside vertebrates' with that. [c8] [c15] [c16]
- **OK**: Sepia officinalis typically lives one to two years, breeds once, and dies shortly after spawning.
  ADW: lifespan one to two years; 'typically breed only once'; both sexes die after spawning; females may lay eggs several times at the end of life. [c11]
- **OK**: Proverb 打草惊蛇 dǎ cǎo jīng shé, labelled 'a proverb we tie to the Year of the Snake'
  Real idiom, correctly glossed; origin Nantang Jinshi (Wang Lu); 13th of the Thirty-Six Stratagems. Page shows simplified 惊; traditional is 驚. [c10]
- **OK**: Purple Star (Zi Wei Dou Shu) claims
  No star is assigned from the sign pair on this page; the text correctly says a chart is cast from lunar birth date and hour. (no source)
- **WRONG**: Source list: 'NCBI PubMed · Passing Cloud displays during hunting in Sepia officinalis (Adamo et al., 2006)'
  No paper with this title. Correct citation in c13. [c13]
